Tony Sheng's Owned Audience Notes

01

Note 61

Online business is booming, and I wonder if I can also take a slice of the pie. Tony shares some observations and thoughts:

01 Who takes responsibility, who makes the decision. There's a lot of content on the internet, and you can definitely find a video or article that supports your view. However, they won't be responsible for your decision, nor can they bear any responsibility. Take responsibility for your own decisions.

02 WeChat Channels have been getting a lot of attention in recent years, and they are indeed opportunities for ordinary people. However, blindly entering the field comes with a cost, at least in terms of time. Time is our life, and it's expensive. Clarify whether you really want to do this—it's the key.

03 For example, after consulting with a user, I advised him not to start live streaming yet. First, he should clarify his business and get the most basic product process running smoothly. Rushing into live streaming not only scatters his focus but also makes it unlikely to see immediate results. Focus on surviving first, then consider other things.

04 There are many types of online business models, and promoting across multiple platforms and channels is fine. However, energy is limited, so spend your time where you can get results. The long-term value lies in our commitment to important matters and consistent investment. Where you spend your time, that's where the value is created.

05 Why is it hard to succeed when blindly following trends and listening to so-called advice? Because you're not analyzing and thinking based on your own situation. Like reading, people often like to get book lists, but they may not be suitable for us, or we may not even need them. With so much information coming in, the next step is to make your own decisions and carefully weigh them.

06 If I have a judgment principle for whether to transition to online business, it's whether you can endure a period of no income. It could be a few months, or even a year or two, or even longer. After all, online isn't just moving your offline business up. When you enter the online space, there's already a group of people who have been building their presence there for a long time.

07 Developing new businesses, expanding existing channels, and trying to transition online—I support you in exploring, but you must objectively and calmly analyze. If you're unclear, ask those who have done it well and see what challenges they faced. It's more important to understand the common types of mistakes than to focus on how to succeed.

I'm done. Thank you for your support if you're someone who can make things happen!
