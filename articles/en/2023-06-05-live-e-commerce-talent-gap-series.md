The main argument of the article is that live e-commerce is a rapidly developing, highly specialized, and highly promising content e-commerce field, but it currently faces serious talent shortages and challenges in talent cultivation. The purpose of the article is to analyze the causes and impacts of the talent gap in live e-commerce and to propose solutions and recommendations.

Live e-commerce requires more professional, career-oriented, pragmatic, and innovative talent, not speculative, showy, gambling, or underhanded talent.

Live e-commerce refers to a content e-commerce model that showcases products, facilitates interaction, and promotes sales through video live streaming. In recent years, with the development of internet technology and changes in consumer demand, live e-commerce has become a rapidly growing, highly specialized, and highly promising content e-commerce field.

> According to statistics, China's live e-commerce market size reached 961.5 billion yuan in 2020, a year-on-year growth of 226%, and is expected to exceed 150 billion yuan in 2021. At the same time, live e-commerce has also created a number of new professions and positions, such as hosts, operators, guides, and supply chain roles, providing a large number of employment opportunities for society.

However, the live e-commerce industry also faces some serious challenges and problems, with the most prominent being the talent gap and difficulties in talent cultivation. According to surveys, the talent gap in China's live e-commerce industry currently exceeds 1 million people, and this number is still growing. Why is this happening?

The two charts below show 28 live e-commerce-related job roles. A mature host generally requires 4–10 operators, meaning that some roles are often held by one person at the same time.

The live e-commerce industry has gone through the process of bubble formation, deflation, and hollowing out. From 2019 to 2020, the live e-commerce industry experienced explosive growth, with various platforms, brands, and hosts flooding in, creating a bustling yet chaotic market.

However, starting from the second half of 2020, with the tightening of regulatory policies, consumer fatigue, and market saturation, the live e-commerce industry entered a phase of deflation. Many unregulated, unprofessional, and untrustworthy platforms, brands, and hosts were eliminated or transformed, and the market began to return to rationality and quality.

But during this process, a phenomenon of hollowing out emerged, where some outstanding talents left the industry due to being discouraged or losing confidence, leading to talent loss and a generation gap.

There is a contradiction between the demand for and supply of talent in the live e-commerce industry. After the process of deflation and hollowing out, the live e-commerce industry remains a rapidly growing and highly competitive field, with a strong demand for talent.

However, on the supply side, there is a serious shortage and mismatch.

On one hand, live e-commerce is an emerging and special field that requires a wide range of knowledge, skills, and qualities, such as product knowledge, sales techniques, interactive abilities, and data analysis. These are not something that the general education system can provide or cultivate.

On the other hand, live e-commerce is also a field with high risk and pressure, requiring a lot of time, effort, and resources, and facing various uncertainties and changes. These are not something that everyone can accept or adapt to.

> According to a report by iResearch, China's live e-commerce market size reached 961.5 billion yuan in 2020, a year-on-year growth of 122%, and is expected to exceed 1.2 trillion yuan in 2021. Live e-commerce has become the mainstream form of content e-commerce in China, covering categories such as cosmetics, clothing, food, and home goods, attracting over 400 million users and millions of hosts.

The phenomenon of bubble formation, deflation, and hollowing out in the live e-commerce industry. With the popularity of live e-commerce, some negative phenomena have emerged, such as fake orders, fake traffic, fake reviews, false advertising, and low-quality products, leading to a crisis of consumer trust and an increase in complaints.

At the same time, live e-commerce also faces pressure from homogenization, vulgarity, and lack of differentiation, leading to a decline in user engagement and an increase in user churn. These phenomena reflect the trend of bubble formation, deflation, and hollowing out in the live e-commerce industry.

The contradiction between the demand for and supply of talent in the live e-commerce industry. Live e-commerce is a highly specialized and complex field, involving multiple aspects such as content planning, product selection, host training, operations management, data analysis, and marketing promotion. However, the supply of talent in the live e-commerce industry is severely insufficient, not only in quantity but also in quality.

> According to a survey by Niuren Planet, China's live e-commerce industry currently needs about 2 million talents, but the market only has about 500,000, creating a gap of 1.5 million talents. Among these talents, less than 10% possess professional capabilities and qualities, while the remaining 90% are half-baked or ignorant.

The lack of talent training and evaluation systems in the live e-commerce industry is a key reason for the talent gap and low quality. Currently, the live e-commerce industry lacks a complete talent training and evaluation system.

There are no professional and authoritative live e-commerce education institutions or courses on the market. Many so-called training programs are either deceptive or scams. On the other hand, the live e-commerce industry also lacks a unified standard for professional qualification certification. Many companies or platforms rely on their own experience or intuition for hiring or evaluating people.

In 2023, content e-commerce is still being vigorously developed, and the trend of live streaming is unstoppable. In the future, it may truly become a situation where everyone is live streaming, and entertainment and mass traffic content will be more favored.

Live e-commerce is the first retail model in the history of global commerce to have a person as the ultimate node. It leverages the advantages of live streaming platforms such as traffic, interaction, and trust to establish a new shopping relationship between hosts and viewers, thus achieving rapid product conversion and sales.

Live e-commerce not only provides consumers with a richer, more convenient, and more interesting shopping experience but also creates significant value for hosts, brands, and platforms.

> The speed and scale of live e-commerce development are astonishing. In 2017, the national transaction volume of live e-commerce was over 30 billion yuan. Last year, it exceeded 400 billion yuan, and this year it has broken through 1 trillion yuan. It is expected to surpass 2 trillion yuan next year. Its share in internet retail e-commerce will rise from 0.5% to 14.3%. According to data from Chanh Mama, in 2020, more than 50% of internet users had made at least one purchase through live e-commerce each month. Even more surprising is the question of whether people prefer live e-commerce or traditional e-commerce. Surprisingly, 43% of people prefer live e-commerce, and 35% prefer traditional e-commerce. This is a trend that is difficult to change.

Live e-commerce meets the social needs of consumers. During the pandemic, many people felt lonely and bored due to isolation and staying at home. By watching live streams, they could interact in real time with hosts and other viewers, experiencing a sense of warmth and belonging.

At the same time, live streaming allows consumers to see the real effects and usage scenarios of products, increasing their trust and purchasing desire.

Live e-commerce meets the entertainment needs of consumers. Live streaming is not only a shopping method but also an entertainment method.

Many hosts have strong appeal and influence. They not only introduce products but also share their lives, hobbies, and stories, making viewers feel relaxed and happy.

At the same time, live streaming also has various interactive elements and games, such as lotteries, gift-giving, quizzes, and rankings, giving viewers more participation and fun.

In live streaming, consumers can enjoy more discounts and benefits, such as time-limited discounts, coupons, red packets, and cashback, making them feel they are getting more than they pay for. At the same time, consumers can support their favorite hosts by purchasing the products they recommend, expressing their taste and attitude.

Live e-commerce is a new retail model that aligns with the trends of the times and consumer psychology. It not only changes consumers' shopping habits and experiences but also transforms the business models and competitive landscapes of hosts, brands, and platforms.

In the future, live streaming will help China solve 25 to 30 million new employment opportunities. This prediction may further increase.

The talent cultivation issue in live e-commerce involves multiple aspects, including hosts, operations, technology, and supply chains. Each aspect requires professional talent to support. However, the current talent market for live e-commerce is not yet mature, lacking systematic cultivation mechanisms and standardized evaluation systems.

The more water-talent, the bigger the bubble. Due to the popularity of live e-commerce, a large number of investments and entrepreneurs have entered this field. However, not everyone has sufficient professional qualities and capabilities. Some people are only interested in making quick money, not focusing on quality and service, and even using improper means to attract traffic and sales.

These water-talents can bring negative impacts to the industry, damaging user trust and consumption willingness, and causing resource waste and market chaos. This situation is similar to the APP development talent bubble around 2011–2012, when the rise of mobile internet led to a surge in APP development demand, but also resulted in many low-quality, repetitive, and insecure apps, causing problems for users and the industry.

Live e-commerce will return from a celebrity industry to a relatively decent business. Currently, live e-commerce is still in a high-speed development phase, with many opportunities and dividends to seize. However, as the market gradually becomes saturated and regulated, live e-commerce will also face more competition and challenges.

In the future, live e-commerce will no longer be a celebrity industry that can be easily profitable. Instead, it will become a relatively decent business that requires more professionalism, differentiation, and innovation to stand out. Therefore, live e-commerce needs to place greater emphasis on talent cultivation to enhance its core competitiveness and sustainable development capabilities.

External support and training models are not viable. The base needs to be built first, and the key is to secure operational talent. Hosts are not cultivated but attracted. The goal of a short-video base is to attract more high-quality hosts to join, thereby gaining platform traffic shares, advertising revenue, and brand collaborations.

External support models are costly and low in effectiveness. The short-video industry is highly competitive, and all platforms are vying for high-quality hosts, so signing fees and revenue-sharing ratios are high, and some platforms may even operate at a loss.

Moreover, even if you sign a high-quality host, you cannot guarantee that they will replicate their previous success on a new platform. Because each platform has different user groups, recommendation algorithms, and content styles, hosts need to adapt and adjust.

Hosts also face issues such as fan loss, increased competition, and creative fatigue, leading to a decline in content quality or reduced update frequency.

Training models are difficult and time-consuming. The knowledge system of the short-video industry is still in an early stage of rapid development, making talent cultivation challenging. This is true for Douyin as well as for Kuaishou.

To cultivate high-quality hosts, you not only need to teach them shooting techniques, content strategies, and data analysis, but also to develop their personal style, creative thinking, and emotional expression. These abilities are not something that can be learned overnight; they require long-term practice and feedback. Moreover, training models also face high attrition rates, difficulty in measuring effectiveness, and challenges in building loyalty.

So, is there a better model?

That is the operational model. The operational model refers to creating an ecosystem suitable for short-video creation by providing high-quality services, environments, and atmospheres, naturally attracting hosts and encouraging them to stay long-term.

The core of the operational model is people.

## Training Institutions Targeting Gamblers: A Cautionary Tale

Training institutions catering to gamblers are capitalizing on the popularity of live streaming and the shortage of talent, attracting a large number of students eager to enter the field of live stream operations.

Their training content often consists of superficial, non-practical theories or outdated case studies that are no longer applicable to the current market.

Their training methods are typically one-time, without follow-up or guarantees. Once students pay, they only get a single lecture with no further support or guidance.

Their training objectives are often profit-driven rather than genuinely helping students improve their skills or find employment. They don’t care about students’ learning outcomes or job prospects, only about their wallets.

The results of their training often leave students disappointed and deceived. They spend a lot of money but learn nothing useful, no real skills, and no valid certificates or recommendations. They can only rely on luck in the live streaming industry or give up on their dreams.

Among the truly profitable, pragmatic players in the live stream industry, those who work hard to earn high returns are not interested in job hopping or showing off. The more water content, the bigger the bubble.

Industry participants, live stream platforms, companies, and professionals must collaborate to strengthen self-regulation and supervision, improve the overall quality of professionals, and reduce the risk of salary inflation. The following are some feasible strategies:

Transparent Disclosure of Realistic Profit Data: Live stream platforms and companies should truthfully disclose data such as return and refund rates and actual profitability when showcasing successful cases, avoiding exaggeration of profits and reducing the misleading effects of salary inflation.

Enhancing Vocational Training and Evaluation Mechanisms: Establish vocational training and evaluation systems within the industry to improve the professional competence and practical skills of professionals, reducing the presence of fake talents and false promotions.

Reasonable Salaries and Performance Evaluation: Live stream companies should establish reasonable salary and performance evaluation systems to avoid excessively high salary levels that lead to salary inflation, encouraging actual profitability and business skill improvement.

Strengthening Industry Self-Regulation and Supervision: The live stream industry should enhance self-regulation and establish industry regulatory bodies to standardize the behavior and operations of professionals, preventing salary inflation.

Improving the Overall Quality of Professionals: Live stream operators should focus on improving their overall quality, enhancing professional skills, business capabilities, and teamwork, reducing reliance on salary levels, and lowering the risk of salary inflation.

Multi-Dimensional Assessment of Professionals' Capabilities: In addition to focusing on the performance and salary levels of live stream operators, their actual work experience, project outcomes, and teamwork should also be considered comprehensively to avoid salary inflation caused by a single metric.

Enhancing Industry Information Transparency: Provide more transparent industry information, including real experiences and successful cases of professionals, helping professionals and employers more objectively assess the capabilities and experience of professionals, preventing the emergence of salary inflation.
