Why reading isn't a necessity, but buying books is?

As living standards continue to rise, more and more people are reading, yet many don't know what kind of books they actually need.

Some people have piles of books but don't really understand them, and this behavior is not advisable.

Reading isn't a necessity, but buying books is. Knowing what you need is key.

Understanding yourself and choosing the right books is important. Don't guess or make assumptions, or you might end up disappointed with the books you buy.

We should pay more attention to our interests, follow our cultural background and personality traits, and choose books that suit us.

For example, if you love stories, choose novels. If you like history and factual accounts, go for books in those categories.

Only by understanding yourself can you find the right books.

When buying books, choosing official channels and buying genuine editions is very important.

Nowadays, many people like to buy books from websites like Taobao, but this might lead them to purchase pirated copies.

Pirated books are often poorly edited and printed, which can negatively affect the reading experience.

Choosing official bookstores and buying genuine editions is essential.

When selecting books, we should also pay attention to some details. We can check the cover, title, author, and other information, as well as the edition, any errors, or missing pages.

Additionally, we can look up online reviews and consider what other readers think of the book, giving us a more comprehensive understanding of its content and features.

After gathering this information, we can better choose books that suit us.

Buying books requires not only understanding our needs but also choosing official channels, buying genuine editions, and paying attention to details.

Only then can we buy good books that we truly enjoy. Reading is a comfortable and pleasant experience, and this feeling is only achieved when we read books we love.

In fact, this is like a mirror. The inside and outside of a mirror can be completely opposite.

We've all stopped in front of a mirror, looking at our reflection, imagining a world that's almost the same as the real world but slightly different.

However, what you think might not be the truth. The world inside and outside the mirror might be completely opposite.

The concept of the mirror world comes from the nature of mirrors. What we see in a mirror is actually the result of light reflecting and flipping, so the mirror world seems to be the same as the real world.

Yet, the world inside and outside the mirror might have different characteristics. Scientists have proposed the mirror world hypothesis, suggesting that matter and antimatter in the mirror world might compete, and their rules and properties could be entirely opposite.

This means that an apple falling from the left in the real world would appear to fall from the right in the mirror.

The concept of the mirror world has sparked widespread discussion and research in physics and quantum mechanics. Some scientists believe that there might be channels connecting the mirror world to our world, which has led people to think about parallel universes and the multiverse.

However, since our understanding of the mirror world is still limited, our knowledge of it remains speculative and partial.

Beyond scientific speculation, the mirror world also appears in literature and art. Many writers and artists use the concept of the mirror world to explore metaphors and symbolic meanings of the real world.

For example, in Liu Cixin's science fiction novel *The Three-Body Problem*, the mirror world serves as another dimension in the universe, becoming a crucial part of the story.

The mirror world is a mysterious and intriguing concept that has sparked people's thinking about the relationship between the real world and the imagined world.

Although our understanding of the mirror world is still in its early stages of speculation, the concept itself has certainly enriched our understanding of the world.

Perhaps, as we explore the secrets behind the mirror, we are also reflecting on our relationship with the world.

In daily life, we all have our own views and understandings of the outside world, but are these views accurate? Are our thoughts influenced by certain misconceptions?

Just like the mirror effect, we need to recognize the blind spots in our thinking and find ways to address them.

One common thinking bias is "prejudice and stereotypes." Because we view the world from different angles and positions, we often develop varying degrees of prejudice or rely too heavily on stereotypes for judgment.

To avoid this, we should actively seek out different voices and opinions, especially those that differ from our own. Broaden our horizons, learn to think from others' perspectives, and understand both our own and others' viewpoints.

Another common thinking bias is "overconfidence." Sometimes we believe we have the correct judgment and decision-making abilities, leading us to become overly confident. This confidence is often not based on objective judgment or knowledge, but rather on our own subjective desires and emotions.

To prevent overconfidence, we should learn some scientific knowledge and methods, summarize past experiences and lessons, and continuously improve our thinking patterns and decision-making abilities.

We should also be cautious of the "fixed mindset" bias. A fixed mindset is a limited way of thinking that allows us to understand things quickly but ignores deeper ways of thinking. A person with a fixed mindset often struggles to accept new and different ideas and perspectives.

To avoid the influence of a fixed mindset, we can try to change our lifestyle and thinking patterns, avoid limiting ourselves to a fixed environment, and try different things and ideas, maintaining an open and flexible mindset.

Thinking biases are common problems we face in life. We must constantly remind ourselves to understand and correct our blind spots, so that we can develop healthier and more accurate ways of thinking.

Only in this way can we better understand the world and others, and better solve the problems we encounter in life.

From the question of whether buying books is a necessity, to the opposite world inside and outside a mirror, and then to our thinking biases, what we're doing is essentially two things: knowing ourselves and knowing the world.

Start by knowing and understanding yourself, from within to outward, making yourself better and more useful to yourself. What do you think?
