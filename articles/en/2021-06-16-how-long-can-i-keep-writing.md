# How Long Can I Keep Writing?

> Published: 2021-06-16 · [中文](../zh/2021-06-16-%E5%86%99%E4%BD%9C%E8%83%BD%E5%86%99%E5%A4%9A%E4%B9%85.md) | [English](../en/2021-06-16-how-long-can-i-keep-writing.md)

---

Writing is something I'm deeply passionate about, sometimes even fiery. Li Xiaolai teacher lit the spark for my writing journey, completely changing my perception of writing—something I once thought had nothing to do with me, or that I should just focus on reading. Li Xiaolai told me that if you want to write, you can write. There's no barrier to it; it's a physical job. For me, of course, that's no problem. I'm a student, I've been a teacher, and from different perspectives, I should write well. Since I've thought about things, I should express them. So I never worry about having nothing to write. Instead, I hope my words can pass on some power, encouraging more people to join the writing community, at least among those who believe they can write.

The future is unknown, but I want to keep doing this, writing every day, genuinely wanting to write, writing whenever I have time, recording my own growth, gradually refining my skills. The practical knowledge I learned in the writing camp should be put to use, practiced well, and continuously honed—circle words, perception, analysis, recording, rhetoric, logic, and so on. These are the abilities I need to keep practicing and refining. On the other hand, while learning foreign courses, I should also focus on academic writing, doing things professionally and putting in enough effort. If possible, I plan to start an English writing column on my new platform, writing a little every day. English is a "naive and sweet" language, you know? <(｀^´)>

Starting from February 11th, I began publishing my writing on the new platform, including my practice from the writing training camp, daily learning reflections (organizing content from various platforms, reading, watching videos, and writing down my thoughts), watching movies and holiday records, participating in group readings and logic class reflections. I've already completed eight topics, totaling 209 articles! Each is over 2000 words, which means over 400,000 words in total—unimaginable! This is just the beginning. In 2021, I want to write a million words. Maybe it's verbose, maybe it's not nutritious, but I'll take it slowly. If I'm going to do this, I'll do it thoroughly, pushing myself hard.

Can I keep writing for a long time? I definitely can. I don't set a target for the number of words, only hoping to record what I think, and to do what I've come to love for as long as possible. I remember Li Xiaolai saying that what determines fate seems to be choices, but deeper down, it's values. In simple terms, values are about what we think is good, better, and best. Writing and reading are the best things for me. Since this is my value, I can definitely keep doing it. Why wouldn't I do something so important? If I'm going to do it, I'll do it thoroughly. The saying "be strict with yourself" is right, but more importantly, I just want to do it. If someone doesn't let me, I'll get angry with them, and that's when I'll be strict.

Writing is something that requires long-term accumulation. There's no quick payoff, of course, but those with solid foundations might see results faster. That's still the result of enough prior accumulation! I don't envy others; I just focus on doing my own thing. Writing is about making the right choice, and once the choice is right, writing will flow naturally. One of the biggest obstacles to writing is content. How can you write without content? In teaching English, what's the hardest? Many think it's writing, and I used to think so too. But now I see that many people actually have nothing to write about. After all, English writing often requires independent thinking, rigorous logical arguments, sufficient evidence, and insightful analysis. Some students may lack all of these, not just in English, but even in Chinese. So I fully agree with Li Xiaolai's views on the current state of Chinese language education in primary and secondary schools. It's truly poor, with too much time spent on memorizing texts and doing exercises, turning humanities into the style of natural sciences.

To be honest, building up content for writing is something that requires long-term practice. In the first two sessions, we talked about reading input and gathering materials, which are closely connected. Using output to drive input is also great. Starting from imitation, gradually moving to writing on your own, from long essays to concise expressions, continuous practice, and gradual improvement—writing will naturally bring you gains. Many people can't keep writing because they lack sustained motivation. Thank you to the writing camp platform, the support of fellow writers, the encouragement from the mentors, and the guidance from so many excellent experts. Why not write? Why not practice?

Protect your initial motivation. No matter what difficulties you face on the writing journey, there are always people quietly supporting you. Let's walk this path together. Courage is always there, waiting to be awakened. The writing camp awakened my courage. Let's cherish it, keep going, and keep up the good work (´-ω-`).

During the writing process, you'll face bottlenecks and hesitation. Take it slowly. Even if it's just a few dozen or a few hundred words, they're your own thoughts. Write them down, and record our growth. This path is full of thorns and obstacles. Nothing is gained naturally. If it is, maybe it's just our innate talent. Let's give a shoutout to humans, haha! Treasure everything we have. This amazing brain has so much stored inside. Sometimes it hides things in a warehouse, and we can't find them. No worries—writing can help. It's like a universal key, always ready to find what you need.

Selling your time multiple times, improving your ability to earn outside the field—writing isn't the best way to do that? We don't necessarily have to publish books, but at least we can leave a record of our growth. Keep going 😊

To embrace all rivers and thus become vast, to swallow mountains and rivers and thus know the power of writing!
