# Keeping It Simple Isn't Easy

A simple, clear idea or expression often captures an audience's attention more effectively.

Using concise and straightforward titles and content helps to better grab viewers' attention. For example, a financial WeChat Official Account once simplified the complex debate around "the central bank's comprehensive reduction of reserve requirements" in its title, gaining a million followers while appearing more relatable and impactful, thus receiving massive attention.

Similarly, simple images, voiceovers, and original songs can also become popular elements in content creation. For instance, a legal WeChat Official Account gained popularity online by using a single, straightforward image to highlight the extreme objectivity and limitations of current law, deeply inspiring the general public.

Simplicity does not mean being crude or lacking thought. A simple idea must also align with the principle of emotional resonance, allowing the audience to understand and accept it quickly.

Sometimes, the more simple the effect, the more it can trigger people's empathy and resonance. For example, during the height of the pandemic, some self-media used short, concise phrases to break through the confusion of communication, helping more people regain confidence and hope.

The power of simplicity also lies in personalization and uniqueness. In content creation, one must maintain simplicity and clarity while also developing their own style and characteristics to stand out from other creators.

Even if something seems plain, as long as it's a true highlight, it can produce a motivating effect. Therefore, for new media creators, staying sincere and simple is the best creative guideline.

A simple way of thinking allows people to focus more on their goals, such as Pony Ma, who always insisted on building Tencent into a social networking giant.

Don't easily be distracted by others; your own thoughts are the most valuable. For example, Jack Ma always adhered to his e-commerce vision, eventually founding JD.com.

Being willing to take risks and try new things can lead to more opportunities for success, such as Mark Zuckerberg's investment in developing Facebook, which eventually made him one of the world's top IT entrepreneurs.

Don't give up on your goals just because of mistakes; instead, learn from failure and use it as an opportunity to improve. For example, Jeff Bezos highly values the management of Amazon but also learns from failures to continue improving.

Patience is needed to achieve your goals, as seen with Yao Ming, who remained committed to basketball and eventually became a famous professional player.

Dare to imagine the future, such as Elon Musk's focus on sustainable energy development, leading to the creation of SpaceX and Tesla.

Stay happy and enjoy the process, as Li Ning always adheres to the slogan "Anything is possible," enjoying the success brought by sports and entrepreneurship.

Professor Wu Bofan once shared that many failures are due to foundational weaknesses, meaning failures in basic skills.

Most of the basic skill failures I've seen stem from poor initial choices. For example, most people who create content for WeChat Channels simply transfer their offline experience directly online, which is not really a choice.

A common question is: what skills do you need to learn for WeChat Channels? Buying books, taking courses, learning editing, using software, filming, finding materials... But where exactly is the foundation?

Prioritize your choices. Only by thinking clearly can you then properly refine your basics; otherwise, you're just chasing trends. For example, positioning is actually simple: find a category that is popular on the platform, has high transaction volumes, and you have the resources and interest in. Maternity and food are excellent niches.

The fundamental skill for short video creation is content. From the start, you should focus on refining high-quality content. Other elements like visuals, sound effects, and plot can be gradually improved, but the core content text is key.

Well-known bloggers like Li Xiaoyi, Nanxiang, and Fang Qi spend a lot of time refining their copy. Don't consider anything else—just focus on improving the content.

What you focus on at the beginning determines whether you'll end up copying or creating, whether you'll keep improving, and whether you'll practice well enough that others can't keep up.

My advice is to clearly state one thing at a time.

Stating one thing clearly and explaining one point thoroughly makes it easier for the audience to understand and accept the content, increasing views, shares, and subscriptions.

For example, "Programming Daily" explains programming knowledge with clear and straightforward language, gradually delving deeper and categorizing according to a logical order. This not only attracts more attention but also makes it easier for people to understand and learn.

For self-media videos, it's also important to convey knowledge in the simplest and clearest way possible. For instance, breaking down knowledge into small steps makes it easier for the audience to understand and accept, and increases the convenience of viewing.

Additionally, self-media creators can use the "one thing at a time" approach to highlight their professional expertise and depth of knowledge, thereby enhancing their brand.

For example, a self-media account that interprets current political, economic, and cultural contexts by analyzing and providing examples makes it very easy for the audience to accept. This kind of presentation also strengthens their influence and earns more trust from viewers.

Stating one thing clearly and explaining one point thoroughly is an essential method for improving abilities and achieving better performance in self-media content creation.

Here's an interesting phenomenon: the law of excluded middle.

The law of excluded middle states that in the same thought process, two contradictory ideas cannot both be false; one must be true. In simple terms, it means: to live or to die—there is no middle ground.

The biggest issue is "wanting everything," and sometimes this is framed as "moderation," which is clearly not the same. More importantly, wanting too much.

In my consulting work, I often encounter this "having it all" mindset, especially in short videos and live streams. In reality, a person's energy and creative capacity are limited, and doing one thing well is already impressive.

For example, you might have seen videos where food items line up one after another, with the creator cooking themselves, and no one is visible, or animals like cats are used to make the food. It looks great, but how much time does it take? If you've done it, you know. And these kinds of videos may not be easy to monetize either. It's important to clarify from the start what you hope to achieve through your videos.

Most of the time, we want followers, likes, comments, and monetization, which means we want a lot, but our goals are unclear. Finding the focus and aiming precisely is key.

For example, in writing or creating video scripts, when expressing opinions, you might want to criticize a viewpoint to gain traffic but also avoid being too absolute and thus be cautious. Expressing your views clearly requires defining your stance.

Avoid ambiguity and be firm. It's okay to be wrong.

Simplicity isn't easy. Avoid complicating things, and learn to think about the core question: what matters most?
