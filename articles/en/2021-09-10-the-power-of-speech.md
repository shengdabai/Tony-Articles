Everyone has their own story, and experiences vary. Within these stories lie certain wisdoms. As long as you can find those unique experiences and wisdoms and share them, it becomes a great speech.

002 Speech is Like Storytelling

People love stories, and everyone can learn to tell them well. “I’m a very boring person, with no stories.” Is that really true? The sources of stories are actually numerous: think back to the most outstanding events in your work over the past few years. What was the last time you felt excited or angry? What was the most proud thing you ever did? The last time someone said, “That was really interesting” during a conversation? If you had a magic wand, what would be the thought you most want to pass on to others?

003 Speech Promotes Learning

Speech is an excellent opportunity to focus and deeply study a topic. Have you ever had an experience in school where a teacher assigned a topic, and you stayed up all night reading books, then gave a presentation in class and received enthusiastic applause? Have you ever had an experience at work where your boss assigned a seemingly impossible task, and through your careful preparation, you gave a report in front of many colleagues, only to hear them praise you? If you have, great — challenge yourself with another speech! If not, that’s okay too — just prepare well!

When studying a topic, the following questions help you grasp the big picture: which questions are most important? How are they connected? How can you clearly explain these issues? What still feels confusing? Where is the main controversy?

Opportunities are everywhere. Any chance to speak in front of a group can become a learning opportunity. All you need to do is take action, be brave, think about what is unique to you, and refer to the sources of stories mentioned above.

004 Speech Topics and Structure

The fewer topics a speech covers, the better — ideally just one, which is explored deeply and powerfully, rather than a superficial overview of everything.

A classic speech structure is a great reference:

Introduction — clearly state the topic

Context — why this issue is important

Main concepts

Practical significance

Conclusion

005 The Five Most Common Tools for Speech

Connection, storytelling, explanation, persuasion, and demonstration.

1. **Connection**: Speech begins by establishing a bond of trust with the audience. Whether or not this trust is built directly affects the success of the speech. The best tool for building connection is a smile, and eye contact with the audience also helps. If you are humorous, that can be very useful, but humor is a skill not everyone can master. People naturally love stories. Starting with a story can spark interest, resonance, emotion, and curiosity, thus capturing attention to the topic. Of course, stories can also appear in any part of the speech, not just the beginning.

2. **Storytelling**: Pay attention to the linear structure of the narrative, which makes it easier for the audience to follow. When describing a story, keep in mind these four points: the story should have a relatable protagonist; create tension through interest, suspense, or danger; provide appropriate details — too little makes it dull, too much makes it drag; and a satisfying ending, which can be interesting, touching, or enlightening.

3. **Explanation**: Understanding builds layer by layer, with each layer based on the previous one. Using metaphors and examples to explain concepts is a great approach. Use linking words to make sentences clear and logical, such as: “Although...”, “An example is...”, “On the other hand...”, “For now, let’s consider a different opinion...”, “Tell a story to prove...”, “In summary...”.

4. **Persuasion**: Logical reasoning can lead to a relatively clear conclusion, making it easier for the audience to accept. There is also a logical argument method called *reductio ad absurdum*, which involves first presenting an opposing view, then revealing its contradictions. If that view is invalid, your point is validated. Other methods to provide strong support include adding an interesting story, providing vivid examples, or using third-party evidence.

5. **Demonstration**: There are many ways to demonstrate, depending on the content being presented. Combining personal strengths with the method yields better results, such as using striking images, engaging videos, or rich audio to enhance the speech.

006 Summary of the Whole Book

The importance of speech needs no further explanation. To date, there are thousands of books on public speaking worldwide, countless examples of speeches, and countless analyses of methods and techniques. There are also various masters of eloquence and speech coaches who offer innovative training programs to improve speaking skills and enhance public speaking abilities.

Despite all this, for most people, public speaking remains an elusive goal. Though we may not reach it, we still long for it. Many people, including myself, have tried hard to read many tutorials and learn various methods, but after learning, we still don’t know where to start or what to say. This awkward situation is like a pot of dumplings with no way to pour them out — you have the content, but you can’t express it.

In short, speech is like swimming. No matter how much theory you learn, without jumping into the water and practicing, you can’t master the skills or improve your ability. Seize every opportunity to speak in front of people, communicate, and report — practice as much as you can.
