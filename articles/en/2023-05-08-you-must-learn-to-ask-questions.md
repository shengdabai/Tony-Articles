When we ask questions, we need to pay attention to the above points. Some of these are suitable for computer-related topics, while others are general. From the title, you can tell which are general question-asking methods.

To ask an effective question, you can do the following five things:
1. Specific and clear questions: Your question should be clear and specific so that the answerer can better understand your question and provide an accurate answer.
2. Questions with clear answers: Ensure your question has a clear answer rather than a subjective one that cannot lead to a definite conclusion.
3. Avoid complex questions: Simplify your question as much as possible to avoid the answerer misunderstanding your question or providing an irrelevant answer.
4. Polite and clear expression: Use proper polite language and clearly express the background and reason of your question, which helps the answerer understand and answer your question more accurately.
5. Conduct a search before asking: Before asking a question, first search for relevant information to ensure your question does not have an existing answer.
If the above methods still fail to find an answer, try different search engines or keywords, such as Google or Bing. Search tools have different strengths. If you do not know how to search effectively, look up a guide first or add terms such as “effective” or “efficient” to your query. You can also consult specialists through platforms such as Zaihang, Dedao, Fanshu, and Chaos Academy. Relevant forums and social media can help too, but assess every recommendation carefully.
At this point, we have a powerful tool—artificial intelligence. Large language models like ChatGPT, Bing, and Bard can help us. Be careful to distinguish between opinions and facts, and ask AI for facts along with relevant evidence, such as web links, to make the answers more reliable.
Another option is to continue researching the problem. If the question is very specific or complex, you may need to continue researching and deeply understand it to find the answer. Alternatively, you can record the problem and revisit it later, as some questions require time to mature.

On forums, you might also be asked to search the forum's documentation. In fact, someone might even be willing to provide clues about how this problem was previously solved. But don't rely on this kind of help. Before asking a question, you should search the documentation first.
Usually, the person who asks you to search has already opened the manual or webpage that can solve your problem, and is reading it while typing on the keyboard. These replies mean that:
First, the information you need is easy to find. Second, you should learn more by finding it yourself than by having someone feed it to you. You shouldn't feel offended by this. According to the hacker standard, the responder is not ignoring you, but showing you some respect. You should thank them for their eagerness to help you.
If you still don't understand... If you don't understand the answer, don't immediately reply with a request for explanation. First, try the same tools you used when you first asked the question (like the manual, FAQ, web pages, knowledgeable friends, etc.) to try to understand the answer. If you still need an explanation, show what you've understood.
For example, if I tell you, "It looks like there's an input item that's problematic, and you need to clear it," and then comes a bad reply: "What is the input item?" While a good reply would be: "Yes, I read the manual, and the input item is only mentioned in the -z and -p switches, but it doesn't explain how to clear them. Are you referring to one of them, or did I make a mistake?"

The last part of the article mentions how to answer better, and I've included the translation below:

Be polite. The pressure from questions often makes people seem rude or foolish, but it's not really the case.
Reply privately to newcomers. There's no need to shame someone who honestly made a mistake in public. A true beginner might not even know how to search or where to find the FAQ.
If you're unsure, say so! A seemingly authoritative wrong answer is worse than no answer at all. Don't give random advice just because it sounds like you're an expert. Be humble and honest, and set a good example for both the questioner and your peers.
If you can't help, don't interfere. Don't joke around with specific steps, as that might ruin the user's installation—some unfortunate fools might take it as real instructions.
Use exploratory questions to get more details. If you do it well, the questioner can learn something too—you can learn as well. Try to turn a bad question into a good one. Don't forget that we were all beginners once.
Although it's justified to complain to lazy people with "Read the damn manual" (RTFM), pointing out the location of the document (even just suggesting a Google keyword search) is better.
If you decide to answer, give a good answer. When someone is using the wrong tools or methods, don't suggest clumsy workarounds. Recommend better tools and reorganize the problem.
Answer the real question! If the questioner has already done their own research and explained that they tried X, Y, Z, A, B, and C without success, then replying with "Try A or B" or giving a link that says "Try X, Y, Z, A, B, or C" will be extremely unhelpful!
Help your community learn. When you answer a good question, ask yourself, "How can I modify the relevant file or FAQ document to avoid answering the same question again?" Then send a patch to the document maintainer.
If you answer after some research, show your skills rather than just giving the result. After all, "Give a man a fish, and you feed him for a day; teach a man to fish, and you feed him for a lifetime."
Finally, a few more words.
Be clear about your intention: Before you ask a question, you need to be clear about your intention, determine the information you want to learn or the goal you want to achieve, which can help you better formulate the question and accurately get the answer.
Pay attention to the way you ask the question: After determining the intention of the question, you need to pay attention to the way you ask the question. The question needs to be targeted, accurate, and clear, so that it can better guide the answerer.
Avoid bias and subjectivity: When asking questions, you need to pay attention to your own bias and subjectivity, and not bring your personal views and emotions into the question. Instead, you need to start from an objective perspective, and ask questions based on facts and data.
Think deeply about the question: Before you ask the question, you need to think deeply and evaluate the value of the question and its impact on the answer, so that you can better formulate the question.
Listen to the answerer's opinion: When facing different answers, we need to patiently listen to the answerer's opinions and views, and think about them, so that we can form a more complete and accurate understanding.
