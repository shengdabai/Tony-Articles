**What Why How** summarized as **2W1H**, this method comes from Li Xiaolai's writing camp, helping us break down the structure of writing. Applied to each of us, using these three words to ask questions makes some answers clearer. My first English book about logic is exactly this one, **Beyond Feelings**. In one sentence: _**People with poor logic can't do anything well.**_ One key way of thinking, **Critical Thinking**, is translated as "critical thinking," which isn't very accurate. In Chinese, the word "criticism" still carries some negative connotation. Critical Thinking is one of two main modes of thinking; the other is Creative Thinking. I want to focus on Critical Thinking because the key step to improving it is to keep asking questions, to ask ourselves, what exactly do we need?

Take an example: One day, Steve Jobs went to the office of a Mac computer engineer. There was a new model of computer about to be released. Jobs asked the engineer to turn it on, but the boot-up process took several minutes. Jobs told the engineer, "Can you make it boot faster?" The engineers looked at each other, and quickly worked overtime for several days to improve the boot speed. They were excited to show Jobs the result. After watching the boot-up, Jobs asked one question: "Is this the best you can do?" Then he turned around and left. The engineers had no choice but to keep improving. Eventually, they managed to improve the boot time by a few seconds. This time, they thought it was done. But Jobs said, "I bet there will be 5 million people who open their Macs at least once a day. If you can improve the boot time by 10 seconds, multiplied by 5 million users, that's 50 million seconds a day, which adds up to 12 human lives a year. So, improving the boot time by 10 seconds is like saving 12 lives." Imagine that—raising the issue to the level of saving lives. What else could the engineers say? They had to keep improving. And in the end, they did manage to improve the boot time by another 10 seconds. The core of this story is one question: "Is this the best you can do?" This question is a form of self-pressure, pushing out great potential. It also symbolizes a kind of corporate culture that kept Apple innovative and driven, making it a high-value tech company. A good question can help us change many things in our lives. Tony Sheng hopes to help more users ask more questions.
