A couple of days ago, a friend asked me, "Xiaolai, why do you do this family education community?"

So, today, I want to share with you three reasons why I started this family education community.

**01**

**I Am Doing This, and I Need to Do It Well**

My answer is: **"The thing I currently spend the most time and effort on is family education and family building, so I started this community, which serves multiple purposes."**

Of course, there's another perspective I've shared with everyone many times: **Teaching is the best way to learn.**

When I need family education and family building, I definitely need to learn, right? And when I'm learning, the best method is to teach.

I've shared this idea with many people, including in my book *The Art of Self-Learning*, which I wrote specifically for self-study.

This is something that those who are familiar with my work, whether as readers, listeners, viewers, or community members, have experienced firsthand.

For example, I once had a community that's now closed, called the "Dedicated Investment Life Classroom."

At its peak, it had over 10,000 members, and now it's close to 10,000, with over 8,000 active members.

This community has been around for nearly 1,200 days, and during that time, I've given at least 500 lessons.

**In this process of repeatedly explaining the same content and answering questions, in a certain sense, Li Xiaolai himself gained the most.** Because when you repeatedly explain the same content and answer questions, you have the opportunity to think about the issue from all angles.

Everyone who hears the same content and the same ideas will have different questions or feelings of confusion from different angles. And I might not have even thought of these angles before.

But every time I encountered such an unexpected confusion, I would think again and realize that it was actually a very normal angle, and even more so, it might be an angle I wouldn't have thought about if the person hadn't asked the question.

So in this process, the same content was improved many times, and I also made significant progress.

Over 1,000 days, I gave over 500 lessons, and many students often say that Xiaolai's help was very, very valuable.

I also often tell the people in our community: **Your help to me is also very significant.**

So **teaching is the best way to learn.**

I've held this belief for over twenty years, even thirty years ago. Over the years, I've been practicing this method.

When I say "teaching is the best way to learn," it's not a lofty statement. Rather, it's a humble one.

Why?

Because in the process of teaching, you come into contact with many different perspectives. So every student in our community, I often say: **Xiaolai is not a teacher, he's your classmate, only he's better at sharing.**

Through meeting more classmates, my knowledge broadened, and my thinking became more comprehensive. In the end, my gains were greater.

Learning can generally be divided into three types.

**The first type is something that someone can teach you in person.**

For example, when we were young, we learned to fold paper, like a paper crane, a paper airplane, or a frog that jumps with a finger press. These are skills that can be taught by someone's hand. For instance, when we were in school, teachers taught us geometry and physics, which, in a way, is also teaching you by hand. This is the first type of skill you can learn.

**The second type is something you can learn through reading alone.**

As time passes, teachers are no longer enough. You won't meet many teachers in your lifetime, so what do you do?

You read books and learn from them, right? You buy a book and learn from it through reading alone.

Of course, in *The Art of Self-Learning*, I specifically mention this point.

> ****You must have at least one skill that you can learn entirely through reading, without anyone teaching you by hand.****

This is a major test of your learning ability. If you can do this, it means your learning ability has reached a new level. **The third type is something that no one can teach you, and there's no specific book, but you still manage to learn it.** Doing this is impressive. In a certain sense, I was only able to complete such a remarkable task many years later. For example, when I first learned about Bitcoin and blockchain, I realized I was facing this situation. In this new and emerging field, there was no one to teach me. You can imagine, in 2011, even on Amazon, there were no books about cryptocurrency or blockchain. No one had written such books, right? The only reliable source was the *Bitcoin White Paper*. There was no one to teach you by hand, and there were no books. In such a situation, if you managed to learn it despite the difficulties, the changes and benefits you would experience would be unimaginable, right?

You yourself should become a person who knows how to learn. My second goal is to **teach you how to teach your child.** Everyone should understand that learning and teaching are two different actions. You can learn, but you also need to know how to teach. Although the best way to learn is to teach, it's indeed two different actions. So even though I will first give a simplified version, it's still comprehensive, and I will gradually iterate it. My core goal is this: first, I will teach you how to learn, and then I will teach you how to teach, and then you can go teach your child. This teaching is very different from a teacher giving a lecture in a classroom. A teacher in a classroom gives a lecture to students who have certain prerequisites. I assume you have a certain foundation to understand this lecture. But parents teaching their children are not like this. You assume he has a foundation, but he doesn't. So what do you do? You have to patiently explain and teach him over and over again, right? So how to teach is indeed a specialized skill. This is the core of our new course: **I will teach you how to learn, and I will teach you how to teach. You just need to listen carefully and then you must practice, and you can't avoid practicing because the child is yours.** This learning is different from what people usually think. When we talk about learning, we often say vaguely who is good at learning and who is not. In reality, "learning" includes at least three concepts. The first is **studying**. The character "study" is the key. When the child asks you to study, and when you ask him to study, these are two different concepts, one is active, one is passive, very different. The second is **practice**. In English, "study" and "practice" are two different words. "Study" is learning or studying, and "practice" is practice. So it's often said that it's not learning that makes perfect, but practice that makes perfect, **practice leads to perfection.** The third is **application**, which I often mention in my books and community. So "learning" should be three characters: **study, practice, and application**, three stages. **You need to apply the knowledge you've learned in practice.** If the knowledge you've learned doesn't work, it might be because you only studied without practicing, or you only practiced without applying, so it doesn't achieve the desired effect. And if you don't care about your child, you might think that just because he goes to school, he's learning, and just because he does homework, he's practicing. That's not right. Practice needs to be **targeted**. But honestly, I tell you that this insight about learning isn't something only I have. I used to think that only I saw it, and I clearly distinguished between study, practice, and application, and I thought I was so great. But later, when I used a literature reverse search tool, I found that over 300 years ago, there was a Confucian scholar in the Qing Dynasty named Yan Yuan, who had a book called *Xizhai Yanxianlu*, which mentioned these eight words:

> ****Study must be practiced, and practice must be applied.****

After you study, you must practice, and after you practice, you must apply. Otherwise, it's a waste, right? You can see that three hundred years ago, people already said this, but I didn't know at the time, and I thought I had come up with it myself, right? So your understanding of learning can really change your learning habits and decisions, and thus change your entire life. It's for further improvement and enhancement. That's what it is. So learning is something every parent should be able to do.
