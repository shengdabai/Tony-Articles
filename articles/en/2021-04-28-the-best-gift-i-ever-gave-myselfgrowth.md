In English teaching, we use a "milestone" learning method to help students. This involves breaking down the ultimate goal into smaller targets—milestones—and tackling each one step by step. The learning process is like a hero's journey, full of challenges and progress. Some might think this is like playing a game, and they're right. It's just an upgrade path for English learning, where you can level up one by one or jump ahead. More specifically, we can track students' growth through the Cambridge English proficiency system. Each "milestone" event is a key moment that motivates students of all ages and skill levels to learn English gradually, moving forward toward free communication in English. You might think this is just an English exam, and I do too. But on the path of learning and growth, you have to repeat, repeat, and repeat again. Isn't the learning technique always the same? This is also a test for me, but it's a long one. I keep recording, constantly accepting the test from every supporter who clicks on this article. Every comment and suggestion is energy for my improvement, accumulating until the journey is complete.

I'm currently a graduate student. 2020 was an extraordinary year for me. I decided to quit my job, and the main reason was growth! After graduation, I switched to the education industry, working as an English teacher at an institution. Teaching was my norm, and at my busiest, I might teach up to 13 hours a day, with less than an hour of break in between. A friend once asked me, "Don't you get tired?" I really don't, because what I'm doing is more like physical labor. Friends often say, "How could that be? You're joking!" There are many jokes about teachers, and Luo Luo once said something like, "Anyone can teach English." I was quiteunwilling to accept defeat (unconvinced), thinking I had good English and was capable. With so many people not doing well in English, wouldn't they be envious of English teachers? Luo Luo said that since so many people understand English, native speakers can teach; non-native speakers, even those who look foreign or have studied abroad, can also teach. If you're good at English, you can teach, and this can be broken down into speaking, listening, reading, or writing. Even those who know many words or have good grammar can teach English.

Did I go off-topic? I think English is just a different way of expressing the same ideas. But for English teachers, whether you're learning and thinking continuously is crucial. Li Xiaolai once shared his teaching experience at New Oriental, and he was my mentor. He believed that students elevate teachers, and when you're surrounded by excellent students, teachers naturally become better. Li Xiaolai is a prime example of this. As a children's English education teacher (English teacher for children), I mainly accompany my students, encouraging them. But I myself often fall into a comfort zone, because English at the primary school level is easy for me. Academic language and teaching language are only used during training and assessments, so if you don't use them, they rust over time, just like a car.

I was learning while working, but it wasn't enough. After quitting, I had plenty of time to study and completed two international teacher training programs. Now I'm about to start a new one that will last a year, and I'm excited. I was also fortunate to receive offers from 11 British universities and ultimately chose to pursue an MSc in Educational Leadership at the University of Manchester. Friends who care about me asked if I was abroad and if I should be careful. Don't worry, I've chosen an online degree that suits me better, offering more time and energy guarantees. Plus, I definitely want to read more of Manchester's books and enjoy its resources. Of course, I also have my important practice group and writing camp. In 2021, I won't be lonely or bored. Learning brings me joy!

Let me talk about my learning journey. I've followed Luo Ji's thinking since university and got to know him early on. His 60-second daily insights opened up a new world for me, and I couldn't help but get addicted. Later, after getting the Dedao app, I registered immediately, but due to a lack of awareness about paying, I didn't spend much. It wasn't until after I started working that I began purchasing courses, choosing content I wanted to learn or was interested in. I gradually fell in love with this platform, understanding that free access lowers the barrier, even eliminating it, leading to a wide range of skill levels, especially with a lot of critics. Without realizing it, I've spent over 3,000 yuan on Dedao!

> Data summary: Purchased 253 courses, 42 not completed, some rewatched more than once. E-book membership: listened to 10 books. Audiobook membership: one book per day. Nuggets: occasionally checked. Daily study time averages 240 minutes, but due to speed, it's usually 2–3 hours.

> In summary, I listen a lot but write little; I read a lot but respond little; I learn a lot but think little!

This kind of input only gives me a general idea, and it affects my retrieval. Some courses are meant for this, while others I need to listen to repeatedly—not just to gain knowledge, but to break through my cognitive boundaries. Teachers like Wu Boyuan, He Fan, Wu Jun, Zhuo Ke, and Wan Weigang have all expanded my thinking. I want to record these insights, making my records more detailed and my thinking deeper!

> Here, I especially want to mention Li Xiaolai's *The Road to Financial Freedom*. The 52 concepts refined, and I believe there's more. My operating system has truly been upgraded, maybe in my own eyes. The teacher asks questions every week, leaving us to think. Since I started learning this column in 2020, I'm sorry I didn't record my understanding, so I'll start a new topic, irregularly documenting my thoughts on the column, my reflections on the teacher's questions, and my insights from reading others' responses. This is indeed the most commented column on Dedao, with no likes or saves, just real records!

I also study with Fan Deng's book club and the Chaos University. A friend once asked, "Why do you listen to so much? Isn't it repetitive?" Actually, they're all different. Dedao focuses on general subjects like business, management, and humanities, with experts teaching in an accessible way, each with their own style. Fan Deng's brand is centered around his personal influence, aiming to change people's perceptions of books, develop a reading habit, and use reading guides and questions to reduce the burden of reading, making it easier to enter. Chaos University is less known, but I met Professor Li Shanyou by chance. I also have a dream of starting my own business. After quitting, I thought about my future direction and how to start a business. Chaos University lit a fire in me, helping me not be confused. It's an internet-based business university with no boundaries, pioneering a new path for innovation education in China. I've gained a deeper understanding of entrepreneurship and have had the chance to attend various events, learning from and consulting with outstanding entrepreneurs.

All these platforms are my learning resources. Chaos University is where I record the most, as many business issues are complex and hard to write about. Plus, recently, I've been learning in the Li Xiaolai Writing Camp, and I've solidified my idea of daily output. The number of words isn't my goal. I started with a few hundred, and now I can write thousands, feeling very satisfied. Next, I want to record everything I learn on different platforms and my daily thoughts, witnessing my growth and practicing with everyone!

> For my 25-year-old self, I give a thumbs up to the current me who joined the practice group and writing camp. I'll definitely thank myself in the future. Good night!
