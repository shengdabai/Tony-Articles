Life is long, what do you think is most important?

The world is chaotic, what's most important is what becomes your values!

Money and wealth, though pursued with great eagerness, seldom yield results?

The river of time, we can't manage you and me......

> Note is a concept in medicine or psychology, belonging to the part of cognitive processes, and is a selective concentration of perception that leads to an increased level of awareness in response to localized stimuli. For example, listening attentively to someone speaking while ignoring conversations of other people in the room; or answering a phone call while driving a car. Attention is one of the most researched topics in psychology. Among many cognitive processes related to human consciousness, attention is considered the most specific, and it has a very close relationship with perception.

From Wikipedia, we can see that attention is part of cognition, but our more concrete understanding of attention might be insufficient. So how should we understand it? Li Xiaolai first tells us about three "pits" in his course. Each "pit" is so close to us, although subjectively many people are unwilling to admit they have ever fallen into them. For example, I thought I had not fallen into any of them, so let me calm down and think again:

> Joining the crowd for no reason, rushing to follow the herd, and worrying yourself sick over other people's affairs
>
>
>
>
> Concept comes from Dedao Column 《Towards Wealth Freedom》--Li Xiaolai

Look, it seems as if we won't do such things, won't fall into such traps. Let me talk about myself! Hearing the sound of an argument in the distance, seeing a group of people gathered together, something came up — **curiosity**. Some people say curiosity killed the cat. This so-called curiosity made me move forward without thinking, and I started to join the crowd... However, why did I join the crowd? I didn't know the reason, I just felt that if everyone is doing something, I should do it too. Or maybe it's the effect of the "mob," always wanting not to miss out on so-called information. Calming down and thinking again, this really easily breaks my focus. For example, while listening to a Dedao course, I suddenly jumped out and rushed to join the crowd. Thinking about it, it's really not right. Then I would start to complain to myself: What's the big deal? Why did I go over? Nothing really happened. If something did happen, could I just join the crowd? Of course not. Most of the time, these things have nothing to do with me, and I don't need to pay attention. But I also thought, if there was an accident, I should go over to help, call the police or an ambulance. Thinking a little deeper, if I had to participate at that moment, I would definitely do it. And often, there are not just me, so would there be a situation where no one dares to "help"? Perhaps there would be. Then, when the time comes, we should take action. Such situations are rare, and most of the time, it's better to quietly leave!

Following the description of "going with the flow," many people naturally avoid it, but I am different; I am unique. At this moment, I recall that popular song as the BGM -- **We Are Different**, perhaps, we might indeed be different. Thinking back to university life, many people thought they were free after the college entrance exam, thinking that university would be easy to graduate from? This has to do with our university's admission system of "**broad admission and strict graduation**," but I don't think the entry barrier for university is that high. Although I am not a top student, I think that merely getting a diploma with scores of 6 or 7 in a few subjects doesn't hold much value, but it can only show that one performed well in those subjects in high school, limited to that knowledge only. The trend is to study hard, and this flow is too scary; everyone is eager to jump in, thinking that once they finish the exam they are free, but this is just the beginning... Upon entering university, we come to campus with various backgrounds, yet everyone's goals and thoughts are different, but most just want to graduate smoothly and have a relaxed and happy life, with failing grades becoming the norm. Some students' lives are very similar: dormitory, cafeteria, classroom, the three points in a line, and then there's gaming, binge-watching, and playing around... Before we know it, senior year comes, and with it, the well-known autumn recruitment season arrives, with a flood of job applications, "high salary, good location, good benefits, less work..." and then comes the fantasy! When the interviewer asks, "Why should we choose you?" one might say, "I am different from them," but is that really true?

**People follow the crowd, because they can't afford the cost of being independent and unique.**

Then there's worrying about others' affairs to the point of exhaustion—does that really have anything to do with us? In the end, **it's just boredom**. Others' matters, shouldn't they take care of themselves? After all, most people have a curious, gossiping heart. Focus on your own affairs first, pay attention to yourself, and dollar-cost-average yourself!

Some things I really can't remember anymore, but I'm very grateful to the Practice Group. In fact, my attention used to be easily distracted by external things, and I didn't stick to doing things consistently. The only things I did were listening to lessons, studying, exercising, and daily reflection and organization. By joining the Practice Group, I've embraced the five core values: **accompanying family, helping friends, reading, fitness, and investment**. I've been practicing every day, doing a little bit or a lot, and it's not at all off-putting. I'm full of motivation. Perhaps the charm of the community lies in this: **a group of people doing one thing**!

> 1. Have you fallen into any of those three big pits?
>
> 2. Do you think how to spend your attention is the most reasonable?
>
> 3. Every day during this week, review your daily attention expenditure, and you will gain great insights from it... Record it down.
>

I once fell into the first pit, just for the sake of joining the crowd. My curiosity is still quite strong, and sometimes I would get distracted from my focus, mysteriously going to join the crowd. For example, WeChat, although I have it on silent, I still occasionally open it to check if there are any messages. To be honest, I'm currently in graduate school, and there aren't that many important things. In fact, it's hard for us to define the four quadrants of importance and urgency. Just completing the three important tasks is enough. As for messages, I'm afraid of missing out on something important, but actually, it's not that important, since information streams are updated every day, no, actually every minute. It's like the US presidential election, where I joined the crowd to check the vote results. I don't know why I paid special attention to this election, maybe because I was at home due to the pandemic and had nothing to do!

I think attention is scarce, and spending it on helping oneself grow is necessary. Of course, accompanying family and helping friends also requires attention. Perhaps what's most important is what we consider essential in the present moment. I often joke with friends, **"If I can't see the sun tomorrow, I have no regrets."** In fact, I've never regretted it. I know that it was futile, and if I could do it again, my attention would still be spent in the same place. I've always believed that the level of cognition determines behavior, just as I've acted according to my own thoughts before, not caring much about others, though I still sometimes hesitate and reflect afterward. After joining the practice group, I realized the most important thing is the five key areas. I've been reading and learning continuously, but writing has been something I've done very little. Now I must start practicing it, writing every day, and have been doing it for more than two months. I will continue for 100 days, 1,000 days, 10,000 days... Accompanying my family is also an opportunity created by the pandemic, giving me more time to be with my family, and this feeling is amazing! Helping friends is also a long-standing belief of mine. Being a teacher is my choice, and helping others is how I find happiness. As for investing, I never thought about it before, thinking it was something only the rich do. Now I've started regular investing, even if it's a small amount, but I'm doing it consistently, practicing it, **"investing the money from the eggshell for the future."**

Finally, reviewing my Todoist app, I record daily tasks but didn't write down the time spent on each. Recording time helps adjust attention. After reading, I felt my focus improved, allowing me to calm down and complete things one by one without external distractions, fully immersed in the process—what a great feeling! But I still can't help multitasking. Honestly, I feel more efficient that way, but it's actually costing my focus. I need to adjust gradually, **do one thing at a time, do it well, practice and practice again**!
