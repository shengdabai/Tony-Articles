# Listen a Lot and Listen Well

The 147th Original Piece

In language teaching, listening, speaking, reading, and writing are the four core language skills, which can be divided into two categories: input skills and output skills.

Some people are stuck on their inability to produce output—how can they say or write? Should they think carefully before expressing, ensuring their input is sufficient enough?

As an English teacher, in our teaching, we usually have a preset assumption: if a student receives input on ten points, they can output one point, and that's already achieved our teaching goal. However, most people don't take this seriously, as it seems too little. They might think they could at least output five, or even more.

Input, we emphasize, should be effective input. If the input is problematic, the output will likely be in chaos as well.

Here, I want to focus on "listening." Are you really listening? Is listening just about using your ears? Even if you listen, are you sure you understand?

Let me tell a story. My younger brother is 12 years younger than me, and he's currently a sophomore in junior high. One evening, we went out for a walk, and he shared his story with me. I didn't take it seriously, thinking that student life was just so much. After he finished, I wanted to show off, so I started talking about my thoughts, acting like the know-it-all teacher. Later, when I reflected, I realized I hadn't really listened to what he was saying.

Sometimes he would ask me questions, and I assumed I knew the answers. But that's definitely wrong. How could I possibly know everything? To protect my authority, I'd give him a vague answer or ask him back, letting him find the answer himself.

Understanding what the other person is saying during a conversation is crucial. Many problems in daily communication arise from not understanding, let alone not listening at all.

Listening—these two characters seem so simple, simple enough that anyone can do it. The reality, however, is that most people lack the patience to listen. Instead, they choose to talk at length, since showing off is a way to make an impression. But what kind of image do we leave behind by doing this?

Listen more, listen well! Listen more, listen well! Listen more, listen well!

I hope next time I can be patient and listen carefully, then think seriously—did I really understand?

Good night, listen…
