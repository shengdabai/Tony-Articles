Warren Buffett said: No one likes to get rich slowly!

This is a common mindset, and it creates a long-term pattern. Some compare it to a bridge, where different fields can find solutions with a single strategy.

For us who practice dollar-cost averaging, it's just one move after another—buy, buy, buy, and conquer the world with one move.

Li Xiaolai often advises us not to chase quick money. Those who chase quick money tend to spend it just as fast as they earn it. Why is that?

In short, they lack the ability to sustain long-term wealth.

For example, many take loans to buy a house, paying the loan on time and in full. But in reality, the total cost of a house is the principal plus interest, which is effectively the price of two houses. The truth is as straightforward as that.

Some might think, isn't this also long-term thinking? Isn't it also about getting rich slowly? Not really. This is forced repayment, with fixed timing and amounts. If you were told you didn't have to pay it back, you'd be flying high already.

At its core, it's still a lack of the ability to hold onto long-term wealth. So, in our pursuit of wealth, we should keep investing in ourselves, so we can earn what we deserve.

Dollar-cost averaging is really applicable to all aspects of life. It's that simple and direct. Further thinking reveals that the reason this strategy works is because it aligns perfectly with the truth of reality.

Why aren't there casinos everywhere in the world? This shows that society as a whole recognizes that casinos shouldn't be a normal part of life. Ultimately, the world encourages people to work hard and earn money honestly, rather than relying on luck.

If you're going to work long-term, you must choose a job you enjoy. Otherwise, it's like a long prison sentence. Turn your job into a lifestyle, even a belief.

Choosing your attitude toward life and work is your values and worldview. Slowly getting rich, long-term accumulation, and becoming an extreme practitioner!

My takeaway is to stop dreaming and focus on doing things solidly. Writing is practice, and daily updates without interruption. Even if I don't become a writer, I can still give myself a great gift of growth.

I update my WeChat video channel every day, not worrying about comments or views. What matters is my own accumulation and change. I keep doing and learning, studying similar accounts, reflecting and organizing. Time will give me the answer.

Don't fantasize about the future results. Treasure the attention you have now. Study seriously, read books well. Although I'm an English teacher, it's only after my resignation that I truly started using English every day. My real progress came after that—studying for a master's, taking exams, attending training... With motivation, everything becomes manageable. I don't consider myself a success yet; I'm just a master's student in progress. Keep practicing, keep going!
