In *The Truth of Thinking*, Li Xiaolai proposed a unique methodology: constructing "Concept Tables," "Relationship Tables," and "Process Tables" to refine the thinking process.

In an era of mastering complex knowledge systems and information overload, clear and efficient thinking has become especially important.

The above three tables are tools for thinking, helping us identify core concepts, understand their relationships, and grasp the process of problem-solving within vast amounts of information.

**Concept Table: Building the Foundation of Knowledge**

A concept table is the foundation of knowledge. We need to clearly define and understand the basic concepts of the issues we are thinking about. By organizing related definitions, attributes, and categories, we can build a solid knowledge framework.

**Relationship Table: Understanding Connections Between Concepts**

Knowledge does not exist in isolation. A relationship table helps reveal the connections between different concepts, including causal, parallel, and opposing relationships. By analyzing these relationships, we can gain a deeper understanding of the multidimensional nature of a problem.

**Process Table: Grasping the Path to Problem-Solving**

Understanding the process of things is crucial for solving problems. A process table helps us outline every step from the emergence of a problem to its resolution, understanding the sequence, conditions, and potential variables in the process.

When applying these three tables to specific problems, we can use mind maps or tables to visualize them. This helps us better understand complex concepts and processes, enhancing the depth and breadth of our thinking.

In the face of increasingly complex problems, these three tools can help us avoid blind spots and logical errors in our thinking. By continuously updating and refining these three tables, our thinking can continue to improve.

Li Xiaolai's "Concept Table," "Relationship Table," and "Process Table" offer us a systematic framework for thinking.

They are like a set of precise thinking tools, allowing us to more clearly understand and analyze problems. By practicing this mode of thinking, we can enhance the clarity of our thinking and the efficiency of our decision-making, enabling us to make more informed choices in the complex real world.

Further, how we handle these three tables is through CRUD: a dynamic cycle of thinking and growth.

In a constantly changing world, our thinking methods must also evolve.

CRUD—Create, Read, Update, and Delete—represents the basic operations for data processing in software development. These can also be applied to our thinking habits.

Through these four actions, we can dynamically optimize our Concept Table, Relationship Table, and Process Table, making our cognitive structure more flexible and efficient.

**Create – Creating New Concepts and Connections**

Creativity is the starting point of knowledge growth. Continuously adding new concepts to our Concept Table, building new relationships in our Relationship Table, and designing new steps in our Process Table form the foundation of our cognitive development.

**Read – Reading and Understanding Existing Information**

Deeply reading and understanding the concepts, relationships, and processes we have created is essential. This process deepens our understanding of knowledge and serves as the foundation for self-reflection and absorbing new information.

**Update – Updating and Refining Our Knowledge Structure**

As we gain new information and experiences, we need to regularly review and update our tables. This may involve correcting outdated concepts, strengthening connections between concepts, or improving the steps in our process.

**Delete – Removing Outdated or Inaccurate Information**

Deleting outdated or no longer accurate information is key to maintaining a clear and accurate cognitive structure. This step prevents us from being influenced by irrelevant or incorrect information when making decisions.

**CRUD Cycle: Continuous Knowledge Iteration**

Applying the CRUD cycle to thinking allows us to build a dynamic, self-correcting cognitive system. Through continuous creation, reading, updating, and deletion, our Concept Table, Relationship Table, and Process Table become living maps of our thinking.

**Deepen Cognitive Dimensions: Concept Table, Relationship Table, and Process Table**

In the ocean of knowledge, we are all navigators. Our cognitive maps are composed of three important dimensions: the Concept Table, the Relationship Table, and the Process Table.

They represent the foundation of our understanding of the world, the logical framework, and the action guide. By refining these three dimensions, we can gain a deeper understanding of the world, make more informed decisions, and create unique differences in our actions.

The Concept Table is the foundation of our understanding of the world. It consists of a series of clearly defined, interrelated basic concepts. Like the foundation of a building, a solid Concept Table supports complex knowledge structures. Through continuous learning and thinking, we enrich and deepen these foundational concepts, enhancing our understanding of the essence of concepts.

The Relationship Table reflects our understanding of the logical relationships between concepts and is a manifestation of our judgment. It includes, but is not limited to, causal, comparative, and hierarchical relationships. By exploring and defining the connections between different concepts, we can build a multidimensional logical network, making our reasoning and judgment more accurate.

The Process Table is a mapping of how we handle tasks and determines the efficiency and effectiveness of our actions. It includes a series of steps arranged in time and logical order and is the fundamental reason for our unique value creation. Optimizing the Process Table means improving our execution, allowing us to create unique value in the process of achieving our goals.

To effectively enhance our thinking and execution capabilities, we need to integrate the Concept Table, the Relationship Table, and the Process Table. Understanding a concept, clarifying its relationships with other concepts, and embedding it into an effective process—this integrated approach allows us to think faster, deeper, and more comprehensively in complex situations.

The three-dimensional structure of cognition—Concept Table, Relationship Table, and Process Table—is the core of how we understand the world, make decisions, and take action. Only by continuously refining these three dimensions can we navigate the increasingly complex information environment with ease, achieving growth and success in both personal and professional life.

Invest more effort into deepening these dimensions and building a solid cognitive framework to become a more outstanding thinker and doer.
