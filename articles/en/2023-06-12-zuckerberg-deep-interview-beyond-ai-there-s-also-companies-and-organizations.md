# Zuckerberg Deep Interview: Beyond AI, There's Also Companies and Organizations

> Published: 2023-06-12 · [中文](../zh/2023-06-12-%E6%89%8E%E5%85%8B%E4%BC%AF%E6%A0%BC%E6%B7%B1%E5%BA%A6%E8%AE%BF%E8%B0%88%E8%B6%85%E8%B6%8A%E4%B8%AA%E4%BD%93%E7%9A%84%E4%B8%8D%E5%8F%AAAI%E8%BF%98%E6%9C%89%E5%85%AC%E5%8F%B8%E5%92%8C%E7%BB%84%E7%BB%87.md) | [English](../en/2023-06-12-zuckerberg-deep-interview-beyond-ai-there-s-also-companies-and-organizations.md)

---

Zuckerberg's interview is truly remarkable.

As an entrepreneur often living in memes and jokes, people's perception of him has shifted from the genius "Zuck" to a punchline about the metaverse in just a few years.

These may not align well with 39-year-old Zuckerberg, the founder and CEO of a tech company ranked among the world's top ten in market value.

In this interview, facing the usually calm and sharp Lex Fridman, Zuckerberg offered many profound insights.

The original interview lasted nearly three hours, and we've selected excerpts on his understanding of AI, his views on Apple's Vision Pro, and some life lessons he learned from his recent passion for combat sports.

Below is the interview content, edited and compiled by Founder Park.

**Social Networks** No. 1

**Understanding the Future of AI**

**Lex Fridman**
What does Meta have in store for the future of AI?
**Zuckerberg** People will have conversational assistants or agents. The biggest difference between me and companies like OpenAI and Google is that they're building a single AI. You can talk to ChatGPT, Bard, or Bing. I believe people will interact with many different AIs, just like you use various apps to complete different tasks, or like you build different emotional connections with different people in life. In the business world, there's also this need—**every global company will want to have its own AI**, just like you have a profile on Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, or other platforms, and you want to guide people to interact with your AI. This way, the AI will recommend more of your products, not just your products. So, **(the future) is not a single AI answering all questions, because it may not align with the interests of each specific company.** That's why I believe there will be a clear demand for multiple AIs in the market and in life. This involves **research technologies that make personalization and customization possible**, a large model that can be fine-tuned for specific needs, styles, businesses, and brands. Additionally, it should make it easy for people to create these models to help them interact. This is a very unique and interesting **product direction**, and it aligns well with our open-source approach. We believe in a more community-oriented, more diverse way to build products and technology. Natural language becomes the interface we use to interact with computers and the digital world. More people will use natural language for interaction. This seems like a great product for businesses and for humans, not just personal assistants, but also assistants that represent individuals to communicate with the public, in both directions. There's an interesting aspect to this: interacting with the external world through AI systems, including humans.

**Lex Fridman** Do you think some of your friends will be these AI systems on social networks in the near future? Will there be mixed conversations on social platforms, with some parts human and some parts AI?
**Zuckerberg** I think we will achieve this. People have a need for companionship, especially the elderly. And most people don't have as many friends as they'd like. If you look at the demographic studies on this, you'll find that **on average, people today have fewer close friends than they did 15 years ago.** This comes down to the core question I've been thinking about while building services that help people connect. So we will provide tools that help people connect with each other, which is our main focus. You can imagine an AI assistant reminding you of your friend's birthday, how to celebrate it. Such a social assistant could tell you what's happening in your friend's real life and how to connect with them. But beyond that, there are many different types of personal AIs that could exist. They might be like cheerleaders, encouraging you when facing various challenges. There's also an interesting innovation in the functional relationships AI can provide, such as the role of an educational counselor. Kids can ask their AI teacher any question at any time, and this teacher will be very patient and never get angry.

**Lex Fridman** Have you considered the long-term impact of AI on interpersonal communication? When people can "communicate" with others through a chatbot, using automatic enhancements to language ability rather than actively developing their social skills, **will people give up natural language communication in a generation?**
**Zuckerberg** To some extent, **all these social technologies are helping people express themselves better.** Language communication has many barriers, especially in cross-language and cross-cultural situations. **Language is an imperfect way for people to express their thoughts and ideas.**
**Lex Fridman** But language is also how people think, how they view the world, and how they see themselves. **I recently talked to a friend who said that the tone of his emails used to be too harsh and rigid. With GPT, the liveliness and friendliness of his emails improved a lot.**
**Zuckerberg** Yes, all these technologies are helping us deal with the complexity of social interactions and better convey our feelings and thoughts. There are also concerns that people might lose memory, self-control, and so on. Or that when most work is outsourced to AI, small errors and vulnerabilities might be missed. I can only say that human civilization faces a strange trade-off. As we build increasingly powerful tools, as we stand on the shoulders of ever-taller giants, we can do more, but we forget what we've done.

**Open Source**

**Suitable for Current AI Development**
**Lex Fridman** Meta developed a model named LLaMA with 65 billion parameters, and it also involved some open-source issues. Why did you decide to open-source it?
**Zuckerberg** Meta has taken a quite open and academic approach to developing AI. Part of the reason is that we want the world's best researchers to work on this field, and many of the best researchers want to share their work. If there are many different people, not just a few big companies, who can build the most advanced technology, that's good. As development continues, the efficiency of training AI will increase, and more people will be able to use these technologies. At the same time, by observing the innovations created by students, hackers, startups, and others in the open-source community using the technology, we will also learn a lot from them.

**Open source has unleashed a lot of innovation in the ecosystem, making our products better and providing us with valuable feedback on security and safety, which is crucial for building a good system.**

Another reason is that open-source software is often safer because more people review and find vulnerabilities, making it more secure. At the current scale of AI, **open-source software makes alignment and understanding easier to achieve.**
**Lex Fridman** Some interesting fine-tuned models have emerged after the release of large models, such as Alpaca, Colossal AI, etc. They can run in various environments. What do you think about this?
**Zucker,berg** This is very useful for many ordinary users because individual users don't have the same budget as companies to deploy cloud computing resources. The fine-tuning mechanism is the best choice for running on a local computer.
**Lex Fridman** Some people are very fearful of the existence of AI and say we must be cautious about open-source. From this perspective, what do you think about the future of open-source? Also, even if this technology may have a huge positive impact, do you want to put powerful tools into the hands of people with ill intentions?
**Zuckerberg** In the current stage, which is generally considered not to have reached the AGI goal, open-source is a better choice than closed-source in a more open environment. But **when we are close to AGI and super intelligence, this issue needs to be reconsidered and explored.** We are doing more open-source work than most tech companies. But we won't open-source everything. For example, the core application code of WhatsApp or Instagram, we won't open-source because they are not general-purpose software. The software we open-source, whether it's open-source server design or caching tools like Memcached, is very useful for building custom things.

## **Next-Generation Models**

**Open Source Has Many Possibilities**

**Lex Fridman** Can you share any information about the next generation of LLaMA? What are your thoughts on the release and vision?
**Zuckerberg** **Meta is currently building a version with the latest security measures.** We **are using more data from our various services for training.** A lot of internal work is focused on ensuring AI compliance and responsibility. We are building our own open-source infrastructure. However, Meta mainly focuses on building product experiences that help people connect and express themselves. **In the future, every creator will have an AI agent that can represent them and communicate with their fans.** I hope to achieve a goal where every small business basically has an AI agent that people can talk to for business, customer support, and so on. The infrastructure work we are advancing has gone beyond the scope of research projects and is becoming something ready to be a core infrastructure, not only for our own products but also for many other fields.
**Lex Fridman** Will the second generation of LLaMA also be open-sourced?
**Zuckerberg** We are actively pushing forward in this field, but unfortunately, there's not much to share at the moment. The technical details are still under development.
**Lex Fridman** What do you think about RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) and the alignment process? Would you like to comment on it? **Yann LeCun (Meta's Chief AI Scientist) previously asked me to ask you if you think fine-tuning large models will need a Wikipedia-like approach, not having one team fine-tune and another team evaluate, but instead having people participate in the fine-tuning process through crowdsourcing?**
**Zuckerberg** I often discuss this issue with him. We discuss how to train these models as safely, aligned, and responsibly as possible. Different teams have different approaches to development, testing, and data fine-tuning. But you mentioned the **crowdsourcing idea, which is a direction that needs further research and exploration. It is very beneficial for ensuring that the development of language models better reflects and serves the needs and interests of the entire society.** If we can achieve a Wikipedia-like collaborative approach, allowing a broader community to participate in the fine-tuning process, that would be great. Of course, there are many challenges involved, including **infrastructure, community management, and product perspectives**, which I have not yet fully resolved. As an idea, it's very attractive and aligns well with the open-source technology philosophy of training through a community-driven approach. However, we need to make as much progress in this field as we have in core intelligence capabilities.
**Lex Fridman** Wikipedia, although controversial on some topics, is striving for a balance. The path to truth, knowledge, or wisdom is not easy. AI models also need to be able to generate content that is as close to the truth, knowledge, and wisdom as possible. As humans, we need to collectively define what truth is and how to help AI systems generate content that aligns with the truth. Language models excel at generating convincing content, but they can also be completely wrong. So how to align the models to minimize errors depends on training and the alignment approach.
**AI Risks** **Don't Confuse Intelligence with Autonomy**
**Lex Fridman** Some people are very worried about AI's threat to human survival. Do you worry about misalignment and human survival issues?
**Zuckerberg** As technology develops, I think a superintelligent entity will definitely emerge. And we also realize that humans may use AI to do some harmful, unethical things. But I think people are overly focused on some far-off issues and ignore the real risks we face. And in these safety debates, **many people confuse the concepts of intelligence and autonomy.** I think **it's very likely that we've created something with high intelligence, but there are no safety issues people are worried about.** When people talk about safety and responsibility, what we really need to develop is **the management of system autonomy.** We need **progress in how to responsibly manage these systems.** **Building superintelligent systems is also an act of granting power.** I think Meta may be one of the few companies developing superintelligent systems, maybe even the main one.
**Lex Fridman** As Meta's CEO, building AGI could make you the most powerful person in the world. Do you think this power will corrupt you?
**Zuckerberg** I think this question actually goes back to the open-source issue. If only a few organizations have this technology and it's not widely available, that's not good for the world. Looking back at history, when there's this kind of monopole progress and power imbalance, strange situations usually arise. So open-source is one of the best ways to ensure that the system is as safe as possible when it reaches AGI. Not only because of its wide accessibility, but also because of continuous review by many parties. Everyone can check for vulnerabilities, anyone can interfere, people can start their own projects and experiment in various ways. The end result is that the system will become more stable and safer. Therefore, I think it's possible that we'll eventually adopt this approach, which helps the healthier development of technology and a more balanced distribution of it. So **when the development is approaching superintelligence, open-source remains the best choice.**

**The Other Side of AGI**

**Companies and Organizations Are a More Intelligent Entity**
**Lex Fridman** What's your view on the development of AGI and superintelligent systems?
**Zuckerberg** I think most people haven't realized that **there are already organizations and structures in the world that demonstrate intelligence beyond human intelligence.** For example, companies, which act like an entity with a unique brand. Obviously, they are composed of many people, but I hope Facebook, with thousands of employees, can make decisions smarter than a single person. So there are already such network systems, either combining the intelligence of multiple people or combining the intelligence of multiple people and technology to form **something much smarter than any individual in the world, and capable of positively influencing our society.** So I don't know when AGI will appear, but from previous cases, we've already seen that **a more intelligent system is a common phenomenon that benefits human progress.** To some extent, individual AI systems make the interaction between individuals and others more intelligent, thus making collective intelligence also more intelligent.
**Lex Fridman** Do you think AI needs a body to achieve AGI?
**Zuckerberg** It depends on the different goals. In this issue, there's an assumption that intelligence should be similar to humans. However, as we discussed earlier, you can have an intelligence entity more powerful than a single human. They can have no body, and don't need to communicate through language, but have their own systems. I guess that purely intelligent systems may have limitations in understanding human conditions unless they have the same senses. So, if the goal is to understand humans as much as possible, I think simulating these senses is quite important. But I think having intelligent systems independent of these senses can also create a lot of value.
**Lex Fridman** One characteristic of humans is that we all face death. Do you think there will be human AI copies that continue to exist after our death?
**Zuckerberg** I think we will have the ability to do this, and we've been thinking about whether we have the right to make this choice. I think the answer should be only the person themselves. That is, someone can create an AI version of Lex Fridman, but whether there will be a Lex AI should be decided by yourself.
**Lex Fridman** If it's really possible, I'll consider open-sourcing Lex AI.

# **Apple Vision Pro**

**Opening the Market for Meta Quest**

**Lex Fridman** Apple just announced a mixed reality headset called Vision Pro. As a leading company in the VR and AR fields, does Meta feel any sense of crisis?
**Zuckerberg** First of all, this is definitely an acknowledgment of our field. Previously, we've always said that VR, AR, and MR will be important parts of the next generation of computing platforms. Apple joining and sharing their vision will make many people who like their products start to take this issue seriously. Of course, the product is priced at $3,500. On one hand, I can understand the price considering the various technologies they need to integrate. On the other hand, many people may find the price too high to afford. So I think Apple's entry will increase the overall market demand, but Quest 3 will ultimately be the main beneficiary. It's the most cost-effective choice for most newcomers to the field. Moreover, to some extent, Meta has many leading features. Over time, different companies will focus on slightly different areas. **Apple has always focused on creating high-end products, while our focus is more on affordable concepts. We hope to create products that more people can access.** We've already sold tens of millions of Quest devices, and as far as I know, Apple can only manufacture about 1 million Vision Pro units. So in terms of sales, Quest will continue to be the main product in the market for the foreseeable future. Of course, in the long run, this depends on the execution capabilities of each company. Our focus is different. Meta is very focused on social interaction, communication; and more active activities, such as fitness and gaming. The many application scenarios seen in Apple's release are more about people sitting and staring at the screen, which is also good. But in terms of the different application scenarios our company is pursuing, we still have some differences from Apple.
**Lex Fridman** Yes, their release didn't focus on games, which is an interesting point. It feels like MR games play an important role in the headset field, so it's interesting to see them being ignored in Apple's release.
**Zuckerberg** There are some design trade-offs here. They mentioned not wanting to use controllers. On one hand, **navigating the system through eye and hand tracking is reasonable.** I think the issue actually involves **which use scenarios you want to optimize.** If you want to play games, you'd want a different way to design the system. We focus more on social experiences and entertainment experiences. If you want to ensure the text on the screen is as clear as possible, you need to make design and cost trade-offs, which is why a $3,500 device is released. So I think this use scenario definitely exists, but I just think **we and Apple made different design trade-offs to achieve the use scenarios we're pursuing.**

# **Talent View**

**Choosing Employees You'd Want to Work for**

**Lex Fridman** How do you recruit and select talent to build an excellent team?
**Zuckerberg** On one hand, I choose people who want to do interesting work and have vision. For me, choosing is an art, and sometimes it's quite challenging. Choosing people who fit the company culture and can effectively integrate is not simple. Especially when an organization is growing rapidly, the team faces an important question: should we hire this person who looks good now, or wait for a better one? I've always applied the rule that **if in a parallel universe, I'm the subordinate and this person is the boss, I would be willing to work for them, I will hire them.** Because I think I can learn from them, they have good values, and insight, and I want to follow and respect their judgment. This is my standard for evaluation. In a company in a stable growth period, we tend to let candidates, especially recent graduates, intern at the company for a while, so both sides can have a better, face-to-face understanding. This way, the evaluation of employees is not just limited to the two pages of a resume. You can also observe how well they match the company culture and environment. This helps better identify potential outstanding talent.

**Combat Sports**

**Being Beaten Teaches Humility** Zuckerberg started practicing Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu in early 2020 and won gold and silver medals in two matches at the first Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu Championship held in Redwood City, California.
**Lex Fridman** What advice would you give to beginners in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu? What have you learned from this interesting art?
**Zuckerberg** You must realize that in Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, you have to be willing to be hit many times. But after some time, I found that there is a flow to it, which is my experience. If you want to achieve a goal, a lot of it just requires you to be willing to push forward, have courage and determination to overcome difficulties, which is the key difference between someone who can stand out and someone who can't. Whether it's working repeatedly or learning from Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu or surfing, **you can't force everything to happen.** Compared to managing a company, this is a very sharp lesson learned from sports. Because the cycle of managing a company is too long. You start a project, and it takes months to get feedback, and then you can make a decision. **Sports with high cycle density are very helpful for the mind. You get feedback quickly from the sport, and if you don't do something right, you get a punch on your face.** All these analogies contain important life lessons. Like in surfing, sometimes you can't freely turn, going in the opposite direction always has some limitations that are unfavorable, and the potential is too strong, so it doesn't work. But I also think that's **a form of humility.** **For people engaged in management and creation, many games just require you to push forward and tackle complex problems. But you also need to understand clearly which things you can't force and which things you need to use some techniques.**

**Lex Fridman** Life is like a journey that starts off bad. It's a day-to-day strict practice and improvement. Any kind of success requires such hard work. Many sports can teach you this, but you say it so easily, as if, "Nothing, it's just part of the process."
**Zuckerberg** I just failed and embarrassed myself so many times in life, and that's become my core ability.
