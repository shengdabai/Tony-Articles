# Three-Step Questioning

> Published: 2024-02-06 · [中文](../zh/2024-02-06-%E6%8F%90%E9%97%AE%E4%B8%89%E6%AD%A5%E8%B5%B0.md) | [English](../en/2024-02-06-three-step-questioning.md)

---

In daily life and work, questioning is an important skill that can spark thinking, promote communication, and drive problem-solving.

**First Element: Task Summary**
A task summary is an overview of the background and purpose of the question. When asking a question, the task summary serves as a guide and a point of reference, helping the audience understand the scope and intent of the issue. A clear task summary can stimulate the audience's interest and curiosity, prompting them to think and explore the problem. Therefore, we need to carefully consider and accurately express the theme, goal, and significance of the question so that the audience can grasp its core and key points.

**Second Element: Task Description**
A task description is a specific explanation and requirement of the question. It provides more detailed information, helping the audience understand the specific requirements and limitations of the problem, as well as the factors that need to be considered. A specific task description can guide the audience to think about different aspects and details of the issue, enabling them to provide more accurate and in-depth answers. We can constrain and guide the audience's thinking and responses by providing information on specific conditions, time constraints, and resource limitations.

**Third Element: Role Scenario**
A role scenario is the background and context in which the question occurs. By setting a role scenario, we can help the audience better understand the practical application and related impacts of the issue. A role scenario may include specific identities, contextual backgrounds, and related challenges, giving the question more dimensions and perspectives. By imagining different role identities and contexts, we can guide the audience to think about the issue from various angles, offering more comprehensive and diverse answers, and promoting deeper discussions and exchanges.

**How to Use the Three Elements to Ask Deeper Questions:**
1. **Engaging Task Summary:** Use an engaging task summary to spark the audience's curiosity and desire to think, encouraging them to actively engage in exploring the issue.
2. **Specific and Detailed Task Description:** Provide a specific and detailed task description so that the audience understands the specific requirements and limitations of the problem, enabling them to offer more in-depth and targeted answers.
3. **Vivid and Diverse Role Scenarios:** Build vivid and diverse role scenarios to guide the audience to think about the issue from different perspectives, offering varied and insightful answers, and promoting cross-disciplinary discussions and exchanges.

Questioning is an important skill for thinking and communication, and the task summary, task description, and role scenario are the three fundamental elements behind a question. By carefully designing the task summary, providing specific task descriptions, and creating vivid role scenarios, we can ask more profound and insightful questions, triggering deeper thinking and discussions, and promoting personal and team innovation and growth.

Fully utilizing these three elements, we can ask questions with insight and influence, contributing to personal progress.
