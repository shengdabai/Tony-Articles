How do the psychological, fundamental, and market aspects influence each other, driving the cycle upward or to a downturn?

**Fundamental Factors:** Corporate earnings and cash flow, as well as future expectations of both.
**Psychological Factors:** Investors' perception and evaluation of fundamental factors, their optimism, and risk appetite.
**Market Factors:** The aggregation of fluctuations from fundamental and psychological factors, plus randomness, forming market performance. The relationship between the three aspects is bidirectional, interlinked, and mutually influential. Changes in one aspect will cause changes in the others. The speed at which events unfold varies greatly across different cycles, and even within the same cycle, different stages can be very different. Finally, one more point: cycles are not necessarily smooth and steady. Instead, they can fall, rebound, or sometimes fake a move, briefly dip slightly before continuing to rise, or rebound slightly before continuing to fall.

What are the three stages of a bull market and a bear market? Why do we say that smart people act first, while foolish people act last?

**Three Stages of a Bull Market:**

1. Only a few particularly insightful individuals believe that the fundamental situation will improve.
2. Most people realize that the fundamental situation has indeed improved.
3. Everyone concludes that the fundamental situation will only get better and will never get worse.

**Three Stages of a Bear Market:**

1. Only a few thoughtful investors realize that despite the favorable situation and widespread optimism in the market, the fundamentals will not always be smooth sailing.
2. Most people recognize that the fundamentals are getting worse.
3. Everyone believes the fundamentals will only get worse.

Smart people act first, foolish people act last. In the first stage of a bull market, there is almost no reason to be optimistic. Only a few particularly insightful individuals dare to buy, and their cost is extremely low, with the potential for significant future appreciation. These early adopters, by definition, are a very small group of people with both insight and courage: one, they have insight, seeing the future more accurately than others; two, they have courage, daring to buy even when the majority does not yet agree. Courage allows them to seize the unclaimed profit potential that others have not yet noticed, and courage allows them to buy assets at extremely low prices. However, in the third stage of a bull market, many investors follow the trend to buy, as the market becomes overly optimistic. They must pay high prices to buy in, and later suffer huge losses. Every investment trend eventually goes too far, pushing prices to an extreme high. Therefore, when the bull market is nearing its end, the last person to buy pays a price that is overly inflated. The last person to buy receives a punishment from capital, not a reward.

Where are we currently in the cycle? What lessons can we draw from Newton's investment story?

The market is currently in the second stage of a bull market. It has experienced a wave of growth, and more and more people have become optimistic about investing. Seeing others profit, people fear missing out, and their attitude is gradually shifting toward risk tolerance. This is a characteristic of a bull market. Based on the current overall situation, we are in the second stage, where most people believe the fundamentals are improving. Newton once said, "I can calculate the movement of celestial bodies, but I cannot calculate the madness of humans." This reflects how human nature, psychology, and emotions influence investors beyond imagination. Investors find it difficult to remain rational throughout the entire investment process. Human nature swings between risk tolerance and risk aversion, leading to investment outcomes that fluctuate between risk compensation and risk premium. Even Newton, the great scientist, was not immune. He bought assets that had already risen significantly at the peak of the bull market and sold assets that had already fallen significantly at the bottom of the bear market. Either way, he was either buying at the top or selling at the bottom. This reminds us again that investing is not an easy task. The long history of market cycles, with inevitable ups and downs, has always been part of the market. In the realm of human effort and progress, few fields compare to finance in terms of the weight of history. Past experiences are just part of memory, yet they are often dismissed by the young as the refuge of old-fashioned people, because those old-fashioned people lack insight and cannot appreciate the incredible miracles of the modern financial world.
