119th Original Piece

What prompted me to join the writing training camp? It's simple. Li Xiaolai, the teacher, I joined *The Road to Financial Freedom* a long time ago, but didn't take it seriously until 2020, when I started to study it seriously. It really made me feel regretful—what was I doing all that time? I missed out on such a valuable resource. When I saw Li Xiaolai's writing training camp, I felt a lot of motivation. How does Li Xiaolai teach writing? After all, we're both English teachers, and I must have fallen into a logical trap. English teachers' writing skills are strong, right? And the facts confirm that! Plus, this one-year course is so valuable—what reason do I have to say no? No one recommended it, but just by following the learningWeChat Official Account, I decided to sign up without hesitation!

Before joining the camp, the biggest challenge in writing was lack of motivation. I never thought about writing, let alone writing consistently. Although I knew writing was important, I thought it would help me a lot. At the very least, it could help me express more, and the more I expressed, the higher my input requirements would become. That's the gap between thinking and doing. To change my habit of rarely writing, joining the writing camp was a great opportunity.

How could I not have motivation? With hundreds of fellow participants in the camp, their encouraging words—how could I not be motivated? Plus, everyone around me is excellent. Once I start writing, I can't stop. Excellence is a habit, and this kind of habit has long-term value. From the first day of the camp, I wrote every day, and I wrote more and more. This feeling is amazing, and there's also support from many fellow participants, which makes me happy 😁

Writing consistently, regardless of how many words, but being able to write every day, even if it's just copying or paraphrasing the original text, is still a form of practice. I think I might never be able to break this habit!

Learning to appreciate and praise others. Around me are all excellent people. Sometimes, my introverted self doesn't know how to express it. But when I received so much encouragement and support from fellow participants, and even some compliments that made me a bit embarrassed, I also wanted to give back to more people. From the beginning, I actively became a group reviewer. Reviewing is also a form of practice for my writing, and it's an opportunity to connect with fellow participants. I've gained growth and a lot of love (⑉°з°)-♡

Writing has become more methodical. Techniques like highlighting, sensing, analyzing, comparing, and using rhetoric are all practical and effective. They're easy to implement. With all these tools in hand, I've come to understand writing better. After all, this isn't something with high barriers—once you want to do it, you can!

After the training camp, what learning and writing habits will I continue? Maybe I don't need to stick to them anymore. I remember Li Xiaolai said that, in his understanding, there's no such thing as effort or persistence. This saying has stayed with me. Why not use effort and persistence? Because of passion, because I feel it naturally. Before, I needed to persist with writing because I thought it was difficult and had to endure and keep going. Now, for me, writing doesn't seem to require effort or persistence anymore. Just write, write sincerely.

After the camp, I write every day. I have a new column called *Tony's Learning Perception*, where I record my daily learning insights, internalize knowledge from various platforms, and form my own understanding for better output. I also complete my two columns: one is *My Story with My Brother*, documenting the growth stories of two little pigs, and the other is *My Story with Martial Arts and Taekwondo*, sharing my experiences and understanding of martial arts. My next plan is to launch my WeChat Official Account. Although I've already registered an account, I haven't decided on the specific content yet. I hope to find a direction and dedicate myself to it every week!

Do we need self-discipline?

No, we don't!

What we truly need is external discipline, group discipline—walking together with a group of people!
