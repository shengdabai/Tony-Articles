Note 62

Wanda Plaza Live Stream Brings Some Thoughts #LiveStream##CaseStudy#

01 A slow-motion shot with music — isn't this a common sight in live streams? For example, the People's Daily live stream often plays a slow-motion shot of the Huangpu River in Shanghai. Slowing down the stream isn't necessarily a bad thing during the broadcast.

02 To some extent, Wanda Plaza represents traditional offline commerce. By using live streams, it promotes its brand, and more specifically, it recommends the merchants inside Wanda Plaza to users, offering them a free advertising platform.

03 Live streams can showcase things in 360 degrees, keeping the ad space focused on oneself. The interaction with users is a revolutionary aspect of traditional commerce. When users are valued, the overall atmosphere is elevated.

04 A friend asked me, "Wanda Plaza already has its own brand value. Can I, as a small business owner, also do live streams?" Of course you can. For example, there was a boss who runs a dumpling shop, and he managed to get his dumpling shop's live stream to over 100,000 views. From then on, you can promote it as a dumpling shop that has been visited by 100,000 people. Isn't that appealing?

05 Live streaming doesn't require overly complex technology or equipment. For instance, the picture here isn't very clear. Maybe using a monitor for live streaming is enough. Of course, phones and cameras can also be used. Sustainability is key.

06 It seems this live stream has no heat rewards or product sales. Is it meaningless if over 100,000 people watch it? Definitely not. Reaching 100,000 people is valuable in itself. Isn't this free advertising great? The next step is to retain these users, such as by getting them into a group and offering them various services from Wanda Plaza.

07 Our perspective shouldn't be limited to a single point. Find the most important meaning of your live streaming and assign it significant value. This will help ensure long-term thinking is carried out.
