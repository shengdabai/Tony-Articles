# Shaping Personality: How to Begin

Firstly, specific time and space shape our personality. Our personality is constrained by specific environments, beliefs, habits, and so on. For example, the previous generation had to save money, take care of the whole family, ensure everyone had enough to eat and drink, send children to school, and support the elderly. That was considered good enough. For the post-90s generation, we haven’t experienced hunger, and we haven’t lived through much collective life. Many of our choices and attitudes are centered around ourselves. Specific time and space influence everyone’s personality, so I’ve come to understand and respect differences more. We often say there’s a generational gap, but in reality, our cognition and personality differ. We shouldn’t force any individual to conform to our expectations. Respecting each other is important, and we must also clearly understand that our personality is also shaped by the environment, not something we are born with.

Secondly, mass culture influences us in every aspect of life. Our attention is constantly taken away, and it’s rare to find time to read quietly. Isn’t that terrifying? There’s a book called *Brainwashing by Brands*, which explains how companies use people’s fears for marketing. For example, hand sanitizer. In the past, it wasn’t widely used and had little market. But now, whether at home or in public places, we almost always use hand sanitizer when washing our hands. Hand sanitizer took over the market from soap by exploiting people’s fear during the avian flu outbreak. At that time, many hand sanitizer manufacturers ran extensive advertising campaigns, saying that to prevent the spread of bacteria, we should wash our hands frequently. However, soap is used by one person and then passed on to another, making it easy for viruses to spread between people. Using hand sanitizer avoids this contact, so it became the preferred choice, and the market was opened up. Why does using fear in marketing work? From an evolutionary perspective, we are naturally prone to fear. Think about it—when you’re walking alone at night, even the slightest sound can make you feel scared. When you feel threatened or fearful, your body enters an automatic mode, blood flows to your limbs, and your brain doesn’t get enough oxygen. At that moment, your logical thinking is weak, and you’re more likely to make irrational decisions. Marketers take advantage of this by selling you products. Sometimes advertisers use our fear of a bad image to trigger our sense of insecurity. When you feel ashamed, you’re more likely to buy something without realizing it. This method of marketing using shame works by first pointing out a problem you haven’t noticed, then increasing your anxiety about it through promotion, and finally offering a solution. For example, if someone has body odor, an anti-odor product might advertise by saying that in summer, people wear sleeveless shirts, but someone with body odor can’t wear them. It then describes body odor as extremely unpleasant, and finally introduces its product, telling consumers that using it can perfectly solve the problem of body odor.

How do the different media we encounter deeply influence our lives? There’s no doubt that we live in an era where various media are integrated. Newspapers, radio, television, and mobile internet provide us with information in many ways. We can now receive information from around the world more conveniently than ever, and the entire world has become a global village. These media technologies are created by humans, and people are the creators of tools. Today, we live surrounded by various media technologies, and we may not even realize that the tools are also shaping us. In the process of information dissemination, what’s truly important isn’t the fleeting information itself, but the evolving and changing media. These media change the way we receive information, shape our perception of space and time, and ultimately influence our thinking and behavior.

Regarding how family shapes personality, I have some different views. In the book *The Myth of the Spoiled Child*, the author Harris mentions that we often hear child-rearing experts say that the main factor influencing a child’s development is the parent’s parenting style. But this view is wrong.

> The main factors influencing a child’s growth are two: one is innate genetics, and the other is the environment after birth.

Does the parent have an impact on the child? Of course, they do. However, the most significant and obvious influence parents have on their children is through genetic inheritance. This is almost without controversy. The point of contention lies in the second factor. In the environmental factors after birth, we can further divide them into family environment and the environment outside the family, such as schools and communities. Which of these environmental factors has a greater impact on the child? According to child-rearing experts, the family environment is more important. A clear example is immigrant families. The United States is a country of immigrants, and in many immigrant families, the parents, as the first generation, speak English with an accent. However, the children, as the second generation, often grow up in the U.S. and can speak fluent English. In the case of immigrant families, there are both family factors and external social factors. If family factors are more important, then the children’s English should be more like their parents’, and also carry a strong accent. But the reality is the opposite. In this example, the external social factors are clearly more significant. The most significant reason for differences in children’s personalities is genetics, not the family environment. We also often see that within the same family, if there are biological children and adopted children, these children may have some similarities during their growth. However, once they become adults, the similarity between biological and adopted children is almost nonexistent. This suggests that the influence of the family environment has completely disappeared.

I am an English teacher, and the teachers around me often chat about students during breaks. For example, this student is very excellent, and everyone nods in agreement. Someone will always say, “Their parents are really great, they care a lot about their child, and they are very polite to teachers. The family has a huge impact on the child.” Oppenheimer’s story has greatly inspired me. Parents’ behavior can help children develop more resilience on their growth path. Collaborative upbringing isn’t just the family’s responsibility; schools, society, and others also play a role in providing a better environment for children. However, from a genetic perspective, we can’t ignore it. This reality may seem cruel, but I want to say that each child has different genetic potential, and we are not educating children just for easier management or to make them all the same. Can everyone go to Tsinghua or Peking University? Of course, some people will say, “I just want my child to be happy.” Whether it’s helicopter parenting or relaxed parenting, there are problems. We need to be authoritative parents, which requires us to act first. Li Xiaolai often reminds us bluntly that knowing is not the same as doing. So, it’s not just about reading books and expecting the child to read them. We need to read first, and only after we’ve done it can we tell the child that we are growing together. Isn’t this again about the influence of the family environment?

> After-birth factors shape children in many ways, and the family environment is just one aspect, not the most important. What’s more important is the environment outside the family. Children learn more from other children than from their parents. Therefore, parents cannot monopolize a child’s learning, and excessive parental interference can actually hinder a child’s growth. Teachers also play an important role in shaping children. Leaders influence groups in three ways: shaping the behavior norms of a group, defining the boundaries of a group, and clarifying the group’s self-image, which is essentially a stereotype.

> In summary, no one’s personality is innate. It is shaped by many factors. What we need to do is focus on mastering the power to shape our personality and take control. First, treat your first reaction to anything, anyone, or any situation as temporary and don’t accept it until you’ve checked it. Don’t jump to conclusions, don’t let your feelings influence you, and engage in independent thinking. Second, ask yourself why you reacted that way? Were you influenced by others? Then ask yourself what influenced your judgment, was there a specific event? Could there be a better response? Is there a more suitable reaction than the first one? And so on. Ask yourself these questions repeatedly—it’s the process of independent thinking.

> Finally, here’s a golden quote for myself: the greatest freedom is the freedom to choose your attitude!
