What should I drink?

The earliest drinks I remember were the ones I drank during my student days. I wonder if you've had the same ones. If you have, it means we're still young in the same way.

They were simple plastic bottles, filled with brown, green, or orange liquids. No, they weren't proper carbonated drinks. They just tasted like cola, Sprite, or Fanta.

There was also milk tea. Yes, the kind that had no milk and no tea, just flavoring and additives. It came in all sorts of colors and flavors, and it seemed like they all had the same store name: "Bifengtang."

But that wasn't all. There were other drinks I can't describe or even name, but I just drank them.

As I grew older, I started drinking real beverages. I don't know when it began, but it became popular to drink real milk and tea-based milk tea.

The first time I had it was during university. In 2014, while I was studying in Hefei, I finally had milk tea. I once thought this store was only available on campus, but I was wrong—it was actually very powerful.

Its name is "Moxi Ice City." The prices were really cheap, and I thought this was only available at the university gate. But it turned out this was true nationwide. However, Moxi started expanding into various cities, probably during the pandemic.

More and more milk tea shops opened, with various brands and many different flavors. People's consumption preferences gradually formed, like only drinking a specific item from Coco or a specific one from Chabao Dao. In short, people had their own unique milk tea choices.

The rapid rise of milk tea also accelerated the development of the coffee market. Starbucks entered the Chinese market, and coffee shops sprouted like mushrooms after rain.

According to statistics, Shanghai has over 7,000 coffee shops, making it the city with the most coffee shops in the world. I definitely felt that in the big metropolis of Shanghai—coffee shops everywhere, a unique kind of charm.

At first glance, it seems like a competition between tea and coffee, but in reality, it's between milk tea and dairy coffee. Yes, the sweet, delicious kind.

The new forces in the beverage market have further intensified the competition. With the popularity of carbonated water like "Moyu Forest," the pursuit of health has become more evident.

Wait a minute, is drinking carbonated water really for zero sugar, zero fat, and zero calories? Are they really healthy? Actually, it's just psychological comfort. Even if it's sugar-free, isn't it still sugar? It's just replaced with artificial sweeteners.

Which is more harmful, sugar or artificial sweeteners? You'd say sugar, but artificial sweeteners are so healthy, right? However, research shows that the harm of artificial sweeteners hasn't been discovered yet. There's no large-scale experiment to confirm its safety. By the way, one of the artificial sweeteners, aspartame, has been proven to be harmful to the human body in experiments.

The beverage market is still a part of the food processing industry's development. It's now possible to provide whatever the user wants.

If you want zero sugar, they have artificial sweeteners. If you want zero fat, they just don't add fat and use other ingredients. If you want zero calories, that's easy—foods with less than 17 calories per 100g are considered zero calories. They just add various ingredients to achieve that.

If you want taste, they can give you taste. If you want low calories, they have a way. If you want something, they can provide it. The main point is to make food unrecognizable.

How much of our nutrition comes from unprocessed food? How do processed and ultra-processed foods fully cover our lives?

Carbonated water has raised some doubts. Then, are there truly zero-added products? The tea drinks like "Dongfang Shu Ye" from Nongfu Spring have become popular, but online opinions and reviews are divided into two camps.

Some say the taste is not good, not sweet, or just bland.

Let me add a note about sweetness. We used to judge the quality of fruits by how sweet they were. If something is sweet, it's considered good. But are today's grapes still sweet enough? Are oranges still sour? Genetic modification has changed fruits from the root, making them all taste the same—sweet.

But why have our taste buds become so insensitive? No matter what we drink, it's still sweet.

Is this not due to the development of the food industry changing consumer preferences, especially among young people?

An interesting thing is that traditional Chinese medicine shops might become an alternative to milk tea shops. A series of healthy drinks, like plum blossom soup, have entered the public eye.

The formula for plum blossom soup from traditional Chinese medicine shops is indeed authentic, and the taste seems more refined than regular plum blossom soup. But the question arises: is plum blossom soup suitable for users with different constitutions? It might be selective.

As new drinks continue to emerge, how should we choose? Or, more importantly, what do we really want to drink?

Consumers are demanding more from drinks. But what do they value more—health or taste?

More and more consumers are paying attention to their health. They place greater emphasis on the nutritional value and benefits of drinks.

In their eyes, healthy drinks should be low in sugar, low in fat, free of additives, and made from natural ingredients. They prefer drinks based on fresh fruits, vegetables, and whole grains, such as juices, salads, and yogurt.

Maintaining good health is a common pursuit for everyone, so healthy drinks have gained widespread attention and popularity in the market.

However, consumers are not satisfied with just healthy drinks. They also value the sensory experience of taste and flavor. Deliciousness is one of the key factors that attract consumers.

They enjoy drinks with rich textures and distinct layers. A cup of rich coffee, a bowl ofrich chocolate drink, or a cup of milk tea can all bring consumers a pleasant taste experience.

These delicious drinks also have a broad market share because consumers want to enjoy their taste while also satisfying their taste buds.

In the demand for drinks, health and taste are not mutually exclusive but can complement each other. Consumers want to enjoy the sensory pleasure of deliciousness while pursuing health.

Therefore, beverage manufacturers and stores need to find a balance between health and taste. They can use natural ingredients and healthy production methods to provide nutritious drinks while focusing on taste and flavor to meet consumers' comprehensive expectations.

Consumers want drinks that are both healthy and delicious. This is their comprehensive requirement for drinks. Healthy drinks can meet their attention to health, while delicious drinks can satisfy their taste buds.

For beverage manufacturers and stores, finding the balance between health and taste, focusing on natural ingredients and taste, is essential to meet consumers' comprehensive expectations and gain continuous attention and recognition in the market.

Health and taste are not conflicting. From my perspective, it's about adjusting our taste to the point where we can feel the natural flavor of food without needing extra seasonings, and truly experience the unique taste of each ingredient. Only then do we truly understand the ingredients.

Removing others' so-called standards of what's good, finding our own choices, and pursuing the natural taste of food, I think this is a more lasting choice.

Of course, I still want to talk a bit more about drinking water. It's a good habit. A glass of warm water can refresh your mind. Drink it anytime, don't be afraid of the hassle. If you can't, set a reminder on your phone to remind yourself to drink water.

Last night, I talked a lot with my dad about what to drink. We talked about a business in the family. My dad is making soy products, and soy milk is an essential part of our family.

How can we provide high-quality soy milk to users has become a problem?

Fresh soy milk is a popular healthy drink for many people. However, compared to other drinks, its shelf life is indeed short. Why is the shelf life of fresh soy milk so limited?

Fresh soy milk is clearly different from store-bought soy milk. Store-bought soy milk often extends its shelf life through high-temperature sterilization or the addition of preservatives, while fresh soy milk is a natural, additive-free drink.

The process of making fresh soy milk is simple: soaking beans, grinding them, and filtering. It completely relies on the natural components of the beans, with no preservatives or stabilizers added. This pure natural method is what limits the shelf life of fresh soy milk.

Soy milk is rich in nutrients such as protein, fiber, and vitamins. However, these nutrients in fresh soy milk are easily affected by oxidation.

Antioxidants like vitamin C and vitamin E in fresh soy milk cannot prevent the oxidation of nutrients, which leads to a relatively short shelf life for fresh soy milk.

Within a few hours after making fresh soy milk, the vitamins and proteins in it gradually oxidize and degrade, losing their fresh flavor.

Another reason for the short shelf life of fresh soy milk is microbial growth. The beans in fresh soy milk contain a certain amount of moisture and nutrients, which provide conditions for microbial growth.

If the soy milk isn't consumed within a short time or stored properly, microbes will start to multiply, leading to spoilage.

The short shelf life of fresh soy milk is caused by multiple factors. The natural production method and the absence of additives make soy milk healthier, but they also limit its shelf life. The easy oxidation of nutrients and microbial growth accelerate the spoilage of soy milk.

Therefore, for fresh soy milk, the best way to enjoy its nutrition and flavor is to drink it as soon as possible while it's still fresh.

So, the soy milk sold for several days in the market must have additives. And that popular ice jasmine soy milk, which comes in plastic bags, is questionable. If it's just powder, then it's not better than Nongfu Soy Milk Powder.

From the discussion about soy milk with my dad, we moved on to business. Some businesses are not as simple as they seem. I thought soy milk was a good product, but I didn't realize it could cause food poisoning. Ensuring freshness makes it hard to control the production volume.

Running a business requires understanding user needs. Truly understanding user needs and ensuring quality is no easy task.

For example, when Ford did user research, they found that users wanted faster carriages. How should they do it? Is it by finding faster carriages?

Schumpeter once said, "A car cannot be a collection of carriages. Disruptive innovation breaks the implicit assumptions."

Instead of the horses on the carriages, we needed to create devices that could be adjusted and operated continuously. That's how the car was born. But how would users know they wanted a car? Just like how Jobs created the smartphone, users only realized that phones didn't have to be durable.

Li Xiaolai once shared a six-character key in his new course: finding demand. The key is to identify needs: necessity, frequency, and wide reach.

This has given me a new perspective on demand. Often, working in isolation is not enough. We need to truly find user needs, which is actually quite difficult.

A mobile phone charger is a classic example of a product that meets the necessity, frequency, and wide reach of user needs. People almost need it every day, whether at home, in the office, or on the go.

1. **Necessity**: A phone charger is an essential item in daily life. Almost everyone needs it. Whether students, white-collar workers, or the elderly, almost everyone has a phone, making a phone charger a must-have item. Without a phone charger, people would be unable to use their phones continuously, limiting their ability to make calls, send messages, and access the internet.

2. **Frequency**: The usage frequency of a phone charger is very high. Due to the limited battery capacity of phones, people almost need to charge their phones every day to ensure normal use. In various public places like homes, offices, cafes, and libraries, people can be seen using phone chargers. Especially when traveling, the need for a phone charger becomes more urgent, as a phone without power can't handle emergencies or maintain communication with others.

3. **Wide Reach**: The demand for phone chargers covers almost all phone users. Whether it's an iPhone, an Android, or any other brand of smartphone, they all need corresponding chargers. As smartphones become more widespread, the demand for phone chargers also spreads widely. At the same time, the diversity of phone chargers meets the different needs of users, such as standard chargers, fast chargers, and car chargers, to satisfy various charging habits and scenarios.

A phone charger, as a product that meets the necessity, frequency, and wide reach of user needs, is deeply loved and valued by the public. It is not only an indispensable companion in daily life but also a convenience brought by technological progress. Due to its importance and large market demand, the phone charger has become a massive industry chain, attracting participation and competition from various manufacturers, further promoting technological innovation and product upgrades.

Running a business is about developing continuously in practice, thinking as you go, and doing as you think, step by step.

I hope my dad's business goes better, and I hope everyone can make their own business better. Let's keep working hard together.
