**First Book: *Think and Grow Rich* by Napoleon Hill**

*Think and Grow Rich* is called "the ancestor of all motivational literature." It was the first book to boldly ask, "What makes winners?" The person who proposed and listened to the answer, Napoleon Hill, is now considered part of the world championship elite. The most famous of all success teachers spent "a large sum of money and most of his life's effort" to create the philosophy of "success laws," which formed the basis of his book and was powerfully summarized in this work.

In the original 1937 edition of *Think and Grow Rich*, Hill cited the stories of Andrew Carnegie, Thomas Edison, Henry Ford, and other millionaires of his generation to illustrate his principles. In updated versions, Dr. Arthur R. Pell, a nationally renowned writer, lecturer, and human resources management consultant, also an expert in applying Hill's ideas, skillfully interwove anecdotes about contemporary millionaires and billionaires like Bill Gates, Mary Kay Ash, Dave Thomas, and Sir John Templeton on how they achieved their wealth. Outdated or obscure terminology and examples were faithfully refreshed to remove any obstacles for new readers.

> Almost all great wealth begins in the form of personal service compensation or through the sale of ideas, which should be encouraging.
>
> *Think and Grow Rich*, Chapter Six — Napoleon Hill

This passage opened the door to the study of personal branding. A century ago, people began to think more about branding, especially personal branding. Although success philosophy has made some people wary, associating it with terms like "spiritual pyramid scheme, fraud, and PUA," my advice is to set aside external opinions, media reports, and others' views. Think about whether the content of the book is helpful to you. The so-called "motivational advice" is nutritious, but only if the audience is right or if you approach it without preconceptions.

**Second Book: *Small and Beautiful: The Rules of Sustainable Profit* by Sahil Lavingia**

Minimalist Entrepreneurs: How Great Founders Do More with Less.

This book is a revolutionary roadmap for a startup company, showing how it can emerge from the cracks, grow continuously, and eventually become a company worth billions through an IPO. They are unprofitable, unethical, and unsustainable — so why chase unicorns? Minimalist entrepreneurs are a declaration by the new generation of founders, Lavingia, who prefer to build great companies rather than big ones.

The Chinese translation of this book is *Small and Beautiful: The Rules of Sustainable Profit*, which is based on Lavingia's journey in building Gumroad, a company that accumulated hard-earned, battle-tested experience. It teaches founders how to start building any kind of software business from anywhere. The book shares how to resist investments that lead to founder failure; how to manage tight situations in the gig economy and remote work; how to develop and launch products without rushing or frequently failing; and how to achieve profitability and stay there.

Minimalist entrepreneurs provide the necessary knowledge for every founder who wants to build a company worth building. I saw that even the smallest individual has infinite possibilities, and this is real. The numerous case studies in the book made me realize that in today’s era of easy information dissemination and content creation, we truly have multiple channels to share and sell our own content.
