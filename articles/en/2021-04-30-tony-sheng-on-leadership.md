Regarding the meaning of leadership and the qualities a leader should possess, Jack Ma said, "Today's world needs leadership not to prove how powerful you are, but to work together with humanity to solve problems. 'As a leader, you must do more than others. Leaders should do things others don't want to do, things society needs, things consumers need, and things the people need. Leaders must not only be smart but also wise, because they know when to let go. Leaders must have courage and know how to change. Becoming a leader isn't because you're physically strong, but because you have love in your heart.'"

Jack Ma also discussed the relationship between technology and leadership. He believed that the first technological revolution led to World War I, the second to World War II, and now we are in the third technological revolution. Today's technology is the most powerful in history. We have the best resources and equipment, yet we lack the best leadership. "Technology does not improve leadership. Imagine weapons becoming more advanced, but leadership remains unchanged—it's a threat to you and to the whole world."

Jack Ma emphasized that "doing no harm is not the goal of a tech company, but a bottom line." "We must create value and take responsibility. If others are worried about unemployment, high-tech companies should find ways to create jobs." Additionally, "we should also fight against poverty, disease, and environmental pollution."

What exactly is leadership? Many people think that if you're not a leader, you don't need leadership. Liu Lan, a teacher, gave an accurate answer: Do you need responsibility? If you believe you need to take responsibility and solve problems, that ability is leadership. Leadership can be described as a combination of behaviors that inspire people to follow the leader to where they need to go—not just simple obedience. According to the definition of leadership, we can see it all around us, in management, in classrooms, on sports fields, in government, in the military, in multinational corporations, in small companies, even in a small family. We can see leadership at every level and in every field; it is the core of doing everything well. A title or position cannot automatically make someone a leader.

As many people try to define leadership, there are as many definitions. Some believe that a leader is someone at the core of organizational change and activity, striving to realize a vision. Others think leadership is an innate quality that enables a person to guide others to complete tasks. There's also the view that leadership relates to the power relationship between the leader and their subordinates, with the leader having power and using it to influence others. And leadership is also seen as a tool to achieve goals, helping team members reach their objectives. Although the definitions vary, they all share a core concept.

> Leadership is a process. Leadership involves mutual influence. Leadership occurs within an organization.

So what is leadership? It is a special kind of interpersonal influence. Everyone in an organization influences others and is also influenced by others, so every employee has potential and actual leadership. Within an organization, leaders and members work together to push the team toward a set goal, forming an organic system. Within this system, there are several key elements: the leader's personality traits and leadership skills, the employees' initiative, the positive interaction between leaders and employees, the formulation and realization of organizational goals.

Whether the system functions properly depends on whether these elements can develop in harmony. The key to harmonious development lies in the interaction between the leader and other members. Whether this interaction can form a unified understanding, emotion, and behavior is a necessary condition for leadership to be properly exercised.

> Becoming a Leader of Others

Leadership can be divided into two levels: one is organizational leadership, which refers to the influence an organization as a whole has on other organizations and individuals. This level of leadership involves the organization's culture, strategy, and execution. The second is individual leadership, which for a company means the leadership of its managers and leaders at all levels. Organizational leadership is based on individual leadership. How to break through and improve leadership, how to become a leader of oneself, then a leader of others, and eventually an outstanding leader, is a pressing issue we need to address now.

                Leadership is "bringing a vision to an organization and bringing the ability to realize that vision."
