Today, I want to chat with everyone about wealth. I think this topic is something everyone enjoys. When did you form your views on money?

To make wealth a pleasant force in your life, you need to establish the right mindset about money from a young age.

Money itself is neutral, but it reveals people's true nature. "Dreaming of becoming rich" isn't bad at all—you can take it as your life goal and work hard to achieve it.

I think many people probably have this dream too! Have you ever had such thoughts?

As long as you can identify what your heart truly desires, know what you can do and what you're good at, focus your energy on these things, keep going consistently, and over time, you'll be rewarded with financial success, helping you realize your dreams.

But no matter when, you must stay rational. Don't spend your earned money carelessly. Instead, let your money become a goose that lays golden eggs.

This is the first particularly fun metaphor in this book, and the golden-egg-laying goose reminded me of the fable by Aesop. Let's take a look!

There was a farmer who raised a goose that could lay a golden egg every day. He made a lot of money by selling the eggs. Soon, the farmer began to think that the goose only laying one egg a day was too little. He believed that the goose's stomach must be full of gold and decided to kill it to take out all the gold. But when he opened the goose's belly, he found that this golden-egg-laying goose was no different from a regular goose. There was no gold inside. In the end, he didn't become a rich man and lost the daily income of a golden egg.

What are your thoughts on this story?

I mainly summarize it into three points:

The goose is an asset; killing it will lead to regret. If you treat the goose as an income-generating asset, the golden eggs are the positive cash flow it brings. Only with the goose can you get the golden eggs.

The goose is an asset or skill that helps you reach your goals, and the golden egg is the desired outcome. Focusing only on the golden egg and ignoring the goose will result in losing even the capital to produce the golden eggs. Conversely, those who prioritize the goose over the egg may end up unable to support themselves, let alone the goose.

In the story, the goose lays one golden egg a day, representing that wealth accumulation requires patience. More important than how many eggs the goose lays in a day is whether it can lay them consistently every day. A stable cash flow can sometimes be more useful than a lump sum of money all at once.

This book is full of interesting expressions. The author avoids dry financial jargon and tells children four aspects in an easy-to-understand way:

First, form the right mindset about money. Money is neutral and doesn't determine whether a person's nature is good or bad.

Second, take "dreaming of becoming rich" as your goal, and create a dream album and a piggy bank for this goal.

Third, focus your energy on what you can do and try to earn your first bucket of money.

Fourth, act with rationality and don't spend your earned money carelessly. Let your money become a goose that lays golden eggs.

These four points are for you, dear readers, to share your thoughts on.

Finally, I'd like to ask you a question: What are some ways children can earn their first bucket of money? I look forward to your creative ideas!
