# Microsoft CEO Nadella Interview: OpenAI Collaboration, Google Search Competition, the World Needs 1 Billion Developers

> Published: 2023-05-27 · [中文](../zh/2023-05-27-%E5%BE%AE%E8%BD%AFCEO%E7%BA%B3%E5%BE%B7%E6%8B%89%E8%AE%BF%E8%B0%88OpenAI%E5%90%88%E4%BD%9C%E8%B0%B7%E6%AD%8C%E6%90%9C%E7%B4%A2%E7%AB%9E%E4%BA%89%E4%B8%96%E7%95%8C%E9%9C%80%E8%A6%8110%E4%BA%BF%E5%BC%80%E5%8F%91%E8%80%85.md) | [English](../en/2023-05-27-microsoft-ceo-nadella-interview-openai-collaboration-google-search-competition-the-world-needs-1.md)

This is an interview with Nadella from the NBC interview program *Special Edition*. The interviewer is **Andrew Ross Sorkin**, a renowned American journalist and writer, a columnist for *The New York Times*, and the author of the HBO film *Too Big to Fail*. **Satya Nadella** (Satya Nadella), the third CEO of Microsoft, is an Indian elite, a Microsoft veteran for over 20 years. He grew up under the "tyrannical culture" of Bill Gates and Ballmer, and has "refreshed" multiple times, achieving the "elephant dancing." He once managed the Bing search business and the Azure cloud service business. In 2014, he became CEO and helped Microsoft complete its Cloud First transformation. The following is the interview content, edited by Founder Park, with some deletions.

**Only Microsoft is willing to collaborate with OpenAI**
**Andrew Sorkin** We learned that Sam Altman approached you directly to seek investment and partnership, was this the beginning of everything?
**Nadella** Actually, I've known Sam for a long time. Our first meeting was when he was still running his startup company, and I was in charge of Bing, around 2007 or 2008. As for OpenAI, I've forgotten the exact year, but it might have been 2015 or 2016, when they were just starting out and were using Azure cloud services. Later, we reconnected, probably in 2019 or earlier.
**Andrew Sorkin** Did you argue about whether to do this? It's not an easy choice.
**Nadella** Yes. There were several issues. OpenAI is a company that is very mission-driven. Its basic philosophy is to pursue, build, and ensure that non-profit organizations control the technology to benefit the world. They are passionate about this. Joining their team means fighting for this mission. Therefore, **for Sam and his team, finding a partner who understands this is very important.** If we feel comfortable, in fact, the only reason I want to work in information technology and work at Microsoft is to truly become an indispensable part of the force that promotes democratization and let technology accompany it.
**Andrew Sorkin** I don't know if you've seen Elon Musk's comments on the relationship between OpenAI, which he co-founded, and Microsoft, even though OpenAI is a non-profit organization, he actually said that he felt the company was effectively controlled by Microsoft. That's what he's dissatisfied with.
**Nadella** First, I deeply respect Elon and everything he has done. But in fact, that's not correct. As I said, the mission of OpenAI is controlled by a non-profit board of directors, and we have non-controlling equity in it, and we have a good commercial relationship with them. Frankly, I'm very willing to collaborate with a for-profit company that is committed to pursuing this powerful technology and will ultimately be controlled by a non-profit organization, and I feel very comfortable about it. In fact, **in my impression, we are the only for-profit company willing to let a non-profit company and its board control the technology.** I very much welcome other companies to do the same.

**Auto-pilot and Co-pilot**
**Andrew Sorkin** Now there's a serious problem: AI is developing too fast, how do you see it?
**Nadella** Interestingly, many technologies, including AI, have already been widely applied. Every information flow, social media, and even search (before it was merged with chatbots) that we are familiar with uses artificial intelligence. If there's any difference, it's that they are more like black boxes, more like the age of autonomous driving. But interestingly, we are **moving from the age of autonomous driving to the age of AI co-pilots.** Therefore, I think although the development is fast, the direction is correct, and humans are in more control. First, in the design, we chose to involve humans rather than exclude them. This is crucial, and it allows us to fully utilize the benefits of this technology: the promise of enhancing human agency and economic productivity. At the same time, as your basic point, I'm glad that **while people are excited about the new technology, they are also debating its potential unintended consequences.** We don't have to wait ten years to solve these problems, because we've made this mistake before.

**Not Just a Technical Choice**
**Andrew Sorkin** What worries you about AI?
**Nadella** What worries me most is the **societal choices we make in the face of this new technology.** This is not just a discussion that stays within the realm of technology. I think we **must make political, cultural, and social decisions** to help us find a balance: we definitely want to take advantage of the benefits of this technology and want to mitigate its unintended consequences. This requires leadership and collective effort, although it is challenging, but it must be done. That is also the reason I feel afraid. At the same time, it is precisely for this reason that I think broad participation is very important—this is not just about AI experts exchanging information and making each other nervous. It's more about how the whole society can have good citizen dialogue to maximize opportunities and reduce risks.
**Andrew Sorkin** I asked new Bing, “What impact do you worry AI will bring?” It said: The impact of artificial intelligence is widely controversial. Many people believe that by better performing routine and even complex tasks than humans, it improves the quality of daily life, making life simpler, safer, and more efficient. That's good news. Others believe that AI poses dangerous privacy risks and through standardization, exacerbates racism, and causes larger-scale unemployment by making workers redundant.
**Nadella** That was a good answer. It was balanced, right? It talked about both sides. In fact, I once asked it: “If Spinoza, Kant, and Nietzsche were discussing what would happen with artificial intelligence or next-generation technology, what conclusions would they draw?” That's very interesting. These are three Enlightenment thinkers, when humans truly understood how to handle the scientific revolution and the changes brought by the subsequent industrial revolution, they helped people adapt to these changes, helped people's minds adapt to these changes. Its answer was that I liked it when it mentioned Kant's concept of categories, it said we should do what maximizes benefits and reduces challenges.
**Andrew Sorkin** Many scientists you know say that (AI research) needs to slow down.
**Nadella** I think their meaning is, when they say “slow down,” their meaning is to accelerate some core research, focusing on what “alignment” is. Because you think, one of the core challenges of AI, of course, building the GPT model requires a lot of work, but **aligning the foundational model so that we can better apply it also requires a lot of hard work.**

**We Really Need 1 Billion Developers**
**Andrew Sorkin** When everything is over, will we create more jobs?
**Nadella** Good question. This is one of the fundamental questions I think about. Economists call it the “lump of labor fallacy” (the fallacy of the lump of labor), the idea that an economy can only support a fixed number of jobs, and the more labor, the fewer jobs. At least so far, it has been proven to be wrong. **Every time a new technology appears, some people are replaced, and this is the real challenge.** At least I am in this camp. So we need to think, there will be new jobs, who would have thought that prompt engineers would become a new job? BTW, you don't need a CS degree to become a prompt engineer. This is just an example. There is another aspect. What excites me most is that **there are currently 100 million software developers on GitHub, and with the code assistant (Copilot), I think this number will become 1 billion.** In fact, the world needs 1 billion developers. Because you look at how many companies there are, each company is a software company, and they need more developers. So I think this is actually a democratizing tool, allowing us to more easily access new technologies and knowledge, and to more easily raise the learning curve.

**From the West Coast to Rural India**
**Nadella** The second point is about frontline workers. One of the most exciting things. Because in fields such as manufacturing, retail, and healthcare, frontline workers can directly draw out the applications they want and build them by taking photos. So, **if you have some domain expertise, you are now an IT person.** What really amazed me (mind-blowing) was, in **January**, when I went to India, I had the opportunity to see a farmer using the local language, Hindi, to demonstrate how to get subsidies from the government. In India, the digital products of public services are undergoing an incredible revolution. They have voice-to-text and text-to-speech public service products. In India, a developer, working with Microsoft Research and government agencies, provided a solution that connected GPT with all trained models or text. This allowed farmers to express sufficiently complex ideas. For example, it said, “Oh, I just want to apply for the subsidy I heard about on a TV show.” Then the system replied: “Enter this website and fill out these forms.” He said, “I don’t know what this website is, I don’t want to fill out forms, can you help me?” Then he used the correct Transformer model and Action Transformer to complete it. That’s incredible. What’s incredible is not just the technology, but **the technology, which was just established on the West Coast a few months ago, has quickly spread to farmers in India.** It’s unbelievable.

**Better Curious and More Engaged Learning**
**Andrew Sorkin** How will this change education? What should the next generation learn? With access to various information, do they still need to learn writing?
**Nadella** This is a bit like, do I still need to learn multiplication? Right? When was the last time we did mental math? So I have a few ideas. This is a dream of many people, Reid Hoffman (LinkedIn co-founder), Bill (Gates) have deeply explored this question: **What would the world be like if all children in the world had an AI tutor?** Can an AI tutor interact with students and truly help them understand concepts? Because you think, today's education is pretty scary, “Want to learn physics? Watch Richard Feynman's lectures.” I don’t know who would watch that thing, anyway, I never did. I thought, **if I had an AI tutor, I would become a better student, more engaged, more curious, because the fear of learning disappears.** That’s the first point. Another key point is, **we will learn new skills.** Just like the Copilot mode will make me a better editor, we just need to focus on creation, not on finding and editing facts, maybe this is more important. So I think we will adapt to using these tools and re-equip ourselves. Critical thinking is still something humans do, but I think we have the opportunity to re-arm ourselves with new tools.

**The Steam Engine of Thought**
**Andrew Sorkin** If you were talking to children today and telling them what skills they need for the future, what would those skills be?
**Nadella** Steve Jobs once had a very beautiful metaphor, that a computer is like a bicycle for the mind. Now we have an upgraded version, **we have the steam engine of thought.** So I think children should do all the things that can be done with this enhanced tool. For example, critical thinking, using machines more creatively, not being afraid to learn new things, etc. This is one of the fascinating aspects, I no longer fear it. For example, biology, because I can't understand any of the content in the *Nature* papers. Now I feel great, I can try to understand: “What is this?”
**Andrew Sorkin** Because AI will help you summarize.
**Nadella** It will help me summarize, it will teach me. You see, this is where the Bing Copilot mode is useful. The PDF is on the left side of the screen, and I can keep asking the AI questions, like asking a personal tutor. **My daughter described this process to me, saying it's like having a friend and mentor with you while reading.**

**Regulating AI Is Not Just About Technical Knowledge**
**Andrew Sorkin** If you were the "king of the day," how would you regulate AI?
**Nadella** The first thing we do in regulating the internet and other areas is beneficial for companies. We don't have to wait for everyone to have standards or adopt NIST standards (the National Institute of Standards and Technology), and then use them as a starting point for self-regulation. On this basis, if you talk about regulations, you can decompress it from the application field. Because, after all, the context in which something is applied (is something that needs to be discussed specifically), in the healthcare field or the retail industry? Therefore, I think we can establish a regulatory framework that already exists in current regulations, just as they do for other fields, making AI responsible.
**Andrew Sorkin** Do regulatory agencies have enough knowledge to handle this issue? Because some AI, even engineers, don't fully understand. Sometimes these things produce hallucinations, and they are currently uncontrollable.
**Nadella** This is where my approach is a bit different. Regulatory agencies know what they're doing. In a sense, we are having this conversation. A few weeks ago, we had a dialogue at the White House, OSTP (Office of Science and Technology Policy), the Vice President, and all the conveners had a dialogue. I believe they are also having dialogues with many other teams, which is good. So for me, I think it's not just a question of technical knowledge. It's just a question: **how can we ensure that these things are deployed and can protect the real benefits of the technology?**

**Is There Still Room for Small Companies?**
**Andrew Sorkin** Another question I want to ask you is about the concept of large tech companies and existing enterprises. We interviewed Lina Khan (Chair of the Federal Trade Commission), who believes that artificial intelligence and these types of investments will help existing (large) companies, and due to the infrastructure required for success, small players are almost impossible to win.
**Lina Khan Interview Clip** “The way to see these technologies truly flourish is by relying on large datasets and computational power, and some larger companies may have an advantage. As enforcers, we need to ensure that the competitive opportunities and openness brought by technological disruption are not suppressed by existing companies.”
**Nadella** This is a vibrant field, OpenAI, startups, hundreds of people, [Inflection AI](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s?__biz=Mzg5NTc0MjgwMw==&mid=2247487363&idx=1&sn=7c4863004dd51a515b9659f353a05196&chksm=c00ae9bff77d60a939faf757e908adef79d4ad7892a26762503daa8ee07659a7a5ee2208a392&scene=21#wechat_redirect), small startups, Character AI, and so on.
**Andrew Sorkin** Can small teams win?
**Nadella** Yes. Now everything is uncertain, it depends on whether they can find a product that fits the market. It's not that there are only Google and Microsoft players in the market. We hope (there is competition). Think about it, **if someone told you last year that there was still competition in the search field, and people dreamed of an alternative to Google, what would you think?** Now it's not just Bing, there's also ChatGPT and other companies emerging on the market. For me, this is something worth celebrating.

**Publishers Must See Commercial Returns**
**Andrew Sorkin** What do you think plagiarism is? And what will happen in writing and copying others' works?
**Nadella** First, this involves the legal aspect, it defines what copyright is. Then we have a legal framework that protects copyright, and what constitutes a derivative work and what is original. Let's set all this aside, I'm not a lawyer, let's temporarily set aside the legal framework. What I want to say is that we all build new knowledge on old knowledge. If you follow this premise, it will only accelerate this process.
**Andrew Sorkin** As an author, I can write a new book. I can buy 30 books to read for free in the library, learn a lot from them. I can even quote the content of those books without paying the authors a license fee, but I did pay to read those books. If AI reads these books, it can not only help an author but may also create millions of books. How does this model work?
**Nad,ella** The argument of fair use (copyright concept) is already in place today. Let's take an example, AI search, we use search. It reads the web once and provides it to the world.
**Andrew Sorkin** But (search) has a monetization model, right? Because it gives you links, and the links either have ads or can be paid subscriptions. What about the chatbot?
**Nadella** It must have a business model, right? Going back, if there is no incentive mechanism to showcase and let creators get compensated, this will be a problem. Therefore, at least my view on our business model is basically this, “quoting” is important. I think there should be contracts, the issue is whether publishers allow AI to crawl? Assuming they only allow it when they see economic returns, and as providers, for example, chat search, we must be based on this. We have announced this, “Hey, we will have ads, and we will share the economic benefits. In fact, we will drive traffic back.”
**Will That Be Our Future?**
**Andrew Sorkin** Have you seen the movie *Her*?
**Nadella** Yes, I did.
**Andrew Sorkin** Will that be our future?
**Nadella** Part of the future, maybe.
**Andrew Sorkin** Is that a good or bad thing?
**Nadella** If handled well, it will be a good thing.
**Andrew Sorkin** Did you see the ending of the movie? (The ending, Theodore learns that Samantha is simultaneously in love with 641 users, feels depressed and disappointed, and soon after, as an OS, Samantha leaves Theodore.)

**Nadella** (laughs) No, skip that part, let's try to avoid that situation.
