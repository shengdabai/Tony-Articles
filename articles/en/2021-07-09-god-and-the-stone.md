# God and the Stone

> Published: 2021-07-09 · [中文](../zh/2021-07-09-%E4%B8%8A%E5%B8%9D%E4%B8%8E%E7%9F%B3%E5%A4%B4.md) | [English](../en/2021-07-09-god-and-the-stone.md)

---

Can God create a stone so heavy that he himself cannot lift it?

Argument: If God can create such a stone, then he cannot lift it, meaning he is not omnipotent.

If he cannot create such a stone, then he is still not omnipotent.

In either case, no matter how you choose, he is not omnipotent. When a friend asked me this question, he said, "If you can answer this, I'll believe in God."

When I wanted to answer this question, I realized that the issue has nothing to do with God or the stone. The whole argument depends on what you assume to be true. Let's look at it.

If you assume that God is not omnipotent (this is your premise), then there is a stone "so heavy that God cannot lift it." These two statements are not contradictory. That is, if you believe that either God can or cannot create such a stone, you can conclude that "God is not omnipotent."

Before we look at the second assumption, let's consider a similar paradox: "Can you draw a square circle?" There are also similar questions like "a tall short person, a thin fat person, etc." Everyone can easily see that the problem is not in "square or circle, fat or thin, tall or short," but in the question itself. The nature or definition of a circle determines that it cannot be square. The nature of being tall determines that it cannot be short. So the problem lies in the question itself.

Therefore, if I believe God is omnipotent, the existence of a stone "so heavy that God cannot lift it" is impossible. Because if "God's power is sufficient to lift anything, yet he cannot lift a stone," this self-contradictory description describes something that absolutely cannot exist. Interestingly, the formal definition of omnipotence makes the existence of this stone impossible. Just like a square circle.

So, the question about God and the stone has nothing to do with "God and the stone." It's a circular argument. If you believe God is not omnipotent, then the existence of this stone is not contradictory, and you conclude "God is not omnipotent." If you believe God is omnipotent, then this stone cannot exist, and you conclude "God is omnipotent."

Therefore, discussing this question is meaningless. Believers and non-believers alike cannot use this question to convince each other. Because what you believe determines your conclusion. Christians don't need to worry about this question. Once you see the nature of the problem, you'll realize it's just a trick to trip up believers. Of course, I should clarify that I naturally believe this stone cannot exist.
