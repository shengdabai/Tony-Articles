A debate topic from *The Odd Couple*:

> Should independent women accept a dowry?

First, let's define what an independent woman is. It can be summarized into four abilities: financial freedom, independent thinking, the ability to weigh pros and cons, and the ability to make choices. The key point is independent thinking, or Critical Thinking. It seems that there are not many Critical Thinkers around. With these abilities, the shining traits of an independent woman are more rational, calm, and able to know when to advance and when to retreat, and to understand the benefits and drawbacks. She knows how to think about emotional and marital issues with a market and social mindset, rather than being limited to emotions and feelings.

Next, what is marriage? Marriage is an economic act based on emotion. The economic foundation determines the superstructure. Usually, the person who earns more has a natural advantage in a marriage. In *The Odd Couple*, Professor Xue Zhaofeng compared marriage to two people starting a business together. Women bear the responsibilities of childbirth and child-rearing, which means they invest more resources and face greater risks than men. Therefore, when entering a marriage, "a woman asks a man for some collateral, to serve as a safety net in case something goes wrong."

Dowry doesn't need much explanation—it's a form of material security, including things like money, cars, and houses. Once we have a clear definition, let's talk about many women who call themselves independent. Because they earn their own money, they don't want to talk about money anymore, fearing trouble and hurting feelings. This mindset doesn't align with economic principles or human nature, and it even lacks independent thinking, which is harmful to both herself and her marriage. From a female perspective, first, women invest more in marriage. The investment women make in marriage is a physiological issue—once you have a uterus, you can't avoid it. Second, marriage affects a woman's "career prospects." Workplace discrimination is essentially fertility discrimination. Pregnancy and childbirth can be a career-ending blow for women. Even excellent women, after giving birth to two children, usually can't return to important positions in their companies. From a natural gender perspective, whether independent or not, the rules of marriage are that women invest first and bear greater risks. Gaining some property support before marriage is essentially a compensation for the losses women face after marriage. Independent women aren't accepting a dowry—they're getting a kind of marriage insurance, which is necessary when the maternal protection system is incomplete and workplace discrimination is rampant.

I thought of Li Xiaolai's advice to invest in quality assets regularly. Essentially, marriage is like that too. It's someone we've carefully selected, even if someone says marriage is an unavoidable choice. Isn't it also the best choice for us after careful selection? Recently, I've been learning in a writing training camp, and I read Mr. Qian Zhongshu's only long novel, *Fortress Besieged*. It's incrediblyremarkable. The novel describes many types of love, and the main character ends up marrying a colleague, possibly due to patriarchal pressure, company gossip, and psychological burdens. It's undeniable that we might feel some helplessness, but it's ultimately a good ending for the main character. Comparing this to today's topic, independent women, when choosing quality investment assets, should they have some kind of protection, should they buy insurance for their investments? I think they should. The dowry fulfills this function, offering additional security under the current incomplete female social security system.
