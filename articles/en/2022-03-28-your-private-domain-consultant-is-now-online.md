First Note

01 Revisiting "Free," we often hear the phrase "paying is like getting a bargain." In other words, "free comes at a cost."

02 Attention is a scarce resource, and free things are highly attractive to us. Gradually, free becomes your first choice.

03 Is free stuff high quality or just cheap?

04 Consider the cost, take into account the spread, save where possible—since it's already free, who wouldn't want it? If everyone wants it, I can't afford to lose out, so I'll just do a quick job.

05 For example, you often meet people on the street who give you tissues or small gifts you can't name. Then you notice the logos and ads on them.

06 There's someone who doesn't think like that. He believes free is because he wants more people to benefit. So he started teaching math online, made a lot of short videos, and many people benefited from it. The courses grew, the scale expanded.

07 When the content and products you offer for free are your best, you might get investment. For example, Bill Gates invested in Salman Khan's "Khan Academy."
