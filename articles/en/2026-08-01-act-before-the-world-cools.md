# Act Before the World Cools

> 发布日期:2026-08-01 · [中文](../zh/2026-08-01-趁世界还没冷却.md) | [English](../en/2026-08-01-act-before-the-world-cools.md)

---

The most important time to act is often not when everything is obvious.

By then, the world has already cooled.

This is a strange sentence, so let me slow it down. Some situations are still hot. Many paths are possible. Several explanations sound reasonable. The rules are not fully written. People are still arguing over names, standards, habits, interfaces, and what counts as good work.

Then, quietly, the situation cools.

One path becomes the default. One interface becomes natural. One word becomes the category. One workflow becomes the way things are done. What felt like a temporary choice starts looking like common sense. Later, historians and consultants arrive to explain why it was inevitable.

But it was not inevitable when it happened.

It was a small break in symmetry.

## A Tiny Difference Can Become a World

In 1972, a paper in *Science* used broken symmetry to explain why different layers of reality cannot be reduced to the same simple rules.[^1] A perfectly symmetrical situation may look elegant, but it is also strangely empty. Nothing has chosen a direction yet.

The universe itself carries this lesson. NASA's WMAP mission measured tiny differences in the cosmic microwave background, the afterglow from the early universe. These small temperature variations became part of the story of how matter later clumped into stars and galaxies.[^2]

The detail is almost insulting to our intuition. A tiny unevenness, carried forward through time, becomes a large structure. Once that structure exists, moving it is no longer a small matter. A galaxy can be born from a small early difference, but after it forms, you do not simply move it aside.

Human systems are not galaxies, of course. But they often behave with a similar rhythm.

At the beginning, a field is soft. The category is unclear. Nobody knows what the product should look like. Nobody knows which workflow will win. Nobody knows whose vocabulary will become normal.

Later, the field becomes hard.

After the World Wide Web was released into the public domain by CERN on April 30, 1993, an open set of protocols and habits could spread widely.[^3] That decision did not determine every website, every browser, every business model, or every cultural consequence. But it helped set the temperature and direction of a whole environment.

The same pattern appears in institutions. The U.S. two-term presidential tradition existed as a precedent for a long time before the 22nd Amendment formally codified it in 1951.[^4] A repeated choice became a norm; a norm later became a rule.

This is how cooling works.

First something is possible. Then it is repeated. Then it is expected. Then it is defended. Finally, people forget it was ever a choice.

## AI Is Still Hot

This is why I keep thinking about the current AI moment.

Many people are waiting for AI to become mature before they learn it deeply. This sounds sensible. Wait until the tools are stable. Wait until the winning platforms are obvious. Wait until the messy parts disappear. Wait until there is a clean course, a clean interface, a clean playbook.

But that is exactly the risk.

When a tool becomes clean enough for everyone, the gears disappear. You can use it, but you no longer understand why it behaves the way it behaves. You can click the button, but you cannot see the workflow. You can accept the default, but you cannot question the shape of the default.

The early stage is frustrating because it is unfinished.

It is also valuable because it is unfinished.

Right now, a normal person can still see the machine being assembled. You can compare models. You can break prompts. You can watch an agent misunderstand a task, then change the context and watch the behavior change. You can build a clumsy workflow, fail, rebuild, and develop a practical intuition that no polished interface can give you later.

This is not about becoming a technical priest.

It is about not becoming a passenger too early.

The person who waits for the final interface may become a competent user. That is fine. But the person who learns during the messy window has a different kind of power. They develop the instinct that things can be arranged differently. They know the interface is not nature. They know a workflow is a choice. They know a model's output is not an oracle; it is the product of context, tools, memory, constraints, and verification.

That instinct is rare.

And once a system cools, it becomes harder to acquire.

## The Window Is Not the Hype

There is a trap here. People hear "window" and immediately think "trend."

That is not what I mean.

A trend asks, "What is everyone talking about?"

A window asks, "What is not settled yet, but soon will be?"

These are very different questions. Hype is often loud after the important choices have already been made. A real window is usually quieter. It feels confusing. You see three or four plausible directions. Smart people disagree. The vocabulary is unstable. Early tools are powerful but awkward. Users are inventing private hacks. Companies are buying software they do not yet know how to use. Individuals are quietly building better workflows than their organizations.

That is the moment to pay attention.

Not because every early experiment wins. Most will not. The point is not to predict the exact winner. The point is to understand the structure while it is still visible.

In a hot system, learning is not only consumption. Learning is participation.

You name things. You make small examples. You write down standards. You create templates. You explain the difference between a toy and a workflow. You show what good looks like before people have a shared language for good. You do not need to control the whole field. You only need to make one useful local pattern real enough that others can copy it.

That is how a tiny difference becomes a default.

## Ordinary People Can Still Shape Local Defaults

Most of us will not define the global future of AI. That is not the point.

But each of us lives inside smaller systems: a team, a classroom, a client relationship, a writing habit, a personal knowledge base, a small business, a family routine, a community of practice.

These systems also cool.

The first AI habit inside a team may become the team's long-term AI culture. The first shared prompt may become the template everyone copies. The first successful automation may become the standard for what automation is allowed to touch. The first bad dashboard may teach everyone that AI is theater. The first useful workflow may teach everyone that AI is a tool for responsibility, not laziness.

This is where ordinary people have leverage.

You do not need permission to observe the temperature of your own environment.

Ask a few questions.

Is this space still arguing about what good means? Are people secretly using tools because official tools are too slow? Are there repeated tasks with no agreed workflow? Are there words everyone uses vaguely but nobody defines? Are there small mistakes that keep happening because no one has turned experience into a system?

If the answer is yes, the system is still warm.

Then do not write a manifesto. Make a sample.

A sample is stronger than an opinion. A sample says, "Here is the way this could work." It gives people something to react to, improve, reject, copy, or argue with. It lowers the cost of agreement. It turns private judgment into shared material.

In the AI age, a sample might be a reusable prompt, a checklist, a tiny tool, a reviewed workflow, a before-and-after example, a standard for what must be verified, or a short guide that helps one person do a real task better.

The sample does not have to be perfect.

It has to be concrete enough to cool around.

## The Zero Class Acts at the Moment of Formation

I often use the phrase "zero class" to remind myself what AI is for.

It is not a fantasy of doing nothing. It is not about becoming lazy while machines work. It is about escaping the old layer of mechanical execution and moving upward into judgment, design, verification, and system-building.

The zero class does not merely use finished systems.

It participates while systems are forming.

That is the difference between consumption and agency. A consumer asks, "Which tool should I buy?" A builder asks, "What workflow should exist here?" A passive user asks, "What does the interface allow?" A stronger user asks, "What assumption did the interface freeze?"

This is why learning AI now matters. Not because every person must become an engineer in the narrow sense. But because everyone who wants to remain strong needs some builder's intuition. You need to know when a default is merely a default. You need to know when a system is still soft enough to shape. You need to know how to turn a personal trick into a repeatable method without turning yourself into a slave of the method.

The future will not be divided only between people who use AI and people who do not.

Almost everyone will use AI.

The deeper divide will be between people who met AI while it was still warm and people who met it only after it cooled into buttons.

## Before Common Sense Arrives

There is a peaceful feeling in waiting for common sense.

Once everyone agrees, you no longer risk looking foolish. Once the category is mature, you can copy best practices. Once the interface is smooth, you do not have to wrestle with the gears. Once the rules are formal, you can say you were being prudent.

But common sense is often late wisdom.

It tells you what the world has already cooled into.

The better question is not "What is certain?" The better question is "What is still formable?"

When a field is too early, everything is vapor. When it is too late, everything is cement. The valuable moment is in between: warm enough to shape, cool enough to hold.

That is where a small, serious act matters.

Name the thing. Build the sample. Publish the note. Test the workflow. Teach one person. Write the standard. Remove one bad default before it hardens. Give one good habit a body before it disappears.

The world does not become fixed all at once.

It cools through repeated choices.

So if you care about learning, work, AI, or the kind of person you are becoming, do not wait only for certainty.

Watch for temperature.

When the system is still warm, move.

Not wildly. Not blindly. Not because the crowd is excited.

Move because this is the brief season when your action can still become structure.
