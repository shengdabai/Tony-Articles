The China Internet Network Information Center released the 52nd *Statistical Report on the Development of China's Internet* in August 2023.
As of June 2023, China's internet user base reached 1.079 billion, with 1.076 billion mobile internet users, accounting for 99.8%. The internet penetration rate reached 76.4%.
The following data can help us better understand the composition and digital skills of internet users.
I read through several recent statistical reports and wanted to take a look at the development of internet users in recent years. One thing caught my attention.
Starting from the 48th report, the data on the scale and structure of internet users focused mainly on the overall user base, urban and rural user bases, and the size of non-users. User attributes were limited to gender and age.
In previous reports, data on user attributes was more comprehensive. Let's look at the data from the 47th report, which was the last one to mention educational background, occupation, and income among internet users.
The 47th report showed that as of December 2020, China's internet user base reached 989 million. (Approximately 100 million new users were added during the three years of the pandemic.)
Here are three of the data points, let's take a closer look.

Approximately 589 million users have not completed high school or higher, accounting for 59.6%. About 289 million users have a monthly income of over 5,000 yuan, accounting for 29.3%. Students and individual entrepreneurs/freelancers together account for 37.9%.
(Other detailed data can be viewed and downloaded at the China Internet Network Information Center https://www.cnnic.cn/）)

In the era of information overload, we often see people viewing the same issue from different angles. However, with the popularity of social media, a phenomenon often arises: people with limited thinking resonate with each other online, criticizing views that differ from theirs, and dismissing facts they haven’t seen as deception.

"Well of the Frog's Resonance" refers to a group of people who, due to limited perspectives, can only hear their own voices, forming a resonance and rejecting or attacking any views that differ from theirs.

This phenomenon is especially common on the internet, making it difficult for people to reach consensus when discussing specific topics. Because these people haven’t been exposed to broader information and perspectives, they are easily misled and unable to see the bigger picture.

At its root, this is due to cognitive limitations and ethical constraints in the ecosystem. If people are not exposed to new information and perspectives, they will only see and hear information from within their own community, and they will reject and attack any information that contradicts their existing beliefs.

At the same time, people tend to believe they represent the correct view, and any differing opinion is seen as wrong and dangerous. This "we are right, they are wrong" stance can continuously expand and exaggerate on the internet, intensifying the resonance effect of the "well of the frog," leading to more severe information silos.

How can we avoid "Well of the Frog's Resonance"?

First, broaden your reading and perspective, and expose yourself to different viewpoints and information.

Second, don’t easily criticize those with different opinions. Try to understand them or look for common ground within a broader perspective.

Finally, develop critical thinking and approach the information you receive rationally, without placing too much trust in rumors and biases.

In the internet age, various problems and challenges, including "Well of the Frog's Resonance," keep emerging. These issues stem from our cognitive limitations and information constraints.

To address these issues, we should broaden our horizons, maintain an open mind, and respect different voices and perspectives, whether in real life or online. We should resist cognitive limitations and view problems from a broader perspective.

We now have access to more information than ever before. However, we are also more prone to falling into the trap of cognitive echo chambers, isolating ourselves within an information filter that cuts us off from the outside world.

The internet has introduced us to a completely different information ecosystem, and the information we encounter in our lives has entered a new dimension.

But as we enter this new dimension, we are also increasingly affected by inherent information biases. We only accept information that aligns with our values, and we exaggerate the negative impact of contradictory information, interpreting it through our own beliefs and finding confirmation within our own "certified group."

Due to algorithmic recommendations, we have already enclosed ourselves in an information filter that only includes agreement, certification, and praise. For voices that seem "heretical," we generalize and fail to understand them, placing them outside our mental screen.

After repeated exposure, we find ourselves trapped in a dilemma in our lives and work, forming our own cognitive echo chambers.

To break through the "social barriers," engage in conversations with different people, gain insight into different perspectives, and conduct more field research to seek information verification and assess the reliability of sources.

We should avoid "internet filter bubbles" and escape from the ecosystem that limits our thinking.

We should not confine ourselves to our own information sphere but instead strive to remain open and connected within the real ecosystem of the world.

We should overcome our own "certified group" biases, resist human nature's instinct, and embark on a journey of intellectual exploration, discovering new opportunities and ideas in the external world.
