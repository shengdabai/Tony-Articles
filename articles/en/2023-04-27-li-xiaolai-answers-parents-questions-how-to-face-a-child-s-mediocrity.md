He fears that the child will not have a promising future, fears that the child will be mediocre, fears that the child will end up like himself.

So you see, **first, fear that the child will be mediocre in the future, then fear that the child will be like himself.**
In real life, we can also see examples that make you think you've lost the starting line, and then you become very anxious.
But what is the reality?
**Money and time are limited resources.**

Everyone faces limited resources. What is most lacking?
Time and money.
If everyone had an endless amount of time to spend and an endless amount of money to spend, how could you possibly be anxious?
Like the first parent mentioned, we are both working parents, and money is not enough. You need to earn money, right? If you have a lot of money, both of you wouldn't need to work.
So you have to sell your time to earn money, so you don't have time to spend with your child. After work, you take the child to various interest classes, and you're running around, and you spend very little time with your child.
**In fact, you are confused about what is more important.**
I want to tell you that I am not someone who believes that the more time parents spend with their children, the better. **I even don't believe the statement that "companionship is important."**
**Most of the time spent with children is meaningless, most of it is a waste of time, and meaningless companionship can only bring meaningless dependency, which is of no use.**
**But you misunderstood something:** it's not that the child has no interest in something, but that the child doesn't know how to learn that thing.
The child doesn't know what he can do with that thing, so even if he is envious, he doesn't know how to improve, and gradually he gives up.
So in fact, you, as parents, have a greater responsibility.
Why?
Because you yourself don't know how to learn, you don't know how to improve, so you can't help your child learn, you can't help your child improve, so she can't do it well.
Everyone is the same: when you can't do something well, you might still try a few times, but if you can't do it well for a long time, you lose interest in it.
Think carefully, when you were a child, were you interested in everything? But why are you not interested in anything now?
It's because you didn't do it well, right? So **as a parent, you yourself must first become someone who is good at learning and improving.** If you are not, when your child does something poorly, you will be at a loss.
At that time, the child can't improve, he gets no help, and then he loses interest.
Who is to blame?
Don't think that because you spent money on interest classes, the child can automatically learn well. **In education, money is the least powerful.** Although money is sometimes necessary, it actually causes more side effects.
For example, when we talked about interests before, I said that many parents see that their children don't have results after learning, and then think their money is wasted, they feel like they've suffered a financial loss as adults, and blame the child for being unambitious.
This is a huge mistake. The root of the error is not that you are stingy, but that as an adult and as a parent, you haven't realized that money is actually the least important in the entire educational process. What's truly important is your own learning ability.
This is a particularly strange struggle between a positive and a negative cycle.
Let me give a simple angle, which might sound a bit harsh.
In today's world, if you feel that money is not enough, it means you don't earn enough. And if you don't earn enough, it's almost 100% due to your poor learning ability.
The "learning ability" we're talking about here is not about school grades, but about skills.
Skills are divided into soft skills and hard skills. You think your learning is about school exams, but there are many important skills that cannot be measured by exams.
For example, emotional intelligence, self-learning ability, judgment, etc., these soft skills are all things you need to learn.
Don't think that school exams are everything, so **if you don't earn enough money, it's almost 100% due to your poor learning ability.** That's the first point.

Besides the fast pace and rapid development, it's also because your lifespan has increased.
Our previous generation was thirty and had to establish themselves, forty and not be confused, fifty and know their fate, right?
Our previous generation, at thirty, felt that life might be like that, but this world is different now. You can start learning again at thirty, fifty, even sixty. What about ninety? Because our current life expectancy may be a hundred years. You can still walk at ninety.
As long as you exercise regularly and live a healthy life, you can start learning at sixty, learn a skill for ten years, and by seventy, you can still be productive for ten, twenty, or even thirty years, right?
You're thirty or forty now, and you have one child. You're still very young, extremely young. So please take this fact seriously.

So if she had been twenty-five when she retired and started learning something new, becoming an expert in a new field, and then producing in that field, spending five years to learn, she could have produced for twenty years, right?

Rather than enrolling your child in interest classes, you should ask yourself: Do I have something I'm particularly interested in now that I want to learn? You learn it, and the child follows you.
So I give you another idea: **If you don't have interest in the interest classes, don't bother.**
This interest sometimes seems to be inherited. I'll tell you how this interest is passed down. There's actually no such thing as inherited interest, but any learning has a common characteristic: it has difficulties.
**When a person encounters difficulties in the learning process that they can't solve, they stagnate and eventually give up.**
This phenomenon is more obvious when you're young.
I was pretty good when I was a kid, could participate in computer competitions, and won first place in the three northeastern provinces. But today's Li Xiaolai is not an excellent programmer, nor is he the leader of a tech startup.
Why?
Because I also faced problems, and I became mediocre in that area later.
You don't see me as the first place in the computer competition in the three northeastern provinces, right?

Today, the internet is somehow a million times stronger than any teacher, because you can ask Google for any question, right?
When I graduated from high school and went to college, I wanted to pick up programming again, but people had already moved on from BASIC programming language. Databases had started to appear, called Fox PRO. Programming languages had become more advanced, called C.
I bought a classic C language textbook to read, and then I encountered a problem. This problem was strange, and looking back now, it's laughable.
I was used to the BASIC programming language, where you compare two variables with a single equals sign. So we often wrote **F A = B then** how, right?
But how does C language compare two variables?
It uses **A == B**. Assignment uses a single equals sign, comparison uses two equals signs.
Note that Li Xiaolai is not a person without good reading habits. Li Xiaolai has always known the importance of reading since childhood. But when I read that textbook, I really didn't know what was wrong. I couldn't see two equals signs, I could only see one, and the code wouldn't compile, and the error message was unclear. I couldn't figure out where the problem was.
How long did this delay me?
It delayed me for ten years, really, it's funny, right?
So you can imagine, when I think back to this, I even doubt my own intelligence! You know, it's clearly printed with two equals signs, how could you see it as one? Then it failed, and you couldn't compare them, and it was just two equals signs.
From this example, you can understand a truth: **Everyone has their own weaknesses.**
**When people make mistakes, in some extreme cases, they don't even know how they made the mistake. At this point, if someone who understands the subject is around, they can point out the problem and solve it instantly. But if no one can help you, you face an insurmountable gap.**
Later, when I taught at New Oriental, I re-read the C language textbook and realized I had misread the two equals signs, and I thought, how could this delay me for ten years!
Of course, the learning environment had changed by then. Soon after, Google appeared, and later, when learning any programming language, you wouldn't face such an insurmountable obstacle. Any problem could be solved by searching Google on the spot.
Back to the point: why is your child indifferent to interest classes or failing in them?

So truly high-quality teachers are those who carefully observe their students. They can notice the student's mistakes that the student doesn't even know about, and then correct them, so the student can learn well.
But how many such teachers are there? So in most cases, only the parents can do this.
This determines many phenomena in reality: parents who can play the piano, their children can too. Parents who can sing, their children can too. Parents who are doctors, their children may also become doctors. Parents who are teachers, their children may also become teachers.
