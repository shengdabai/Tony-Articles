Reflections on the "IQ Tax" #Plain Language #Case Study Sharing#

01 The term "IQ Tax" suggests that this is clearly a scam, money that shouldn't be spent. Paying for intelligence, paying for the inability to discern the truth—it's a fee for not being smart enough.

02 At first glance, the "IQ Tax" seems shameful. If we've paid it, we might feel embarrassed in front of others. But how do we define the "IQ Tax"? Is what you think is an "IQ Tax" the same as what others think it is?

03 For example, buying a bottle of mineral water, A pays 1 yuan, which is a good deal, while B pays 10 yuan. Would A think B has been scammed, paying 9 yuan in "IQ Tax"?

04 To some extent, the so-called "IQ Tax" is more likely a "brand tax," a fee for the brand's pricing power. At this point, it's not just about cost and profit—it's a reflection of consumer upgrading.

05 WeChat Channels have seen some scamming behaviors, products that charge an "IQ Tax." But if we look at it from another angle, is it not worth paying for knowledge services? Just because books are so cheap and free resources are everywhere, should we ignore the value of the product?

06 Here, I'm clearly not talking about real scams. We don't need to discuss random price hikes or wild assumptions. Instead, we should deeply think about the brand, improve its value—this might be more important than worrying whether we're paying an "IQ Tax."

07 If we think about the "IQ Tax" for every item, isn't that a sign of our economic environment and brand consumption advancing further? Perhaps it is.
