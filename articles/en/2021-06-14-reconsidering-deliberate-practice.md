74th Original Piece

Deliberate practice is a way of practicing that can yield faster results. Deliberate practice requires staying in the learning zone, receiving clear feedback, continuous supervision, and adjusting strategies when there is no progress. Due to limitations in resources, energy, and motivation, not everyone has the opportunity to engage in deliberate practice. Mr. Wan Weigang mentioned four steps of deliberate practice: first, practice only in the learning zone. Second, do a lot of repetitive training. Third, continuously receive feedback. Fourth, maintain high concentration.

What am I doing now? I am pursuing a master's degree, which is my deliberate practice. The educational leadership course falls within my learning zone; although it's challenging, it's not easy enough to make me comfortable. The academic journey of a master's student is a great learning environment for me. It requires a lot of repetitive training, reading and reflection, continuing to read and reflect, massive input, and constant comparative learning. Professors and classmates are all sources of feedback for me, and there's always feedback, which is very helpful for my growth. Maintaining high concentration naturally means being fully immersed, entering a state of flow, and being deeply engaged in the process of my master's studies. In addition, I believe that my current reading, writing, and short video creation are all forms of deliberate practice. The reason is simple: these are all things I spend a lot of time on, allowing me to focus on them. The feedback I receive from deliberate practice also thanks the power of the community. In many cases, we can only do things silently on our own. Among the four aspects mentioned earlier, three can be done independently, but feedback is relatively harder to achieve without a partner. We can actually do self-feedback ourselves, but for most people, this is difficult. It requires continuous deliberate practice in the ability of self-feedback.

Thinking about deliberate practice, I recall the difference between ordinary athletes and top athletes: ordinary athletes are more willing to practice familiar moves, while top athletes focus more on high-difficulty moves they haven't mastered yet. Over time, even if the training time is the same, or even if ordinary athletes invest more time, they will never become experts. The reason is that they always stay in the comfort zone, repeating at a low level. Mental training is the same. We need to step out of the comfort zone and engage in deliberate practice. In the long run, deliberate practice is very challenging, and I need to keep doing it. I can plan my deliberate practice projects, break them into detailed parts, and practice according to the plan. Every day, I can ask myself questions, deeply solve a problem, and for the thinking process, I need to collect materials, organize resources, analyze and argue, and seek solutions. Use problems to practice, engage in deep thinking, and continuously improve my problem-solving abilities. Also, in today's fragmented era, how to efficiently use time and constantly reflect is a simple and practical method we can practice.

What I'm doing now is still far from enough. I once thought deliberate practice was the 10,000-hour rule, and as long as I could do it long-term, I could achieve it. But that's not the case. The 10,000-hour rule has two major issues: first, the time required to learn a skill varies across different fields. Second, moving from a novice to an expert requires a lot of practice, not just repetitive training that's the same every time. A person needs to break out of the comfort zone, practice in the learning zone with goals and methods, and engage in deliberate practice to make progress and grow. The correct approach is deliberate practice, so this is not an easy thing to do. It requires doing it for a long time, thinking deeply, and acting firmly. This is very difficult. Be friends with time, and take it slowly.

I believe deliberate practice can yield quick results. After all, skills acquired through deliberate practice are not easily obtained; abilities mastered through deliberate practice are powerful and naturally bring benefits. However, the problem is that this process is very long. I'm doing my best, continuously adjusting. Doing things correctly is not enough; we also need to do the right things, and keep doing them, with a long-term perspective, and practice together!

Stay true to theoriginal purpose of deliberate practice. Why do we call it "deliberate" is because it requires focusing attention and being fully engaged. Protect your courage, and keep doing it long-term. Our courage may fade over time, so we need 120% of courage, even if some is consumed, we still need to keep 100% of it and act practically (●°u°●) 」

After all, deliberate practice is in the learning zone, so it's definitely not as easy as the comfort zone. It requires a lot of repeated practice, which may be painful and tough. But without effort and being strict with ourselves, how can we get through it? Deliberate practice requires testing and feedback, which means exams. How long has it been since many of us took an exam? That's not right. We also have performance evaluations, but the nature of these is different. Performance evaluations check the results of work, while process-based evaluations are hard to reflect. Exams are unpleasant, but such testing is essential for deliberate practice, no matter what form it takes. Deliberate practice will make us go through what we call pain, but after breaking through, things will naturally develop. Thank you for the current process that may feel painful; it will get better soon ⊙▽⊙
