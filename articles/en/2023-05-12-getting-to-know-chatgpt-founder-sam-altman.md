My full listening experience was that Sam is extremely truth-seeking, which is his normal state of being. After all, representing humanity in exploring the edges of cognition leaves little room for emotional intelligence. Those who watched the whole video can feel the moment when Sam seemed to shut down instantly.

  1. Sam's vision of the future world.

Artificial intelligence and humans are very different, just like how humans referenced birds to invent airplanes, but the end result is entirely different. He was excited that ChatGPT could guide the direction of AI development. Large language models have become the breakthrough for AI, with a speed ten times faster and a cost a hundredth of the original. He doesn’t make long-term predictions, but he believes that within a few decades, the cost of on-demand cognitive intelligence and energy will drop to one percent to one thousandth of the original, approaching zero. This will be an incredibly beautiful world. Interestingly, he seems to rarely consider the dark clouds of war over Europe.

  2. Sam believes society needs to make three changes regarding AI.

Although AI has been heavily hyped, its medium-term impact is still underestimated. Because the integration of AI with scientific research can accelerate scientific progress by a thousand times, but human society is not yet prepared for this. First, human social structures will undergo significant changes. With unprecedented productivity, the way resources are allocated based on capital should change. Humans should be given basic wealth, which sounds somewhat like socialism. Second, the right to use general AI (Artificial General Intelligence) is very important. Once we have superintelligence, how should we distribute the limited rights to use general AI? Finally, AI governance must have a global agreement. Similar to "nuclear" countries, how should "AI" countries coordinate and develop?

  3. Sam believes there is an opportunity for mid-sized models on top of large models.

AI development is also generative (Generic, as in the **G**eneric **P**retrained **T**ransformer in **GPT**). A year ago, if you had asked the founder of today’s image-generating AI what the current image applications would look like, he wouldn’t have known. Back then, he was thinking, “Shouldn’t there be a better search engine?” We should discover new ways to use technology rather than speculate on what will happen in 100 years. The number of large models in the world is limited, but there will be many mid-sized models based on these large models. Large models first gain a broad understanding, and mid-sized models then specialize in specific areas. Similar to healthcare and education, many large companies will emerge.

Finally, Sam gave the most important advice to founders: “Don’t miss the AI wave—act quickly!”
