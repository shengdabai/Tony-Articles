What factors influence the potential long-term growth of an economy? Long-Term Economic Trends
A country's long-term growth trend determines its potential growth rate. The factors that influence changes in this long-term trend mainly depend on fundamental factors like birth rates and labor productivity. But they also depend on changes in other social and environmental aspects. All these factors change relatively little each year. Therefore, economic growth tends to be relatively stable in the long run.

**Birth Rates**: Population growth means more people will produce and sell products, and more people will buy and consume them. This further encourages expanded production, which is equivalent to creating more GDP. Therefore, the total number of working hours naturally increases, thus driving GDP growth. However, changes in birth rates are influenced by a series of factors, such as national policies, wars, economic conditions, and other social trends. These factors all affect changes in birth rates. Moreover, it takes decades for birth rates to change. Even if the current birth rate has already changed, it may take several years before this actually impacts GDP growth.

**Labor Productivity**: The value of output produced in each hour of labor is determined by "productivity." Changes in labor productivity mean increasing the output per hour of labor. Like changes in birth rates, productivity changes also take decades to show effects. Productivity improvements mainly come from advances in the production process. For example, the first major productivity leap occurred during the Industrial Revolution, the second during the era of electricity and the automobile revolution, the third with computers and other automation changes, and the fourth is the current information revolution. However, these productivity changes occur gradually, not overnight.

Birth rates and labor productivity together influence the long-term trend of the economy. But these two factors are not the only determinants. Many other factors can influence changes in these two factors. For instance, population migration, unemployment rates, average working hours, expectations for the future, education levels, technological innovation, automation, and globalization. All these factors will influence changes in birth rates and labor productivity.

What are the factors behind short-term economic cycles? Short-Term Economic Cycles
The long-term trend determines the potential growth rate of an economy. However, the actual annual GDP growth rate still fluctuates. This fluctuation is the short-term ups and downs that occur around the long-term trend of economic growth. The main factors influencing short-term economic cycle fluctuations fall into two broad categories:

  1. **Endogenous Factors**: Short-term economic performance may be affected by changes in decisions made by individuals within the economy. For example, changes in consumer spending, whether businesses expand or contract production, whether inventory increases or is sold off. These factors are all influenced by people's psychology, emotions, and decision-making processes.

  2. **Exogenous Factors**: Short-term economic performance is also affected by external events. Human-made events include wars, changes in tax policies, adjustments to tariffs, or decisions to alter the prices of goods by governments of other countries. Non-human events include natural disasters such as droughts, hurricanes, and earthquakes.

People try very hard to predict short-term economic changes in order to use them as investment criteria and gain profits. However, most predictions are based on current levels combined with long-term trends. Since the economy usually doesn't deviate significantly from current levels and long-term trends, these predictions, although technically correct, are not very useful. Only a very small number of people can accurately predict a significant deviation in economic growth from the long-term trend.

People try very hard to predict annual changes in the growth rate of the economy, using these predictions as a basis for investment decisions to gain potential profits. On average, investors' predictions are close to the actual levels most of the time. However, very few people can consistently make accurate predictions; only a few people's predictions are significantly better than others'; and only a few people can accurately predict a significant deviation in economic growth from the long-term trend.
