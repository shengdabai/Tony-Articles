Li Xiaolai has gone from writing five books over ten years to five books a year, and now is working on new courses. It's not just about writing books, but also about writing courses, and more importantly, living as an example.

Doing multiple things at once, the difference in productivity is the difference in wealth and growth.

Productivity is a very important topic in modern society. Improving productivity not only helps us complete tasks more efficiently but also creates more opportunities and wealth.

Mr. Huà Luógēng, a famous Chinese mathematician, once proposed the famous "Boiling Water Theorem," which sparked widespread attention. The boiling water theorem tells us that if we need to boil two pots of water at the same time, to save time, we can light the fire for both at the same time, rather than boiling one pot first and then the other. This simple and clever method not only improves the efficiency of boiling water but also demonstrates the importance of doing multiple things at once.

In his 1964 book *Popular Talks on the Method of Arranging Work*, Mr. Huà Luógēng gave many examples that are easy to understand, helping people clearly grasp what "time allocation" means. One example is the order of doing things in the morning. Suppose using the restroom takes 5 minutes, washing up takes 5 minutes, and boiling eggs takes 10 minutes. What order should these tasks be done in? If you do the restroom first, then wash up, and then boil the eggs, it will take a total of 20 minutes. But if we "arrange" these tasks, we can first put the eggs in the pot to boil, then go to the restroom, and then wash up. At this point, the eggs will also be done, and the entire process only takes 10 minutes, saving half the time.

The most famous example in *Popular Talks on the Method of Arranging Work* is undoubtedly "Boiling Water to Make Tea." Boiling water to make tea involves five steps: one, boiling water; two, washing the teapot; three, washing the teacups; four, getting the tea leaves; five, brewing the tea. Assuming the time required for each step is as follows:

Boiling water takes 15 minutes, washing the teapot takes 2 minutes, washing the teacups takes 1 minute, getting the tea leaves takes 1 minute, and brewing the tea takes 1 minute.

There are two methods for brewing tea. One is to first boil the water, then wash the teaware, get the tea leaves, and finally brew the tea. The other is to first boil the water, wash the teaware, get the tea leaves while the water is boiling, and then brew the tea once the water is boiled.

The time required for each method differs. Mr. Huà Luógēng said, "Although this method cannot directly solve all problems, considering problems in this way is still beneficial."

Doing multiple things at once means completing multiple tasks simultaneously, which is crucial for improving productivity. By handling multiple tasks at the same time, we can make full use of our time, avoiding the waiting and downtime associated with single-task processing.

This efficient method is not only helpful in work but also applicable to everyday tasks, such as handling emails and phone calls simultaneously, or completing household chores and studying at the same time.

The difference in productivity directly affects our wealth and growth. High-efficiency work can bring more income and opportunities, enabling us to better achieve personal and professional development.

In the business world, improving productivity is also an important way to create wealth. Companies can reduce costs, improve product quality and production speed, and thus gain a larger market share and profit.

Doing multiple things at once is an important method for improving productivity, and it can be applied in various fields and aspects. The difference in productivity directly determines the difference in our wealth and growth.

Make full use of time and cultivate the ability to do multiple things at once, thus achieving personal and social development. In this era full of opportunities, continuously improving our productivity will allow us to seize more opportunities and create greater value.

Productivity is the goal we pursue in our daily lives and work. In the process of achieving efficient task completion, the concepts of series and parallel are two key ideas.

In circuits, series and parallel are common connection methods. From the perspective of productivity, series means completing tasks one after another in sequence, while parallel means handling multiple tasks simultaneously. Just like how wires are connected, we can also connect tasks in series and parallel. Understanding these two concepts is crucial for improving productivity.

The advantage of series is that it allows for systematic and orderly task completion, ensuring that each step receives adequate attention and processing. However, series also has potential issues, such as if one step is delayed, the entire process will be affected.

In contrast, the advantage of parallel is that it allows for handling multiple tasks simultaneously, effectively utilizing waiting time and improving overall efficiency. However, parallel also requires careful resource allocation and task prioritization.

To achieve efficient task completion at the personal level, we can draw on the principles of series and parallel. First, understand the order of tasks and prioritize them based on importance and urgency, then complete them one by one using the series approach.

Second, for tasks that can be handled in parallel, use the parallel approach to improve efficiency, such as handling emails and replies simultaneously, or completing brief tasks while waiting for another task to finish.

The concepts of series and parallel can help us better understand and improve productivity. When handling tasks, flexibly applying the principles of series and parallel according to specific situations can maximize the optimization of time and resource utilization, thus enhancing efficiency in both work and life.

When we can efficiently handle tasks, we not only achieve better results but also free up more time to pursue more goals and interests.
