Original Work

Originality: In general, it refers to a work being entirely new, not derived from copying, adaptation, plagiarism, imitation, or derivative works. New inventions can be called original works.

-- Wikipedia

Let me tell a story. Xiaowan from the finance column once interviewed Wang Xing, the founder of Meituan. He asked a question: "Many people say you've never created anything truly revolutionary?" Wang Xing replied: "I agree that not everything I've done is 100% original, and that's not what I'm aiming for. People might have a skewed understanding of invention and innovation. Originality is one thing, and making choices and judgments is another. Take the most exaggerated example: if every character in an article is encoded as a Chinese character, your work is about rearranging these characters to present a different idea. You don't create any individual characters, but your creativity lies in the arrangement and combination. This actually reflects your judgment. **Users fundamentally care about who can better meet their needs, not who uses a completely different idea.**" Xiaowan further asked: "Some people think Meituan is too ordinary, lacking innovation, which seems inconsistent with your geek identity?" Wang Xing responded with one sentence: **"A gentleman is not a tool."** I don't think I must do this or meet certain expectations.
