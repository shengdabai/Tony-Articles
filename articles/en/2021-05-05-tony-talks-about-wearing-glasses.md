Li Xiaolai once compared education to wearing glasses, allowing us to see more clearly. I've also tried wearing glasses, and I think it's a very unique experience. Let me explain. At first, I was quite resistant to this topic because I felt uncomfortable with the idea of agreeing to it. It felt like there was something restraining me, making me seem less good, yet I had to face this reality.

Since 2019, after being diagnosed with keratoconus, wearing a corneal molding lens was really uncomfortable. Then I realized I had to control my own habits and intentionally limited my daily screen time. But I always ended up going over the limit, and I couldn't keep it up consistently. This time, I went back to the hospital for another checkup, and after the examination, I decided to wear glasses. They were quite expensive, costing 1,300 yuan. But overall, I thought they were pretty good. A few benefits, for example, seeing things outside more clearly, without wind or rain and snow blowing at my eyes. Before, my small eyes would easily get sand in them, but now, with glasses, nothing can get in. That's great, right? But there are also some awkward situations. For instance, the viewing angle changes, and many things become blurry and smaller. Because the deeper the myopia, the smaller the image formed, so I often have to walk carefully, moving my hands around, as if the whole world has shrunk by one size. So every time I walk, I have to be especially careful to avoid bumping into someone or getting hit. Mostly, I'm worried about bumping into others, which always feels awkward. But thinking about it, if someone loses their sight, they might suffer more. So I don't feel that way now. I think wearing glasses is still okay. So I'll gradually get used to it and learn to coexist with them. Recently, the teacher's writing assignment at school made me feel the concept of perception more deeply. My eyes now perceive these surrounding things, giving me more understanding.

Then I remembered Professor Li Sanyou's talk at the Chaos Theory lecture, where he said everything we see is just an illusion. It seems like that. Whether your perspective is magnified or reduced, whether it's clear or blurry, ultimately, what you feel is not what your eyes see, but what appears 0.05 seconds later. So using the senses of eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body, and mind to feel this vast world, I think it's a particularly wonderful experience. Ah, today is just a simple little sharing. I'm feeling a bit uncomfortable and not used to it, but at the same time, I have a little anticipation. And I want to take care of my eyes, use them reasonably, enjoy life, and see a more beautiful world. Maybe it's about stepping into my own world. Enjoy！！！
