What is a "chive" (a metaphor for a victim of exploitation)?

The general understanding is that a "chive" refers to an individual in the trading market who doesn't make money or even loses money, a powerless retail investor.

Li Xiaolai, in his book *The Self-Cultivation of a Chive*, gives a more precise definition: a "chive" is someone who believes they are playing a zero-sum game in a market that is not inherently zero-sum.

This book was originally written by Li Xiaolai as part of a crisis management strategy, but these 30,000 words helped many people understand what a "chive" really is. The term "harvesting the chives" quickly spread everywhere.

But I can understand it too. After all, the first step in "harvesting the chives" is to complicate the simple, obscure the clear, elevate the ordinary, and mystify the plain. — *Half Buddha Sage*, *The Human Cultivation Guide: Be a Clear-Minded Smart Person*

This sentence from Half Buddha Sage made me think of some so-called experts who do nothing but complicate things, creating all sorts of so-called concepts and using their own methods to explain everything.

If I need to give examples, there are nutritionists who promote the idea that "you should eat what you need to supplement," or scholars who blend ancient classics into a day's worth of traditional learning, or education experts who push so-called secret methods for cultivating top students from nowhere. There are countless people around me who love complexity, fully embodying the traits of mystery, ambiguity, sophistication, and complexity.

Some people further amplify their sense of being "cut" by others, as if everyone is cutting them.

Paranoia is a common mental illness and a clinically complex and difficult condition to treat. For patients, the overexposure of delusional thinking can not only affect their daily lives but also lead to serious consequences.

The main symptoms of paranoid delusion are persistent delusional behaviors, meaning the patient holds an unrealistic belief with unwavering conviction.

For example, someone might believe that people or organizations around them are secretly tracking and monitoring them, causing anxiety and fear, which can worsen the condition.

Arguing or debating with patients is often ineffective and may even reinforce their beliefs and emotions.

When treating paranoid delusion, doctors often use cognitive therapy.

For instance, by communicating with the patient and understanding the origins of their thoughts and beliefs, which are often the result of a combination of historical events, family experiences, and other factors.

Based on this, doctors can then use the patient's individual psychological characteristics to improve their thinking patterns through cognitive therapy, eliminating unnecessary delusions and fears.

Experiential therapy is also an effective method for treating paranoid delusion. Experiential therapy is not only a form of psychological treatment but can also be a way of taking practical actions and making changes in daily life.

By teaching cognitive or behavioral techniques, patients can feel less persecuted in their daily lives or more easily accept the people and events around them.

After reading about paranoid delusion, I think most people who believe they've been "cut" feel they've been deceived and harmed, with inner cracks.

In an interview, Li Xiaolai said: "I **do not believe that so-called 'harvesting the chives' is the best way to make money in this world. It's inefficient and harmful. I believe there are more efficient ways to make money in this world.**"

The term "harvesting the chives" is used to describe a situation where an individual is manipulated or exploited in the process of learning or investing.

However, some people overinterpret this phenomenon as a delusion, always believing they are being harvested.

First, we need to clarify one thing: information asymmetry is an inevitable part of modern society. Everyone has different levels of information and knowledge, which leads to unavoidable information gaps in learning and investing.

But simply assuming that others are deceiving you because of an information gap is an overinterpretation. In the process of learning or investing, we need to keep a calm mind and use critical thinking to analyze, evaluate, and verify information.

Chive delusion often arises from a bias in one's self-perception and ability.

Some people, when learning a new subject or investing in a particular field, easily fall into anxiety and self-doubt, believing they are always being used.

However, this way of thinking only weakens their confidence and judgment. We should believe in our own abilities and continuously learn and study to improve our knowledge and skills.

In addition to personal cognitive biases, social media is also a major reason for the spread of chive delusion.

The information on social media is filled with various opinions and voices, easily leading to information overload and misinformation.

Therefore, we need to maintain rational thinking, not blindly believe everything the media says, and learn to screen and assess the credibility of information sources.

Chive delusion is a form of overinterpretation and misjudgment. In the process of learning or investing, we need to maintain rational thinking and use critical thinking to analyze and judge.

We should not doubt our abilities just because of information gaps, nor should we easily believe others' promotions due to social media's influence.

Only by continuously learning and improving can we enhance our cognitive abilities and stay away from the troubles of chive delusion.

In his book, Li Xiaolai offers the following five pieces of advice to avoid being a chive:

**First, be humble and know yourself.**
**Second, if you've lost something, make sure you still have something to recover.**
**Third, the deciding factor between winning and losing is not luck, but strength.**
**Fourth, pay attention to cycles and understand trends.**
**Fifth, improve your risk-to-reward ratio. Risk-to-reward ratio = potential return / potential risk.**

In summary, not growing is being a chive. Live well, do things well, and strive to grow a little every day.

There was a young entrepreneur named Xiaoming (shouldn't be the same Xiaoming often used in jokes), who one day received a call from an institution called "High Return Investment Company," which claimed to offer extremely high interest returns.

Xiaoming was tempted, though he hesitated internally, and still invested his savings.

However, when he tried to withdraw some of his profits, he faced various obstacles from the company. His investment gradually fell into trouble, and he even lost contact with the company.

Through his own investigation and help from others, Xiaoming finally realized that the so-called "High Return Investment Company" was just a scam, and he had become a victim of being harvested.

This story seems so simple, how could someone be tricked? No one would be like Xiaoming, but the cruel reality is that there are indeed people like Xiaoming.
