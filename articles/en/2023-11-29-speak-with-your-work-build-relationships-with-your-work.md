Having work gives you higher connections.

People seek higher levels of life and career connections, and having work is the most crucial step toward achieving these goals.

Work can prove our abilities, knowledge, and personality, and it makes it easier to attract the attention of people at a higher level.

The importance of work lies in its ability to prove our talents and actions. Without actual work, all our abilities and knowledge remain just concepts.

Through a compelling piece of work, we can showcase our talents and also enhance self-awareness. Establishing work is one of the essential criteria for evaluating talent.

Work is the key to building high-level connections. High-level talent and opportunities are relatively scarce, and they come with their own filtering standards.

Through a compelling piece of work, we can attract their attention and build relationships. Moreover, work often draws people with similar interests and values, further strengthening our connections with others.

Work is the most important part of a personal brand. Whether you're an entrepreneur or a professional, a personal brand is one of the most valuable assets in your career or profession.

Creating an outstanding piece of work not only enhances the reputation of your personal brand but also generates significant influence in terms of networking and market expansion. Work can help us stand out in the workplace and even become industry leaders.

Work proves our abilities, knowledge, and personality, and it is an indispensable part of building high-level connections and a personal brand.

Successful people with work can earn the trust and respect of others, drive the development of their personal careers, and even influence a group of people.

People with work better understand the difficulty of creation; comments from those without work lack substantial reference value.

Whether it's artistic creation in literature, music, film, painting, or any form of creative work such as product design or technological innovation, it requires the creativity and inspiration of artists, designers, and entrepreneurs.

This process is both long and lonely, and only those who have created work can truly understand the hardships and challenges involved. Therefore, comments from those without work often lack relevant experience, and their evaluations cannot provide useful guidance or references to real artists.

Creation is an extremely personal process, requiring unique ways, methods, and imagination to express one's thoughts and emotions.

Many people often project their own thoughts and values onto a piece of work, without considering the artist's or creator's intentions. This leads to comments that contradict the original meaning of the work and offer no real value in terms of the work's worth.

People with work better understand the difficulty of creation because they have personally experienced the challenges and difficulties of artistic creation. Creation requires certain skills and professional knowledge, as well as rich imagination and extraordinary innovative thinking.

Only those who have created work can truly understand others' suggestions and extract valuable information from them.

People without creation experience often overlook the technical difficulty of creating work or specific historical contexts, cultural differences, and social backgrounds.

For example, many people commenting on films focus only on the plot and moral aspects, ignoring the meticulous arrangements of the film director regarding music, lighting, and actors. Such comments do not genuinely help creators improve or refine their work.

In fields of artistic creation and other original work, a lack of close experience with the work limits the depth of critical analysis.

People with work better understand the difficulty of creation, and their evaluations are more accurate and valuable. Let us cherish every piece of art, appreciate it with an open mind, and respect the thoughts and ideas of every creator. Only then can we truly experience and appreciate the beauty of art.

Having work is key to growth and accumulation: why do we need a lot of productive input?

As creators, we often long to create meaningful work, which not only brings us personal rewards and honor but also helps and inspires others.

Work does not come out of nowhere; it originates from input and experience from the outside world.

To create meaningful work, we need to master specific skills and knowledge in a particular field, a process that requires a lot of input and experience.

According to the author Carl Newport, we need to engage in a lot of "productive input," rather than just "consumptive input."

Consumptive input is a passive process of browsing the world, such as watching TV, reading websites, or using social media. These activities do not provide useful information to help create new work.

In contrast, productive input includes a broader range of activities, such as reading literature, writing, conducting research, creating, and experimenting.

These activities require us to actively create, experiment, and think, making productive input far more essential and effective for creative work than consumptive input.

We should note that while a lot of input is beneficial, it does not guarantee success. To gain value from a lot of productive input, we need efficient output and production, and we must be in a long-term feedback and iteration cycle.

In other words, we need to receive feedback and improve our work through failure and refinement.

Creating meaningful work is a difficult process that requires a lot of productive input for iteration. We need to actively explore the world, discover potential materials and inspiration, and then continuously improve our work through output, experimentation, and feedback loops.

Whether in work or creation, the key lies in how to optimize productive input and how to transform input into meaningful output and creativity.

Try to increase the ratio of input to output to 10:1, and through continuous practice and experimentation, make our work more refined and meaningful in influencing and inspiring others.

With the development of social media and the internet, personal branding has become an essential part of our continuous growth. How can we build a strong and unique personal brand? Having work is the core.

More importantly, we need to have good work and avoid plagiarism.

Original work is a crucial part of building a personal brand. As is well known, original work represents our creativity and uniqueness, which are essential for standing out in the market.

Original work also helps build our professional expertise and reputation, serving as a richer expression of our brand. Original work that spreads online can help us reach potential customers or employers, ultimately enhancing our influence and brand value.

Avoiding plagiarism is a key part of building a personal brand, as plagiarism damages our reputation, expertise, and uniqueness. Plagiarism is not only an immoral act but can also lead to legal issues, harming our personal brand and business interests.

To avoid plagiarism, the best approach is to create our own original work and protect our work's copyright. We should carefully check the licensing and copyright information before purchasing commercial content to avoid unintentionally infringing on others' copyrights. At the same time, we should make full use of our professional knowledge and skills, continuously create unique works, and clearly mark our creations.

And how can we create good work? This is the foundation for building a brand and its continuous growth. A good work should make the audience or customers feel familiar, entertained, or inspired.

We need to be good at using our professional knowledge and existing skills to maintain practice and improvement through as much productive input as possible. At the same time, we should constantly explore our creative inspiration and ideas, and over the long term, continuously accumulate and iterate.

In this way, we can create better, more distinctive works and maintain a long-term state of having work and good work.

Having work is a key part of building a personal brand, and having consistent, good work is even more important. Only original work and avoiding plagiarism can preserve our professional expertise and uniqueness, helping us build a continuously growing personal brand.

Creating good work requires making full use of our professional knowledge and skills, engaging in a lot of high-quality productive input and iteration, and through continuous accumulation and iteration, ultimately building our brand and influence.

Building a personal brand is a difficult but worthwhile path. Throughout this process, we always need to fully commit and keep learning and exploring.
