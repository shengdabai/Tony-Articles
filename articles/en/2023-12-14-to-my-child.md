**To My Child**

Author: Jihai Ji Boleun

Your children are not your children.

Your children, in fact, are not your children.

They are the sons and daughters of Life's longing for itself.

They are the children of Life's yearning for itself.

They come through you but not from you,

They are born through you, but not from you,

And though they are with you, yet they belong not to you.

Though they are with you, they do not belong to you.

You may give them your love but not your thoughts.

You may give them your love, but not your thoughts.

For they have their own thoughts.

For they have their own thoughts.

You may house their bodies but not their souls,

You may provide them with a home for their bodies, but not for their souls,

For their souls dwell in the house of tomorrow, which you cannot visit, not even in your dreams.

For their souls dwell in the house of tomorrow, which you cannot visit, not even in your dreams.

You may strive to be like them, but seek not to make them like you.

You may strive to be like them, but do not seek to make them like you.

For life goes not backward nor tarries with yesterday.

For life does not go backward, nor does it linger in yesterday.

You are the bows from which your children as living arrows are sent forth.

You are the bows from which your children, as living arrows, are sent forth.

The archer sees the mark upon the path of the infinite,

The archer sees the mark upon the path of the infinite,

and He bends you with His might that His arrows may go swift and far.

And He bends you with His might so that His arrows may fly swiftly and far.

Let your bending in the archer's hand be for gladness;

Let your bending in the archer's hand be for gladness;

For even as He loves the arrow that flies, so He loves also the bow that is stable.

For even as He loves the arrow that flies, so He loves also the bow that is stable.

After reading this piece, I was deeply moved. How many times have we acted as if we are the ultimate authority, believing that adults are always right, and that our children are angels who come to teach us how to grow.

In truth, the ones we should be learning from are our children. We should learn from them, for they are not the property of their parents.

In the process of a child's growth, parents play a crucial role. However, many parents often overlook one of the most important goals of education—cultivating an independent personality.

Ji Boleun points out in his writing that children are independent individuals, equal to their parents. Parents can give their children love, but they cannot replace the formation of their thoughts and souls.

Cultivating an independent personality means not only that children can live independently and make decisions, but more importantly, that they can understand themselves and recognize themselves.

Parents can guide children in self-reflection, encourage them to express their thoughts and feelings, and thus foster their self-awareness.

At the same time, to help children better face the challenges of future learning and growth, parents should also stimulate their curiosity and thirst for knowledge, and cultivate their learning abilities. Only through independent thinking and learning can children become well-rounded individuals.

Cultivating an independent personality also involves developing a child's confidence and autonomy. When children can solve problems and make decisions independently, they gradually build confidence in themselves and are willing to take on more responsibilities.

Parents can give children some autonomy at appropriate times, allowing them to participate in family affairs, manage their own time and finances. This enables children to fully develop their abilities and self-confidence.

Only with confidence and autonomy can children persist in the face of difficulties and setbacks, continuously growing and improving.

Cultivating an independent personality also involves a child's creativity and adaptability. Creativity is a crucial factor in developing a child's innovative thinking and problem-solving abilities.

Parents can provide children with diverse learning resources and creative environments, encouraging them to use their imagination and creativity, and thus foster innovative thinking.

At the same time, adaptability is also key, as independent individuals need to adapt and be flexible in different environments. Parents can help by exposing children to various experiences and interpersonal relationships, thus cultivating their adaptability and social skills. Only with creativity and adaptability can children grow and succeed quickly in real life.

Cultivating an independent personality is an important goal in parenting. Ji Boleun reminds us that parents cannot replace the formation of a child's thoughts and soul.

By promoting self-awareness and learning abilities, encouraging the development of confidence and autonomy, and cultivating creativity and adaptability, we can help children become individuals who think independently, are confident, and self-reliant.

Imagine the scene of a child sitting alone in their room, working on homework with tools and learning materials nearby. The child is focused at the desk, with a determined expression. The child is learning and thinking independently at home, full of confidence and autonomy.

The truth is that it should be the parents, not the children, who are learning and growing: a child's growth cannot be separated from excellent parenting.

When we talk about a child's growth and education, we often focus on the child. However, in reality, it is not the child who needs to learn and grow—it is the parents.

A child's growth begins with the education andfirst introduction provided by their parents. Parents are not just role models for their children; they are the first teachers in a child's life.

Parents' words, actions, and values greatly influence their children and leave a deep impression on their hearts. Therefore, as parents, we need to always maintain good behavior and positive thinking to create a positive educational environment.

Parents' growth is not only for themselves but also to better support their children. Through continuous learning and growth, parents can better understand their children's needs and developmental stages, and provide appropriate guidance and support.

This includes not only knowledge guidance but also emotional care and a sense of security. Only when parents have the ability to grow can they better meet their children's needs.

A child's values are often learned from their parents. As parents, we need to always pay attention to our own values and communicate and share our life views and values with our children.

Through both verbal and non-verbal teaching, parents can pass on correct values to their children, helping them understand what is truly important and thus enabling them to make the right choices and judgments.

A child's growth cannot be separated from the learning and growth of excellent parents. As parents, we play the most important role in our children's growth. We not only give them love and care, but we also need to learn and grow to become better parents.

Only by constantly improving ourselves can we provide better education and support for our children, helping them grow healthily and walk further on the path of life.

Verbal and by example: A child's arrival gives parents the opportunity to learn.

When a child enters our lives, our world immediately becomes something more than just ourselves. However, raising a child is not only a parent's responsibility—it is also an endless learning opportunity.

A child's arrival gives parents a rare chance to learn. Verbal and by example are the best approaches.

A child's arrival brings not only endless love and joy but also many challenges. As parents, we need to learn how to respond to the various needs and difficulties of our children.

This ability to respond is not just learned theoretically, but accumulated through practice and continuous reflection. In the process of raising a child, we constantly discover our own shortcomings and grow and improve from them.

Verbal and by example is one of the most profound and effective ways of education. Children pay more attention to our actions than our words. Through our actions, they learn how to interact with others, how to handle problems, and how to express their feelings. As parents, we must always maintain good habits and set a good example for our children.

Being influenced by environment and culture is another subtle way of learning. Through the permeation of the environment and family culture, children naturally absorb important values and knowledge.

Various activities and interactions within the family, such as reading together, listening to music, and discussing issues, allow children to learn a lot unconsciously. Creating a positive and uplifting atmosphere in the home and providing children with diverse learning opportunities is essential.

A child's arrival gives parents the opportunity to learn. Verbal and by example are the best approaches. We should actively face the challenges brought by our children and continuously learn and reflect to improve ourselves.

At the same time, we should set an example for our children through our actions, showing them the correct values and behavioral standards. We should create a positive and uplifting atmosphere in the home, allowing children to grow in a joyful learning environment.
