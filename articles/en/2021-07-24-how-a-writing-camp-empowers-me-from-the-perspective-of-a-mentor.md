The 112th Original Piece

For the role of a coach, the first thing to clarify is what a coach is. I understand a coach as someone who guides learners in studying and practicing. The key is continuous practice, helping learners better understand the material, being present throughout the process, and offering timely encouragement and feedback. A coach should set a good example but also mentally prepare for the possibility of being surpassed by learners.

1. Completing check-in tasks and actively participating in class and group activities requires a lot of time from group leaders, mostly in operations and communication. With the experience from the first session, the main focus is on practice. For most learners, they are new participants, so how to help them quickly adapt, complete check-ins on time, and participate in activities requires patient communication from group leaders. Some experienced learners who participated in the first session are already familiar with the entire model, so they have higher expectations. How to bring them new excitement and challenges requires more communication and interaction.

2. Communication among group members is essential. As a coach, how to ensure every learner gains something and progresses better is crucial. For example, the time each student spends on check-ins and the completion rate vary. By understanding their schedules and work-life rhythms, we can help them complete assignments during their free time. Some students may use PowerPoint or mind maps to create more visually appealing content, which can be helpful for others. One-on-one communication helps group members find their own position, pay more attention to writing, and invest more time. This naturally leads to better results.

3. Timely feedback is the foundation of everything. Group leaders and class reviewers should ideally provide feedback on all group members. Since the number of people in each group is relatively small in this session, timely feedback and suggestions not only facilitate communication and exchange but also help learners better understand the course content. Encouragement combined with suggestions will make group members happy, and this is something we all need to work hard to achieve together.

4. Group leaders should share their own experiences. From our perspective, sharing our writing journey sincerely is important. Taking myself as an example, why did the writing camp teach me to write every day? It's about organizing and expressing what I've learned and gained that day. We think and learn a lot, but can we solidify these insights through external forms, such as speaking or writing? These are excellent ways. There were also periods of confusion and difficulty in maintaining consistency, due to a lack of feedback and encouragement. The writing camp helped me through that phase, and after that, writing became a natural thing. Why not write? The sharing from group leaders comes from their personal experiences, and group members can learn more practical methods. Everyone can practice together!

5. The final point is daily reflection. Timely reflection and organization of daily work helps solidify thoughts, supporting continuous iteration and updates. This is similar to a coach's training notes, recording key points to help learners grow better. Of course, this is also the best way for coaches to grow.

These are the five points I've thought of. I welcome everyone's exchange!
