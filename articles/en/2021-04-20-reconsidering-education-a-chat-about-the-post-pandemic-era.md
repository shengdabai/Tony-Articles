The table above summarizes my learning reflections. Here, I'd like to highlight two documents: *China's Education Modernization 2035* and *Implementation Plan for Accelerating Education Modernization (2018–2022)*. These two documents respectively outline requirements and measures from the long-term national strategic planning and short-term goal realization. Two key points stand out: 1. The document systematically proposes eight "more emphasis on" basic concepts, namely, morality first, all-around development, for everyone, lifelong learning, teaching according to individual aptitude, integrating knowledge and action, integrated development, and shared construction. These eight basic concepts follow educational laws and talent growth patterns, and align with international educational development trends.

2. The main development goals for 2035: First, to build a modern education system that serves all citizens for lifelong learning; second, to popularize high-quality preschool education; third, to achieve balanced and high-quality compulsory education; fourth, to fully popularize secondary education; fifth, to significantly enhance the service capacity of vocational education; sixth, to markedly improve the competitiveness of higher education; seventh, to ensure that children with disabilities have access to suitable education; eighth, to form a new pattern of education governance involving the whole society. These eight goals cover aspects such as system structure, popularization level, education quality, talent cultivation structure, and service contribution capacity, reflecting the requirements for education modernization. At the same time, it also proposes the main quantitative targets for the development of education and human resource development by 2035.

I believe all of the above represent opportunities for the future development of the education industry. I feel very excited about this. The country is promoting education for all and lifelong learning, and many people are working behind the scenes. According to data released by the Ministry of Education, in 2019, there were 5.3 million schools at all levels and types across the country, with 282 million students enrolled, 17.32 million full-time teachers, 33,099 private training institutions, among which there were 211,959 full-time teachers, and 10,754,715 students in training institutions. These figures reveal a vast market and opportunities that are worth further exploration. I work in the K12 English education sector, and I hope to help more Chinese students find motivation for language learning, enabling everyone to become a language expert.

Here are my thoughts on the future development of the education industry:

Online education is in the early stage of the digital transformation of education. Only when it reaches the stage of intelligent education will this new educational model possess the power to disrupt traditional education. Only by leveraging technological and management tools can education cease to be a product and no longer merely a service. Online education that remains in the product economy stage can evolve into a service economy, and eventually into an experience economy, such as intelligent, personalized private teachers, or immersive and interactive educational environments that replace traditional classrooms. Although online education has already shown signs of prosperity, it is still in its early stage of digital education. As cloud computing, digital technology, computing technology, data technology, and intelligent technology continue to advance and converge, they will truly disrupt traditional industries.

True digital education is still far off. The sudden popularity of online education caused by the pandemic is a form of limited game. Some online education companies that performed well during the pandemic have products and business models with clear characteristics of limited games. To ensure their growth is sustainable, they must make their limited games also have the characteristics of infinite games. They must continuously deepen their understanding of the essence of education and the needs of users in this industry, and keep their products, services, and experiences closer to the essence of education and learning.

I am interested in exploring the OMO model. Hybrid learning has already been studied abroad, and the pandemic has accelerated its adoption in China. However, current technological applications are still not mature. I recommend the model of Khan Academy, where students can preview videos before class and learn at their own pace. The main purpose of in-person or online classes is to address questions. Of course, if technology can enable everyone to have intelligent teachers, it will further drive educational transformation and achieve the true goal of student-centered learning. After class, through online exercises that resemble game-based learning, this model and scenario are something I look forward to.

Finally, I recommend two books, *The Future School* and *The Innovative Classroom*, which contain many thought-provoking questions. The education system in Finland is also worth further exploration. These are my thoughts and collected information. I would like to share them with my fellow partners for discussion. Thank you!

Education—first and foremost—is alive, inquisitive, and exploratory. Without thinking, there is no discovery (even a small, seemingly insignificant one), and without discovery, there can be no creativity in educational work. In my view, true education begins when a person is inspired by an ideal, understands themselves, reflects on the meaning and purpose of their life, and examines who they are and who they should become.

—Sukhomlinsky
