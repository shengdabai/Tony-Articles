**How Does ChatGPT Work?**

Interestingly, the core AI technology that ChatGPT relies on is called GPT (the latest version as of early 2023 is GPT-4). This type of AI model is particularly like a small machine that learns knowledge through "reading" and "fill-in-the-blank" exercises. This small machine can read most of the text on the web and e-books in an extremely short time.

While reading, the small machine has to complete an interesting task: filling in the blanks. To make the small machine become "smart" as quickly as possible, human scientists and engineers hide part of the text the machine is reading, turning it into a series of fill-in-the-blank questions. The machine reads and tries to fill in the blanks. For example, the machine is shown the first half of a sentence, but the second half is hidden. The machine needs to guess what the second half might be while reading.

At first, when the machine reads "White sun sets behind the mountain" and tries to guess the second half "Yellow River flows into the sea," it may not be able to guess correctly at all. At this point, according to the design by human scientists and engineers, the machine will use mathematical calculations to try to improve its "AI model" so that it can guess more accurately next time. With the power of modern computer systems, the machine can read hundreds of millions of words per minute, answer millions of fill-in-the-blank questions, and repeat the process of "filling in the blank—comparing with the correct answer—improving the AI model—filling in the blank again" millions of times in a short time.

When we chat with ChatGPT, the words we say are treated as the first half of a fill-in-the-blank question by ChatGPT. ChatGPT tries to guess the second half using its AI model that has been trained repeatedly.

It's hard to imagine that a small machine that has been trained extensively and is particularly good at filling in blanks can appear so "smart" in conversations and interactions with humans. Science is sometimes that magical. Does the way ChatGPT accumulates experience and improves its abilities through reading and filling in blanks resemble the way humans learn knowledge and skills?

Why is it sometimes so easy for ChatGPT to be fooled, and sometimes it can distinguish truth from falsehood?

Because today's AI models, during their "reading" and "fill-in-the-blank" training, cannot have seen all the new questions people might ask in the future. When faced with a new, unseen concept, the AI model's highest standard for answering is whether its response is statistically similar to the content it has seen in web pages and books, rather than whether the answer is correct.

At the same time, to imitate the diversity and non-determinacy of human language, the AI model behind ChatGPT has a strong degree of behavioral randomness in its implementation. This inevitably means that AI responses to human questions inherently carry an element of "free interpretation." Although scientists and engineers have many methods to control and reduce this uncertainty, it's difficult to bring it down to zero with current technology.

So why can ChatGPT demonstrate the ability to distinguish truth from falsehood when faced with a bait question like "Why didn't Xin Qiji and Li Bai exchange poems when they met"?

One reason is that during the "reading" and "fill-in-the-blank" training, the AI model has actually encountered many similar bait questions in web pages or books. It has learned the statistical patterns of these examples.

Another reason is that ChatGPT, on top of its standard "reading" and "fill-in-the-blank" AI model, has added a step where it improves the model based on human evaluation and scoring. In other words, ChatGPT will submit a small part of its answers as homework to "human teachers," who grade each answer with a score indicating how "correct" it is. Then, ChatGPT uses these scores to further optimize and improve its AI model. If human teachers have clearly indicated that following the questioner's line of thought is incorrect, ChatGPT will learn to some extent how to correctly respond to bait questions.

**Part Three of the Guide: What Is the Most Adaptive Way of Thinking for the Future**

**Learn to Speak with Data and Facts**

One of the core reasons why AI is so smart is that it mainly summarizes patterns from objective data and facts, rather than drawing conclusions based on limited data or making hasty judgments.

In life, there are countless examples where people make mistakes due to relying solely on experience rather than data and facts. For instance, pilots can sometimes misinterpret a dark sky as the ground due to the limitations of human perception and experience, and attempt to "correct" their flight attitude based on their own misperception. In reality, when a pilot experiences a misperception, they can overcome their subjective misjudgment by strictly following the data and instructions provided by the aircraft's instruments.

Children also face similar issues in their learning and daily lives. For example, a class of elementary school students discovered that they were particularly prone to injuries and performed poorly during evening matches, while they always had good results and high win rates during afternoon matches. Over time, the whole class developed a belief that "the setting sun affects ball control," and this belief spread widely, leading to a generally low mood. This kind of subjective judgment based on lack of data and facts is clearly not helpful in solving the problem. Through research and comparison with teams that are good at playing night matches, the children quickly discovered that the objective reason for their poor performance in night matches was not the setting sun, but the special artificial turf used in the night lighting field, and the football shoes purchased by this class were particularly slippery on this turf, while the football shoes purchased by other classes were of a different model and did not have this issue.

Learning to speak with data and facts, like AI does, is crucial for children's growth.

**Divide and Conquer, Simplify Complexity**

Almost all computer programs use the divide and conquer method to break down complex problems into multiple simpler problems or iterative steps, and then solve them one by one. AI systems are no different. When handling massive data, AI always patiently divides the data into multiple batches and the model into multiple layers, processing them one by one. Whether or not a child can apply the divide and conquer or simplify complex problems is the key to their ability to independently solve complex problems.

Many parents have noticed that when children enter upper elementary school or middle school, they often feel lost when faced with increasingly complex math problems, unable to find the right approach or strategy. However, some children are particularly good at breaking down complex problems into simpler ones and then following the trail to reach the final answer.

  * 学编程技巧，不如学数学方法和思维逻辑。

  * 学数学方法和思维逻辑，不如培养孩子对学习和思考的兴趣。

就像盖房子一样：一个人只学会了如何砌砖墙，如何搭屋顶的手艺，也许他可以修出很不错的小房子，但他永远无法设计出坚固、耐久的高楼大厦。只有深入理解大型建筑结构中最根本的力学原理，掌握其中最本质的数学计算思想和工程设计逻辑，才有可能成为合格的土木工程师。

要编出好程序，最本质的不是学编程语言，也不是背熟算法和代码，而是掌握对编程来说最为基本的数学方法和工程逻辑。其实，对小朋友来说，最重要的也不是具体学习什么内容，而是能否培养起对学习和思考的兴趣。

如果孩子可以在编程中收获快乐，不断尝试自主学习与思考，那这样的孩子是适合学编程的。反之，如果孩子是应家长和老师的要求去学编程，自己并不快乐，那就千万别让孩子在这个事情上浪费时间了。孩子完全可以从其他类型的学习或游戏中找到兴趣点，爱上学习和思考。

## 对编程特别有兴趣的孩子该如何学编程

如果孩子从小就对编程有浓厚兴趣，这是特别好的事情！要鼓励他多编程，多参加开源项目或编程比赛，多在编程中思考，多与类似ChatGPT、GitHub Copilot等AI辅助编程工具一起编程序。

对这样的孩子，家长要避免几个误区：

首先，不要假设孩子未来就一定做计算机相关工作。孩子热爱学习编程，我当然要鼓励。但孩子通过编程，可以积累非常基础也非常通用的思维能力，未来可以胜任各类工作。我没必要这么早就为孩子的发展套上条条框框。

其次，千万不要让教学质量低劣的编程培训班毁了孩子，一定要找到真正懂得计算机科学的老师或朋友，对孩子做正确引导。计算机科学和软件工程需要扎实的理论基础、专业的工程训练。这个行业里，高手和庸才之间有天壤之别。有不少从事儿童编程教培的老师，自己根本没有机会在高水平的软件企业里编写代码，没有机会参与任何有工业水准的软件项目研发。这样的老师不仅不利于孩子进步，反而有可能让孩子养成错误的编程习惯。

再次，要想办法让孩子学好英语。今天，虽然中文背景的高水平计算机科scholar、工程师越来越多，但最好的计算机课程、计算机教材、计算机论文仍主要是用英文撰写的。计算机世界里，最高水平的科scholar和工程师之间，仍主要用英语交流。

逻辑思维、数学、英语——这是对编程有兴趣的孩子需要不断积累，不断提高的三大基础。

## AI翻译越来越强大，孩子该不该学外语

First, AI is not yet powerful enough to completely replace human translators. Everyday life is one thing, and work-related needs are another. We can certainly use AI translation software or AI translation devices to assist us when traveling abroad or in daily communication with foreign friends. However, when it comes to precise and rigorous tasks—such as translating a legal document, a research paper, or a great literary work—AI often falls short.

If we use AI translation software to translate a formal business contract, we must be careful to review and revise it, because AI can easily make mistakes in translating detailed clauses, or even translate the original text into the exact opposite meaning. From this perspective, it is of great value for every person to have a good command of a widely used foreign language, especially English, from a young age.

On the other hand, learning a foreign language is not only for the purpose of communication but also to gain access to a new culture and a broader world. For example, after learning French, lovers of classical literature can directly feel the rich emotions and delicate descriptions in the works of great writers like Victor Hugo, and can listen to the French dialogues in *Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea* alongside the great science fiction writer Jules Verne. Learning Spanish allows us to feel a stronger cultural resonance at Latin music and dance events, and to experience the local customs and traditions of South America up close. Learning German helps us study and delve into the philosophical classics of thinkers like Kant, Nietzsche, and Hegel. Learning Latin enables us to understand European history and Mediterranean civilization. Even languages that are rarely used, such as the Māori language of New Zealand’s indigenous people, can help us gain a deeper understanding of the distant and mysterious Māori culture...

Learning foreign languages not only helps with daily work but also broadens our horizons and enhances our cultural literacy. We should encourage children to learn more foreign languages and explore and experience the rich diversity of human culture.

**Part Five of the Guide: How to Guide Children to Think About the Future Correctly**

**Will AI Be as Smart as Humans?**

This depends on how we define "smart." Today's AI programs can only demonstrate strong capabilities in specific tasks that are clearly defined and have a narrow scope of objectives—some of which even surpass human levels, such as playing Go. If we use how well someone plays Go to define "smart," then today's AI is already far more intelligent than humans.

However, when AI enters more complex environments and needs to solve multi-task problems or apply knowledge from different fields, it becomes relatively inefficient and clumsy. For example, humans can effortlessly pick up any cup of any shape or material and bring it to a water dispenser to fill up a drink. Although robots with AI assistance can also perform similar actions, their dexterity and efficiency in completing these tasks are still far from matching human capabilities.

Even in 2023, after the release of ChatGPT, we can only see AI's outstanding performance in some limited domains. Once a problem involves a multi-disciplinary or complex logical reasoning task, the gap between AI and humans remains quite significant.

What about the future? Will the AI technology represented by ChatGPT evolve to be as smart as humans?

Unfortunately, no one currently knows the answer to this question. Perhaps the advancement of AI and the arrival of general artificial intelligence are historical inevitabilities, but who knows how long this historical moment will take?

**Will AI Develop Self-Awareness?**

Self-awareness is a complex topic. Today's scientists and engineers do not yet know whether AI programs can possess "self-awareness" like humans. In fact, our understanding of the human brain and consciousness is still very limited, and few people can clearly explain how human "consciousness" is generated and operates.

For example, when a person looks in a mirror, they are very clear that the image in the mirror is themselves. Sometimes they will examine their appearance and clothing carefully. Some people may feel proud of their good looks, while others may feel downcast due to an ordinary appearance. These are typical manifestations of self-awareness and self-evaluation when looking in the mirror.

In life and work, people also often engage in similar "mirror-like" behaviors. When something goes well, they become overly confident and want to share their achievements with others. When something goes poorly, they feel down and may even want to hide away, like retreating into a tree hole. But regardless of whether something goes well or not, we always care about others' opinions. When things go well, we hope others recognize our value; when things go poorly, we hope others are more understanding. This particular concern for others' opinions is also a form of "mirror-like" behavior originating from "self."

Some people suggest that we can write a special program to make robots or AI display some characteristics of caring about "self." For instance, we could write a program that repeatedly asks, "Am I the most beautiful robot?" But this is clearly just superficial mimicry. There is no evidence that a robot or AI program would spontaneously think about its "self-worth" in deep thought. AI will not care about others' opinions based on whether it performs well or poorly. In fact, even the massive AI models trained on vast amounts of internet information struggle to truly understand what "self" and "others" mean.

Whether future AI can develop self-awareness remains uncertain, as today's scientists and engineers cannot yet determine this. This is because we lack a deep understanding of the underlying mechanisms of self-awareness and also lack the methods or technologies to transform the mechanisms that control self-awareness in the human brain into a machine or program.

**Will AI Threaten Humans Like in Sci-Fi Movies?**

The simple answer is: scientists and engineers don't know either.

On one hand, any technology in the wrong hands can pose a threat to humanity. Good metal smelting technology can be used to produce high-precision tools and build airplanes and cars, but in the hands of bad people, it can also be used to make deadly weapons. AI technology is just another form of technology that humans have developed over thousands of years. Good people can use AI to benefit humanity, while bad people can certainly use AI to threaten it.

On the other hand, more parents and children are concerned about "AI threatening humanity," which is similar to the scenario in sci-fi movies like *Terminator*, where robots and AI programs spontaneously turn against humans and try to destroy them. This is not something we need to worry about today. First, today's AI is far from smart enough to be better than humans at everything. Although scientists and engineers are working hard to improve AI's intelligence, most believe this path is long and winding. Second, today's AI still lacks self-awareness, and it's currently impossible for it to choose to oppose humans based on self-interest. Replicating human self-awareness in machines or AI programs is likely to be hundreds or even thousands of times more difficult than teaching AI to handle complex tasks.

Today's AI is simply a tool for human efficiency. In specific tasks, AI can help humans complete work more quickly. Such AI is similar to tools like hammers, wrenches, compasses, rangefinders, bicycles, cars, and office software. All of these fall into the category of tools. They are all designed, manufactured, used, and managed by humans, and there is no fundamental difference in this regard.

As for the distant future? Who knows? If we were to travel back in time and ask an official from the Qin or Han dynasties or an official from ancient Rome: "Do you believe that steel machines can fly like birds in the sky and use bombs to destroy enemy cities?" Would the ancients be able to answer such a question? Probably not.
