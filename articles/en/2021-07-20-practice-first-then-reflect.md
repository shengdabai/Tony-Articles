**Insight**
Self-efficacy: In 1977, the psychology master Bandura became aware of this issue. He found that although some people clearly knew what to do, their behavior was not ideal. Because there is a huge gap between knowing what to do and actually doing it — we only act when we believe we can do it. In short, we need not only knowledge but also "heart power." Knowledge is not just for the mind, but also for the heart. Bandura believed that people's judgment of their own abilities plays a central role in their self-regulation system, and from this he proposed the concept of "self-efficacy." Self-efficacy (Perceived self-efficacy or sense of self-efficacy) refers to people's confidence or belief in their ability to achieve specific behavioral goals in a particular domain. In short, it is confidence in a particular area.

> Knowing + Self-efficacy = Doing

After long-term research, Bandura found that self-efficacy mainly comes from four sources:
1. Personal experience of success or failure: For example, I don't dare to drive because I once hit the gate of the courtyard and developed fear
2. Vicarious experience: Seeing everyone around me writing, I also dare to write, but seeing some people fail in investing, I also have some concerns
3. Social persuasion: Having a harsh friend nearby who constantly mocks and criticizes, which is an invisible destruction
4. Emotional and physiological influences: A person's emotional and physical state also affects confidence. For example, when sick, tired, or in a low mood, it's easy to feel a decrease in self-efficacy.
