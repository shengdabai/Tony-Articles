88th Original Piece

Are your current job and your mission aligned? If not, what improvement or adjustment plans do you have?

Aligning work with one's mission—this is something I deeply admire in Fan Sir. I've read many of her stories, including her book *Where My Heart Belongs: Dunhuang*, which vividly demonstrates how her mission drives her life. When I place this question on myself, I need to think carefully.

Last year, Professor Shanyou encouraged me to rethink my mission. I realized I wanted to be in education, to use English education to drive educational transformation. I chose to resign and pursue further studies, currently enrolled in the Master's program in Educational Leadership at the University of Manchester. At the same time, I'm also working on the Master's program for International English Teaching Professionals. This not only enhances my professional skills in English education but also deepens my understanding of education itself. Through educational leadership, I aim to further guide this transformation.

The word "leadership" sounds impressive, as if it's something only for leaders or managers. But in fact, leadership is something everyone can use. The Chaos Live series has a theme called "Leadership Everyone Can Use." In simple terms, leadership is about solving problems, guiding us toward truth, goodness, and beauty, and handling various issues. It's not about holding a leadership position or managing others—it's about leadership, or perhaps, leadership as a force.

As a teacher, I can influence my students, but there are boundaries. As a trainer, I can influence more teachers. As an educational leader driving change, I can push for educational innovation and help more people use leadership to transform education.

Right now, I'm a graduate student. Before starting my studies, I had over two years of experience in education training. In the education field, I'm still a newcomer, and my understanding and thoughts are limited. But I'm not a complete beginner—I'm not clueless. I just came late! For the rest of my life, I will dedicate myself to promoting educational development and driving educational transformation. Some may think I'm too young, or that my ideas are unrealistic. All these doubts I accept. If I've chosen to enter the education field, I've made the commitment. There will be many experiences in my life, but one thing I've never left is growth. I label myself as a person born after 1995 lifelong learner. Not growing is something I can't tolerate. If I need to grow, then I need education, and I need to keep transforming.

I'm grateful to be working in education during such a great era. Today, the importance of education is also continuously increasing. Education doesn't show results quickly—it requires long-term investment. Isn't this exactly what a lifelong learner like me needs? Long-termism isn't just talk—it's about doing things solidly, moving forward slowly. I hope to love this path for the rest of my life. I am Tony Sheng!
