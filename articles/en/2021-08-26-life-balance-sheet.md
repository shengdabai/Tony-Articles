# Life Balance Sheet

> Published: 2021-08-26 · [中文](../zh/2021-08-26-%E4%BA%BA%E7%94%9F%E8%B5%84%E4%BA%A7%E8%B4%9F%E5%80%BA%E8%A1%A8.md) | [English](../en/2021-08-26-life-balance-sheet.md)

---

Just like a balance sheet, the assets and liabilities of life are also in balance. Gaining one asset is usually achieved by consuming another asset or increasing liabilities. If you want to gain something, you may need to pay something else to maintain balance.

The most obvious asset is money. Many people admire the wealthy, and having a lot of money seems like a sign of success. However, assets and liabilities occur simultaneously. The hard work behind earning money, the reduced quality of life, and the risks taken are considered benign liabilities. Sacrificing health or family is a matter of personal judgment. If the liabilities are unethical or even illegal, then it's truly a loss.

There are also initial assets, such as parents and social class, which are our original assets. However, obtaining these assets also comes with the long-term liability of supporting and accompanying them. Of course, you still have plenty of time and energy, which are also assets, allowing you to purchase many things.

Friendship is also an asset, but it comes with the liability of mutual support and keeping promises. Getting married is like two companies merging—assets double with passion, intimacy, commitment, and financial support—but liabilities also increase, such as the effort to harmonize daily life, the relationship between two families, and the cost of giving up part of yourself. This is also a balance. Someone who chooses to start a business gains a team, possibilities, challenges, and great achievements, but also takes on the liabilities of losing balance in life, having a stable income, and working day and night.

There are also many hidden assets, such as experience. We all admire people with stories, and experience is indeed a valuable asset. However, it also comes with the liability of enduring many successes and failures, leaving home, and wandering around. Influence and personal brand are great assets, but they also come with the liability of being careful with one's reputation, being under public scrutiny, and even having one's personal life exposed. Those celebrities who become infamous after fame are like companies that have listed their reputation on the stock market. If you choose to make a living through your persona, you need to disclose your financial statements publicly and bear the liability of being monitored by everyone in your life.

Viewing balance from the perspective of investment liabilities, we can at least focus on the following three aspects to create this balance sheet:

1. View life with an investment mindset, knowing that assets come from liabilities—behind every resource, there is a curse.

2. Assets are not important; only "net assets" matter. Focus on those transactions that truly feel "worth it" to you—this naturally forces you, as a life accountant, to think about one thing: financial accounts are easy to calculate, more money is better, but when it comes to life stages, what in life is truly worth it? This is your life's big theme.

3. Once you have a life theme, work, life, career, and self will gradually form a synergistic industry, and they can further integrate and merge into a "corporation." When your worldview, career view, family view, and leisure view are integrated, your life value will be fully merged and take a step up.
