Historical "Black Monday" stock market crashes have led readers to imagine they can avoid the worst days precisely, but these ideas are unrealistic because the best days often come right after the worst. When facing black swan events in investing, the only effective approach is to "do nothing." For example, missing the 10 best days in a 10-year period could result in a loss of 66%. Missing more of these best days could mean your investment returns are worse than those from government bonds. For example, many take loans to buy houses, paying the loan on time and in full. In reality, the total amount spent on a house is the principal plus interest, which is equivalent to the price of two houses. The truth is as realistic as that. Some might think, isn't this also long-termism? Isn't it also a pattern of getting rich slowly? Of course not. This is forced repayment, on time and in a fixed amount.
