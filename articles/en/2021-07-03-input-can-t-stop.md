【What am I doing now?】
I spend 2–3 hours daily listening to courses on Dedao app
Read at least three books on Fan Deng Reading
Daily listening and note-taking on Hunchun University courses
Learning on Manchester University courses
Reading English books for more than two hours
Reading Chinese books for one hour
Other courses, videos, and audiobooks—time is flexible
My input methods are reading and listening, combining both to increase efficiency. For me, the choice varies by time of day. At home, with more space, I can read, and books are easy to access. When I'm out and have nothing special to do, listening is a great way to input. While doing simple tasks, listening helps me focus better. I usually choose 2.5x speed, and if the speaker is fast, I go with 1.5x to 2x. A friend once asked me, can I understand that fast? For me, the same tone played at a slow pace is boring and easy to lose focus. The speed-up method keeps me more engaged and improves information absorption!

【What is my vision for the future?】
Today, in the Practice Group, I heard Li Xiaolai's sharing. Reading is important, but it's far more difficult than most people imagine. Everyone knows Zhonghua, the assistant teacher, who shares one point at a time. Zhonghua dissects one book every day and deeply reflects on it through her official account, which is impressive. But when we want to ask Zhonghua how she does it, we shouldn't just imagine whether we can read a book a day. Shouldn't we think about the accumulation and effort behind Zhonghua's success? Water dripping on stone—this power isn't gained overnight. Zhonghua's persistence is the compounding effect of time. The 10,000-hour rule is familiar to us, but we don't need to be bound by it. How could a person practice 10,000 hours and stop? Like Lang Lang, the pianist, who practices far more than the average person. Practicing 10,000 hours with the wrong method is just wasted time. Even with the right method, how long to reach 10,000 hours? One hour a day takes 27 years, two hours a day takes a lifetime, four hours a day still takes a lifetime. Charlie Munger practices six hours a day, a rich and fulfilling life, not just in material wealth, but also in spiritual wealth.
My future plan is to increase my reading time from three hours to four hours. Sometimes I can even manage six hours, but my attention is often scattered by other things. I hope to reduce mental distractions and focus entirely on reading. I also need to keep up with exercise, because values are expressed through action. Reading good books isn't just about knowledge—it's about cultivating good values!

【The deeper the valley, the harder you must work. Don't waste any crisis】
This sentence really touched me. After university, I was supposed to go to Germany for further studies, which was a dream for an engineering major. But things came one after another. My grandfather fell ill and passed away, my girlfriend broke up, and I was afraid of leaving for three to four years in Germany. I didn't want to be away for so long. I wanted to stay and be with my family. That period was really tough, with three things weighing on me. I saw a school counselor, and my sense of responsibility came out. I cried in front of the doctor, and that time was really hard. I locked myself in the office, from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m., and then ran until I was exhausted. I only had buns and eight-treasure porridge for meals, and the total time I spent eating was less than ten minutes a day. I immersed myself in work, forgetting everything, but I still couldn't help crying in the office. Those three months were hard to get through. I finished my graduation project and won the national first prize. Though I left the civil engineering field, the abilities I developed during that low point had a big impact on me. A crisis, a low point—this might be the best gift.
