114th Original Piece

My friends who know me well know I'm someone out of sync with the times, seriously behind the curve. Let me tell you a few things, and you'll get the idea!

I don't play games. If card games count, then I have played them.

I don't watch live streams. The distance between me and Douyin and Kuaishou is as vast as that between the world's richest person and me.

I don't chase trends. I rarely check Weibo, and I almost never read Toutiao. Most of the news I get comes from private messages from friends. Even for the hottest TV shows, I usually get to know about them through recommendations from others.

There's no end to the list...

Since I've been so far removed from games, why am I talking about this topic today? This might be a turning point for me in 2021.

On February 6, 2021, the 25th anniversary of my "broken egg," I decided to give myself a gift: a WeChat Channel!

Daily video updates on the Channel became my new interest, and I gradually came to understand an important space within the Channel: the live stream room. A person who had always been insulated from live streaming, I began my adventurous journey into learning how to live stream.

Perhaps because I created content on the Channel myself, I didn't resist live streaming. Here I met my "important people"—Xiao Daye, Du Zijian, Wang Dong, and Li Hongliang, several teachers who became my learning models and role models, in the order I met them.

My guide on the Channel was Big Brother Xiao Daye. Through his knowledge planet, I met outstanding people from various fields. I was lucky enough to win a spot in his communication course, and later joined the Channel training camp. Not only did I learn, but I also took on service work, which helped me improve and develop my operational skills. The power of the community was beyond my imagination!

Isn't the adventure on the Channel just like a game?

In the live stream room, I absorbed energy, learned rhythm, and there were countless points that inspired me. Without realizing it, I began to have a shallow understanding of live streaming!

Teacher Wang Dong live-streamed with Teacher Du Zijian, and his real conversation partner was Du's son, known as the "Young Commander." When Dong Brother and the Young Commander discussed games, the comment section was filled with various opinions. After the live stream, Dong Brother shared his thoughts, and I won't comment much on that. Instead, I'll share my understanding.

First, when we evaluate a viewpoint, how do we respond? With reason or emotion? Are we being argumentative or just echoing? When we keep falling into various logical traps, have we ever considered that we might be lacking a certain ability?

Independent thinking, Critical Thinking—yes, you can call it critical thinking, but not in the sense of criticism.

Second, do our responses have a basis? Are they testable? And more importantly, are we willing to listen to the other side?

Let me talk about the topic Dong Brother shared. He encourages children to play games, and what he truly hopes for is that parents listen carefully to their children!

Dong Brother and several teachers discussed this further in the live stream room in the evening. One point stuck with me: Do we really need so many educational experts? Or should our parents all become professional educators?

What we truly need are those who can accompany children's growth, nurturing this little sapling with love!

Honestly, I heard the idea of encouraging children to play games, and I was particularly resistant. What's good about games? They're addictive, they affect children, and there are so many downsides...

See, I've fallen into my own logical trap. Without reason, without evidence, I just started making subjective statements. Someone might say, "How can you make such a claim without evidence? Look, that person..." Is this inductive reasoning? Summarizing truth from a few cases? Or drawing an absolute conclusion?

Why do I resist? I agree with Dong Brother on one point: when I haven't played 10,000 hours, I have no right to comment!

Even further, I should ask myself: Why don't I want to play games? It might be because I didn't have the conditions to play when I was younger. My family didn't have the equipment or a computer. Later, when I did get a computer, I was afraid I wouldn't keep up. In short, no one wants to take on a teammate like me. Gradually, I became more and more self-conscious about games, and naturally, I didn't like them, even developing a subtle resistance.

This formed a bias in my life later. Anything bad would be linked to games. Is there really any connection? That's just my own wishful thinking.

Dong Brother's children all play games, and he's willing to let them learn and grow through games. In his words, games are at least half the source of his current achievements.

Maybe some of you are like me—never played online games, never touched electronic games. But is it not important that we learned something from games when we were young? Or is there something we really need from games?

The times are different, and so are the forms of games. If you don't like games, maybe it's just self-deception. At some stage in your life, you'll be drawn to a certain form of play!

I am an English teacher. Do you know what I do most in my job?

Play games!

In various ways, you play. Why are you interested in a language you don't understand? Excluding the exam factor, you must have some interest, right!

Game-based teaching is a field I specialize in. How to guide students to use language in games, to create real language scenarios, theory and practice, continuously iterating and improving!

Why can't we play electronic games? After I thought about it carefully, I believe there's no problem with it. And I won't add "you can play, but..." There's no "but" at all!

Games are an important part of learning and growth. More interestingly, games are the main content of children's social interaction. Whether we admit it or not, do children talk about learning? Let's wait and see!

If a child can't keep up with their peers, how can we help them learn to socialize? If they can't join in the conversation, how do you think the child feels?

The above thoughts are mainly to raise a question: Have we truly listened to our children?

Ignoring them, thinking for ourselves, games are the flood that will corrupt anyone who plays. Please forget these thoughts!

Don't interfere. Listen carefully to what the children have to say!

We were young once. We also spent time doing what you now consider a waste. What's the root of your demands for children?

Please accept yourself!

Game of life, life as a game. Come on, let's play a round!
