Today I completed an interesting task, which I want to share starting from a conversation I had with my younger brother during the summer.

My brother began a new life during the summer, and we decided to treat seven years as one lifetime, with each phase being a rebirth. The next three years of high school and four years of university would form the framework of this lifetime.

My brother has started to think about his future in some interesting ways. He shared with me his wild ideas, but more importantly, he has developed a greater sense of hope for this era.

The development of artificial intelligence has once again made us reflect on what education truly is, how we should face an information explosion, and whether we still need to go abroad in the future. These questions have led us into deep thought.

After several days of discussion, my brother began to plan a new life strategy. He wants to study abroad for his undergraduate or even graduate studies, and he has started to think about and form his ideas about international education.

I also need to keep up with his pace. Although I've applied for a master's program in the UK, I know almost nothing about high school, undergraduate education, and the various aspects of studying abroad. So what should I do? I should learn by myself.

I started gathering information extensively—websites, books, consultations, videos, live streams, and added various resource channels. I collected and organized a lot of information, and eventually had some initial understanding.

Besides self-study, I also needed to add practical experience. I gathered materials related to international courses and competitions in high school, and the family's resources started to pile up.

If there were professional books, I bought them; if not, I searched for them. English original books were hard to find, so I worked hard to find resources and printed them out. Some books were kept by me, while others were sent to my brother for school.

Sometimes, plans aren't one-sided; they are the result of joint effort. I did what I could, and my brother did what he could within his capacity. Our study abroad planning and synchronized learning both at home and abroad began.

At one point, I trained myself to become my brother's exclusive study abroad advisor, and I gained a lot of knowledge, becoming somewhat professional.

I just happened to come across the official British Council-certified advisor training and credentials. After some study and exams, I also obtained the certification. At least, I now know a bit about studying in the UK.

Other countries and regions are also important. So my brother and I started collecting and gathering information, and we now have a basic understanding of the study abroad planning and related information for major countries and regions.

Currently, our plan is to proceed with the national college entrance exam and the AP international curriculum simultaneously, and we are also considering competitions and related projects. Our goal is to study abroad for undergraduate studies, but if time and energy are insufficient, we may adjust to the graduate level, or start from a Sino-foreign cooperative program.

In summary, we learn, think, and act, continuously deepening our learning and research. It's not that complicated; we can do it ourselves.

Today, I want to talk about: Why is studying abroad a necessity?

In the context of globalization, an increasing number of students choose to study abroad. Studying abroad not only provides access to international education but also broadens horizons and enhances employment competitiveness.

Studying abroad offers students the opportunity to broaden their perspectives. By studying abroad, students can be exposed to different cultures, languages, and educational systems, gaining a deeper understanding of the diversity of the world.

Living and studying with students from other countries can enhance understanding of different cultures and develop cross-cultural communication and collaboration skills, making students more competitive in a global society.

During their time abroad, students can also participate in various international exchange activities, internships, and social practice projects. These experiences not only enrich students' resumes but also help develop leadership, teamwork, and problem-solving skills.

Studying abroad provides access to high-quality educational resources. Many foreign universities and colleges offer world-class educational resources, authoritative professors, and advanced research facilities.

Students can receive top-tier academic training, deeply explore their interests, and learn alongside excellent students from around the world.

Beyond academic growth, studying abroad also offers opportunities to expand professional networks and relationships. Students can meet classmates and mentors from different countries, building international friendships and partnerships. These connections can have a positive impact on students' future employment and career development.

Studying abroad makes students more competitive in the global job market. Graduates from prestigious foreign universities often have an international background and degree, which makes them attractive to employers. Cross-cultural communication skills, global perspectives, and language abilities have become significant advantages in the job search process.

At the same time, studying abroad also broadens students' employment opportunities. Many domestic and international companies value employees with international experience and cultural sensitivity, and thus are open to hiring international students, offering more job opportunities.

However, studying abroad is also subject to criticism, which is a current issue. Is studying abroad worth it? What are the risks and challenges of studying abroad?

The high cost of studying abroad is one of the main reasons for criticism. Studying abroad requires students to pay tuition and living expenses, as well as visa fees, flight costs, and additional living expenses. Plus, some professional courses and internship programs come with extra costs, making the financial pressure quite significant.

For many families, the cost of studying abroad can be a heavy burden. Moreover, the income upon returning home is often much less than the expenses, making it seem like a financial loss—spending a lot of money and still earning a modest salary.

Cultural adaptation difficulties are also a key factor in the criticism of studying abroad. Living in a foreign country requires students to adapt to different lifestyles, eating habits, and social norms, while also facing language barriers and feelings of loneliness.

When facing different educational systems, students may encounter academic pressure and learning adaptation challenges. Differences in teaching methods, classroom assignments, and examination formats can lead to learning difficulties.

The path of studying abroad is destined to be a winding and challenging journey, but through overcoming these challenges, students will gain unparalleled rewards and self-improvement.

How can you determine if you are suitable for studying abroad? Here's a five-point analysis.

First, the key to determining if you are suitable for studying abroad is your desire for learning and growth. Studying abroad requires hard work and adaptation to a new academic environment and learning style. If you have a strong interest in learning and knowledge and are willing to put in the effort, you are likely a good fit for studying abroad.

Second, studying abroad requires independent living skills. In a foreign country, you will need to handle daily life tasks such as shopping, cooking, and managing personal affairs. If you have the ability to live independently and adapt to a new environment and culture, you are likely a good fit for studying abroad.

Third, cross-cultural communication and adaptability are key factors in determining if you are suitable for studying abroad. In a foreign country, you will face challenges with different cultures and languages, requiring you to actively integrate into the local social circle and lifestyle. If you are good at communicating with others and maintain an open attitude toward different cultures, you are likely a good fit for studying abroad.

Additionally, determining if you are suitable for studying abroad also involves considering the support and understanding from your family and friends. Studying abroad may bring feelings of homesickness, and having family and friends who support your decision can provide a sense of belonging in a foreign country. If your family and friends understand and support your decision to study abroad, you are more likely to be a good fit.

Finally, studying abroad should align with your future plans and career development. The experience of studying abroad can provide you with an excellent educational background and cross-cultural experience, offering significant advantages for your future career. If you have clear goals for studying abroad and understand how it will benefit your career, you are more suitable for choosing to study abroad.

Further, from studying abroad, we can talk about our own learning and career planning. In fact, you have the best mentor right by your side.

Rebuilding your life blueprint and becoming your own academic and career mentor.

Academic and career planning is an essential part of everyone's growth journey. Becoming your own academic and career mentor is a process of self-guidance and reflection.

As your own mentor, the first task is to clearly identify key factors and set clear goals. Understand your strengths, interests, and potential career preferences. Having a goal-oriented approach will make your academic and career planning more targeted, allowing you to better leverage your strengths and potential.

Integrate resources comprehensively and expand information channels. School academic counseling centers, teachers, classmates, and the internet are all valuable sources of information and resources. From professional books, industry reports to course websites and social media, actively collect information to broaden your knowledge.

Create specific plans and schedules. Break down your goals into specific, achievable steps and set completion times. Reasonably arrange the order and time allocation for learning, practice, internships, and other activities, and continuously review and adjust your plan to ensure its efficiency and feasibility.

Use diverse strategies to advance your planning. Actively seek opportunities for academic tutoring, exchange activities, and practical projects to develop your skills and expand your professional network. At the same time, continuously self-assess, identify areas for improvement, and keep a positive and proactive mindset.

Maintain a flexible mindset at all times to deal with challenges and opportunities. Any plan may face uncertainties, and the key is how to adjust and adapt to changes. Expand your social circle, build partnerships, and embrace new challenges. Every change is an opportunity for growth.

Believe that through continuous progress and optimization, we will discover a more authentic and powerful version of ourselves.

Finally, I want to share a small point: a new choice in the internet age—studying abroad without leaving home.

In the internet age, we can use various online learning platforms and resources to enjoy the academic and cultural experience of studying abroad without leaving home.

Choose the right online learning platforms and courses. High-quality platforms like Coursera, edX, and FutureLearn offer a wide range of courses and resources, covering fields from humanities to business. By participating in these online courses, we can connect with professors and scholars from around the world, broadening our knowledge and academic horizons.

Like studying abroad in person, interaction with other students and scholars is an important part of online studying. Most online learning platforms have forums and social functions similar to those of traditional universities. By actively participating in these communities, you can meet students from around the world, share learning experiences, and engage in cultural exchanges. Such interactions allow us to experience the joy of cross-cultural communication from the comfort of home and broaden our horizons.

Studying abroad online can also enrich your academic knowledge through online libraries and resources. Many prestigious universities and libraries offer free online library services, allowing you to access academic resources from around the world. Additionally, you can use academic search engines and social platforms to share research findings and engage in academic discussions, deepening your understanding of your field and advancing your academic exploration.

Studying abroad is not just an academic experience—it's also a cultural one. During your online study abroad journey, you can use various online cultural experiences to understand and feel the cultures of different countries and regions. For example, watching foreign concerts, theater performances, or participating in online cultural festivals. These activities allow you to immerse yourself in the cultural atmosphere of a foreign country and experience the charm of internationalization.

Whether now or in the future, the path of studying abroad always has open doors. Don't forget about artificial intelligence; let it be your mentor, and together, we can see a broader world.

Studying abroad is also a form of learning. We are always on the path of learning and growing. In our journey of enriching our knowledge, gaining experience, and accumulating life lessons, I suggest that everyone take a trip, go to a distant place, and maybe gain some new feelings and thoughts.

Keep learning, keep growing, thank you for reading.
