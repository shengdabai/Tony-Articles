What are the characteristics of the bad debt cycle? How does it occur?

Characteristics of the bad debt cycle:

(1) **Highly cyclical**: Opportunities to profit from investing in bad debt are highly cyclical and depend on the development of other cycles. Therefore, the bad debt cycle can serve as an example of how cycles operate.
(2) **Repetitive nature**: The main theme of the cycle is absolutely like the rhythm of a poem, repeating endlessly. The cycle is indeed a chain reaction, one event leading to the next, and the author believes the cycle will continue in this way in the future.
(3) **Causal chain**: Each event in the cycle triggers the next.

The process of the bad debt cycle occurring:

(1) **Unreasonable credit expansion**: As the credit market heats up, a race to the bottom begins. Loan institutions, eager to make quick profits, will provide financing to borrowers who are not worth investing in, accept weaker debt structures, and thus result in newly issued bonds lacking sufficient safety margins. Once conditions slightly worsen, borrowers find it difficult to repay principal and interest on time.
(2) **The spark**: Usually comes from a recession. A recession leads to declining corporate profits. Economic downturns and declining corporate profits are often accompanied by a credit shortage, with the credit window suddenly slamming shut, leading to existing corporate debt not being refinanced, and thus resulting in defaults.
(3) **Back to the starting point**: Eventually, the economy begins to recover, and the credit market window reopens. These two changes lead to a reduction in high-yield default rates. As the economic situation improves, default rates drop, and the pace of selling slows down. At this point, we have gone through a complete cycle.

What is the relationship between the bad debt cycle and other cycles?

Many underlying cycles influence the bad debt cycle. The fluctuations in investment opportunities in the bad debt market come from the interaction of other cycles. These cycles include the economic cycle, investor psychology cycle, risk attitude cycle, and credit cycle.
The economic cycle influences investor psychology, corporate profitability, and the rate of defaults. The psychology cycle drives fluctuations in the credit market, pushing the willingness of investors to lend, buy, and sell. The risk attitude cycle is favorable for the smooth issuance of bad debt at the cycle's peak, but at the cycle's bottom, it refuses to provide capital for the refinancing of maturing corporate debt. The credit cycle has two profound impacts: first, it affects the ease with which corporate debt can be refinanced upon maturity; second, it influences the extent to which future bond issuers are subject to strict credit standards.

Many underlying cycles influence the bad debt market, so the bad debt market is far from being separate or isolated from other cycles. As I mentioned earlier, these cycles themselves fluctuate; each cycle triggers fluctuations in others, and each cycle is also influenced by fluctuations in others. However, the combined effect of these cycles results in a bad debt cycle with a much greater amplitude of rise and fall than the underlying cycles it contains.
