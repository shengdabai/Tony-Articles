# Context Must Be Able to Leave

> 发布日期:2026-08-23 · [中文](../zh/2026-08-23-上下文必须能带走.md) | [English](../en/2026-08-23-context-must-be-able-to-leave.md)

---

The most important question about an AI product is not whether it can answer me.

The more important question is: after it answers me, what happens to the context?

That sounds like a dry technical question. It is not. It is a question about power, memory, learning, and dignity. In the old internet, the platform wanted your data because data made advertising, recommendation, and lock-in stronger. In the AI era, the word data is too small. What we are really producing is context: recordings, drafts, corrections, decisions, preferences, failed attempts, personal vocabulary, domain assumptions, and the small private history that explains why a sentence means one thing to me and another thing to you.

If the context stays inside the product, the product becomes smarter and the user becomes dependent.

If the context can leave with the user, the user becomes stronger and the product has to earn trust again tomorrow.

That is the fork.

Many AI products still think like old platforms. They want to sell a device, an app, a subscription, or a model wrapper, then keep the user inside a private garden. The garden may be pleasant. The answers may be good. The interface may be polished. But the commercial logic is still: create value from the user's life, then charge the user rent to access a processed shadow of that life.

This logic is especially strange for AI hardware. A recorder, camera, wearable, note-taking device, or always-on assistant does not become valuable merely because it has a model inside. Its real value comes from the context it captures at the edge of life. A meeting does not repeat itself. A walk does not repeat itself. A flash of thinking before sleep does not repeat itself. The hardware is only the net. The catch belongs to the person who lived it.

So the honest payment point should be different. Do not charge me mainly because you can trap my context. Charge me because you help me bring it home.

Bring it home in a structured format. Bring it home in a way my own tools can read. Bring it home with timestamps, source boundaries, provenance, permissions, and enough metadata that a future agent can understand not just the words, but the situation in which the words were produced. Bring it home so I can feed it into another model, another notebook, another workflow, another future version of myself.

This is not a romantic demand. It is already the direction of serious digital rights. The EU's GDPR Article 20 established a right to data portability under defined conditions: people can receive their personal data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format and transmit it to another controller. The Data Transfer Project, announced in 2018 by major technology companies, tried to make direct service-to-service transfer technically easier through open-source tools. These efforts are not perfect, and they are not written specifically for personal AI workbenches. But they point to the same civilizational instinct: a person should not have to abandon their digital memory in order to change tools.

AI makes this more urgent because context is no longer passive archive. Context is now executable memory.

A normal note sits there until you read it. AI-readable context can be called, compared, summarized, checked, transformed, and used as fuel for future action. A transcript can become a decision record. A decision record can become a checklist. A checklist can become a skill. A skill can become an agent. An agent can run a workflow, observe the result, and feed the result back into the next round.

This is where the old metaphor of "saving time" becomes shallow.

If AI only saves time, it often saves the wrong part. It removes the friction that used to grow the person. A student who outsources every draft may submit better homework and still lose the muscle that closed-book exams expose. A writer who lets the model produce finished prose too early may gain volume and lose voice. A builder who lets an agent implement without recording why may get more code and less judgment.

The more mature use is different: use AI to make the thinking visible, then keep the visible thinking.

That is why decision records matter far beyond software architecture. Michael Nygard's short note on architecture decision records popularized a simple pattern: record an important decision along with its context and consequences. Thoughtworks later placed lightweight ADRs on its Technology Radar and recommended keeping them with the code. This looks like boring documentation until you work with AI. Then it becomes obvious: when machines can generate the how, the human must protect the why.

The why is context.

Why did we reject the seemingly faster option? Why did we accept a temporary compromise? Why did this user pain matter more than that shiny feature? Why did we stop instead of continuing? Why was the model allowed to act here but not there? If those answers disappear, the system may still function, but the person behind it becomes less capable of steering it.

AI increases decision density. One person can now test more ideas, write more drafts, generate more prototypes, and run more parallel experiments than before. That sounds empowering, but it also creates a new kind of amnesia. When ten decisions used to take a week, you could remember the path. When a hundred decisions happen in an afternoon, memory becomes fiction. You will remember the result and invent a clean story afterward. The messy real reasons vanish.

Portable context is an antidote to that amnesia.

It lets the next tool inherit the last lesson. It lets your future self inspect the cost of an earlier choice. It lets an agent see the rejected alternatives, not just the final artifact. It lets a collaborator understand the boundary between fact, assumption, taste, and experiment. It lets a small team grow without forcing every new person to repeat every old mistake.

More importantly, it changes what kind of person AI trains you to become.

There are two very different AI users.

The first user asks for answers. Each session begins almost from zero. The user becomes good at prompting, but not necessarily good at thinking. When the tool changes, the accumulated advantage leaks away because most of the learning stayed inside the platform.

The second user builds a portable context system. Each interaction leaves behind a usable trace: a concept clarified, a decision recorded, a source linked, a failure diagnosed, a pattern named, a constraint tested. The next session starts higher. A new tool can read the trail. A better model can inherit the work. The user is not merely consuming intelligence. The user is compounding it.

This is the difference between renting a smart room and building a mind with doors.

The room can be beautiful, but you live inside its walls. A mind with doors can invite tools in and let tools go. It can change models without losing itself. It can use one assistant for research, another for coding, another for critique, another for translation, because the center of gravity is not the assistant. The center of gravity is the user's own organized context.

This also reframes product trust.

The old platform asked, "How can we make it hard for users to leave?"

The better AI product asks, "If users can leave at any moment with their context intact, what would make them choose to stay?"

That is a much harsher question. It kills lazy lock-in. It forces the product to compete on real service: better capture, cleaner structure, stronger privacy, more reliable export, richer integrations, better feedback loops, lower switching anxiety, and a clearer relationship between user effort and user-owned improvement.

Some founders fear portability because they think it weakens retention. I think the opposite is more likely. When users know they can leave, they dare to invest more. They record more. They write more. They connect more workflows. They expose more of their real thinking to the system because the relationship no longer feels like a hostage situation. Freedom can produce deeper commitment than captivity.

There is a human version of the same principle.

The teacher who wants to be worshiped hides the method.

The teacher who wants the student to grow makes the method transferable.

The tool that wants dependence hides the context.

The tool that wants the user to grow makes the context portable.

This is why I increasingly care less about whether an AI product gives me a spectacular answer on day one. I care more about whether it helps me leave a better trace after day one. Can I see where the answer came from? Can I export the transcript? Can I preserve the reasoning boundary? Can I turn repeated work into a reusable skill? Can I review what changed? Can I recover if the platform disappears, the price changes, the model declines, or a better tool arrives?

If the answer is no, the product may still be useful. But I should treat it like a cafe, not a home. I can visit, get something done, and leave. I should not store my life there.

If the answer is yes, something deeper becomes possible. The product becomes part of a personal learning engine. Every interaction can feed the next. Every mistake can become a guardrail. Every decision can become searchable memory. Every project can make the next project easier.

This is not about owning data in a legalistic sense only. It is about owning the path by which you became more capable.

In the coming years, many people will have access to the same models. The scarce thing will not be raw intelligence. It will be accumulated, organized, portable context: the record of what you care about, what you tried, what failed, what you learned, what you refuse to do, and what you are slowly becoming able to see.

The person who owns that context owns the compounding curve.

The person who leaves it scattered across platforms has to keep becoming a beginner.

So my rule is simple: do not let AI become a black hole for your life.

Let it become a loom. Let it weave your scattered moments into something structured enough to carry, question, reuse, and improve. The best AI does not merely answer you. It helps you take more of yourself with you.

And the best product does not fear the user walking away with their context.

It is proud to have strengthened the user enough to walk.
