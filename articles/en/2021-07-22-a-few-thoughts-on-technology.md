110th Original Article

Last week I finally got my COVID-19 vaccine. Before that, I had some things to take care of, and more importantly, I had to go to the community center early in the morning to wait in line for a long time, which made me hesitate and even reluctant, and I kept delaying it.

The development of technology has already made me accustomed to online operations. In fact, most regions have online registration for vaccine appointments. But when I could only call to inquire, the response I got was that the vaccine was in short supply, with daily limits, and I had to queue up on-site for a number. This would not have raised any questions for me ten years ago. What about today?

I won't go into detail about the process of visiting a hospital. Online registration and in-person consultation is a natural process, and even better service is scheduling an exact time for my visit. No need to queue for registration, payment, or to check reports—this is a habit I've developed thanks to technology.

In the intelligent era, everything seems to be personalized. The services we enjoy are like those of a monarch. Though this analogy might not be entirely accurate, it's true that many people are serving us. Having everything done for us, global shopping, private drivers, all kinds of personalized services—this is not just the life of a noble, is it?

Before we were fully trained to operate various devices and software, we still had some patience. But today, technology has completely transformed our lives. Take payment, for example—payment methods have already changed entirely.

Bank cards, passbooks, going to the bank to withdraw cash, or waiting for a stack of cash from our salary. Today, all you need is to scan it or have it scan you. One-dimensional codes, or barcodes, are no longer sufficient for today's needs. The magical QR code has entered our world.

No matter how long we spent, we did spend some time adapting to this payment method. To encourage us to use QR code payments, both Alipay and WeChat Pay have done a lot of promotion, especially during the New Year when the blue side launched a massive campaign with red envelopes, which opened up the world of Ant Financial!

Speaking of this, I haven't mentioned my experience with the vaccine appointment yet. I don't want to complain or praise it. My perspective is to imagine a few possible ways.

Option 1: Queue up on-site for a number, get it, then queue up to get the vaccine, register information, and receive the shot.

Option 2: Queue up on-site for a number, schedule a time, come at the scheduled time to get the vaccine, register information, and receive the shot.

Option 3: Schedule via phone or online, come to the site at the scheduled time to get the vaccine, register information, and receive the shot.

Of course, there are other ways and possibilities, but more importantly, how do we register the information?

Previously, we needed to check our ID, sign a confirmation. Today, we need to take a photo, press a fingerprint, and sign with a finger on the screen. After receiving the vaccine, we have to stay for 30 minutes for observation, and the vaccination slip will have a QR code. Only after half an hour can we scan it and leave through the gate.

It seems our services have become increasingly intelligent and advanced, but what do they bring us—more convenience or something else?

I've used a few kiosks before. At government administrative service centers, one device can complete various procedures you need. That's amazing, isn't it? But when I realized that this one-time completion requires you to prepare a list of materials, I felt overwhelmed!

This was my first time using it. Naturally, I didn't know the process, and every time I needed to learn the process and understand all that information, I felt like I was getting slower. Gradually, I realized this might also be a source of some contradictions.

Some staff members and people coming to handle affairs have arguments. It's usually because the person doesn't understand, and the staff patiently explains again and again until the person understands. Of course, there might be other possibilities, but I won't judge right or wrong. What I want to say is, why do we have to understand so much information at once?

In the intelligent era, violations and human errors can be avoided, but the learning cost is still not low. Technology empowerment is meant to provide convenience, but if it makes our lives more complicated and requires us to master more information to understand and adapt, this might not be the right direction.

Through sharing some of my experiences, I want to express my understanding. I've always been interested in technology and love the intelligent era. I'm very optimistic about the future of artificial intelligence.

But this has made me overlook the role of people in this process, and more broadly, human nature, such as our emotions. When facing a cold, emotionless machine, I'm very cautious, as if a single mistake could lead to mockery. Let alone someone who can't read.

Maybe my content has some contradictions and conflicts, but I hope we can think together about whether there are other side effects in the intelligent era besides our improvement?

Services don't necessarily need human involvement, but the core of service comes from understanding people. Technology and the progress of technology have given us more possibilities in life. The future is bright, but please don't ignore the power of the simplest and most primitive methods. Let's think about the beginning!
