140th Original Piece

What comes to mind when you see the word "chatty"?

My mind immediately conjures up myself……

Without realizing it, I've transformed from an introverted teenager into a chatty young man. Yes, I'm a young man.

The story might start with university. During my time in college, I did some work and was lucky enough to hold several positions, such as school information officer, student party branch secretary, student union general secretary, and head of the martial arts club. Why the "etc."? I also held roles like department head and member, which is basically a career promotion story.

When I was still a low-level member, I hated meetings. Every meeting felt like a tea party, with everyone speaking their minds, as if there was never an end to the topics. Ideas floated around endlessly… but problems remained unsolved, and new troubles were created instead.

When I started hosting meetings, my intention was naturally to solve problems efficiently and end the meeting as soon as possible, which turned it into a one-person show. Everyone was silently taking notes, and I comforted myself by thinking they were actually writing something down.

But isn't that just a notice? Why hold a meeting at all? Why not just send a message in the group chat? Once I realized this, I immediately started adjusting my approach.

Encourage more interaction and participation, let attendees give their suggestions, and ask how they plan to proceed. There's no need to repeat the task three times; let the responsible person go through it once, and the result is usually good.

During my meetings, I'd think, what if no one speaks? I need to control the atmosphere! Then I'd fall into my chatty nature, rambling on and on.

Being chatty means being overly concerned and repeating things, going back and forth on the same point for a long time. Why do I always have so much on my mind?

Suddenly, I realized that back then I already had the potential to be a teacher. No offense intended to the teaching profession.

Thinking about some parents and teachers, it seems they always have so much on their minds. If you ask them why they do things that way, they might just say, "It's for your own good!"

I fear the silence that comes when the air gets still. Chatting until everyone has nothing to say—it's really hard. What would I do in such a situation? Depending on the context, if I can speak up and save the day, I'd step in to ease the tension. If it's not appropriate for me to speak, I'd just watch and wait.

Sometimes, being chatty has a certain quality, like a leader's vibe, guiding the conversation and making the audience follow my rhythm. In that sense, I can't help but want to talk more.

Today, I'm talking about this topic because I sometimes get chatty, and once I start, I can't stop. I wonder if it's because I have a strong desire to perform or if I'm just afraid of awkward silence.

Some people need to increase the density of their chatter, making it seamless with no gaps. When communicating, it's important to leave space for the other person, to share with each other, and to communicate sincerely.

I think I might be quite suitable for being a streamer. A solo show, but if I have a guest on the mic, I need to control myself, nod a lot to show agreement—it's basic respect for the other person. Express my own thoughts at the right time, and keep the conversation going back and forth.

What color is the chatty nature? It's the foundational color for depth. Those who are afraid to express themselves should first become chatty people. Being chatty isn't necessarily bad. After becoming chatty, they can gradually refine their language and develop depth and meaning.

So, are you chatty?

In any case, I'm chatty today!
