Commercial Thinking in Phone Repair (Part 1)

01 Why would you choose this store when queuing for phone repair? Because it's an official repair center. Even if the experience and service aren't up to par, you have no choice but to come here. When considering how to get users to come to your store, the cover of your video and live stream should give users a reason to enter.

02 How can you retain users once they're in the store? What is the purpose of a user coming to a repair center? It's to solve their phone problem, which generally falls into three categories: solve it today, delay the solution, or it can't be solved at all.

03 Users come to the repair center only when they have a problem, and their minds are naturally anxious, hoping for a quick resolution. How you greet them is key. For example, if the staff at the store respond with a blaming attitude, how would you feel?

04 You could also choose to patiently listen to the user's concerns, just like a doctor during a consultation. Using evidence-based medicine thinking to analyze what the patient says, the difference between doctors becomes clear. Explaining with professional jargon might not be something the user can truly understand.

05 Going back to the phone store, the engineer's professional explanation leaves you stunned, but the user is still dissatisfied because he doesn't know whether the problem has been resolved. Can the phone make calls? Can the camera take photos? Is the sound clear? These are very unprofessional questions, and the user just needs answers.

06 If you can't guarantee the repair or if it's not possible to repair, does the user have to leave helplessly? Obviously not. The repair center can also sell new phones, isn't that just business?

07 At this point, a key question arises: What about my phone backup? This isn't a difficult issue, just requiring patience and someone to handle the service, but it directly affects the reputation.
