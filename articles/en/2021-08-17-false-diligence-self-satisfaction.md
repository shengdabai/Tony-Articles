# False Diligence, Self-Satisfaction

129th Original Article

Diligence is a positive word. When we describe someone as diligent, we are praising them.

I once heard someone say that diligence is now a standard for everyone, and this statement filled me with motivation.

When the word "diligence" appears in your mind, what do you think of? Just one word: busy.

Where is the diligence? Is having an endless list of things to do considered diligence?

In the era of knowledge payment, more and more people have chosen to pay for knowledge. Paying for courses has become so normal that it's almost like buying books. You buy a lot of books, but how many have you actually read?

I'm currently engaged in short video creation and writing practice. I update every day, and I know one thing: my audience is forgiving!

Information from any field can be integrated, but the truly valuable content may only be 10 percent. Most of it is not helpful for growth.

We seem to be diligently absorbing knowledge, but how much of it has actually made a real difference?

In my student days, I loved free content. Sometimes I even felt smug, thinking I had an advantage because I could get free content while others had to pay.

After starting to work, I discovered I preferred to pay for content. The reason was simple: paying gives me motivation, since I've invested a cost.

Actually, even when it was free, I had paid a cost—my attention! My attention was wasted on discerning information, and I felt like I was being diligent, spending a lot of effort to find content that was valuable to me.

What's even scarier is that after all that effort, I ended up feeling satisfied. After all, I really tried. What did I get? At most, it was the content filtered by paying, but more importantly, my diligence was just self-comfort. I didn't actually gain any real information.

As a content creator, I seem to have given myself a title that doesn't quite fit. I've seen so many works, but very few have actually helped me.

My own content also doesn't provide enough value to some users.

I'm still exploring on the path of creation. I'm especially grateful to the friends who read my articles and watch my videos. Thank you for your support!

I don't think our daily schedules are reasonable. Spending time reading WeChat Official Account articles or watching videos when we have free time—these things we think are inputs—turn out to be useless content.

You might give me examples, like how someone's content is different and brings you something. I don't oppose that, but you can list how many such cases there are? Probably just a few!

We humans all have curiosity. Curiosity about things drives us to explore, and thus we gradually move further along the path of diligence.

Think about why we need to be diligent. What are we supposed to be diligent about? Finding the place where we should invest our time and energy? When we're unclear, ask yourself more questions.

Li Xiaolai taught me a concept, and I'll share it with you at the end. More important than learning is unlearning!

We always hope to learn more, but more often than not, we need to forget some content, to discard the useless parts from our minds.

False diligence, self-satisfaction. If today's content has some value for you, then I think you should know what to do next!

Life remains the same. Let's be honest with each other, and do something painful, because sometimes growth isn't very fun!
