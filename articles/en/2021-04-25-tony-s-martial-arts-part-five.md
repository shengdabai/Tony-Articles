Hand, eye, body, step, spirit, energy, and technique! Three types of techniques: punch, palm, and hook. I've briefly explained them, and now let's look at leg techniques! Leg techniques are one of the practical methods in martial arts, that is, the technique of kicking with the feet. It is a method to utilize the powerful lower limbs. Basic leg techniques can be divided into the following eight types:

**Basic Leg Techniques in Martial Arts I: Straight Kick**
Preparation stance: Stand with feet together, arms raised sideways.
Action description: Step forward with the left foot, straighten the right leg, lift the toes and kick sharply toward the forehead; look straight ahead. Key points: Keep the chest up, pull in the abdomen, and lift the waist. When kicking, quickly pull in the hips and abdomen, lift and press down the toes, and speed up the movement after passing the waist, with a sudden burst of power.

**Basic Leg Techniques in Martial Arts II: Side Kick**
Preparation stance: Same as the straight kick.
Action description: Step forward with the right foot, spread the toes outward; slightly lift the left heel, turn the body slightly to the right, and raise the arms behind. Then, hook the left foot and kick it upward toward the left ear, while raising the right arm and extending the left arm in front of the right shoulder; look straight ahead. Key points: Open the hips, turn the body sideways, and pull in the abdomen sharply.

**Basic Leg Techniques in Martial Arts III: Outer Sweep Kick**
Preparation stance: Same as the straight kick.
Action description: Step forward with the right foot; keep the left toe tight, kick upward to the right and upper front, then sweep it to the left and upper front, landing beside the right foot; look straight ahead. You can use your palm to strike the sole of the foot in front of you. Key points: Open the hips, sweep the leg outward in a fan shape, and make the movement large.

**Basic Leg Techniques in Martial Arts IV: Inner Hook Kick**
Preparation stance: Same as the straight kick.
Action description: Same as the outer sweep kick, but instead of sweeping outward, it hooks inward, and the requirement is to hook the hips. Key points: Same as the outer sweep kick.

**Basic Leg Techniques in Martial Arts V: Snap Kick**
Preparation stance: Stand with feet together.
Action description: Step forward with the left foot; straighten the right leg, lift the toes, and kick upward sharply. At the same time, change the right fist to a palm and strike the sole of the right foot in front and above. Look straight ahead. Key points: Pull in the abdomen, lift the waist. The kick should be high enough to reach the chest, and the strike on the sole of the foot should be accurate and loud.

**Basic Leg Techniques in Martial Arts VI: Spring Kick**
Preparation stance: Same as the snap kick.
Action description: The supporting leg stands straight or slightly bent, while the other leg moves from a bent to a straight position and springs forward. The sole of the foot is flat, and the force reaches the tip of the foot. Key points: Pull in the hips, and the spring kick should have a sudden burst of power, with the force reaching the tip of the foot.

**Basic Leg Techniques in Martial Arts VII: Thrust Kick**
Preparation stance: Same as the spring kick.
Action description: Same as the spring kick, but the toes are lifted, and the force reaches the heel. Key points, common mistakes, correction methods, and teaching tips are the same as the spring kick.

**Basic Leg Techniques in Martial Arts VIII: Side Kick**
Preparation stance: Into a staggered stance.
Action description: Extend the right leg straight to support; the left leg moves from a bent to a straight position, the toes are turned inward, and the foot is kicked out with the sole, reaching the level of the waist, with the upper body leaning forward; look toward the left. Key points: Straighten the knee, open the hips, and kick powerfully, with the outer side of the foot facing up, and the force reaching the sole.

After listening to all these basic leg techniques, do you feel even more confused? In fact, when I was learning martial arts as a child, there was no written guidance at all. Most of what I learned came from oral instruction. How did I get the key points of the techniques? Through the coach's demonstration and repeated practice. When learning anything, the simplest method is to practice repeatedly, over and over again. It may seem ineffective, but it's actually the fastest way to learn, because it saves a lot of time spent on debating which method is better or searching for so-called efficient learning methods.

When it comes to leg techniques, we must definitely get guidance from a coach, because it's hard to gain enough practice from written instructions or simple verbal guidance, which makes it difficult to improve further. The first four leg techniques are the most commonly used in our training. Most people actually don't use these four techniques. So, what's the significance of practicing them for ordinary people? Based on my own learning experience, I think that the legs are one of the strongest parts of the body. Their weight makes them hard to control, but if we can control them, we can generate incredible power. I also deeply felt this while learning Taekwondo. During training and competitions, the leg techniques of our opponents are similar to ours, but in real-life situations, they don't follow the rules—they just kick however they find convenient. Martial arts leg techniques help us train our control over our legs, so that we can summon them at any time and launch an attack.

Control is the most important thing in training. We focus on controlling our bodies, then our minds, concentrating all our energy into one point. When asked what is most important, some say money, others say time, which we cannot manage. I understand it as attention. In martial arts training, the most important thing is also attention—focusing on one point and waiting for the right moment to act.
