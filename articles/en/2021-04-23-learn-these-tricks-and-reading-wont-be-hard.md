What is "reading comprehension," and it refers to our ability to understand what we read. Even if you read an English book every week, if you don’t know what it’s about, it won’t help your learning. What we need to know is how to improve our English reading skills, not just read more books. When building a house, workers can’t do everything at once. They must build some temporary structures to keep the house upright and help them work at higher levels. These structures are called scaffolding. Scaffolding is also a learning method. The idea is that to learn a skill well, we need to learn some smaller parts to help build knowledge and skills. Reading comprehension is the same. To truly understand what you read, you might need to practice other skills first. For example, you might need to practice reading quickly or slowly, or you might need to stop choosing very difficult books and start with books that match your skill level. Start with something easier, smaller, and slower, and then gradually increase the difficulty.

> 8 Simple Steps to Improve Your English Reading Skills: 1. Always set aside special time for reading 2. Read the right books 3. Ask yourself questions while and after reading 4. Improve fluency 5. Once you’ve learned to read faster, slow down 6. Ask many questions 7. Read again 8. Read various types of texts

**1. Always Set Aside Special Time for Reading**
Interesting reading can happen anywhere, but if you're reading to improve comprehension, you need to focus on learning. This means we need to spend special time reading, a time that should be quiet and free from distractions. Try to spend at least 30 minutes a day on focused reading. Turn the reading process into a ritual, repeating it every time you sit down to read English. For example, find a quiet and comfortable place, sit under bright lights; before sitting down, prepare everything you might need; decide how long you'll read; put all electronic devices on silent mode (or turn them off), and put them away. By preparing for reading in a specific way, our brains will know when to read and will be more focused before starting to learn.

**2. Read the Right Books**
Remember two things: 1. Content you're interested in 2. Books that match your personal reading level. As much as possible, we should read things we enjoy, and also choose books with an English level just slightly higher than our favorite level. In second language teaching, there's a theory called the i+1 theory, which we call the Input Hypothesis, proposed by linguist Stephen Krashen. Comprehensible input is input that's just slightly beyond the current language learner's ability level. If i is the current level of the language learner's foreign language ability, then i + 1 is the next crucial step in the entire development process. In short, choosing books where the number of unknown words doesn’t exceed 10% makes reading more beneficial for input. However, it's not a strict rule—you can adjust based on your own situation. Don’t choose books with more than 30% unknown words, as this will reduce the reading experience. For example, *21 Secrets* is relatively simple and suitable for beginners. Don’t know where to start? You can also search online for many recommendations on books to help with English reading, and choose based on your own situation.

**3. Ask Yourself Questions While and After Reading**
Learning how to read English books isn’t just about reading words. Before reading, quickly skim the text. For example, ask yourself if there are any bold or italic words? Are there titles or subtitles? Which names are mentioned? Are there many dialogues? Are the paragraphs short or long? After reading, you can ask yourself some questions: What is the text about? What is the most important thing that happened in the text? What confuses you? What surprises you? Are there parts you don’t understand? Depending on the type of text you're reading, there may be other questions, but these are the basics.

**4. Improve Fluency**
Fluency is the smoothness of reading. When reading in our minds, we should have a sense of rhythm for the words. Words should naturally flow together, like someone speaking. This is how to read English books like a native speaker. Many words you encounter while reading are "sight words." These words are just seen, and you don’t need to think about how to read them. You can quickly practice sight words by finding a good list of sight words, such as be verbs, subjects, pronouns, etc. Just see them, and focus on content words like nouns, verbs, and adjectives.

**5. Once You’ve Learned to Read Faster, Slow Down.**
After learning fluent English reading, you can no longer worry about speed and instead start thinking about the text and its meaning. Spend time truly reading the text you're reading, rather than reading faster. A good way to slow down is to read aloud. This is something Li Xiaolai teacher emphasizes in *Everyone Can Speak English*. Reading aloud, reading loudly, details can be found in this book.

>
> Not only will you practice reading and understanding, but you’ll also practice pronunciation, listening, and speaking. Focus on each word carefully and pronounce it well. If you can’t (or don’t want to) read aloud, you can try pausing after a few paragraphs to ensure your attention is focused. Another way to keep your pace steady is to take notes and write down questions while reading.

**6. Ask Many Questions**
The more questions you have about what you're reading, the better you'll understand its meaning. Asking questions is also a good way to ensure you understand what you're reading. Asking questions like “What is happening now?” or “Who is speaking here?” can help you stay focused. Asking questions like “Why did he do that?” or “What is she thinking?” can help you think more deeply about the story. For example, place some sticky notes and a pen next to the book. Write down all the questions you think of while reading, and stick them in the text. After reading, go back and see how many of the questions you can answer. If there are still any questions you don’t know the answers to, reread that part and try to find the answers.

**7. Read Again**
Poet Ezra Pound said, “No one ever reads anything for the first time and sees it all.” Sometimes, reading a text just once isn’t enough to understand it. If you're reading something difficult, or even if you're not reading something, reading it multiple times can help you understand it better. Re-reading is very useful when you're reading words but don’t understand them. It’s also helpful for finding things you might have missed the first time. If there are any new words in the text, you’ll see them again every time you re-read, which helps you remember them.

**8. Read Various Types of Texts**
The more you read in English, the better your use of the language will be. Don’t just read books and news. Read anything—magazines you like, various websites—are all good practices. Be proactive in seeking information, not passive in receiving push notifications. No matter what book you read, remember: the more you practice, the more you benefit. Following these steps to learn English will improve your reading level, and even your understanding of Chinese will increase.

Reading is the process of taking in the sense or meaning of letters, symbols, etc., especially by sight or touch.

For educators and researchers, reading is a multifaceted process involving such areas as word recognition, orthography (spelling), alphabetics, phonics, phonemic awareness, vocabulary, comprehension, fluency, and motivation. Other types of reading and writing, such as pictograms (e.g., a hazard symbol and an emoji), are not based on speech-based writing systems.
