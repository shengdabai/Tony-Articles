# From Imitation

> Published: 2023-06-09 · [中文](../zh/2023-06-09-%E4%BB%8E%E6%A8%A1%E4%BB%BF%E8%AF%B4%E8%B5%B7.md) | [English](../en/2023-06-09-from-imitation.md)

---

Imitation is a naturalistic learning method, something we are born with. Through imitation, we explore and learn, eventually finding a way that suits us. This is why people often say: first imitate, then innovate.

Through imitation, you can find your style. At the beginning, don’t be too opinionated. In my consulting work, I’ve noticed some users resist certain approaches, as if doing them is unethical. But it’s likely that we just watch without actually trying them.

If someone has achieved a goal through a particular method or approach, that means the process has been validated. Choosing to doubt and not trust it, while spending more time on other things, is a biased approach.

Imitation requires a rich repository of materials. For instance, when Shen Chen shared insights on marketing logic, he emphasized learning marketing methods by studying cases—domestic and international award-winning advertising campaigns. By analyzing these cases and imitating them in your own industry, you can achieve great results. Such examples are countless.

Be good at using three methods: comparison, feedback, and iteration.

Comparison and feedback are relatively easy to implement. Only after you’ve done a few imitations and practical exercises will you truly understand. The iteration process is actually a further refinement. In this process, new possibilities are born.

Proceed step by step, from imitating the surface to uncovering the core. Understand the brand better, understand the user better, and take it slowly—no rush.

A reminder: imitation is not plagiarism. Be mindful of the boundaries.

Before imitating, consider your own foundation.

Basic skills require long-term development. In the short term, what you can do is work hard. Li Xiaolai has an important definition of effort: effort is the repetition of a task in a short period of time in large quantities.

Building a solid foundation requires effort, which you can do in the short term. Long-term foundational development, however, needs patience and slow practice.

In most cases, we only see the immediate goal. From a sustainable development perspective, we may be **unqualified**.

Here’s a story:

The Japanese archery master, Abo Kenzo, trained his disciples by having them do only one thing: draw the bow. The disciples never questioned this, until a German philosopher, Herrigel, became dissatisfied.

One night, the master asked Herrigel to come to the archery hall, have him light a candle in front of the target, and then turn off the lights. The master drew the bow, shot the arrow, and hit the center. Then he shot another arrow, piercing the first one. The master said, the first time you might say I’m familiar with the environment, it’s not hard. How do you explain the second time? The arrow just shot itself.

From then on, Herrigel practiced drawing the bow for a whole year. This scene has stayed with me for a long time. It’s not supernatural showmanship—it’s the natural flow that comes from building a foundation so solidly.

Another example is the submarine divers in the military. Their training includes a repetitive action: opening the hatch, turning continuously, practicing repeatedly to ensure no mistake is made. This is crucial for everyone’s safe escape.

Are we giving enough attention to the foundation? Have we spent enough time building it?

Continuous creation: when your strength is insufficient, I recommend watching the documentary *Sushi Master*.

Once you’re ready with the foundation, the next step is to practice extensively. My advice is: low-cost mass production.

Creators must prioritize productivity. How to produce content at low cost and in bulk is something you should think about from the very beginning.

For example, Li Xiaolai, when starting his WeChat Channels, spent a month thinking and decided to shoot one-minute videos. He placed his phone on the table, wrote 300-some words of content, and could read it through in a few minutes.

Accounts that maintain a consistent presence are more likely to accumulate followers. Imagine seeing a great video, following it, only to find no updates recently, and then sending a private message with no response. What would you think?

Low cost, today we’re all busy with various things, and being able to do one thing continuously has become rare. This is evident from the progress updates in people’s friend circles. Creating at a faster pace is the choice of long-termists.

For instance, in my writing, I can finish an article injust over ten minutes (about ten minutes). I’ve also made over a thousand videos, and I will also manage eight WeChat Channels simultaneously, continuously creating content.

Mass production: we are often disturbed by various things, but by completing tasks in bulk, you always have stock, and you’ll feel more at ease. However, note that at the beginning, please accept your own clumsiness. We all need to accumulate.

Producers are our inevitable choice. Whether it’s the ability to produce content that can be sold multiple times, or the ability to continuously spread your influence, you must keep producing, creating products, services, and taking action.

As a long-termist, in a future where individual development trends are increasingly positive, you just need to focus on content. You deserve to be seen by more people.

If you want to do something, do it right away. Don’t hesitate.

Goethe said: recklessness also contains energy and magic.

Repeating thoughts may only delay results. This isn’t scaremongering. When I was managing a WeChat Channels live stream, the host hesitated for a long time before doing the live event, even though they did it, they had too many concerns, which affected their live stream performance. After all, live streams reveal your state in real time.

Doing is more important than seeing, thinking, or knowing. The things we do are the best reflection of our influence. However, those who hesitate or hesitate will miss out on opportunities. The fact is, this opportunity isn’t yours.

Plans are limited, but once you think of something, act on it immediately. Sometimes, the results are surprising. Uncertainty is the norm. From the birth of the universe to the movement of particles, how much can we design?

Are we design theorists or development theorists? Thinking from the end in mind will help us realize that development is the trend. We are all on the path of development, becoming better.

But be careful not to fall into survivorship bias, thinking that others’ mistakes have nothing to do with me, that I definitely won’t be like someone else. Use the "from the end in mind" approach again to make your own choice. Once you choose, do it well.

Don’t hesitate anymore. Take action. Action is the core of productivity.
