“He rarely, if ever, used the word 'win,' nor did he talk about defeating opponents or motivating a team to fight for first place.” He once said, “My definition of success is this: doing your best, feeling content with what you've achieved, and thus attaining inner peace. That is called success.”

This is the core philosophy of “Wooden Coaching,” which frees players from the anxiety of the scoreboard, calms them down, and motivates them to work hard.

In sports competitions, especially at the high level, a minor virus can cause a slight deviation in an athlete’s performance, which can determine the outcome of a match.

In *The Bow and Zen*, it describes how the archery master Abo Kenzo, blindfolded, shot an arrow straight into the center of the target, then shot another arrow through the first one. Such a level of skill cannot be analyzed through technique alone.

Mind and body in harmony, maintaining peak condition. In fact, most people are struggling with some imagined scenario, like, “Once I have… I will…” In the process of imagining, they miss the most precious attention of the present moment.

Take the recent hype around artificial intelligence, for example. What are all these messages promoting? They cause public anxiety, use information gaps to teach people how to use various tools, make loud comments, but offer no real guidance at all.

Let’s think about what we’ve learned since the internet began. Did we really learn how to use the internet? When AI came along, did we learn how to communicate with machines?

I think there’s really nothing to learn here!

What we should truly think about is core abilities like English proficiency, programming skills, questioning, and logical thinking. Otherwise, we’ll be overwhelmed by the sheer volume of information.

Victory without battle; win first, then fight.

We must remind ourselves that our enemies are never others, nor ourselves. So who are they?

Perhaps it’s those bad habits. Build good habits, starting with the first step of action.

Don’t take what you see and hear as the whole world.

Obviously, many people do take what they see and hear as the whole world. When told the truth, they refuse to believe it, even thinking that most people think the same way. This is known as pluralistic ignorance.

For instance, I once worked at New Oriental, and I found that most senior executives and teachers sold their New Oriental stocks and options immediately after receiving them. From their perspective, New Oriental had various management problems, like a chaotic, lawless environment, and it was probably doomed to fail.

Of course, there were also teachers who left to work independently. From their eyes, New Oriental wasn’t worth staying in. But over the years, haven’t they done better?

Meanwhile, New Oriental has actually delivered extraordinary returns to investors and shareholders.

I think someone will soon argue that after the “double reduction” policy, New Oriental suffered massive losses, laid off tens of thousands of staff, and must be in a bad state. I think the achievements of Eastern Selection and educational technology will make such people look foolish.

Company development naturally has its problems. But because you’re inside, you think you see things more clearly. In reality, though, it’s not like that. Just like us in the world of self-media, we’re inside it, thinking we understand everything, but when traffic and income are low, we immediately blame the platform, making hasty judgments.

I’m no different. I often analyze and evaluate things with a self-assumed certainty, and we are just so unreliable. Sometimes, it’s just so absurd. Take logic, for example. Most people don’t use logic. Even if they know a little, they still make mistakes and never learn from them.

Post-hoc reflection is good, but pre-hoc practice is also necessary.

Practice in action, learn through practice.

Do your own thing well, and also do well in dealing with others.

Li Xiaolai’s secret to me: effective communication is the key to solving problems.

I often do consulting on video channels and owned audiences. My shallow understanding of the WeChat ecosystem helps users to some extent. When I think about it, it’s because I’m helping them solve problems, and that’s what makes the communication effective.

When I first started consulting, I often advised people to read books and study cases. But later I realized that people usually don’t follow that advice. After all, they don’t care about the process, only about getting the problem solved.

Honestly, most problems, especially those related to operation, can be solved by carefully reading the official product manual. If you’re too lazy, just search online—there’s a high chance someone else has the same issue. If that doesn’t work, just contact customer service. There’s always a solution.

In fact, I rarely bother others. When I have a problem, I actively search for the answer. Of course, with the help of AI today, it’s your assistant. It will listen patiently and help you.

From the perspective of communication, are live streams and short videos really helpful? Do they effectively solve the problem? Please always think about it.

Live streaming is the most direct application of effective communication. It’s about sharing content, product recommendations, and spending a lot of time interacting with the audience to solve their problems, whether material or emotional, like your smile.

Improve your ability to solve problems, get into the habit of searching for answers yourself, and chat with professionals or customer service more. You’ll gain a lot.

It’s quite awkward that we are destined to meet bad people and bad things. So, what should we do?

Live your life well on your own

The best punishment for bad people is to ignore them. Let them keep doing what they do, and the biggest damage will be to themselves.

Why did I suddenly bring this up? It was inspired by Li Xiaolai. When facing various criticisms and smear campaigns, he told me to live my life well on my own. Exactly, who is responsible for my life and who will bear its consequences?

As we grow and develop, we become better, but have you ever considered that the troubles and problems we bring along will also increase? After all, we are not money, and love is not something we need to chase.

Since we are destined to meet bad people and bad things, why should we get so angry, so passionate, so united in anger? I used a lot of words, but it didn’t clearly convey the various emotions that might arise at that moment. Is it worth it? To argue, to debate, to seek recognition? Just let them go their own way.

On the path of self-media, I’ve also occasionally heard these kinds of voices—insider information, dissatisfaction, and smear campaigns. When I encounter unpleasant things, I express sympathy. But what can we do?

Never let these things drain you. Let them do what they want, as they think. Eventually, they will get the feedback they deserve. As for us, we should just do what we need to do. After all, time is precious, and you are more important than them.

I’ve seen, heard, and experienced it all. Along the way, we should just stay simple and humble, do what we want to do.

We ultimately strive to live because it’s an infinite game. Life is like a play, and we should do our own duty, striving to stay in the game. That’s the only goal.

Is our life an infinite or a finite game? Please ask yourself this question repeatedly and record your answers each time.

Since starting with video channels or self-media, some people have shown themselves to be different. They are more willing to participate in the infinite game. Most people, however, compete in the finite game, using the popular term, “cramming.”

Infinite game players think about how to sustain their video channels long-term. They enjoy the process and try to make the game never end. Finite game players, on the other hand, expect to get results—like a million followers, a viral video, or a rise in GMV.

It’s like how parents plan for their children’s future, using a design-thinking approach to plan every step. Every choice the child makes is carefully designed, leading them step by step to the peak, where the children can become dragons and phoenixes, fulfilling the parents’ unfulfilled dreams.

From the above description, you’ve probably noticed the problem. Can life be planned this way? Can self-media creation also be carefully designed?

The development theory or the scientific view of scientific development in complex science is the right path. Long ago, our leader Deng Xiaoping proposed that development is the hard truth. Are we really developing ourselves well, or are we unnecessarily competing and over-competitively “cramming”?

The game mindset reflects value thinking. How to choose, whether to respect the development laws of things, and whether to accept setbacks and failures—let’s explore this long journey with care.

Doing your own duty is a basic moral standard that everyone should follow. It not only helps build a positive image but also improves one’s professional skills and social status.

A doctor’s duty is to treat patients and provide the best possible medical care.

If a doctor does their duty, they will prioritize the patient’s health, approach their work with dedication and technical skill, and solve the patient’s problems. They will also have a strong sense of responsibility for their work.

On the contrary, if a doctor neglects their duty, focusing only on income, high positions, or personal interests, they are violating medical practice and ethics, and will lose the trust and respect of patients.

An educator’s duty is to teach students and inspire their thinking.

If they fulfill their duty, they will fully focus on students’ learning and growth, encourage students to express their talents, and provide appropriate guidance and support.

However, if educators neglect their duty, being lazy and indifferent to their work just to complete tasks and receive wages, or focusing only on their own interests, it will severely harm students’ development and future, and also lose the respect of the education profession and society.

An entrepreneur’s duty is to run a company according to business rules, provide good products and services to customers, and maintain fairness and transparency in healthy market competition.

If an entrepreneur fulfills their duty, they will consider the long-term interests of the company and how it affects the environment and social development, which will be reflected in the form of returning value to customers.

On the contrary, if an entrepreneur neglects their duty, they may pursue rapid wealth accumulation, short-term gains, and unfair business advantages. These not only harm the development of the company but also damage the stability of social organizations.

Doing your own duty and continuously refining yourself in action will help you go further on the path of growth.

If we all fulfill our duties, and the collective fulfills its responsibilities, life will become better, and happiness will increase.

Let’s keep moving forward together!
