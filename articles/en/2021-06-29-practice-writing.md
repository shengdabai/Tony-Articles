**What am I currently doing?**
Writing is divided into five parts:
1. Writing camp from Monday to Saturday, writing daily on the new platform, recording learning insights.
2. Investing in the Life Class logic course, shared reading course, and daily learning: Dedao, Chaos, Fan Deng, and all my thoughts during reading are recorded, allowing me to see my own growth, and continuously iterate so that more people can see it and help me grow better. In the class's daily check-ins, I post notes and reflections on various learning platforms. I've opened my WeChat Official Account for two months and write daily.
3. Writing on the 007 platform without leaving the house, writing a weekly piece is still easy for me, and I can usually manage three pieces a week. I've set up two columns: "Stories of Me and My Brother" and "Reflections from My Martial Arts Practice." Yes, it's the "Wu" from martial arts, and a bit of Taekwondo. I also review 4–5 pieces of my fellow writers' work each week, which is also my writing practice. Fortunately, my post was liked by Qian Jie Lao Da on his WeChat Moments (´-ω-`)
4. I've opened my WeChat Channels, posting short videos daily. There are two columns: morning English reading and sharing content from the same day. I've been doing this daily, building my own material library. All the drafts are created by me, which is also writing, isn't it? Even more exaggerated, the language is relatively conversational.
5. The last part, I feel a bit ashamed. I'm currently studying for my master's at Manchester University, reading a lot of English texts, and my writing is still just some thoughts and feelings, not yet systematic. The English refinement class from the Life Class 118 group has helped me practice more, and the lecture notes are also my writing, combining Chinese and English. I write about 4,000 words in English each week, and I hope to write daily. Let's keep going,keep going😊

**What is my vision for the future of this?**
Writing is something I'm passionate about, and I get quite excited about it. Li Xiaolai's teachings sparked my interest in writing, completely changing my perception of it. I used to think writing had nothing to do with me, or that I should just focus on studying. But Li Xiaolai told me that if you want to write, you can. There's no barrier to it—it's just a matter of effort. As a student and former teacher, I think from different perspectives, and I should write more. Since I've thought about things, I should express them. So I never worry about not having content to write. Instead, I hope my words can inspire others to join the writing community, at least those who believe they can write.
The future is unknown, but I want to keep doing this, writing every day, genuinely wanting to write, and writing whenever I have time to record my growth and gradually refine my skills. The practical knowledge I've learned in the writing camp should be applied, practiced well, and continuously refined. Circle words, perception, analysis, recording, rhetoric, logic—these are the abilities I need to keep practicing and improving. On the other hand, while learning foreign courses, I should also practice academic writing, doing things professionally and putting in enough effort. In the future, I might start an English writing column on New Work, writing daily. English is a "naive and sweet" language, you know? <(｀^´)>

**Am I doing this with enough intensity? Can I do it long-term, think deeply, and act boldly?**
Starting from February 11th, I've been posting my writing content on the New Work platform, including my practice in the writing camp, daily learning reflections (organizing content from various platforms, reading, watching videos, and writing down my thoughts), movie reviews, holiday records, and reflections from group readings and logic classes. I've had seven topics before, totaling 194 pieces. Isn't that a great number? Each piece is over 2,000 words, which adds up to over 300,000 words. Haha, I can't even imagine that! This is just the beginning. In 2021, I want to write a million words. Maybe it's verbose, maybe it's not nutritious, but I'll take it step by step. If I'm going to do this, I'll do it with all my might.
Can I keep writing for a long time? I definitely can. I don't set a word count goal for myself; I just want to record what I think and keep doing the things I love for as long as possible. I remember Li Xiaolai said that what determines our fate seems to be choices, but deeper down, it's values. In simple terms, values are what we believe is good, better, and best. Writing and reading are the best things for me. Since this is my value, I can definitely keep doing it. Why wouldn't I do something so important? I'll do it with all my might. Being strict with myself is definitely right, but more importantly, I just want to do it. If someone doesn't let me, I'll get angry, and that's when I'll be strict.

**Am I doing something that takes a long time to see results?**
Writing is something that requires long-term accumulation. There's no quick return on investment, although those with solid foundations might make money faster. That's still the result of enough prior accumulation! I don't envy others; I just focus on doing my own thing. The key to writing is choosing the right path. Once you choose the right one, writing will flow naturally. One of the biggest obstacles to writing is content. How can you write without content? In teaching English, what's the hardest? Many people think it's writing, and I used to think so too. But now I see that many people actually have nothing to write about. After all, English writing often requires independent thinking, rigorous logical arguments, sufficient evidence, and insightful analysis. Some students might lack all of these, not to mention writing in English, they might not even be able to write well in Chinese. So I fully agree with Li Xiaolai's views on the current state of Chinese language education in primary and secondary schools. It's truly poor, with too much time spent on memorizing texts and doing exercises, turning humanities into the style of natural sciences.
To be honest, building content for writing is something that requires long-term practice. The first two practices discussed reading input and collecting materials, which are closely connected. Using output to drive input is also a great approach. Starting with imitation, then gradually writing on your own, from long essays to concise expressions, continuous practice leads to gradual improvement. When you write, you naturally gain something. Many people can't keep writing because they lack sustained motivation. Thank you to the writing camp platform, the support of fellow writers, the encouragement from the reviewers, and the guidance from so many excellent mentors. Why not write? Why not practice more?

**Always protect your courage**
Protect your initial motivation, no matter what difficulties you face on the writing journey, there's always a group of people silently supporting you. Let's keep writing together. Courage is always there, waiting to be awakened. The writing camp awakened my courage. Let's cherish it and keep going,keep going(´-ω-`)

**The more you're in a low point, the harder you should work, and don't waste any crisis**
During the writing process, you'll face bottlenecks and hesitation. Take it slowly, even if it's just a few dozen or a few hundred words, they're still your own thoughts. Write them down and record your growth. This path is full of thorns and obstacles, and nothing is gained naturally. If it is, maybe it's just our natural talent. Let's give humans a little credit, haha! Treasure everything we have. This amazing brain has so much stored inside. Sometimes it hides things in a warehouse, and we can't find them. No worries, writing can help. It's like a universal key, always ready to find what you need for you.
Selling your time for multiple times, improving your ability to earn money outside the field, isn't writing the best way? We don't necessarily have to publish books, but at least we can leave a record of our growth.keep going😊

Somehow, after these three writing exercises, my spirit seems to be growing stronger, haha. No worries, cherish it! The sea can embrace rivers and mountains, and only through writing can you truly understand it!
