Source: Guest Han Xiaoxing's Tea Talk Session Sharing

Sharing: Ban Du Family Education

Hello everyone, I'm **Han Xiaoxing**, a father of a two-month-old baby. My job is pharmaceutical sales, with main clients being clinics, community health service stations, and village health centers—very traditional industries.

I first heard of Li Xiaolai teacher around 2015, when a friend recommended the book *Time as a Friend* to me.

**I thought it was written very well. At that time, I was actually not a big reader, but this book made me really interested.**

Later, I saw a column on Dedao with a very inspiring title: **"The Road to Financial Freedom"**, and since the author was Li Xiaolai, I bought it without hesitation.

This was probably the most serious **nonfiction content** I've ever read in my life. Many of my views were completely overturned, such as **reading word for word**.

Previously, I never thought reading word for word was important, and I even considered reading fast as a sign of being smart.

**Li Xiaolai teacher said that people without the ability to read word for word are likely to be at a disadvantage.** And I was exactly that kind of person—after university, I couldn't even read a rental contract, and later I was really scammed by a second-hand landlord.

Other concepts like **what financial freedom means**, **paying is not a bargain**, **multi-dimensional competitiveness**, and **metacognition** also left me in awe. **My brain felt like it had been washed every day.**

In 2021, I joined another community of Li Xiaolai's, which was essentially the **practical version of the Road to Financial Freedom.**

**Li Xiaolai said that one day, when you really become rich, you would definitely hope your children are also rich. But if you don't educate them well, it's possible to lose wealth within three generations.** So family building became especially important.

In May of last year, the teacher established a family education community. At that time, my wife was just pregnant, and we were about to become parents.

The **purpose of this family education community is to educate parents, making them qualified coaches so they can better educate their children.** So I joined again without hesitation, because Li Xiaolai's courses are always top-notch.

My wife, although she didn't join the teacher's community, we both listened to the classes together. Now, let me let her introduce herself.

Hello, I'm Zhang Xingxing, a new mom and a freelance handicraft artisan, mainly making handicrafts for sale in Europe and America, and I have my own workshop.

Like most people, I started working in a company right after graduation, as a production participant, as Li Xiaolai described.

But the difference is that my college idol was Steve Jobs, who once said: **"Young people, if you haven't found a job you love, go find it!"**

So right after graduation, I started living by this famous quote. I graduated in 2014, and in the nine years since, I've had 13 different jobs, worked in five industries, including finance, pharmaceuticals, and education, and even did a while as a self-taught programmer.

It was through my husband that I first learned about Li Xiaolai five years after graduation. **From then on, Li Xiaolai became a beacon for me on this lonely path.** Because of constantly changing jobs, I faced many hardships and my family didn't understand me at first.

My father once directly asked me in front of me: "Zhang Xingxing, don't you think you're failing?" **After meeting Li Xiaolai, I surprisingly realized that I was exactly the kind of person he described—focused, not afraid of challenges, and always learning.**

During this process, Li Xiaolai gave me a lot of courage and confidence. Eventually, after many ups and downs, I finally found a career I love and became a producer as Li Xiaolai described, and also found my life partner.

Right now, I'm happy with my life, but I also know **we still have a long way to go, and there's still a lot to learn.** So **we hope to keep growing together here and create a family business.**

**01**

**What impact has Li Xiaolai had on us?**

**1. Influenced my two major life decisions: job hunting and choosing a partner**

Li Xiaolai said that in life, there aren't many important choices—studying, working, and finding a partner. For me personally, Li Xiaolai had a huge impact on both my job hunting and choosing a partner.

**First, job hunting**

**How did I get into sales, and why did I eventually leave it?**

In 2015, I graduated from a vocational college and was looking for a job. My relatives told me that boys should do sales because it can build character. At that time, I had no clear ideas, so I decided to try it out, and I ended up entering the pharmaceutical industry without thinking much about it.

But soon I realized that sales was harder than I expected. As a fresh graduate, I knew nothing and couldn't earn much, sometimes even had to ask my parents for living expenses.

So I once gave up on sales and moved to a less stressful position within the same industry.

**What made me return to sales?**

Later, while reading *The Road to Financial Freedom*, Li Xiaolai gave me an insight. **He mentioned a personal business model: selling the same time multiple times.**

I suddenly understood—sales was exactly that model. So I decided to stick with it again.

**How much have I improved in sales over the years?**

Looking back now, I definitely have improved. My first job was making fried chicken burgers. In 2012, after high school, I worked at a fast-food restaurant to buy a phone I wanted before going to college. I was paid 4.7 yuan per hour. **Ten years later, as a salesperson, my hourly income is equivalent to working 50 hours in the past, and I have more time freedom.**

Without sales, I don't think I could have reached this level, especially since I don't have hard skills.

I'm glad I read Li Xiaolai's books. The teacher often emphasized the importance of sales. For someone born into a civil servant family, my parents expected stability, and sales, being a business, seemed unstable to them.

Now I know that **you have to step out of your comfort zone and give up some security to gain more growth.**

Sales also brought me an unexpected benefit: work-life balance.

Since my sales job doesn't have a base salary, I don't need to clock in, just complete the monthly targets. That means I can spend more time on family building.

For example, I can accompany my wife during her prenatal check-ups, I don't need to take time off for childbirth, and during the postpartum period, I can spend the mornings at the postpartum center with the baby, visit a few clients in the afternoons, and return to the center in the evenings to take care of the baby. Now that the baby needs vaccinations, I can be there the whole time.

In short, I'm rarely absent from important family events, so this is another benefit sales brought me.

**Second, choosing a partner**

Regarding choosing a partner, I also learned about it from the financial freedom column. **The teacher said the most important thing when choosing a partner is whether the other person is rational.** I found this incredibly valuable. After seeing this, I used this guideline to find my current wife. Since we are both rational people, we've developed a great intimate relationship.

Susan once asked me **how to tell if someone is rational.** I think it's a great question.

I don't think it's easy to judge someone's rationality in the short term, because when you're in a romantic relationship, you both tend to put on a good face and show your best side.

I think my wife is rational because of specific things, like she's willing to apologize to me after an argument if she was at fault.

For example, when I spent over 2000 yuan to join the teacher's community, my wife said I was wasting money and getting scammed. But after hearing the course content, she realized the teacher had some good points, so she stopped saying anything and even often listened to the courses with me.

Of course, **if possible, it's recommended that couples live together for a while.**

Because one person can pretend for a while, but it's hard to keep up the act for 24 hours a day for months. Living together makes it easier to see someone's true self.

My wife and I lived together shortly after we met, and we dated for a year before deciding to get married. So **our advice for our children's future is that it's best to live together before deciding whether to get married.**

Now we've been living together for almost five years, and they say seven years will get itchy, but our close relationship has only been getting better. Although we did argue at the beginning, the number of arguments has been decreasing.

Here I have to admire Li Xiaolai teacher, who can keep a marital relationship from arguing for twenty years straight.

**II. We've solidified our stance of not buying a house**

For us two, the biggest influence has been the teacher's view on buying a house in *Good Family Education*.

**Li Xiaolai said buying a house would cut off a family's ability to migrate, and once you buy a house when you're young, you're basically fixed in the area around it.**

Although our parents both mentioned they could help us with the down payment for a house, we decided to rely on ourselves after adulthood, and both my wife and I refused the good intentions of our parents.

Before joining the group, because we were getting married, we also looked at two second-hand houses in the local area. Although the unit price wasn't that high, only 15,000, the down payment plus renovation would cost at least 700,000.

**We would also have to shoulder a mortgage of about 7,000 a month, and one of the properties only had a 25-year property right, but the loan would be repaid over 30 years.**

So we started to think about **what the point of buying a house really is**. At that time, I thought it was mainly for the children's education, but this area is in the suburbs of Fuzhou, and there aren't many good schools. So it's better to rent for now and decide later.

Actually, we've always had the idea of not buying a house, but we weren't very firm. After listening to the teacher's class, we felt relieved, and I think many of our fellow members have the same feeling.

We realized that school districts aren't the key to education. As the teacher said, using the money from buying a house to invest in quality assets can bring much greater returns in the future than the property itself.

Some of my friends who heard that we weren't buying a house gave us friendly advice, saying that having a house gives a sense of home. I appreciate their good intentions, but I also feel a strong sense of home in the rented house we're living in now.

When I was single, I often felt lonely. After I got married to my wife, every time I came home, I was happy because there was someone waiting for me to have dinner.

Now that we have a child, I look forward to coming home even more. I'm currently renting a three-bedroom apartment from Vanke, 120 square meters, costing 3,000 a month. I feel very comfortable living there. My wife is very good at decorating, making the rooms warm and cozy, and she even set aside a room as a study.

So, **whether or not I feel like I have a home doesn't depend on whether I have my own house, but rather on whether my family relationship is harmonious.** Otherwise, even if I had a big house, but only lived alone in it, I still wouldn't feel like I had a home.

So once again, it proves how important it is to have a wife who thinks clearly. Without her also being firm about not buying a house, I might have become a housing slave. After all, my mother-in-law has been constantly urging me to buy a house.

**III. From wanting to have one child to deciding to have three**

Then, **Li Xiaolai's family education class also changed our initial idea of only wanting to have one child.**

Like most young people, we initially thought children were like golden pigs that eat up everything. **The thought of raising several children made us shiver, feeling financially strained.**

The main reasons that changed our minds were as follows:

**1. Not buying a house and only renting greatly reduced our financial pressure.**

**2. Both of us have flexible work schedules, allowing us to give our children ample companionship.**

**3. The school the children attend isn't that important.**

**4. The relationship between my in-laws and my wife is harmonious, and my parents can also help take care of the children.**

Previously, when we thought about raising children, we always imagined various training classes and school districts. Just thinking about it made us headache. We were often influenced by the idea that we shouldn't let our children fall behind at the starting line, and that we couldn't be poor in education.

But after taking the family education class, we realized that **the truly important things schools can't teach, and only parents can. We can think of schools as places for children to make friends, and the most important place for learning is still the family.**

Now we've finally understood. As parents, we still have a lot of confidence in being good role models for our children.

Next, let's talk about our thoughts on marital relationships and intergenerational relationships.

We believe that **marital relationships are dynamic, and both parties need to stay in sync to go far.**

For example, either both of us don't pursue progress much and just live happily every day, which is fine. Or both of us pursue progress, and we're on the path of mutual encouragement. We chose this path, but we're afraid that one of us keeps growing while the other stops, and over time, we'll have no common topics, which will greatly affect the marital relationship and eventually lead to separation.

I think my wife played a more critical role in this part, so I'll let her share this part with everyone.

**02**

**Reflections on Marital Relationships?**

**From a partner relationship to a warrior relationship of upgrading together**

Hello, I'm **Tony**. Regarding marital relationships, both of us very much agree with Li Xiaolai's view that **marital relationships are the most important part of family building, so we've been very careful in cultivating our close relationship.**

On May 13, 2022, **we held our first family meeting.** This was a significant milestone in our relationship upgrade.

Why do I say that? Before this, our relationship was just like that of an ordinary couple, spending days laughing and eating, not really thinking about the future, and rarely reflecting on the past.

Later, after listening to one of Li Xiaolai's classes, he mentioned that he had recently started a business, which referred to creating a family business.

After discussing with Han Xiao, we realized that we had no clear plans for the future, even if we did, they were very vague.

At that time, Han Xiao suggested that maybe we should start holding family meetings, like running a company, to manage our small family.

I immediately agreed, thinking it was a good idea and worth trying. Then we started our first family meeting.

**Holding family meetings is very ritualistic.** First, we choose an independent time, usually in the evening, and turn off our phones. We bring notebooks and pens, some small fruits and tea, and we even have a slogan. I think the slogan is great—it adds a sense of ceremony.

**Our family meetings are held once a quarter.** The main content is:

**1. Review the family rules and see if either of us violated them in this quarter.**

**2. Discuss the family contributions of this quarter:** mainly divided into **economic contributions** and **event-based contributions.** Each person will talk about their own contributions.

Since our financial situations are transparent and our accounts are relatively simple—currently, we have a cash account, a reserve fund account, and a regular investment account. The cash account is managed by each of us, the reserve fund account is managed by me, and the regular investment account is managed by my husband.

We list the changes in each account from the previous month, and also write down what each of us is responsible for.

Event-based contributions are about specific things. For example, in our first family meeting, Han Xiao mentioned that one of his contributions was helping Dingding and Xiaokui find cheap medicine to cure their gastritis.

Dingding and Xiaokui are our two Pomeranians. Usually, when one person is talking about their contributions, the other partner will acknowledge and thank them.

**3. Self-reflection: talk about what we did poorly in the past month.**

**4. Review the completion of this quarter's goals and set goals for the next quarter.**

Generally, our goals are divided into **economic goals** and **growth-oriented goals.**

Goals are set by ourselves. First, we set the goals for the quarter, then break them down into monthly targets.

For example, my goal for June is to invest a certain amount, increase the reserve fund account by a certain amount, read a book, design a new product, swim five times, etc. Finally, I express gratitude and expectations for the other person.

Here, we borrowed a concept from *Nonviolent Communication*, which suggests that before asking someone to do something, you should first thank them, as this increases their willingness to do it.

**Our family meetings have continued to this day, and the results have been very significant. First and foremost, our financial situation has greatly improved.** Currently, we are very satisfied with our financial situation, have no debts, and we've met all our set goals every month. Both of us are very goal-oriented people.

I gave birth in February this year, and I stayed in a postpartum center, but I'm an active person, so even during my month of postpartum, I set a goal for regular investments. My husband often drove me back to my studio to help with orders during that time.

Of course, this isn't a recommended way, as everyone's physical condition is different. I just want to remind all the ladies to rest well after giving birth.

While my husband is in sales, his job is relatively stress-free, but **because of the family meetings, he suddenly felt a lot of pressure, and we started to subtly compete with each other, seeing who could earn more**. Last year, despite being frequently pulled to my studio to help with orders, he still managed to win the second place in his team's annual sales. **He often says that his current achievements were forced out by my high pressure.**

Of course, **throughout this process, we encouraged each other**. We would continuously update each other on our progress toward our goals within a month. If one of us felt overwhelmed, we would first discuss solutions together. If that didn’t work, we allowed each other one chance to adjust our goals, and we would never discourage the other.

Secondly, **because of the family meetings, we also developed the habit of regularly recalling and recording each other's good deeds and expressing gratitude.** This made many small joys in life get recorded, and happiness became traceable.

**Our relationship has become even more solid.** After all, in this era, it's easy to find someone to live with, but it's truly rare to find a partner who can not only live with you but also grow together with you, facing challenges and stacking up buffs.

**03**

**Reflections on Intergenerational Relationships**

**I. Our Relationship with Our Child**

Regarding reflections on intergenerational relationships, first of all, **we both strongly agree with Li Xiaolai's idea that the marital relationship comes first, and the small family is more important than the big family.**

My child is now two and a half months old. At home, I usually call him by his nickname, An'an. Before I had the child, I used to call my husband "Baby." After the child was born, I still called my husband "Baby." Later, because my mother-in-law was around, I stopped calling him "Baby" and started calling him "Abao," which means the same as "Baby," just to avoid making my mother-in-law uncomfortable. I won't change the nickname I use for my husband just because I have a child. **We both clearly understand that each other is the most important person in our lives.**

As for the child, I think the most important things are:

**1. Treat the child as an independent individual and respect them.**

I think this is a common lack among many Chinese parents. As new parents, **we don’t know if we can truly do this, but we will try our best.**

**2. Set an example and be a good role model for the child.**

For example, this year I signed up for a piano class. Every time I went to class, I took notes seriously, and the teacher even praised me, saying, **"I haven’t seen such a focused adult student in a long time."**

Last year I bought a 3D printer and started learning 3D modeling this year. **I hope to show my child that learning never stops and that people of any age should keep learning.**

**II. Our Relationship with Our Parents**

Since my mother-in-law has been helping me take care of the child, my husband and I had already discussed how to get along with our parents long before. I think the most important thing is:

**1. Be grateful and express thanks often.**

In fact, in China, conflicts between daughters-in-law and mothers-in-law are quite common. Many daughters-in-law take it for granted that their mothers-in-law help them raise the child, but **I always believe that having a child is the couple's responsibility, and raising the child is also the couple's responsibility.**

**The fact that the mother-in-law helps out is a great favor that should be remembered. Expressing gratitude to elders is a very effective way to get along well.** I often express my thanks to my mother-in-law, sometimes verbally, sometimes in the family group chat, and sometimes on my social media. The elderly really appreciate it.

**2. Be tolerant and communicate effectively.**

Because of different backgrounds and environments, we often have differences in thoughts and habits with our elders. I think **the first thing is to give up the idea of changing the other person.** Tolerance is important, and as long as it's not a matter of principle, it's acceptable. **If you really can't stand it, just communicate well.**

Here are two books I recommend: **one is "How to Win Friends and Influence People" by Carnegie, and the other is "Nonviolent Communication."**

These two books have helped me a lot. I read "How to Win Friends and Influence People" three times and "Nonviolent Communication" twice, plus its practical version. **They have made me a better person and made those around me more willing to be around me.**

**04**

**How to Grow Together with Family?**

Finally, regarding how to motivate your family to grow with you, I have some reflections. When Han Xiaoxue joined the fixed-investment class, I was actually against it at first.

At that time, I thought it was weird, spending money to join a group, and it cost several thousand. But later, I changed my mind.

Why? Because for a while, after dinner, we would go to the study room together. He started listening to Li Xiaolai's classes, while I was making crafts. **Since he had the phone on speaker, I could also hear it.**

**I listened a few times and thought, "Li Xiaolai actually makes a lot of sense."** So I started to change my mind.

Then, **Han Xiaoxue's transformation** caught my eye. After joining the group, he started **doing push-ups every day.** At that time, he was very consistent, and his chest muscles grew a lot.

He also started **reading before bed.** Although he often only read half of the book, **the habit of reading before bed lasted for a long time.**

Later, I noticed that he had improved. Why do I say that? Because before, when we talked, I was usually the one guiding the conversation, and he just nodded and said "uh-huh." But later, I noticed that he started to have his own opinions. Sometimes, he even started to argue with me, and his arguments were reasonable.

In addition, **he would tell me every day about a great teammate in his fixed-investment group,** describing what he did all day long.

Considering all these factors, I also changed.

From being forced to listen, to listening carefully, and then to actively asking him if he had listened to Li Xiaolai's latest class.

So, **I think the key to motivating family members to learn is to let them continuously see your change and growth.**

**People naturally tend to be drawn to those who are excellent. When they see their partner or family members becoming increasingly excellent, they will also want to change themselves.**

This process may take a long time—maybe half a year, maybe a year—but believe me, as long as you keep going and change, you will gain a lifelong growth partner. If it doesn't work, it just means you haven't been consistent long enough.

So, regarding **how to motivate family members to learn together?**

**First point:** **Keep becoming better, and influence your family through your growth.**

**Second point:** If you haven't married yet, my advice is to choose your partner carefully.

Li Xiaolai said that when choosing a partner, you should choose someone who can reason. I think this standard might not be specific enough. When I chose my partner, my standards were more specific.

For example, **I hope my partner has a habit of reading books**, because people who read are usually more growth-oriented.

Secondly, **I hope my partner has a habit of fitness and exercise**, and I hope they are kind and down-to-earth.

How to judge if someone is kind? You can **see how they treat small animals.**

For example, when I was dating Han Xiaoxue for a while, we adopted a dog named Dingding. Soon after we got him, he got sick and needed to be taken care of several times at night, feeding him glucose because Poodles are prone to low blood sugar when they're young.

I noticed that **Han Xiaoxue was very patient and caring during this process.** How to judge if someone is down-to-earth? **Look whether what they say matches what they do.**

When I was dating Han Xiaoxue for a while, I asked him, "Why do you like being with me?" He honestly said, "Because I think life will be easier with you. I think you're very excellent."

Honestly, my first reaction was disappointment. Why shouldn't I say, "I love you," or "I liked you the moment I saw you," or "Love doesn't need a reason" like that? Later, I thought, his answer showed that he was a straightforward person, not like some men who are full of flattery.

**Third point:** **Take your partner or family members to participate in online or offline community activities, using the group atmosphere to motivate them.**

**Fourth point:** Assume the other person is willing to learn and loves learning. Don't label them as someone who doesn't like learning.

You assume they are a certain way, and you express that in your daily life, and they are likely to become that person. This is a very important point from "How to Win Friends and Influence People."

Finally, **I hope everyone keeps up the good work and creates a beautiful family life together!**
