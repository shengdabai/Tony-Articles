# Questioning Is an Excellent Way of Thinking

Let me tell a story. One day, right at the beginning of class, the teacher asked the students to study an absurd question: Did Hitler bring any benefits to the German people during his rule? We've all been taught that Hitler brought disaster to the world, especially to the Germans. But the first step of independent thinking is to dare to discard existing conclusions and assumptions, and to seek the facts themselves.

Soon someone asked: Who counts as the German people? Do people without German citizenship, like Jews, count? In the end, the class decided that anyone living in Germany counts as a German. Then the children noticed a problem: Hitler always claimed to be serving the German people, but he killed many of them. Look, the second step of independent thinking is to define things clearly. Once the definition is clear, it's harder to be led around by others.

At this point, someone raised another fact: After Hitler came to power, he vigorously developed the military industry, which solved the serious unemployment problem. Isn't that a benefit? But when the children compared the materials, they found that young men were forced to fight, while the elderly and women were forced to work, without union rights. They were treated like slaves. See, by first thinking in reverse and then comparing pros and cons, you can deepen your understanding of things.

After the debate, the children finally reached a consensus: Hitler did not bring any benefits to the German people. This knowledge is worth hundreds of times more than rote memorization. Being brave enough to break through assumptions, clearly defining problems, being good at hypothetical thinking, and comparing facts from both sides—this is independent thinking!

> Questioning is just the first step of independent thinking. How do we think independently? There are four steps: ask questions, search for information, analyze and reason, and express your views. To complete this process well, you need the following abilities: an open-minded skepticism, analytical skills, communication and expression abilities, research and investigative skills, tolerance for ambiguity, and creative problem-solving.

> 1. Ask the right questions in the right way
> 2. Search for information
> 3. Analytical reasoning skills
> 4. Communication and expression skills

Independent thinking is even difficult to put into practice, even if we know what it is. "To learn broadly, to question earnestly, to think carefully, to discern clearly, and to act with determination." These five aspects of learning, whether it's learning book knowledge or a skill, require repeated practice to master. "If you don't learn, don't learn at all; if you do learn, you must learn it well. If you learn and still don't understand, don't give up." This passage is not for geniuses, but for ordinary people. Smart people can learn it once, but you need to learn it a hundred times. Smart people can learn it ten times, but you need to learn it a thousand times. As long as you have this perseverance, even if you start off slow or weak, you will eventually become smart and strong. Keep learning, keep trying, and build a bridge between learning and practice. Use independent thinking to help us better understand and interpret the world, and grow together with the community!
