**Feeling & Thinking: Sensation & Thought**

This is an era where people easily get addicted, such as to videos. We see all kinds of videos, from long TV series, to slightly longer movies, to the globally popular short videos and live streaming, which are new internet formats. These activities are more addictive because they are not just ordinary interactions; they are designed with the deliberate intent to minimize all factors that could produce negative feedback. Basically, there is little to no negative feedback. On short video platforms, you just need to swipe your finger—yes, that's all it takes—and there are various operations that keep you immersed, like liking, sending gifts, commenting, and interacting. This immersive experience is so good that we are completely unaware of it. Li Xiaolai has always told us that the most scarce and important resource is our attention, and isn't technology and trends now constantly taking away our attention and keeping us immersed?

> Here, I want to clarify what addiction is. It's when you know a behavior will have negative effects, yet you still can't stop, repeatedly forcing yourself to do it, and developing a strong dependency on it. — From Zhuoke Teacher

On the contrary, what activities give us a lot of negative feedback? Reading, exercising... For example, after overexercising, the nerves and muscles will give signals of light to heavy pain and soreness, reminding us to stop. Once those actions reach a certain point, you can no longer derive pleasure from them, so you stop. Although you might still want to continue in your mind, you can only wait for a while, until you recover, and then start again. And it's precisely during that stoppage that the concentration of reward substances in the brain returns to normal levels, making addiction less likely. Li Xiaolai defines the core values of our practice group: reading, fitness, investing, spending time with family, and helping friends. In fact, each of these requires independent thinking, doesn't it? When we can focus on these activities, even though the process may involve negative feedback and various resistances, and we keep doing mental fitness, we are still growing, thinking, and moving toward the future, aren't we?

Why do we need the ability to think independently? Three directions:

> Personality Development, Seeking Knowledge, Forming More Accurate Opinions

In terms of personality development, independent thinking is the foundation. If we just follow the crowd, how can we have a unique personality? In this era of sensation-driven culture, we need to regulate and control our sensations, not get swept away by the tide. Building a personal IP was the most important part for me when doing WeChat Channels. The reason is simple: why would someone follow you? Because you create value for them, and it's useful to them. If I always do what's trending, copy what's popular, and follow the latest hits, why would anyone follow me? In the early stages, many people suggested finding a similar WeChat Channel to learn from and imitate, which is the simplest way to go viral. But I thought about long-termism. I considered Li Xiaolai's advice to business people: **CLV—Customer Lifetime Value**, the long-term value provided to users. We can't do anything that harms this value. I'm not good at being funny or attracting attention; I prefer to genuinely provide value. Eventually, I believe people will trust me, and I can run my business well. I should also mention a partner, whose nickname is Haohao. His WeChat Channel is just one thing: push-ups. Many members of our practice group may have seen his videos. He does one thing to the extreme, regardless of likes or comments, using videos to record his growth. Let's cheer for Haohao—he's a real powerhouse!

In terms of seeking knowledge, independent thinking is even more essential. In yesterday's class, the former Harvard president summarized three stages: the certainty of ignorance, the confusion of knowledge, and independent thinking. Struggling through the confusion of knowledge, striving toward independent thinking. Here's a question a student asked Professor Liu Qing, along with an image:

> Professor Liu, hello. I have a question. There's this picture you've definitely seen. A person has read a few books, and he looks out the window from the books he's read, seeing a very beautiful but obviously fake picture. The second person has many books, and from the books he's read, he sees a dark picture. The third picture shows someone who has read a lot of books and finally sees the sunrise, symbolizing hope. You've also said that there's no answer to the question of the meaning of life. So I have a question: if there's a person with these characteristics—first, he's just reached the second stage of reading, having a vague contemplation about the meaning of life, and second, his capacity for thinking is limited and can only go so far—then in a certain sense, is this person less happy than the person in the first stage of reading? If so, what's the point of thinking at all? If not, why is this person still so painful?

The image above suggests there are three levels or stages of reading: the first level is having read little or nothing, the second is reading a limited number of books, and the third is reading a lot. Is this depiction of the real situation accurate? Actually, it's not. It's hard to distinguish these three stages. The process of learning to read is both bitter and sweet, embedded in every aspect of our lives. Are our choices only between the painful Socrates and the happy pig? Of course not. Why not be the happy Socrates? Some people might think this is just inspirational fluff. When we can think independently, we can have more choices, not be confined to black-and-white dichotomies, and pursue a more complete personality, right? In the process of seeking knowledge, through thinking, we constantly improve ourselves. Our attitude toward knowledge is cautious—neither blindly believing nor being overly critical. Using existing mental models to understand and rationally analyzing is also a joyful experience, isn't it?

The final point is forming correct opinions. When facing unfamiliar situations and unfamiliar things, you need to think independently. That was the last year of university, when my grandfather's health deteriorated and he was diagnosed with late-stage stomach cancer. I was completely lost, went to the best cancer hospital, booked the best doctor's appointment, and traveled to Beijing and Shanghai, visiting all the major hospitals. It was really blind, and I was truly blind. Calming down and thinking carefully, we realized it was already late-stage. What could we do? We decided to cooperate with the treatment, choosing the most suitable plan for my grandfather, considering his physical condition and the conservative nature of the treatment. We chose chemotherapy, once a month, each session lasting a week. The last year of university, I spent with my grandfather, while my parents were working elsewhere. All I could do was earn more money so I could take good care of him and spend quality time with him. In the final stages, all I could do was be there, taking him on the high-speed train, making him happy like a child, going to the Huangpu River, taking photos under the Oriental Pearl Tower, and exploring Shanghai together. Being there was the only thing I could do. The most painful part was the last semester of university, when I was busy finishing my thesis and couldn't go back to be with my grandfather for his final moments. When I returned home, I only found his cold body. I don't know if the choices I made were right. When the whole family trusted me, I had to think carefully and make a choice. May Grandpa rest in peace in heaven!

The *Zhongyong* from the Warring States period required scholars to "study broadly, ask questions carefully, think carefully, discern clearly, and act firmly." However, many scholars never even reached the first stage of "studying broadly," and were just called "studying." Our practice group members learn from each other, grow together, and accumulate knowledge, looking forward to our transformation!
