Recently, I've seen many such questions in the community: What should we do when children are addicted to mobile phones? What should we do when children are addicted to games?

Even some parents express deep concern: A child around 12 or 13 years old suddenly becomes obsessed with mobile games, then locks himself in his room, even to the point of not going to school anymore, and is very distressed.

To be honest, if it has already reached this point, where he only plays games, doesn't eat properly, and even says he doesn't want to go to school anymore, it's really very difficult to handle.

But for most parents, this is indeed a serious issue.

**You need to understand from the beginning that such problems must be addressed through prevention.**

You can tell by observing that children who are attracted to games to such an extent usually have two common characteristics.

**First, they don't have many interests or hobbies, and they don't have many things that can captivate them beyond that.**

**Second, they communicate very little with their family members.**

Generally, when we inquire about such families, we find that parents and children don't talk much, and the frequency of communication is also very low. It's even possible that a year or two ago, the child stopped listening to his parents and no longer likes to talk to them.

At the same time, we also find that in such families, communication between parents is also very limited, and the overall communication in the family is very scarce. These are common underlying phenomena, which we'll discuss in more detail later.

So how can we prevent this, or if it has already happened, how can we correct it?

**01**

**Time, money, and attention are all exclusive resources**

First, as parents, we need to understand this point and convey it to our children as early as possible.

**The most valuable resource in our lives is not money, but time, because you can't save time like you can money.**

You can save money and use it later, but time is gone once it's passed, and you can't physically save it for future use. Therefore, **wasting time is far more dangerous than wasting money.**

Time is greater than money, and on top of that, there's another thing that's even more valuable: your **attention.**

**Where your attention is placed, that's where it creates value.**

If your energy is spent on things that don't create value, then your energy is completely wasted, right?

This is also one of the most fundamental concepts I've shared repeatedly in *The Road to Financial Freedom*: **From a value perspective, attention is greater than time, which is greater than money.**

We not only need to cherish time, but more importantly, we need to cherish our attention.

In good family education, we repeatedly emphasize a fact: **Our family education is based on production.**

Why do we build family education around production?

Because from the moment we are born, we are destined to be consumers for life. We are consumption animals, and we have to consume in various ways every day. Therefore, **in order to one day be self-sufficient, we must learn to produce.**

You can only gain benefits by producing. One day, when your income exceeds your consumption, you can be self-sufficient, and you become independent. **If you don't produce, you will always be a poor person.**

I think this is the case. When you talk to your child about this issue, no matter when or which child it is, they will naturally express to you that they don't want to be a poor person.

Ask them, do you want to live a poor life forever? Do you want to be a poor person forever?

I don't think you'll find a child who says, "Okay, I'm poor, so what?" So if everyone understands that they are consumption animals and must produce, they will pay attention to production.

Therefore, we need to instill in them this concept: **Your valuable things include money and time, but the most valuable is your attention.**

Where should this attention be placed to be most meaningful?

Placing it on things that have no productive value is clearly a waste, right?

So it's better to instill these ideas in them as early as possible. If you wait until he's already fallen into it, he'll come up with a thousand reasons to ignore you, which is very dangerous.

We should also note another thing: **During the growth of your child, the outside world will have many black hole-like things that attract his attention, his time, and even his money.**

We call them black holes or temptations, but the most effective way to deal with them is only one: **prevention.**

Attention is the most valuable thing. After you've conveyed this, the next step is to let them understand from the beginning that **money, time, and the most valuable attention are all exclusive resources. You can use them for one thing, not for another.**

Your attention used for gaming can't be used for learning or production.

Production is achieved through learning, because every form of production and every production process requires learning. We can't inherit the process of production from our mothers' wombs, so **learning is actually 99% of the composition of production.** Therefore, your attention placed on learning is more worthwhile than placing it on gaming.

But the core issue is, if you place it on gaming, you can't place it on that other thing, right?

**02**

**Every time you make a choice, you pay an opportunity cost**

Time, money, and attention are all exclusive resources. On one hand, you need to instill this concept in your child.

On the other hand, you can also use this principle to teach your child **to reshape their behavior.**

For example, in our other community, "The Fixed Investment Classroom," we talk about fixed investment strategies, which is an investment strategy.

What is the core essence of investment?

**Using money to make more money.**

So when I first started teaching, I conveyed to everyone a concept: **Money has two uses in the world: one is for consumption, and the other is for investment.**

Everyone understands that money can be used for consumption, but almost 99% of people only know about this one use.

In daily life, you often hear people say things like, "When I have money, I'll..." followed by a list of consumption actions, such as buying this and that, playing this and that, traveling, and taking vacations.

When they imagine having money, the only thing they can do is consume.

But money has a second, more important use: using money to make money. However, most people don't notice this, and even if they do, most people don't know how to do it.

In the "Fixed Investment Classroom," we teach people how to reshape their investment behavior.

After some time, many students have this feedback: Now looking back, I realize I used to spend money too freely. At that time, I only knew that money could be used for consumption, so when I had money, I had to spend it, otherwise I wouldn't be happy. But now, since I know that money can be used for investment, I suddenly don't want to spend it anymore.

This is the experience of many students in our class.

We don't advocate excessive frugality. In fact, I often share with my students: Over the years, Li Xiaolai hasn't been the type of person who is overly frugal with money. When I buy books, I don't hesitate to spend money. When I invite friends to dinner, I don't hesitate. When I take my wife on a trip, I spend the money, and if I spend all my salary, it's okay, right?

But I do place a lot of importance on investment. And **as we become better at investing, you will find that your desire to consume is naturally restrained.**

The reason is that money is **an exclusive resource.** You can use it for one thing, not for another. Then you have to choose, and every time you make a choice, you pay an **opportunity cost.** When you realize that opportunity cost exists, you will be more determined to make another choice, right?

**03**

**Find ways to fight, to resist the attraction of games**

When children are consumed by mobile games, short videos, or social software, you need to understand that trying to save them by scolding, yelling, or sending them to a detox center is ineffective.

The reason for addiction is that **children's time and energy have no better use.** That's why this phenomenon occurs.

So if you want to become a more frugal person, you need to learn to invest.

Trying to become frugal by restraining your desires can make you feel abnormal, but if you learn to invest, you naturally don't spend money as much, and you become healthier.

Let me share my personal experience.

In fact, the electronic games today's children play are not new. I was born in 1972, and I'm almost 51 now. When I was growing up, electronic games had already appeared. I can say I deeply understand the attraction of electronic games.

When I was a child, we started with arcade games. The games we played back then were so simple that today's children would laugh at them. It was a screen with a box, many bricks, a ball bouncing around, and a paddle below. You had to use a game controller to control it. For arcade games, it was a joystick and buttons, and you just had to use the paddle to catch the ball. Sometimes the ball came so fast that it would drop, and you'd lose.

That was a simple game, and we used to play it all afternoon.

Later, game arcades appeared, with more arcade machines. There were fighting games like *Street Fighter*, and even gambling games like running machines.

Then, every household started buying home consoles, which were smuggled in from abroad. You thought they were Japanese Nintendo games? Later, domestic companies produced many knockoffs, the most famous being the Xiao Bawang Learning Machine. Inside it was *Tetris*, which we played all day. Not only did we play it, but our parents also joined in. *Super Mario* and *Contra* were also time-consuming.

Later, PCs appeared. When PCs came out, I was almost graduating from university. I became obsessed with *Tomb Raider* in 1996. At that time, the graphics card was called 9680, which was quite expensive. I saved a little money for it.

There were also games like *Age of Empires* and many others we played. So you can understand how attractive these games are.

First, I want to express my view to today's parents: **Your child playing games doesn't necessarily mean they will be completely destroyed.**

Childhood should have some fun. Maybe my previous generation didn't have electronic games, but I guess they spent a lot of time on other games.

Looking back, I also played games, but why didn't I get addicted? Why didn't I get hooked on games?

I was lucky. When I was around 13 or 14, the Youth Palace in Yanji City, Yanbian Korean Autonomous Prefecture, had a teacher named Jin who started the first youth programming class in BASIC programming language. After I found out, I asked my mom for 10 yuan.

10 yuan in 1984 was a lot. My mom didn't hesitate and said, "You ask your dad for it. The money is with him."

That night, my dad came home, and I was already asleep. The next morning, when everyone was at work, I had 10 yuan on my pillow. I took it and went to the Youth Palace to attend the class.

That's how I learned the basics of BASIC programming and became very interested. I spent a lot of time reading programming books or waiting for computer time.

So in fact, I played electronic games earlier than most of my peers. Before arcades appeared, I had already played many games on rare computers, even spending whole days playing small turtles with LOGO language.

Think about it, programming is an interesting thing, but it's also time-consuming and labor-intensive. Plus, back then, there weren't many computers, and several people shared one. So there was only a little computer time each day. The rest of the time, you had to write programs on paper with a pen and simulate the results in your mind. When it was your turn to use the computer, you'd quickly type it in. That's how my attention was taken away.

Years later, when my classmates were playing home consoles, I had other interests.

At that time, I learned to play the guitar, but I wasn't very good at it. Because I wasn't good, I practiced longer than others. A small piece of music, others could learn in two or three tries, but I had to practice for a whole week, practicing two or three hours a day. Some pieces were actually nice to listen to, even if you played it wrong, it still sounded good. So my so-called "practice" was just self-indulgence.

Look, I have many things in my life that can make me immersed and obsessed. I can already count at least two: programming, guitar, and I also love reading. In a sense, **I don't have much time to be consumed by games.**

**This is a process of resistance.**

In life, there are many such examples. I have a colleague whose child once begged her to buy a good graphics card. Her family had good financial conditions, and it wasn't something they couldn't afford. But her mother knew that buying a graphics card was for gaming, so she was worried and asked me what to do.

I said, "It's simple. Not only buy it for him, but buy the best one, but have him pick it up from my place."

So she bought an NVIDIA 3090, a pretty good graphics card, costing over 10,000 yuan.

When the child came to my place, he asked me, "What graphics card do you use?"

I said, "I use the same one."

The child was surprised, "I saw you using Macs."

I said, "Yes, I don't use that graphics card for gaming."

He asked, "Then what do you use it for?"

At that time, I had placed the graphics card in a server in the laundry room at home. A friend of mine, Huo Ju, helped me set it up remotely from Canada, using it for AI painting.

At that time, he deployed Disco Diffusion, and I showed him how we used Disco Diffusion to paint.

He played with me all afternoon, constantly exclaiming, "Wow! This is more interesting."

Look, time, attention, and money are all the same. They are all exclusive resources. You can use them for one thing, not for another. If you're interested in one, you won't be interested in or have time for the other. Your attention is limited, right?

So in the following period, the questions he asked me were no longer about how to set up a gaming rig. He started asking me how to configure it.

So a middle school student, in less than two weeks, learned most UNIX and Linux commands, and spent his days happily playing with them.

Look, the same amount of money, the same good graphics card, some people just played games, while he learned something new and became a producer, right?

So it's the same principle. In daily life, the application of exclusive resources is very common.

When we were growing up, we also experienced another thing. At that time, computers were a new and novel thing, and some parents saw them as a flood of evil, because computers could be used for gaming. Other parents saw them as good things, because they could be used for learning programming.

But I didn't fall into the same trap. When I got a computer, I also played games. At that time, the main things you could do with a personal computer were two: one was gaming, and the other was watching movies with a DVD.

The internet wasn't as developed then, so for a long time, computers didn't create much value.

However, one day, when I started writing articles, which was after 2005, I realized I didn't play games anymore, and I didn't use the computer to watch movies anymore, because I needed it to write articles, even to write books.

So you see, the concept of exclusive resources is something you should instill in your child as early as possible.

At the same time, **you also need to use it in your daily life to adjust your behavior, so that your child can see you doing it. When he may be interested in mobile games, mobile videos, or social software on his phone, you need to actively respond, and do everything you can to occupy his attention and time. It's a battle.**

**04**

**The real world has always had various temptations**

Let me expand a bit more on this topic and go deeper into it.

Today's parents are more worried, and some even see their children falling into mobile games and short videos. About ten years ago, there was another group of parents fighting against this, but then it wasn't phones—it was computers.

At that time, many parents painfully discovered that their children had what was called "internet addiction," and they didn't want to do anything else except surf the internet.

But please ask, what's the big difference between this internet addiction and today's phone addiction?

I'll ask you another question: Did parents twenty, thirty, or fifty years ago face the problem of their children getting addicted to the internet? Of course, they didn't have the problem of phones either, right?

Did their children have no problems?

Not at all. The same problems still existed.

So what were their parents fighting against?

There were karaoke bars, game arcades, billiard halls, and discos. There were also the things that parents of that time were very afraid of—early romance.

Now, please ask, did parents a hundred years ago have no worries?

Still, there were problems. There were no discos, no billiard halls, no video rental shops, and the concept of early romance didn't even exist back then. But there were brothels, casinos, and opium dens.

**You shouldn't mistakenly think that children being addicted to phones is a special problem of this era. In the real world, there have always been various temptations.**

Every family's construction process faces a problem: **the internal cohesion of your family is fighting against the external attraction. Once you fail, he will be pulled away by the outside.**

External attractions can be good, but they can also be traps, like black holes.

So this is a battle, but the specific details of these battles have been constantly developing and changing over time.

So the core issue is not the phone, games, or social software.

Hating the companies is useless, and hating the short video platforms is also useless. They are one of the normal parts of the world. From the beginning, you need to understand that **the earlier you instill your child with the real phenomena of the real world, the better.**

However, many parents only take the approach of "covering their eyes."

I have a colleague who is quite old. When we visited her home, we were all sitting watching TV, and there was a scene of a man and woman kissing. Her mother naturally came over and covered her eyes.

Not kidding, this has always been the case in her family, and we just laughed. You cover her eyes, and she won't know, right?

So our traditional family education has a problem: we tend to block out the bad things.

Simply blocking out the bad things is not enough. **You must show them and tell them about the real phenomena of the world.**

There is a specific category of education closely related to safety education, and it also belongs to safety education.

**From a young age, you should tell your child that there are things in this world that are easy to become addicted to.**

Besides studying, almost everything that is easy to become addicted to is bad, especially things like cigarettes, alcohol, sugar, tea, computers, and games.

Note that not all games are bad. Sometimes we play football, which is also called a game, and these games are good for physical health. But electronic games have no benefits. You can treat them as appropriate entertainment, but they consume your energy, and their design is such that you don't progress. **So from the beginning, you should tell your child to stay away from these things that are easy to become addicted to, and definitely don't touch them. Even if you do touch them, you should remind yourself to get out of it as soon as possible.**

**05**

**Start early with "harm education"**

To be honest, facing these mobile games and short videos, adults are very helpless.

I once lamented: **In the history of humanity, there have never been so many companies with so much money, using the most advanced psychological principles to design products that are so easy to become addicted to, without any moral burden.**

Many of the content on today's short video platforms, many social software, many electronic games, and mobile games are actually the advanced versions of past drugs.

So from the beginning, you need to educate them not to use them. This kind of education is relatively easy to do.

Before they even start to be interested in something, you need to educate them **with "harm education."**

Let me give you another example, slightly detouring, to explain how to prevent this.

Do you know that new drivers tend to speed when they first get behind the wheel?

At first, they are afraid, they don't dare to press the gas, don't dare to overtake, and even don't dare to change lanes. But after some time of becoming familiar, they gradually become more confident. Then they start to overtake recklessly, and even unintentionally ignore traffic rules, and they start to speed, even doing some dangerous actions.

Yes, that's the case. Do you know how Li Xiaolai treats himself?

My method is simple. I go to video websites and specifically search for the word "car accident." There are flipped cars, side-flipped cars, and various situations of falling from heights.

For a period of time, I spent an afternoon watching this, and then a few days later, I watched another afternoon, and after watching it a couple of times, I no longer drove fast. My driving speed was very slow, I didn't change lanes if I didn't have to, and I always obeyed traffic rules.

So you need to know that **the outside world is full of attractions, and your family's internal attraction needs to fight against it.**

To win against the external attraction, especially things like mobile games and mobile videos, you should search for a lot of cases of children whose families have broken down because of these things, and search for a lot of news, and read them to your child before he gets addicted.

I tell you, this kind of prevention is very effective.

**06**

**Build your family's centripetal force**

Then let's talk about something more fundamental: **One day, your family education and family construction will form an attraction, and eventually face external attractions.**

Not only are there mobile games, but there are also many things parents worry about, such as early romance, and even a group of friends who want to try marijuana.

Many parents who send their children to study abroad in the U.S. find that their children have tried marijuana.

What should we do?

You might blame the friends, saying they corrupted him, right?

That kind of blame may seem reasonable on the surface, but the deeper reason is: **Your family's centripetal force is not strong enough.**

Children who are addicted to the internet or mobile phones usually have two very obvious characteristics: first, they have no other interests or hobbies, and second, the communication time between family members is extremely low, almost nonexistent.

So on one hand, you need to prevent this from happening, and on the other hand, this is a long-term effort.

Many parents don't consider parenting to be a job, and I want to correct this misunderstanding.

In good family education, we repeatedly emphasize one thing: **The first object of family education is the parents, and then the child.**

You need to have a clear understanding of family education and family construction: **It doesn't happen automatically, it doesn't maintain itself automatically, it requires your effort.**

**07**

**Being a parent is actually a job**

**Being a parent is actually a job, and it's the most important job, a job you can't resign from.**

It has no salary, and you have to invest money into it, but you should think of it as a job, not as something that will happen naturally.

What's the further change in mindset?

**You don't even have to think of it as a job. Just remember that you are the boss of the family, or the one who speaks for the family, and the other is a partner.**

You need to understand that your role must first change, and then you will naturally spend more time and effort on the most basic tasks.

For example, **being communicative**. You need to do everything you can to communicate with your child, and you need to become an interesting person so that he is willing to talk to you. You need to become a **problem-solving person**, so that he is willing to talk to you.

Why? Because you can solve his problems. If you can't solve his problems, he will talk to you twice and then never talk to you again.

I tell you, communication between family members and the connection between family members is extremely important. **The fundamental reason why family members are attracted by external attractions is that the connection between family members is too weak.** You can't blame them.

Game companies are not good things, but they do have an attraction. Your child is attracted, because your attraction is not enough. So **you need to do everything you can to build the centripetal force of your family.**

Of course, centrip and force also includes many things: your **marital relationship** should be good, you should be **good at communicating with your child**, and you should be **willing to communicate with your child**, and you should **intentionally increase the time and scope of communication with your child**, right?

These are things you, as the boss or partner of the family, need to do. It's a job you can't resign from.

Since it's a job, we need to do it well.

**08**

**Family construction won't happen automatically, you need to fight forever**

Most parents have already realized that their children are addicted to mobile phones, which indicates that **in the past, they didn't put in enough effort, they thought their children would grow up automatically, they thought family education would happen automatically, and they thought family construction would complete automatically.**

So when this bad thing happens, it's mostly the parents' problem. And since this situation doesn't happen overnight, you need to spend more time to make up for it.

Of course, there are also many superficial solutions. For example, some members of our community say they solved it this way: my child plays games, and I play games with him. He stays up late, and I stay up late with him. He can stay up until a certain time, and I stay up until that time. In the morning, I don't get up to make breakfast for him because I also stayed up late and couldn't get up. After two times, my child stopped playing games because he was afraid his mother wouldn't be serious about her job.

That's a kind of strategy of wits and courage, and it's quite good.

But more importantly, you need to understand the underlying mechanisms of this phenomenon. The core is that you **do some preventive work**, such as telling him **exclusive resources** early on, teaching him to **apply this principle** early on, and trying to **develop his interests** and so on.

You also need to understand the deep principle behind this: **Your family's centripetal force is the key.**

The outside world always has various attractions. In the past, it was phones; before that, it was the internet; before that, it was discos and video rental shops; before that, there were brothels, casinos, and opium dens. Before that, we don't know what else there might be.

In the future, when your child grows up and raises his own children, you will find there are even more things, such as brain-computer interfaces.

**So from the beginning, you should treat things related to addiction as part of safety education and tell your child about them.**

At the same time, you need to build your **family's centripetal force.**

Finally, my biggest suggestion is: **As a parent, you need to understand one thing: being a parent is a job, and it's a job you can't resign from, a job with no salary. You should see yourself as the boss.**

Family education won't happen automatically, problems won't be solved automatically, and external temptations will always exist, so you need to fight forever.
