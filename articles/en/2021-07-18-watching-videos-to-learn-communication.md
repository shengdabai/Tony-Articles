Observing others' communication skills, learning from those who are good at expressing themselves, and borrowing their wisdom—what comes to mind first for most of us is probably Uncle Xiao DaYe's WeChat Channels. I'm ashamed to admit that I only started making WeChat Channels in February this year, and it was only then that I got to know Uncle Xiao DaYe. The first video I watched was titled "Mutual Support in Times of Need," and it immediately moved me with its sincerity. Uncle Xiao DaYe uses warmth and love to create a wave of tenderness within WeChat Channels. There's no need to overly focus on commercial performance or be too fixated on video production; the core lies in conveying words and emotions. In the first lesson of communication, Uncle Xiao DaYe gets straight to the point: the essence of communication is to convey love and intention. With a big heart, a broad vision, and an eye on the future, communication becomes meaningful. What Uncle Xiao DaYe teaches us are things that can be done for a long time and are truly important—this isn't it exactly our values?

Uncle Xiao DaYe gave us many cards in his communication training camp, each one carrying significant weight. Using one well can transform your communication. Among them, one card I understand to be of the foundational level: awareness. We need to be aware of ourselves and others, to live with awareness, to pay attention to others' feelings, to understand the differences and nuances between us, and to feel the warmth of our relationship. Today's assignment is a great exercise in cultivating awareness, and more accurately, it's a skill that can be practiced and refined over time. Breaking down WeChat Channels, analyzing the creators, and refining the copy are essential skills for making WeChat Channels. Today's practice made me think about communication from a new angle, and now I'll summarize my journey of learning through videos!

These are the WeChat Channels I've watched the most. I haven't watched much on Douyin or Kuaishou. WeChat Channels sparked my interest in short videos. Here, I'll break down a video from "WeChat Channels: Little Palace Guide." This video has 7,092 likes, and its title is: "Following Little Palace Guide Ink Ink, Learning the Essence of Palace Museum Culture, Exploring the Secrets of the Forbidden City!"

The child's voice gave me a warm sense of strength, and I felt her sincerity. We call her Ink Ink. In this video, she shared her journey—from being afraid to speak and unwilling to express herself to becoming a WeChat Channels creator, sharing her understanding of the Forbidden City. Through her perspective, she opens up our interest in Palace Museum culture. The essence of communication is to convey love and intention. The child conveys her love for the Palace Museum and shows her growth through WeChat Channels. We may have some knowledge about the Palace Museum, but it's definitely not extensive. This requires experience, research, and reflection, as well as gathering a lot of information. Why do we not like watching documentaries or listening to experts introduce the Palace Museum? I think it's due to the difference in the speaker's perspective. Experts need to express from a professional angle, passing on detailed and accurate knowledge to us, which is a respect for history and a promotion of archaeological research. Why is Ink Ink's video more appealing to us? It's because it's down-to-earth and easy to understand. She conveys her understanding through a child's perspective. It's interesting, informative, and resonates with us. We prefer stories and are more willing to gain information through them.

Another reason is that it inspired my younger brother to see the power of role models. After returning home during the pandemic, I've thought more about one-on-one education. As a teacher, we face a group of students, so our focus is on the collective, and we also need one-on-one communication, but it's still selective. For each family, this is essential, and it's something I'm exploring. Growing together with our children, advancing together, not just about what you should do. I read with my younger brother, communicate with him, and express ourselves freely in front of familiar people. However, he's still shy around strangers. Ink Ink encouraged him, and now he can express himself, convey his feelings. He started writing, posting on QQ Space, and communicating in his own way. I'm really happy for him. Growing together, supporting each other, communicating sincerely!
