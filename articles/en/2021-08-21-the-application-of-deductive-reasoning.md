**Question 1: Thinking about the first principles of this business, and proposing directions for improvement?**

1. Is this scenario suitable for deductive reasoning? Why?

2. What do you think is the first principle of sales?

3. What are the key stages that determine sales volume? Is call volume the most important performance metric?

4. Which stage is the most critical in realizing the "first principle"?

5. What improvement suggestions do you have for this stage?

Based on the above thoughts, I have the following reflections:

1. I believe this scenario can be approached using deductive reasoning. Deductive reasoning requires a foundational assumption, and the case of telemarketing involves identifying the basic components of sales performance, which is also what the second question requires us to consider.

2. The composition of sales can be expressed as a formula: visitor count (traffic) × overall store conversion rate × average order value. For salespeople, they need to think about these three aspects to improve performance.

3. Following the above line of thought, the main process of sales is first customer contact with the product, then stimulating the customer's desire to buy, and finally completing the transaction. Among these three stages, I believe the most critical is stimulating the desire to buy. This stage is particularly tricky because there are many touchpoints in the initial contact stage. If we use this as a performance metric, it's hard to accurately determine whether the touchpoint was actually caused by the sales effort. The transaction stage is somewhat similar to the contact stage, as it also requires sales involvement, but as long as it can be completed normally, it won't significantly affect the overall sales process.

Call volume is actually involved in all three stages, and I think it's difficult to quantify. If an indicator can't be quantified, analyzing it may easily lead to false busyness or perfunctory behavior.

4. The first principle is the major premise in a syllogism. This premise must be accurate in the sales process. Stimulating customer desire to buy is the most critical part, and it's also the key stage that determines whether a purchase will happen. So, let me try to write this as a syllogism:

Major premise: Stimulating customer desire to buy is the key to increasing sales.

Minor premise: Copywriting, sales scripts, and service are factors that influence customer desire to buy.

Conclusion: Copywriting, sales scripts, and service can increase sales.

5. Here are my thoughts. First, increasing traffic: location selection, traffic-driving products, user rewards, and cross-industry alliances. Location selection means finding traffic in underdeveloped areas. Traffic-driving involves trading profit for traffic. User rewards involve gaining traffic through word-of-mouth. Cross-industry alliances involve exchanging traffic for traffic. Conversion rate can be improved by acquiring users through effective traffic, gaining attention through display design, promoting decision-making through decision time, and closing the deal with discounts and promotions. Average order value can be increased by uncovering users' related needs and improving cross-selling. In addition to the causal relationships between products, you should also use big data to uncover correlations and promote through recommendations and bundling.

Finally, by strengthening repeat purchase behavior, we should actively manage the customer lifecycle, incentivize repeat purchases, and uncover the lifetime value of customers. Use membership systems, sign contracts with customers, and build loyalty between users. Once users form a strong bond, the likelihood of them leaving decreases significantly.

**Question 2: How to set up a sales performance evaluation system?**

Task decomposition is breaking down goals into executable tasks, which is thinking about "how to execute." KPI is breaking down goals into measurable indicators, which is thinking about "how to manage."

KPI is a very important management tool, but it has its own applicability. We need to pay attention to three aspects: balance, foresight, and applicability.

For example, we can consider three indicators: the number of products sold, the time taken to complete sales, and effective working time, i.e., the product of the two. Considering foresight, we should avoid lagging indicators, so we can set up potential sales opportunities and consider customers with purchase intent in advance. In terms of applicability, we need to consider the company's development stage. For example, startups are not suitable for the models used by mature companies, and we shouldn't focus only on data metrics.

These are my thoughts. I welcome your comments!
