Tony Sheng's Owned Audience Notes

01

Note 65

01 A lot of experience comes from actual practice. If you only imagine and refer to others, the amount of useful information you can accumulate is quite limited. For owned audience operations, you need to practice—sell when you should, refine content when needed.

02 After leaving school exams, most of the time our evaluations are rather vague. Even with KPIs, the standards aren't precise to every detail, and the evaluation is relatively simple. This means there's no right or wrong, and the final results can't be directly assessed.

03 When we ourselves have limitations, inviting external feedback is a good approach. For example, some companies hire management consulting teams, and some managers bring in coaches. An external perspective can offer a more comprehensive evaluation.

04 Practical experience is the best way to test, but simulating practical scenarios can help the real thing go more smoothly. Here we can refer to the Red Army mindset in military strategy, which is used to propose opposing perspectives, allowing projects to be evaluated more comprehensively.

05 For example, in video live streaming on WeChat Channels, we conduct simulation tests. Although it's a test, the live stream users don't know, and all the processes and content remain consistent. The goal of this process is to identify issues and ensure the smooth progress of the live stream.

06 Some people might say that even with simulation, you can't guarantee 100% error-free results, and that's normal. During the simulation, you're essentially running through the MVP (Minimum Viable Product), validating the smallest product loop. Conduct extensive testing and feedback within a controlled range.

07 After simulating practical scenarios, you should do a post-mortem summary. Additionally, you can do pre-mortem feedback—previewing the situation before starting, considering all possible scenarios. When even the worst-case results are acceptable, business development will generally move in a positive direction.
