Valentine's Day: (From Wikipedia) Valentine's Day, also known as Saint Valentine's Day in the West, is celebrated on February 14th every year. In recent years, Valentine's Day has been criticized for being overly commercialized. Due to religious reasons, certain regions such as South Asia and the Middle East have regulations on related activities. In the Middle Ages, Valentine's Day was most popular in Britain. At that time, there were also some customs, such as writing the names of local unmarried men and women on paper slips, placing the names of men and women in separate boxes, and then unmarried men and women drawing names from the boxes containing the opposite gender's names. Once a name was drawn, they would exchange gifts with each other. A woman would become a man's "Valentine" for the year. She would also embroider her name on the man's sleeve, and it would become the man's sacred duty to care for and protect her. The hallmark of Valentine's Day is the exchange of gifts between couples. Today, people prefer to express their love through love cards. These cards often feature Cupid, hearts pierced by arrows, and other imagery. The image of Cupid, the Roman god of love, frequently appears in Valentine's Day-related items. Regarding the connection between Cupid and Valentine's Day, the dictionary says: "Cupid (Latin 'cupido,' meaning desire or love), the Roman god of love, equivalent to the Greek god Eros. He is usually represented by a cute little boy, with wings on his back, his eyes covered by a cloth, and a bow and arrow in his hand."

Valentine's Day was invented by Westerners, more precisely by an American greeting card company. They printed a lot of Christmas cards during Christmas, but the enthusiasm for buying Christmas cards that year wasn't high. We know that holiday greeting cards are the most typical example of obsolete inventory. Once the time passes, they are essentially worthless. You can't store this year's Christmas cards for next year; once they get old, they have no meaning at all. So the greeting card company in America required its employees to sell all these cards within a month and a half. Imagine, greeting cards aren't like rice, oil, or salt—they are consumed at specific times. After Christmas, how could I possibly promote Christmas cards to others? The boss said, "You figure it out, I don't care." The employees really came up with ideas. They decided that if there wasn't a holiday, they would create one. The boss ordered them to sell all the cards within a month and a half. Since February only has 28 days, if we didn't sell all the cards by February 14th, we would all be fired. So they really had to think on their feet and looked up history. They found a person named Saint Valentine and thought about how to have a passionate romance with a young woman in a medieval atmosphere of asceticism. They heavily promoted this in the newspapers. After Christmas, these young people felt they could still have their own special holiday, a private one. So they believed that February 14th was the deadline for sales, and that day became Valentine's Day. People started buying cards. Later, some other businesses, like those selling roses and chocolates, also jumped on the bandwagon and sold a lot of things. This led to a consensus: we should come back next year and strengthen this holiday, as it would help boost our sales. So gradually, this holiday emerged and later spread to China, where young people also started joining in by buying various things.

After understanding the origin of Valentine's Day, why did I want to write about it? Although I'm still a single person, I think any holiday is a good opportunity. Maybe nothing special will happen, but it's a chance to think and gain a new perspective on the holiday. This year, my angle was a movie, and you've already guessed it—the title of my article, *The Rush Hour*. I watched it with a few kids on Valentine's night, and it seemed to have a slight impact on their small hearts. Let's talk about the connection between Valentine's Day and *The Rush Hour*.

The film is based on the Japanese movie *The Method of Stealing a Key*. It tells the story of a cold-blooded killer Zhou Quan and a down-and-out extra actor Chen Xiaomeng, who swap identities after an accident, leading to a series of humorous misunderstandings. The film is a drama, comedy, and crime film.

During the Spring Festival holiday, I watched a lot of movies, and on this special Valentine's Day, I encountered a film that seemed unrelated. From it, I saw a beautiful understanding of love. In the film, the two male leads accidentally swap identities. Chen Xiaomeng's identity goes to Zhou Quan, and Zhou Quan, played by Liu Dehua, loses his memory and starts to reacquaint himself with a new environment, tentatively adapting to Chen Xiaomeng's identity as an actor, now a background actor. By chance, he meets the single mother Li Xiang. The film uses the pun "Li Xiang" (meaning "ideal"), and the two gradually develop a liking for each other. A few background music pieces brought me back to that classic scene. The classic scene from *Comedy King*, as well as parts of the book *The Actor's Self-Portrait*, are referenced. In real life, we all hope for someone who seems like a small person, but who will give everything to them. In the face of love, everything becomes so simple and natural. I really like Wan Qi's character, the single mother who dares to love and hate, breaking free from constraints, for her child, which is also for herself. I saw the simple and genuine side of love, as well as its cinematic portrayal. Providing a better life for the other person is the simplest wish. Liu Dehua's character is a man who has failed at suicide multiple times. After losing his memory, he starts recording everything around him. For Wan Qi's character, he moves from a benefactor to a friend, then to someone he likes. The question arises: should he like her? This emotional journey resonates with me. Love comes quickly, sometimes it also leaves quickly. In the rush of people, we meet each other—wasn't that the greatest luck? Clearing everything away, like losing memory, to pursue what we love. But when the past meets reality, do we have many concerns? Has that courage and willingness to go anywhere gone? Through the trials of a tense situation, we refine our feelings. I want to dig deeper into my understanding of love. There's a movie called *28 Years Old, Still a Teenager*. What do you think of the movie's name? It was released in late 2016. 28-year-old Liang Xia is in a 10-year relationship with 34-year-old Mao Liang. Her daily wish is to finally marry "Prince Charming" Mao Liang. However, Mao Liang is busy preparing for the second round of funding for his company and has no time to think about Liang Xia's little thoughts. Liang Xia is heartbroken, and by accident, her mind returns to 17 years old, but her body remains the same. At the same time, the young mindset and cold attitude toward Mao Liang rekindle Mao Liang's interest in her. Facing two different relationships, what choice will Liang Xia make?

Perhaps the first principle of love is to embrace yourself, to know the true self. We don't need to care whether you're an adult or a teenager.
