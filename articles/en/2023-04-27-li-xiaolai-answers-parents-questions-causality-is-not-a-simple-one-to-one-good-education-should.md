**01**

**Establishing Causality Is Difficult**

Before we begin, let's discuss this topic: when we see a result, we try to find its cause. So—if we can find the cause of a result, we can replicate that result, right?

But to be honest, causality is not as simple as we think in our daily lives.

For example, establishing causality in pharmaceutical research is full of challenges.

In psychology, there's an important effect called the placebo effect.

Suppose you have a cold. In theory, no medicine can cure a cold quickly. The treatment of a cold mainly relies on the body's immune system. But an ignorant person, like an old man, might believe that he must take cold medicine to get better. So he goes to the doctor.

The doctor knows that a cold is mainly treated by the immune system, and no medicine can cure it. Most cold medicines today are for symptom relief, not for curing the illness.

You take cold medicine, and you stop sneezing and coughing. But the cold is still there. The real reason you get better is your immune system, not the medicine.

The doctor knows this, but he also knows that the old man might not understand this. And the old man and old woman believe that taking medicine is the only way to get better. So what can the doctor do? He can give him a placebo, which is a pill made of starch, not medicine, with no therapeutic effect. But he tells the old man confidently that this is the best cold medicine, and he should take it three times a day, rest well, and be better tomorrow.

Interestingly, the old man does get better the next day. Why?

The reason is that the immune system works less efficiently when a person believes they are sick and is anxious. Their immunity decreases. But once they believe they've taken medicine and their symptoms will disappear quickly, their illness will be cured soon, their psychological pressure decreases, and their nerves are less anxious. So they rest better.

What's the result? Their immunity improves, at least recovery is faster.

This phenomenon is called the placebo effect.

So how do scientists eliminate the placebo effect in pharmaceutical development?

There's a method called a double-blind test: find 200 patients with the disease and divide them into two groups, Group A with 100 people and Group B with 100 people.

**Group A takes real medicine—supposedly it should be effective.**

**Group B takes a placebo that looks exactly the same.**

The two sets of medicine look the same, so the people in Group A don't know whether they're taking real medicine or a placebo. That's one blind.

Another blind: the nurses who give the medicine don't know what they're giving either. If they knew they were giving a placebo, their expressions and attitudes would change, which would give it away and affect the patients' experiences.

During the test, Groups A and B are mixed together, and researchers identify which patient gets which medicine by number.

Group A is the test group, and Group B is the control group.

If after taking the medicine, 70% of the test group's symptoms disappear, but the placebo group also has 70% of their symptoms reduced, then the medicine is ineffective, as its effect is similar to the placebo.

So you see, in the process of pharmaceutical development, scientists have to come up with such meticulous methods to distinguish real causality from the placebo effect.

In the end, we should understand: **Establishing causality is really difficult.**

**02**

**Causality Is Not a Simple One-to-One Relationship**

Even in daily life: a child signs up for a tutoring class—his grades improve. The causal relationship between these two facts is not necessarily as simple as most parents think.

For example, could it be because he spent more time studying this subject?

Can you be sure whether the increased time spent is the more important cause or the fact that he attended the tutoring class?

**It's even possible that the child just got lucky by attending a tutoring class and met a peer he liked. The peer's attitude toward learning inspired him, changing his own attitude toward learning.**

In the process of education, the truly meaningful causal relationships are definitely not simple one-to-one relationships. The real causality is likely more complex.

**In real life, the actual causal relationships are often the result of many causes developing over time to produce many outcomes.** Think carefully about this sentence—it will greatly help you understand family education.

Let me repeat: **When we investigate causality, the real important causal relationships we face are usually not one-to-one relationships, but a collection of many causes.** If we only focus on a single cause to study its result, or a single result to study its cause, it's usually very wrong, even absurd, and typically won't achieve the purpose we want.

**03**

**The Part You're Seeing Now**

**Is a Collection of Many Causes, Forming One of the Results After Years of Development**

Many parents sawEileen Gu at the 2022 Winter Olympics, and she looked beautiful, tall, healthy, and was a world champion in skiing.

People started to explore what madeEileen Gu so excellent. But **in reality, you can't analyze and recreate anotherEileen Gu in 20 years.**

Suppose you sawEileen Gu and at the same time your child was born. You thought you had figured out the causality and started using your analysis to educate your child.

The possibility that your child would become anotherEileen Gu in 20 years is actually very low. Even if they were equally talented, it would be hard.

Why?

Because **the part you're seeing is just one of the many results that developed over time from a collection of causes you don't know.**

So if you try to reverse-engineer the causes, the possibility of replicating this causality is nearly zero.

That's why, over the years, most family education has not been successful at its core.

**04**

**Education Is Not a One-Day Thing**

**Education is a long-term process, not something that can be done in a day.**

**Education relies on development, and development is a process where many causes combine to produce many results.** If you ask Li Xiaolai a question, he won't give you a direct and effective, immediate answer.

Because I can't be sure that I can infer the causes from a small part of the result you describe. **The part you describe may be just one of many results, and the problem you need to solve may be a combination of many outcomes.**

**Education is not medicine.** This point must be clearly understood by everyone.

Even with highly developed modern medical technology, it's difficult to cure diseases in most cases.

**Most of the educational problems you encounter in life are essentially life problems, which are the result of many causes developing over time. So changing this situation is not impossible, but it requires many efforts and a long time.**

**05**

This week, a student asked a particularly difficult question.

Why is it hard to answer?

You chose that way back then, and after more than a decade, you suddenly realize it's become a burden, right? So what do you do now?

Because raising children is really hard work, and someone who is highly dependent can't fulfill the role of a mother, which is a fact. So many men's dating standards today are wrong.

At the same time, many women's dating standards are also wrong. Many women with excellent natural conditions—beautiful, lovable—have ended up in bad situations because of their flawed dating choices.

As a man, your dating standards might have been wrong in the past, which is why you chose that kind of person. Now you're realizing the problem, but what do you do? There's no immediate solution. **06**

**The most important reason for parents indulging their children is being an only child**

Another question: your wife indulges her child because she's a mother. This fact is also the result of many reasons, and it's likely not related to her being dependent. Even if it is, the most important reason for parents indulging their children is being an only child.

Assume, just for the sake of argument, that you have three children. I can almost guarantee you that even if your wife is dependent on you, she wouldn't be able to indulge each child as much.

So what do you do? Have another child, or two, or three? Of course, this brings up many other problems, which we'll discuss later.

**07**

**During development, bad things can become good things**

Let's continue. When we talk about good family education, Li Xiaolai's starting point is different from the majority of people on the market: **I'm not promoting elite education, I'm not promoting struggle education, I'm not promoting competitive education. Our goal is not to raise our children to be elites. No, I just want to raise them to be normal people using the right methods.**

That is, I hope to use simple and straightforward methods to raise my children to be normal people. They just need to be normal.

Many parents have a "one-track mind": they believe one cause leads to one result, and one result comes from one cause.

They then think that if I can find the cause of a result, I can avoid it or recreate it.

No, that's absolutely not true.

Let me give you an example. Many parents have encountered this problem: when their child is around seven or eight years old, they start to understand the importance of money. At this age, they might steal money from their parents. When this happens, parents get scared and punish their children, telling them: "If you do this, you'll end up in prison."

But in fact, I can tell you that stealing money from parents is something that has happened to more than 75% of people.

I used to steal money from my father when I was a kid. I know many people don't admit to it, but it's actually very common.

Okay, if more than 75% of people stole money from their parents when they were young, does that mean 75% of people in the world become thieves?

No, you have to believe that every normal person is continuously developing, right?

**So throughout the development process, sometimes good things turn into bad things, and sometimes bad things turn into good things.**

First, don't think that if your child is indulged by his mother at ten years old, he's doomed for life. That's not necessarily true. Second, don't think you have no influence at all.

I've said a lot, so what do you do?

**Take on more responsibility. Treat your wife like a child, and raise her up.**

Your wife indulges your child, so you don't have to indulge them, right? Parents indulge their children mainly because they are only children, so you can try to have more children.

As you gradually change, you'll face various problems. Don't feel that your future is bleak just because of any problem. Don't think that any problem means the world has collapsed. No, people always develop, right?

In the end, it all comes back to the fact we emphasize in good family education: **as a parent, you need to be a role model from all angles.**

You might think that people are often told that the environment shapes us. But from my life experiences and those of many people around me, I've seen more often that people change their environment.

You might say you can't change the world or society. Yes, you can't change the whole society. I live in Beijing, can I change Beijing? No, I live in Haidian District, can I change Haidian District? No, I can't even change my neighborhood. But I can change my family environment. That's the truth.

Yesterday, we had a community member who is 57 years old, sharing his changes over a year in the fixed investment life course. He told two stories: one was that at 57, he changed his mindset through learning, hired a yoga instructor, and after more than half a year, his body changed dramatically.

As a result, his 30-something son, who used to be a big fat guy, decided on his own to go to the gym to lose weight. Before that, no matter what he said, it didn't work.

His son later lost over 30 pounds, and good things followed—promotion, salary increase, making the whole family surprised. You see, there are many common and widespread examples around us.

A 57-year-old can change, so why are you afraid of your 13-year-old child? Right? So have confidence in life. This isn't just some kind of cliché.

What I want to make clear is: **life is a long development process. This generation is facing a hundred-year life. In this process, no moment is perfect, and no moment is actually the end.**

Only when your life ends, when you're gone, is that the end for you. But for your family, it's just a small event in the process.

That's the end of my answer to this student's question. But everyone also knows this isn't just a response to him.

In short, whenever we face any problem in life, we need to understand: it's likely one of many consequences resulting from many causes over time.

**All the awkwardness in life is like a chronic illness in medicine. There's no quick fix, no immediate solution. The remedy is to correct and develop gradually over time.**

This is a fundamental concept we'll keep coming back to whenever we discuss any problem we face in life in the future.
