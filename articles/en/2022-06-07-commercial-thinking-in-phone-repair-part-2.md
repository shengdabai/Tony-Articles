Commercial Thinking in Phone Repair (Part 2)

01 Users' satisfaction with repairs isn't that complicated. The main criteria for evaluation are two points: whether the problem is resolved, and the service experience during the repair process.

02 For example, the waiting time I experience during repairs is a tough period. My phone is being repaired, which is a bit of a hassle. Is this a concern? If I don't have a spare phone or any alternative product to use, can the service provide something to help with this?

03 Communication during the repair process is also important. For instance, showing the user the repair progress on the screen, keeping them updated throughout the process, and also inserting ads, product promotions, usage tips, stories, or even DIY content options.

04 When hearing a user sigh in frustration, it's a bad user experience. Try to find ways to make up for it or reduce their sense of loss. Giving some benefits is one method, like offering users some perks during live streams.

05 The full marketing process: entering the store, purchasing, repurchasing, and recommending. Solving the user's problem, delivering a quality experience, coming in with a faulty product and leaving with a fixed one, and also taking away a good mood and a gift. This will also help build good word-of-mouth.

06 Hearing the user's voice in a timely manner, for example, complaining about the product while waiting, and then shifting to talk about the pandemic, or even social issues, makes the store environment noisier and the experience worse. Will live streams also have such uncontrollable voices? Can we guide the conversation?

07 There are many places in life where marketing can be optimized. Marketing isn't mysterious; it's specific, detailed, and can be implemented in concrete points.

Doing commercial insights, iterating the marketing process, so you can sell more and sell better.
