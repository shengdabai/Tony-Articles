90th Original Piece

As the old saying goes, failure is the mother of success. So why in the world of investing, success is also the mother of failure? Success itself carries the seeds of failure, and failure itself carries the seeds of success. Success makes people proud, and pride makes them arrogant. They become overly confident, swaggering around, no longer disciplined, no longer diligent… and thus lose the essential qualities needed for success, and so they no longer succeed. A person's failure is not always a bad thing. At such times, they may reflect on their experiences, become more humble and cautious, realize they are not omnipotent. When they start being disciplined again, turning their past failures into useful progress, success will once again beckon to them. Success is also cyclical.

Why is success not necessarily a good thing for most people? Success changes people, and usually not for the better. Success makes people feel smart. To a certain extent, feeling smart can boost confidence, which is a good thing. But once you become too smart, thinking you're good at everything and always smart, it becomes a bad thing. At the same time, success makes people richer, and when people become rich, they tend to be satisfied, which reduces their motivation to strive and work hard.

Why is timing so crucial in investment success? You can't learn valuable lessons from success. Successful people often attribute their success to themselves, which blinds them to the real reasons behind their success. In fact, many people's success is simply due to luck or the help of a mentor. Successful investing often only teaches two dangerous misconceptions: one is that making money is easy, and the other is that you don't have to worry about risk. These are two particularly dangerous false beliefs.

Why is it optimal for investors to choose passivity actively? Why adopt a regular investment strategy, specifically the two-cycle eggshell regular investment strategy, which is the most friendly and anti-fragile approach for ordinary investors?

In the face of a complex world, everyone needs some simple principles. Sardines are also called herring. Each sardine is small and weak, so they must act in groups. Even when attacked by large fish, the entire school remains as a single unit, changing direction flexibly to avoid the attack. "Each fish is an independent entity, with no leader, yet the school acts as one, without scattering. This is the form that future business units should take."

> Their principles are also simple: stay close, align, and separate. Each fish follows the one in front (self-organization), keeps in sync with the fish beside it (self-adaptation), and maintains distance from the fish behind to avoid being caught (dual feedback mechanism). In summary: follow the one in front without leading, stay in sync with the ones beside you without standing out, and keep a safe distance behind to avoid being overtaken! Our purpose in doing things is usually to achieve success. Therefore, before taking action, we should seriously consider whether the matter depends more on luck or more on ability (skills). If we understand this early on, we can then act with purpose and direction in the process.

The same applies to investing. Complex and profound theories are not necessarily correct. We must never underestimate the simplest content, which can be practiced long-term. For things that depend more on luck, we should avoid them. For things that depend more on ability, we can confidently proceed, step by step. One key point in an investment strategy is that we must deeply understand: nothing is always effective; no investment method, rule, or process can consistently beat the market. First, most securities and investment methods are effective in certain investment environments during specific phases of the cycle, but they become ineffective in other environments or at other stages of the cycle. Second, past success itself makes future success less likely. Additionally, as previously mentioned, focusing on short-term gains is a form of short-sightedness, and expecting to profit every day is unrealistic. No one, not even the best investors, can predict every cycle adjustment perfectly. Therefore, actively pursuing short-term gains, chasing highs and selling lows, is a waste of time and energy, and may even lead to greater risks or losses. Compared to actively buying and selling frequently, accidentally buying at peaks or selling at troughs, or even exiting prematurely and suffering greater losses, it's better to "actively choose passivity," hold long-term, and regularly invest, increasing your ability to earn outside the market, focusing your attention on more important matters, and letting time speak for itself.

What role does human emotion play in the cycle?

Emotion plays two roles in the cycle: first, it amplifies the power of objective factors, leading the cycle to extremes and eventually requiring correction; second, it causes market participants to ignore the cyclical nature of things, and at this moment, recognizing the excessive volatility of the cycle is crucial—it is the most likely time to profit or avoid losses, such as in the third stage of a bear or bull market.

Why do we say that as long as there are people, the cycle will always exist? Humans have a tendency to go to extremes, and this tendency never ends, so these extremes must eventually be corrected, not because the cycle itself changes. People tend to bring their emotional and personality weaknesses into their economic and investment decisions. They may become overly excited at times when they shouldn't, and overly discouraged at times when they shouldn't, because people tend to overestimate the potential of an upward cycle when things are going well, and overestimate the risks of a downward cycle when things are not going well. Therefore, human participation pushes trends to the extremes of the cycle.

Human psychology and emotions determine the magnitude of cycle fluctuations. Objective factors influence the cycle, such as quantitative relationships, global events, environmental changes, technological development, and corporate decisions; but when people analyze these objective factors, their psychology and emotions play a role, leading to overreaction or underreaction from investors, thus determining whether the cycle fluctuates too much or too little.

When you understand the role of human emotion in the cycle, how does it help you with your investments and your life?

Humans have a tendency to go to extremes, and this tendency never ends, so these extremes must eventually be corrected. The cycle itself does not change. Economic and market trends have never been a straight line—neither in the past nor in the future. This means that investors who understand the cycle can find profitable opportunities. Human emotional fluctuations are natural and inevitable; precisely because of human emotions and psychology, the cycle always exists and fluctuates. Human emotional fluctuations are natural and understandable; although inevitable, we can try to be more peaceful, objective, and rational by understanding emotions and the cycle. Building a more objective and rational understanding of the cycle can help us better grasp its trends and find more and better profitable opportunities.
