What exactly is a risk attitude cycle?

Risk is the most important variable in investing. At any specific point in time, investors' overall attitude toward risk and the actions they take play a crucial role in shaping the investment environment, and we find ourselves within that environment. Understanding the state of the investment environment at this moment is key—it determines how we should view risk at this moment and how we should act accordingly to manage it. Assessing where the risk attitude cycle stands is the most important content.

When do people become risk-averse? When do they become risk-tolerant?

Risk aversion is an essential element of investing. Since most investors are risk-averse, risk-averse investors approach investments with caution and care. When considering investments, especially those with high risk, they conduct thorough research and analysis. They incorporate conservative assumptions and appropriate skepticism into their investment analysis. Risk-averse investors demand a larger margin of safety to protect against potential analytical errors and unpleasant surprises. They insist on a significant risk premium, meaning they expect higher returns, which makes them willing to take on risky investments. They refuse to engage in irrational or foolish investment trades.

Investors can become overly relaxed in their risk aversion, leading to excessive risk tolerance. Because they feel more comfortable with the environment and more optimistic about future outcomes, their caution during the investment process diminishes. Since they no longer perceive investing as risky, they feel no need to carefully analyze every detail. They often make broader assumptions, replacing doubt with trust. They are willing to accept lower margins of safety in exchange for making investments. They no longer view risk with concern, and they no longer demand such high risk premiums to feel secure. They become less rigid and less particular, as they focus more on the attractive returns of risky investments and pay less attention to the risks behind those returns.

How is the risk attitude cycle connected to investors' psychological and emotional swings?

Positive events increase optimism; increased optimism leads to higher risk tolerance; increased risk tolerance leads to lower required risk premiums; lower required risk premiums mean lower required returns for risky assets; lower required returns for risky assets lead to higher asset prices; higher asset prices mean higher risk levels (but this also attracts "momentum traders" who chase the trend and buy more stocks). People's psychology and emotions can easily become overly optimistic and overly risk-tolerant. When the situation turns downward, people tend to become overly concerned and risk-averse. This means that the time when people are most eager to buy is often the time when they should be most cautious, and the time when people are most reluctant to buy is often the time when they should be most aggressive. Outstanding investors recognize this and strive to act against the crowd, engaging in contrarian investing.

The premise of adopting a regular investment strategy is having sufficient earning power outside of the investment. In the book *Changing Fate Through Regular Investing*, Li Xiaolai clearly explains our income structure, choosing to invest using the "eggshell" method, which removes our daily essential expenses and the savings set aside to guard against risk, leaving only the money we use for investing. We adopt a regular investment strategy, and the only action required is to keep buying. Additionally, we must continuously enhance our ability to earn money outside of investments. We must hold our investments for at least two major cycles, which is something we must do—only buy, never sell! The execution of a regular investment strategy remains consistent regardless of market fluctuations; it's simply keep buying. For us, current market ups and downs mean little. After two major cycles, we will reassess. As long as we continue to invest regularly and persist in practicing the values of our investment community, we can resist risk and become long-term thinkers.

> Perhaps human psychology and emotions are naturally prone to extremes.
