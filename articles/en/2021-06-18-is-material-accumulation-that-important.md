First, let's define what material means: it refers to raw, unprocessed, emotional, and scattered information that authors collect from real life. These materials cannot all be directly used in writing. However, when an author concentrates, refines, processes, and transforms these life "materials" and incorporates them into a work, they become "themes."

You can think of it as first-hand information. For me, the main sources of material now are Google and Wikipedia. More accurately, I'm looking for the most original information—articles, reviews, images, videos, etc.—but they must be unprocessed raw materials. The shares from Dedao, Fan Deng, Hunchun, and the Practicing Group may have been processed and organized by the teachers, and they have had a significant impact on me. Because for first-hand information, I can access more professional content, and I can identify first-hand information in the education field, allowing me to make my own judgments. However, in other areas, my understanding is very limited, so processed information is more helpful to me.

Processed information is the accumulation of teachers' experience, refined and organized before being shared. Therefore, it gives me more inspiration. Some content is even first-hand information directly shared by the teachers, such as Li Xiaolai's column and book content. These concepts and values, although known by most people, are interpreted and perceived in a unique way by Li Xiaolai. That is first-hand information, excellent material. In comparison, content from platforms like Zhihu, Xiaohongshu, Jianshu, and Douyin is mostly processed, and some of it has been processed multiple times. I generally don't read much of it, though I occasionally do.

In terms of collecting material, my approach is to trust professionals and authorities. Their trust allows me to likely obtain good material. Second, I rely on professional platforms, websites, apps, and publishers, but I selectively collect material to avoid being distracted by irrelevant information. Finally, I believe my outstanding peers are also sources of material for me. Their cognition and experiences are unique materials. Learning from the excellent!

Collecting material is a challenge for me, and it also places a lot of demands on myself. If I don't filter it and just collect a lot of material indiscriminately, how can my attention be improved? It would just be spending a lot of time on low-efficiency tasks. Li Xiaolai reminded us long ago that time is more important than money, and attention is more important than time.

What values mean is what is more important and what is most important. Attention is the most important thing, so it shouldn't be wasted carelessly. One thing to note is to avoid greed—don't want everything, or you might lose the big picture by focusing on the small. My future plan is to improve my professional skills, at least develop my own judgment. For content in different fields, I need to know where to find first-hand information. Once I have the material, I will screen and evaluate it, which requires a lot of effort. Reading is a good way to do this. With enough quantity, I can identify good books. As Li Xiaolai shared, no one can read for us; we can't be taught to read. We can only practice it ourselves. I also hope that through reading, I can improve my ability to evaluate material and collect high-quality, useful material!

What I do is very basic and not yet strong. However, I have a vague feeling that I can do this well and even do it more strongly in the future. Collecting material sounds simple, doesn't it? It's just collecting material, right? When I was teaching English, many colleagues gave me an nickname: "The All-Knowing Tony." Why? Because whenever they needed something or had a question, they could ask me, and it seemed like I could solve it. But this was just an illusion. Now I think it's because some people don't want to answer or solve others' problems, maybe to protect their attention. More likely, they just don't care. Some people might not even know, but they don't care either. After all, they can still teach well based on their own understanding and ideas.

I think George Bernard Shaw's criticism of teachers is reasonable. If you can't do it, then teach. Some teachers are indeed in their comfort zones, and whether they teach well or not doesn't matter as long as the students like them and keep enrolling. Some people might think that if students like a teacher, that means the teaching is good. But there are many reasons why a student might like a teacher—like the teacher being funny, for example. Continuing on, I can access many resources and share with colleagues because I simply want to be a thoughtful person and become professional. Knowing that my colleagues aren't English majors and are teaching English as a cross-disciplinary endeavor, becoming professional is essential.

I collect a lot of material, but this might just be resources. I haven't practiced or used it to benefit myself or others. Li Xiaolai always says, "Only what you do yourself can you tell others." However, in some areas, I haven't done it yet. If I haven't done it, that means I don't fully understand the material, haven't chosen the right material, and haven't used it effectively. I hope to become a strong person, collect material more intensely, think deeper, and persist longer!

Currently, I'm accumulating valuable material and organizing these information and resources. Perhaps after my processing, these materials won't be very good, but this is the best I can do within my current capabilities. For example, I've been doing WeChat Channels for a month now. I can easily access resources from other video channels, imitate them, or do things that can make money quickly. However, in the book *The Internet Entrepreneurship for Common People on WeChat*, I learned a term: CLV (Customer Lifetime Value). How can I provide long-term value to my users? I can't be limited to short-term performance. Advertising is not competitive with the platform, and selling products can only help the platform sell. I want to create my own courses and share what I believe is valuable.

So, for now, I'll just focus on accumulating material, take it step by step, carefully choose the sources, process the material with my own understanding and cognition, and convey the correct values. No matter how long it takes to make a profit, I'm always gaining and growing. Keep up the good work!

Protect my courage and my commitment to material. Don't be distracted by short-term gains, and don't collect material without thinking just for easy access. Courage is a precious quality. I understand it as sticking to my values, collecting material, and accumulating it carefully and thoughtfully!

This might be a normal phase in my career. During my time as a teacher, there was a brief period when I felt very lost. To improve my classes and make them more impressive, I studied the experienced teachers at the institution, collected their experience, learned their methods, and applied the collected material to my lessons. After all, they are group trainers, and their classes are very appealing. But the reality is that my class performance wasn't great. In fact, some students found it boring. I wondered why. Actually, it's because our students are different, we're in different teaching areas, and their current interests and abilities vary. A good teacher needs to adjust according to the students. The same material can lead to completely different outcomes. To use the material effectively, it needs to be applied precisely. This career low point made me think more about the necessity for teachers to collect good material and apply it well, ensuring the student-centered approach is reflected in every aspect. Treasure every crisis and keep refining yourself, improving your abilities!

Reading, fitness, investing, spending time with family, and helping friends—achieving these five things, what else can you worry about? The mountains and rivers are all before you!
