Martial arts is a niche circle, and more people know about dancing! If we're talking about Tony and martial arts, we have to go back to 2003, when Tony was only seven years old, just starting first grade. Tony's dad was pretty open-minded, not sending Tony to all sorts of tutoring or interest classes. Back then, students were still busy as ever. Dad believed the body was the foundation of everything, and wanted Tony to be braver, to become a strong and resilient boy. There were also some unscientific ideas—more exercise would prevent Tony's vision from deteriorating. Modern medicine has since proven that myopia is caused by prolonged focus on a single object, not just from looking at phones. Reading can also cause it, the key is not to strain the eyes continuously. So doing eye exercises, outdoor activities with adequate sunlight, and taking a 10-minute break after every 30 minutes of screen time are all effective ways to prevent myopia. Those exercises were still helpful for Tony back then, even though he practiced indoors. His eyes never stayed fixed on one spot—you've probably heard the phrase, "eyes on six directions, ears on eight directions." Martial artists have sharp eyes, always keeping an eye on their surroundings, giving off a sense of a legendary martial arts master, right?

Encouraged by his father, Tony went to a martial arts school. At that young age, Tony had no idea how significant martial arts would become in his life. Since the training was only on weekends, the first time he arrived, the school was full of students, and Tony felt a bit scared. After all, he hadn't seen so many lively classmates before. He thought it would be like a regular school, with all the students being obedient and well-behaved. A burly man walked by, exchanged a few words with his dad, and then led Tony to the registration desk.

"Attention!" The moment the command was given, the whole room fell silent, and everyone's eyes turned toward the burly man. Tony looked closely and realized it was a middle-aged man with piercing eyes, a straight back, and a loud voice, like a legendary hero. Tony was full of admiration for him! This was Tony's martial artsfirst introduction coach—Coach Jiao. Tony didn't know then how amazing Coach Jiao was. There's a story that he could stack eight bricks on his head and hit it with a hammer without getting a single scratch. You want to know if it's true or not? No one can say for sure. The coach never mentioned it, but it's a story that spread among the students.

The Way of Martial Arts is to illuminate virtue.

The Way of Martial Arts is to illuminate virtue. You didn't miss it—virtue is the root of martial arts. In China, martial arts athletes are ranked in six levels: International Class (International Grade Athlete), Grade A (Grade Athlete), First Level Martial Artist (First-Class Athlete), Second Level Martial Artist (Second-Class Athlete), Third Level Martial Artist (Third-Class Athlete), and Martial Youth (Youth Athlete). As for what level Tony will eventually reach, let's look forward to it together!
