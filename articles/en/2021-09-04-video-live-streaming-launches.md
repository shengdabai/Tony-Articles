Officially launching WeChat Channels live streaming, the host is myself, TonyMax Shengdabai. Welcome to sign up for the event!

I've done live streaming before, after all, I used to be a teacher at an institution. When the pandemic hit, the challenge of online live teaching came in waves. But even without online teaching, isn't in-person classes also a form of live streaming? It's just that usually, we don't record it, and the audience is limited to classroom students.

I still remember the nervousness I felt during my first class, even though I had rehearsed several times before class and received feedback from my mentor to refine my lessons. But when I actually stood in front of students, my heart still couldn't help but race.

One important part of teaching is feedback, assessing the class and continuously refining it so that a lesson can flow smoothly.

In life, we're always live-streaming. Being a teacher seems to mean having more live-streaming viewers. Live streaming often brings unexpected situations; sometimes, you speak without thinking, and your words become incoherent. But do you know what's interesting about it?

The rhythm of live streaming and the specific environment can sometimes make users react too slowly, letting the host set the pace, and the users follow along. Think about where something might be wrong, but you can't recall exactly what it is. This isn't like writing, where you have time to double-check.

Teaching can be like this too. Students are led by the teacher, their thinking is limited, and the teacher controls the whole class. This isn't the kind of mode I prefer. The center of teaching should be the student, and a class that students take the lead in is an effective one.

Some might say, if I can teach, why haven't I started live streaming yet? Because the environment is different. On the platform, the audience comes from different ages and industries, and I need to learn how to manage the flow of my live stream.

I started with WeChat Channels in February, and now it's almost seven months later. Watching live streams has been during this summer, and after observing multiple live streams, I've formed my own ideas about the process and rhythm of live streaming.

What's most important in a live stream? I think it's joy, having fun, and chatting comfortably. That's already great. Delivering too much value or sharing various tips might look impressive, but we need to consider why users come to a live stream in the first place. If the tips are so good that users don't want to watch anymore, what should we do?

Clarify your intentions before you start. Learn from accounts that are similar to yours, watch different types of live streams, respect differences, and find a presentation style that suits you.

I want to build a home for myself on WeChat Channels. Welcome friends to drop by anytime and chat. If I can offer a little value, that's my honor. If not, just coming here to have fun is also my blessing!

Learn through practice, and learn while practicing!
