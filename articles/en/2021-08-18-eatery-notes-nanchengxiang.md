For someone like me, a seasoned foodie, the most natural subject to analyze is the catering industry. My foodie nature is fully exposed—among so many delicious restaurants, why did I choose Nanchengxiang? Perhaps because we're both from Anhui, but that's not the only reason. This is a place I really like, and the deeper I get to know it, the more I love it. Let me try to analyze it.

First, I'll break down the basic elements of the catering industry using the MECE principle I've learned. The main aspects include the following:

Analyzing from the supply and demand chain, the supply side includes dining methods, preparation methods, and sales methods, while the demand side covers the number of people, dining scenarios, and the process of eating. The connection point mainly involves both offline and online scenarios.

Let me briefly introduce Nanchengxiang. The company was founded in 2001, adopting a unified procurement, unified processing, unified delivery, and standardized service model. It operates as a fast-food chain with over 80 branches and one central processing base covering 10 acres, as well as a logistics center. It has become a large-scale catering chain integrating R&D, production, supply, sales, and logistics.

The founder of Nanchengxiang started in the catering industry as early as 1993, experimenting with various street foods. After over 20 years of development, Nanchengxiang has consolidated the founder's core products: grilled lamb skewers, rice bowls, and dumplings. This has formed the brand's unique identity: "Rice is fragrant, skewers are fragrant, dumplings are fragrant." This brand model is impressive, offering full-time community dining. Let me now analyze it in detail through combination innovation:

**Supply Side**

1. Dining Method: In-store dining, takeaway, delivery (full-time dining, delivery business performed exceptionally well during the pandemic, with a peak of 10,000 orders per day, ranking first)
2. Preparation Method: On-site preparation in the restaurant, semi-finished product processing (some products are uniformly made in the central kitchen, such as lamb skewers, which must be prepared fresh at the store to ensure quality)
3. Sales Method: Independent SKUs, combo SKUs

What sets Nanchengxiang apart from many other restaurants is its commitment to full-time community dining. The core concepts are full-time operation and community dining. 24-hour operation is not something every store can achieve, and the key lies in cost control. For example, their dumplings are a signature dish, but having a few customers at any time would be costly if cooked on a large stove. Therefore, the central kitchen prepares the dumplings and packages them in boxes. When customers come, they can simply use an electric stove to cook a box, significantly reducing costs. The other is community dining—many restaurants are located in city centers or core business districts, but their customer base is unstable, and customer loyalty can be low. In contrast, community dining customers are residents, leading to an increasing number of returning customers, making it easy to satisfy one's appetite anytime, anywhere.

**Demand Side**

1. Number of People: No limit, mostly depending on the scenario—eating alone anytime, having a meal with two to three people, or gathering for a barbecue with more people
2. Dining Scenario: Individual meal, family meal, work meal, overtime meal, gathering meal
3. Eating Scenario: Satiety, delicious food, convenience

The demand side essentially covers the main needs of the general public, allowing customers to eat anytime and anywhere without much burden, given the wide range of available products. I want to mention that the founder's experience has been very helpful in product positioning. He believes that many street foods will eventually be phased out, but products like lamb skewers, which have withstood the test of time, must be able to be sold long-term. On the other hand, the founder noticed the differences in taste between northern and southern regions. Some people prefer dry, others wet. Therefore, they offer both dry and wet options, which is why they have rice bowls and dumplings. So, the product doesn't necessarily need to be numerous; what matters is whether it can pass the test of customers. This is closely related to the founder's experience.

**Connection Side**

1. Offline: Restaurants
2. Online: Platform delivery

The connection side is similar to most restaurants. Nanchengxiang's core competitiveness still lies in the service and products it offers.

By transforming Nanchengxiang into a "flying saucer" model, the company emphasizes that managers must go to the front lines, and the headquarters is streamlined to a very small size, allowing branches to take on core functions. The central kitchen and store managers jointly control product quality. Regarding online operations, the founder's delivery business wasn't built through promotion. It must ensure consistency in quality between online and offline. He mainly emphasized four aspects: 1. Understand the role of delivery in your business model. 2. Try to use the existing organization's flexibility for delivery, rather than building a separate team. 3. Don't develop exclusive delivery products, but ensure that in-store and delivery products are highly consistent. 4. In terms of online operations, concentrate limited advertising resources to boost order volume. As long as customer repurchase rates are high, you can stand out.

Finally, I'd like to add a few thoughts about Nanchengxiang. At first glance, it seems like a simple and unassuming restaurant, but it excels in every detail. Sometimes when we talk about combination innovation, we always seek novelty and want to be different. From the beginning of my engagement with combination innovation, I was deeply impressed. It's about recombining old elements in new ways, mastering each basic element, and achieving perfection. I think even traditional industries still have new opportunities. With the development of internet technology, we all hope to do "Internet Plus," but many are just "+ Internet." Perhaps it's time to pause and carefully analyze, find the core elements, and create our own unique innovation.
