"If the interface defines how we connect, then algorithms define with whom we connect."

Algorithms are becoming an increasingly important part of our daily lives. From social platforms to recommendation systems, algorithms analyze and interpret our behavioral data, shaping our digital world.

But you might not be aware that algorithms do more than just match us with information more precisely—they also define with whom we connect.

The rules of connection behind algorithms: from social platforms to socialized recommendations. Without algorithms, our interactions on social media would be primitive and chaotic.

However, algorithms, through big data and artificial intelligence, optimize our interactions and social connections. Social platforms use algorithms to analyze our interests, social networks, and real-time behaviors, recommending the most relevant information to us, allowing us to connect more effectively with people we are interested in.

Socialized recommendations work the same way. Algorithms consider our browsing history, purchase records, and social behaviors to recommend content that most excites our interests, giving us the opportunity to connect with more like-minded people.

According to a report published by Twitter (now X), their "Friend Recommendations" algorithm recommends over 5 million Twitter accounts to other users every day, based on an "Interest Graph" algorithm that uses users' behavior on Twitter, including tweets, follows, and likes.

The evolving rules of connection: from "social" to "personalization." Although traditional social rules still exist, as algorithms continue to develop and improve, people are increasingly focusing on personalized relationships.

Algorithms can help us connect with people whose interests and values align with ours, rather than just those we know or our friends' friends. This transformation demonstrates how algorithms are reshaping our ways of socializing and the rules of connection, playing an increasingly important role in the process.

According to Facebook's publicly released annual research report, many people now prefer to spend their time communicating with a small group of people they care about—that is, "tight-knit social networks"—rather than broad social networks. This indicates that people's social rules are undergoing substantial changes, and algorithms are contributing to this shift.

The impact of algorithms: network group effects and the "filter bubble" on social media. Although algorithms provide more precise interpersonal connections, they also have some negative effects.

One of these is the network group effect, which means more and more people are joining certain groups or communities through social media and forming connections with others, creating a "group effect" that may lead to distorted information transmission.

The filter bubble, on the other hand, limits our perspectives, keeping us only with people who share our views. This not only exacerbates racial and political divides but also makes it difficult for us to connect with others.

A 2017 study found that the "bubble effect" caused by algorithmic filtering on social media isolates users from differing opinions, which could have serious implications for the development of a fair society.

Algorithms are becoming the rules of connection between people, and these rules are becoming more personalized and precise.

However, as algorithms redefine social rules, we also face some negative impacts. Facing the sun is positive, just as handling complex social environments is.

We need to better understand and balance the impact of algorithms on our lives and social interactions, so that we can connect with more people in the world.

Before the emergence of the platform society, the rules of connection were mainly "you might be seen." For example, traffic cameras on the road remind drivers "you might be seen," so they must follow traffic rules. The sign on the wall of a supermarket, "Camera, please smile," also reminds customers "you might be seen," so if you take something, remember to pay for it.

After the emergence of the platform society, the rules of connection between people are changing. It's no longer "you might be seen," but rather "you might not be seen."

Algorithms, interpersonal relationships, and social alienation: connection and disconnection in the digital world.

We live in the digital age, and the development of digital technology is reshaping our lifestyles, ways of socializing, and interpersonal relationships.

In this process, algorithms are playing an increasingly important role, defining with whom we connect and shaping the way we connect with the world.

But does this connection based on algorithms align with our interpersonal relationships, social interactions, and social structures?

The interpersonal relationships behind algorithms: the use of social data and new "relationships." On social networks and digital platforms, everyone has their own personal information, behavioral habits, and interaction patterns, which are the data sources that algorithms use to locate our attention and connections.

The results of this data analysis have transformed our relationships from simple classifications like friends, strangers, and relatives into more diverse forms such as digital tags, interest groups, and people with similar views. At the same time, these digital interactions are redefining our real-world social interactions.

A study on crowd behavior during disasters found that using digital platforms like social networks can provide important and effective information for subsequent response actions. Personal data such as location and emotional states on social networks can quickly reflect changes in the external environment and the ability to respond.

The impact of algorithms: social alienation, filter bubbles, and new social anxiety. The use of algorithms has undoubtedly had a significant impact on the way we connect with the world, but if we look more closely at these changes, we can see that they also bring about structural breaks, social anxiety, and fragmentation in society.

People are becoming increasingly inclined to interact with those who are similar to them. Algorithms continuously filter out the "other," creating new "social bubbles," which are unhealthy for our understanding and connection with society.

A study found that the use of social media and digital platforms has caused substantial damage to the social functions of children and teenagers, with these students being at a higher risk of having fewer peer relationships.

Social issues in the digital world: finding a balance and new possibilities for social interaction. Although the development of algorithms may have some conflicts with our interpersonal relationships, we can also better accept and use it by rebuilding the value system of interpersonal relationships and finding a balance in social interactions.

By reasonably utilizing digital technology, better understanding it, and combining these technologies and skills with traditional social activities, we can regain control over our connections with the world and open up more open, healthy, and meaningful social relationships.

The use of algorithms not only shapes our ways of connecting but also shapes our values and beliefs. This digital form of connection naturally requires us to remain vigilant and adapt our ways of interpersonal interaction, expand our choices, and make more people aware of us. This is the necessity and importance of finding a balance in the digital world.

In this era of information overload and algorithm dominance, our choices seem to be controlled by algorithms. Whether it's the recommended content on social media or the personalized promotions on shopping platforms, we seem unable to choose freely. However, we can still reclaim our independence and choice in the age of algorithms.

The Algorithm Maze: Are Your Choices Being Manipulated? In the age of algorithms, every online action is monitored and analyzed, shaping our digital identities and interests.

These data are used to customize personalized experiences, but they also serve to filter and limit. However, we can take some measures to reduce the impact of algorithms on us, such as clearing browsing history, limiting the sharing of personal information, and choosing different platforms and service providers.

A study found that personalized recommendation algorithms on social media platforms have replaced recommendations from friends and family, leading users to be exposed to fewer new information and perspectives.

Building Your Own Information Sources: Cultivating the Ability to Think Independently. In an era of massive recommended content and fragmented information, we need to cultivate our ability to think independently, gather information from multiple sources, and make autonomous judgments and choices. This may mean actively seeking out different viewpoints, reading news from various sources, and even participating in discussions and debates.

A rich variety of perspectives and information sources can promote innovation and the development of thinking, while a lack of diverse information input can lead to cognitive rigidity and fixed thinking patterns.

Protecting Privacy, Guarding the Self: It is important to protect personal information and privacy to avoid being manipulated by algorithms. Using privacy settings and tools reasonably, such as encrypted communication apps and VPNs, can better protect our personal data. Additionally, we need to be cautious about sharing personal information to avoid unnecessary data leaks and misuse.

According to a survey, 63% of respondents expressed concern about data privacy, viewing data leaks and misuse as major issues facing society today.

In the age of algorithms, we can still protect our independence and choice. By reducing the impact of algorithms, cultivating the ability to think independently, and protecting our personal information and privacy, we can find our true selves in the digital maze.

It is important to remember that we are independent individuals, with the right to decide how we view and respond to the choices presented by the digital world, rather than being driven by algorithms. Only in this way can we truly become the masters of our own choices.
