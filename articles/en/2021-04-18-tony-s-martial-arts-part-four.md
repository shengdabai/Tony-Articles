# Tony's Martial Arts, Part Four

Fist, palm, and hook are three basic hand gestures in martial arts, and there are also other gestures that evolved from these three. In fact, our hands guide our eyes, not the other way around. We don't wait until we see something before we act—by then it's already too late. People often ask, why bother with all these hand gestures? Can't I just go straight for the punch? Why not just hit randomly? Honestly, I've also thought about this. After learning martial arts, maybe I'm not even as good as someone who just randomly throws punches. But we're not fighting to be violent. From a bigger perspective, we can protect our country and help those in need. From a smaller perspective, it's about building our bodies and becoming more righteous.

The origin of martial arts is unknown, but its goal is to defeat the enemy. If you just throw punches randomly without training, can you really win? For example, a fist has a knuckle and a palm, and the flat surface of the fist is required. If you just throw a punch, try hitting the wall. You might end up hurting one of your uneven knuckles. If the palm isn't solid, the punch won't have power. Of course, the techniques also need to be combined with body movement. Each of the three basic gestures has its own purpose. Use them flexibly and choose the best one for attack.

Why is martial arts difficult to become an international sport? The reason is simple. Competitive sports must limit actions that are easy to cause injury to the opponent. That makes sense, after all, it's a competition. If martial arts had such rules, it might not be possible to compete, because every move is potentially fatal. Some people think this is exaggerated. Let's look back at the martial arts techniques in Jin Yong's novels. Looking back at history, under limited conditions, our goal was to subdue the enemy or kill them with one move. Why go through so many steps? Some people say Muay Thai is very powerful, with deadly techniques. Why do they have so many competitions? Because their techniques are limited. The depth of martial arts lies in thousands of years of civilization development. Its techniques are countless, but many have already been lost. Martial arts can't be a competitive sport. What we see in martial arts competitions are just forms and routines, and it's hard to feel its power. This makes the promotion of martial arts very difficult.

Earlier, there was a short film called "Gong Shu Dao" made by Jack Ma and other celebrities, which tried to promote martial arts in a new form. But the essence didn't change. Its power is still huge. This is something I've been thinking about. Maybe a child learns taekwondo, and when their belt color changes, their rank increases, and they win medals in various competitions, which is impressive. I also participated in many competitions when I was young. People misunderstood me as being hard to beat. In reality, we also have ranks, but they mainly target professional athletes. Just like in English teaching, why do we test and participate in competitions? It's like wanting to get some honor while playing a game. With such encouragement, it becomes more exciting. I don't oppose this, but we shouldn't try too hard just to get these things. I've also seen some people who got their taekwondo black belt but never trained or improved after that. Thinking deeper, why do we learn it? What can it bring me? These kinds of questions might need to be thought about again and again!
