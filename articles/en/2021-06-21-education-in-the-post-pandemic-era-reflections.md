Here, I'd like to focus on two documents: *China's Modernization of Education by 2035* and *Implementation Plan for Accelerating Modernization of Education (2018–2022)*. These two documents respectively outline requirements and measures from the perspective of long-term national strategic planning and short-term goal realization. Two points stand out as particularly important: 1. The document systematically proposes eight "more emphasis on" basic concepts, namely, morality first, all-around development, for everyone, lifelong learning, teaching according to individual aptitude, integrating knowledge and action, integrated development, and shared construction. These eight basic concepts follow the laws of education and talent growth, and align with international educational development trends.

2. The main development goals for 2035 are: first, to build a modern education system that serves lifelong learning for all citizens; second, to popularize high-quality preschool education; third, to achieve balanced and high-quality compulsory education; fourth, to fully popularize secondary education; fifth, to significantly enhance the service capacity of vocational education; sixth, to markedly improve the competitiveness of higher education; seventh, to ensure that children with disabilities have access to suitable education; and eighth, to form a new pattern of education governance involving the whole society. These eight goals cover aspects such as the structure of the system, the level of popularization, educational quality, the structure of talent cultivation, and the capacity for service and contribution. At the same time, the document also proposes key quantitative targets for the development of education and human resource development by 2035.

I believe all of the above represent opportunities for the future development of the education industry. I feel very excited about this. The country is promoting education for all and advancing lifelong learning, with many people working behind the scenes. According to data released by the Ministry of Education, in 2,019, there were 5.3 million schools at all levels and types, with 282 million students enrolled, 17.32 million full-time teachers, and 33,099 private training institutions, among which there were 211,959 full-time teachers and 10,754,715 students enrolled in training institutions. These figures reveal a vast market and opportunities that are worth further consideration. I work in the K12 English education sector, and I hope to help more Chinese students find motivation for language learning, so that everyone can become a language expert.

Here are my reflections on the future development of the education industry:

Online education is in the early stage of the digital transformation of education. Only when it reaches the stage of intelligent education will this new educational model possess the power to disrupt traditional education. Only by leveraging technological and management tools can education cease to be a product and no longer merely a service. Online education that remains in the product economy stage can evolve into a service economy, and eventually into an experience economy, such as intelligent, personalized private teachers, or educational scenarios with high immersion and interactivity that replace traditional classrooms. Although online education has already shown signs of prosperity today, it is still in the early stage of digital education. As cloud computing, digital technology, computing technology, data technology, and intelligent technology continue to advance and converge, they will eventually achieve a true disruptive transformation of traditional industries.

True digital education is still far off. The sudden popularity of online education caused by the pandemic is a kind of limited game. Some online education companies that performed well during the pandemic have products and business models with clear characteristics of limited games. To ensure their growth is sustainable, they must also possess the characteristics of an infinite game. This means continuously deepening their understanding of the essence of education and the needs of users in this industry, and constantly refining their products, services, and experiences to approach the essence of education and learning.

I would like to explore the OMO model further. Hybrid learning has already been studied abroad, and the pandemic has accelerated its adoption in China. However, current technological applications are still not mature. I recommend the model of Khan Academy, where students watch video lectures before class and learn at their own pace. The main purpose of in-person or online classes is to resolve doubts. Of course, if technology can enable every individual to have an intelligent teacher, it will further drive educational transformation and achieve the true goal of student-centered learning. After class, through online exercises with a game-like experience, such a model and scenario are something I look forward to.

Finally, I recommend two books, *The Future School* and *The Innovative Classroom*, which contain many thought-provoking questions. There is also much to explore about the Finnish education system. These are my reflections and collected information. I would like to share them with my fellow partners for discussion and exchange. Thank you!
