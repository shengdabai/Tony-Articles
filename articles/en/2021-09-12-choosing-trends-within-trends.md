151st Original Piece

What is a trend? According to Wikipedia, a trend mainly refers to a visible direction along a timeline. Statistically, trends are time-bound and represent a cross-sectional observation of information changes. In technological analysis, trends are often used to observe financial market movements.

In English, "Trend" represents both a trend and a current, but in Chinese, the term "trend" may have originated from the idiom "currying favor with the powerful" (literally, "seeking warmth and attaching to power"). Originally from the *Song History* *Li Chui Zhuan*, it meant "seeking warmth and attaching to heat." Later, people changed "heat" to "power."

In modern professional analysis, a trend has a direction. For example, an upward trend is composed of a series of continuously rising peaks and troughs. Additionally, charts and trend analysis are closely related. Many trends are communicated and presented through charts.

In marketing, trends are also a significant area of study. Through market research, industry analysis, and consumer interviews, we can predict future trends that will influence products. Currently, many trend analysis institutions are active in advertising, marketing, and design sectors.

It seems complicated, but let me share my understanding. A trend can be a trend or a current. Like water, a trend can carry you forward or capsize you. Searching for trends, you'll find all sorts of so-called trends—macro or micro, social or industry-specific. There are countless analyses and experts thinking about new trends. However, I particularly agree with Li Xiaolai's long-term advocacy of growing as a compound interest curve. As we continuously upgrade our cognition, we are building a smile curve.

Overall, this is an upward-growing curve, and I believe this trend is real and credible. Often, we follow the crowd, listen to so-called trend analysis, and seek to capture the seemingly within-reach dividend. However, when we have sufficient ability, we don't need to worry about missing one opportunity after another. I understood this truth at a young age: opportunities are always there, but the question is whether I have the ability to seize them. The cruel truth is that I often don't, so trends mean little to me—I just listen to them.

This time is different. Li Xiaolai gave me a lot of motivation. Honestly, it's about thinking from the end goal. It felt like I saw the future version of myself who had achieved financial freedom. I started dollar-cost averaging on myself, making small records—1,000 days of recording three things that happened each day and yesterday's gains, as a witness to my growth. Perhaps trends emerge in such accumulation.

Zhang Xiaoyu, the teacher from the *Personal Investment Course* column on Dedao, mentioned five excellent investment principles: diversification, passive investing as the main approach, cost reduction, perseverance, and not investing in things you don't understand.

It's hard to predict which countries or asset classes will perform best in the coming period, so what we can do is make our investment portfolio as diversified as possible, covering various investment markets and asset types, ensuring sufficient diversification.

Anyone, even professionals, finds it difficult to beat the market long-term, especially when you discover a good fund manager, his performance might have already started to decline.

Therefore, rather than entrusting your money to them or believing in so-called professionals' recommendations, it's better to gain average returns by investing in market indices. In the long run, this is a better choice. At least, we can focus on passive investing, with only a small portion of money going into active investments.

In investing, the cost and fees have a huge impact on returns and are also the easiest to control. Even saving a little bit each year can significantly boost our investment returns. So, when we operate, we should try to minimize costs.

It's nearly impossible to predict short-term market movements and patterns, and trying to make quick money by predicting the market is not feasible. We should think long-term and maintain perseverance.

Because the investment market is extremely complex, most of its elements are beyond our understanding. Therefore, our bottom line should be: don't invest in things we don't understand.

There are always many mistakes, many temptations, and many traps in the world, trying to pull you out of this simplicity and make you do many things that look useful, look sophisticated, but actually offer no real benefit.

As ordinary investors, we should accept our limitations, acknowledge what we can't do, and also use our various advantages. Only then can we ultimately succeed in investing and truly change our lives.
