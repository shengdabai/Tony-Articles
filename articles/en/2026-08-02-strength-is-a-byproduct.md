# Strength Is a Byproduct

> 发布日期:2026-08-02 · [中文](../zh/2026-08-02-变强是副产品.md) | [English](../en/2026-08-02-strength-is-a-byproduct.md)

---

The quickest way to become weak is to make "becoming strong" your direct target.

This sounds wrong at first. We live in a world full of dashboards. Sleep scores, focus scores, follower counts, income goals, learning streaks, model benchmarks, productivity graphs. Everything asks to be measured, improved, optimized, and displayed.

So when AI arrives, the natural question becomes: How can I use it to become stronger?

It is a good question.

But if we answer it too directly, we may ruin the very thing we want.

Some valuable states are not normal goals. You cannot grab them from the front. You have to create the conditions in which they appear. Sleep is like this. Trust is like this. Taste is like this. Courage is like this. Real learning is like this. Strength is like this too.

The social scientist Jon Elster called these "states that are essentially by-products." They can happen as the result of doing something else, but the attempt to produce them directly often destroys them.[^1]

That idea feels old and philosophical. In the AI age, it has become practical.

## The Self-Improvement Trap

Think about sleep.

The person who sleeps well usually does not spend the night managing sleep. They finish the day, reduce disturbance, lie down, and let consciousness fade. The person who desperately wants to sleep starts monitoring: Am I relaxed? Is my heart rate low enough? Did I enter deep sleep? Why am I still awake?

The monitoring itself becomes wakefulness.

Researchers have even used the term "orthosomnia" for cases where people become preoccupied with sleep-tracker data and begin chasing better sleep numbers in ways that can worsen their sleep experience.[^2]

The same pattern appears in thought suppression. In the famous 1987 white bear experiment, participants who were told not to think of a white bear ended up repeatedly signaling that the bear had entered their mind.[^3] The mind has to check whether the forbidden thought is present, and that checking keeps bringing the thought back.

There is a similar paradox around happiness. Research by Iris Mauss and colleagues found that placing high value on happiness can backfire, partly because people compare the actual moment against the happiness they expected to feel.[^4]

The common structure is simple:

The moment a living state becomes an object of direct inspection, it changes.

You are no longer sleeping. You are evaluating sleep.

You are no longer relaxing. You are checking whether relaxation has arrived.

You are no longer learning. You are asking whether this learning is making you look advanced.

This is why self-improvement can quietly become self-surveillance.

At first, the dashboard helps. It gives feedback. It makes the invisible visible. But if the dashboard becomes the master, the original activity shrinks. You do not run because running gives you a body that can do more things. You run to protect a streak. You do not write because writing sharpens thought. You write to maintain an identity. You do not use AI to attempt work that was previously too hard. You use AI to keep the feeling that you are "keeping up."

The target remains beautiful.

The pursuit becomes theatrical.

## AI Makes the Trap More Comfortable

AI is dangerous here not because it is weak, but because it is helpful.

Before AI, pretending to learn was a little harder. If you wanted to understand a subject, you had to wrestle with books, notes, examples, exercises, conversations, and your own confusion. There were shortcuts, of course, but the friction was still visible.

Now the friction can disappear too early.

Ask AI to summarize a book, and you feel you have met the book. Ask it to create a study plan, and you feel you have started studying. Ask it to generate ten product ideas, and you feel you have entered entrepreneurship. Ask it to critique your life, and for a moment you feel serious.

Sometimes these are useful starts.

The danger is mistaking the start for contact with reality.

AI can produce the shape of growth before growth has happened. It can produce the vocabulary of understanding before understanding has been earned. It can produce the language of courage before you have taken a risk. It can produce a plan so elegant that you stop noticing there is still no customer, no reader, no user, no shipped thing, no feedback, no scar.

This is the new laziness.

Not doing nothing.

Producing the appearance of meaningful motion without letting the world touch you.

That is why "use AI to become stronger" is too vague. The phrase can mean two opposite things.

It can mean: use AI to avoid the boring parts of real work.

Or it can mean: use AI to make real work large enough that it finally changes you.

The first path creates comfort.

The second path creates strength.

## Strength Comes From Real Tasks

If strength is a byproduct, then the main question changes.

Do not ask, "How do I become stronger?"

Ask, "What task is worthy enough that doing it will force me to become stronger?"

This is a much better question.

A real task has resistance. It has an outside. It can say no. A reader can be bored. A user can fail to understand. A small tool can break on someone else's machine. A lesson can fall flat. A workflow can save time in theory and create more work in practice. A customer can refuse to pay. A public promise can expose the difference between intention and delivery.

That outside pressure is not a distraction from learning.

It is the teacher.

This is where AI becomes powerful in the right way. It can help you attempt tasks that used to be too large for one person: compare sources, draft prototypes, inspect code, translate between domains, create test cases, summarize feedback, generate variants, build small tools, and keep a record of decisions.

But the task must remain real.

There must be some output that leaves your private imagination and meets a world that does not automatically agree with you.

If you use AI only inside your own head, it may make the room more comfortable. If you use AI at the boundary between your intention and reality, it becomes a force multiplier.

The difference is not the tool.

The difference is whether the work has consequences.

## The Producer's Route Is Indirect

The consumer asks AI, "Make this easier."

The producer asks AI, "Help me stay longer with the hard part that matters."

This is the key distinction.

There is nothing noble about unnecessary pain. If AI can remove mechanical labor, remove it. If it can clean data, draft a first version, find contradictions, format a table, or test an idea faster, let it. Protecting friction for its own sake is just another performance.

But do not remove the friction that carries the lesson.

The friction that matters is usually not typing. It is judgment. It is choosing what to keep. It is deciding what is true enough to publish. It is noticing that a user misunderstood the thing you thought was obvious. It is returning to the same work after the first excitement fades. It is cutting a clever paragraph because it does not serve the argument. It is admitting that the system you designed is too complicated for the person you wanted to help.

AI can support all of this.

It cannot replace your contact with it.

The producer's route is indirect because the producer is not chasing the feeling of being strong. The producer is trying to make something good enough to survive contact. Strength arrives later, almost as residue.

You discover that you can judge more clearly because you have judged many imperfect drafts.

You discover that you can explain better because real people failed to understand you.

You discover that you can use AI better because a toy prompt was not enough for a real workflow.

You discover that you have taste because you have rejected many acceptable outputs.

You discover that you have courage because at some point the work required you to publish before you felt fully ready.

None of this can be ordered directly.

It has to be grown.

## Do Not Optimize the Self. Design the Loop.

The practical answer is not to abandon measurement or ambition.

It is to place them in the right position.

A good loop has four parts.

First, choose a real task. Not an identity, not a mood, not a vague aspiration. A real task has a deliverable: an article, a lesson, a working tool, a sales page, a repaired process, a better explanation, a public note, a useful checklist.

Second, use AI to extend your reach. Let it bring sources, generate alternatives, expose weak points, simulate readers, automate tedious steps, and help you see what you could not see alone.

Third, keep human judgment at the boundary. You decide what is true, useful, tasteful, responsible, and worth releasing. AI can suggest. The signature is yours.

Fourth, let reality answer. Publish, test, teach, ship, show, sell, or ask someone to use it. Then record what happened. Not what you hoped happened. What actually happened.

Repeat this loop, and strength begins to appear.

Not as a certificate.

Not as a persona.

As a changed relationship with work.

You become the kind of person who can hold a problem longer. You become less afraid of confusion. You become less dependent on ready-made instructions. You become harder to fool by beautiful words. You become more interested in feedback than applause. You become more willing to revise the system instead of blaming yourself.

This is what I mean by using AI to make people stronger instead of lazier.

Not more output for the sake of output.

Not more automation for the sake of automation.

More contact with real problems than one person could previously handle.

## The Thing You Get By Not Grabbing It

There is a quiet mercy in the fact that the best things cannot be seized directly.

If respect could be purchased, it would not be respect. If sleep could be forced, it would not be sleep. If wisdom could be downloaded, it would not be wisdom. If strength could be obtained by asking AI for "a plan to become strong," it would not be strength.

This does not make the world unfair.

It protects the meaning of the thing.

So maybe the right question for the AI age is not: How can I optimize myself?

Maybe it is: What can I build, practice, teach, repair, publish, or serve that would make strength a necessary side effect?

Do not stare at your own growth too long.

Put the work on the table.

Let AI sit across from you, not above you.

Use it to make the task clearer, larger, stricter, and more alive.

Then keep going until the work has taught you something your self-image could never teach.

Strength will not arrive because you chased it.

It will arrive because the work required a stronger version of you, and you stayed.
