**01**

**Life Essentially Is a Hand of Bad Cards**

> Parent's question: Hello, Li Xiaolai. I've been greatly inspired by your courses, but I feel like your courses are for families that are complete.
>
>
>
>
> I'm a single mother, my child is 6 years old, and most of the time he's being taken care of by his grandparents. I live and work in a small county town, with little education, and I don't know how to properly educate him. Without my husband's presence, I'm worried about how his growth will be affected. So, how can a single-parent family like mine properly educate our child?

This is an expected question.

First, I want to tell you that **life, in essence, is a hand of bad cards, even a hand of bad cards after another.**

Even if you occasionally have a good hand, it will soon turn into a bad one. And **because everyone has bad cards, how you play them becomes the most important thing.**

In good family education, we say that the real ones who should be educated are the parents, first the parents, then the child, right?

Then we also say that the most important thing in family building is what?

It's the relationship between the couple, right?

But you're a single parent, what do you do?

In my view, this is actually a modern life norm.

The modern life norm is that many families eventually have to break apart, and the main reasons are things like today's human condition.

First, the average human lifespan has increased.

In the past, the divorce rate was relatively low because, in the past, people thought that 50 was about it, 60 or 70 was already rare. Even 30 years ago, people's aging was very noticeable after 30, so the divorce rate was relatively low because once you passed 30 or 40, what was there left to do? You were already on your way to the grave, and there was no second spring or anything like that.

The second reason is that the period of youth and prime has lengthened.

Now, many people are 35 and have been married for 10 years, and then they get divorced. We don't need to talk about men, but even women at 35 may still be the best age in modern society.

They're not the young, fresh-faced girls of old, but they may be more attractive. I believe many women today feel this way. So the lengthening of the youth and prime period means that a large number of people in modern society have more dating opportunities, more time to re-examine themselves, re-examine society, and re-examine relationships, right?

The third reason is that economic independence is clearly easier today than in the past.

In the past, for example, 100 or 200 years ago, living together was almost a necessity.

In those times, it was relatively difficult to survive alone. You had to do it out of necessity, like if your husband died in battle, so you had to take care of your child alone.

But today, people are relatively easier to achieve economic independence. Once people are economically independent, their demands for various kinds of freedom will be relatively higher.

There are also many other reasons, such as increased activity range, better transportation, and so on. So from this perspective, these are all very normal things, right?

Then, based on all these reasons, you, who were young and inexperienced, chose the wrong partner, and at 35 or 40, decided to start over.

Do you have the right to do that?

Believe me, you must have that right. **You doing so does not mean you're any less than others, on the contrary, and it's not true that children of divorced parents can't grow up well, as long as you can handle it properly.**

We say that in good family education, the relationship between the couple is the most important.

What we mean is that if your current relationship with your spouse is good, that's great, keep it going. But **if you have already divorced, that means you need to rebuild your family with more fresh knowledge and understanding.**

So don't mistakenly think that a single mother or a single father can't educate their child well.

That never happened, never happened.

Whether a child can grow up normally, without even becoming an outstanding talent, actually comes from some of the most basic concepts. These concepts don't entirely depend on a complete family.

Let's say, for the sake of argument, that you divorced and your child doesn't have a father at home, and the child can't grow up properly. Then, what about children who lost both parents? How did they grow up?

So that's not the issue at all. Don't think you're unlucky, you've been dealt a hand of bad cards. Everyone has a hand of bad cards. No one has a good hand.

**Family education is an "infinite game," where the cards are re-dealt from time to time, and the environment changes from time to time, to the point where the good cards you had in your hand might even turn into bad ones.**

This is a very common situation.

Let me give you an example to make it clear. Do you know that Kodak has gone bankrupt? But back then, Kodak was at the peak of its power, making money like a printing press every day. Their technology was the best in the era of film.

But the funniest thing was that they were too profitable, so they ruined their good hand.

Few people know that the first digital camera in the world was actually invented by the Kodak laboratory. It was the Kodak lab that created the first truly mass-producible digital camera.

But they kept this invention locked in the lab.

Why?

Because selling film was so profitable. Selling film was their big, good hand.

So they kept delaying the development and market launch of the digital camera, allowing Japanese electronics companies like Nikon and Canon to take the lead. That good hand moved to Nikon and Canon.

But that good hand didn't last long either.

Why?

Because smartphones appeared. The cameras on smartphones have almost wiped out all digital cameras, leaving only a high-end category, like the single-lens reflex cameras.

This is a very common phenomenon in the development of the world.

The same logic applies to each of us. So what I want to say is: you need to understand that not only do you have a hand of bad cards, but everyone has a hand of bad cards. Even if someone occasionally gets a good hand, it won't stay good for long—it will be reshuffled again.

So remember this principle: **life is a hand of bad cards, even a hand of bad cards after another.**

No one has a good hand, right? So you don't need to worry. You just need to keep improving yourself.

You've taken the course, and you know that the most different thing about our community compared to the outside world is that **we are education based on production.**

We believe that if a person understands production and can produce, they will definitely live better than others. Focusing all your attention on production will make your efficiency higher.

So you need to become a better producer. You might not know how to do it at first, but that's okay—just learn.

Today, there are many kinds of production. I'm not telling you to start farming, nor am I telling you to write articles and give lectures like Li Xiaolai. You know, Tao Huabi can make spicy sauce so delicious, doesn't that count too?

In your hometown, if you can make tofu so good that it's the best in the town, the best in the city, I don't believe you won't be economically independent.

I've given many examples. One of my relatives has a sister-in-law. She came from Wangqing County, a town under Yancheng City in Jilin Province's Yanbian Korean Autonomous Prefecture, with just a pair of scissors.

Why did she bring a pair of scissors?

Because she had taken a sewing class in Wangqing County and learned some sewing skills. Then she opened a tailor shop in Yancheng City, learning to make clothes, suits, and shirts from others, and eventually became a well-known brand in the home, then a provincial brand.

Look at her—she was a producer, she knew how to make clothes, right?

To be honest, for a long time in my life, my ability to generate income was far less than hers. She turned a tailor shop into a suit factory, and later even ventured into real estate.

Look, people are like that.

**So whether you're a single parent or not doesn't matter. What matters is that you become a producer.**

Don't think it's too late to learn anything at 30. I'm telling you, 50 or 60 are still okay. Today, the average human lifespan has increased, so you can start at any time.

I'm not just randomly encouraging you or giving you random motivational platitudes. In fact, there are many such examples around us. So since you've taken a course and heard a different message, you should try to become a producer and improve yourself.

Our operating system has three states: **simple, optimistic, and focused**, right?

- No matter whether you're from a single-parent family or not, as parents, you must do these three things, which is the greatest help to your children, rather than because the child lacks a father, this value transmission disappears.

- Going back to our topic, our lives are always a deck of bad cards. Even if you're getting better during your development, you'll still face another awkward situation: the stronger you become, the stronger your enemies will be, and the greater the difficulties you'll encounter. This is an inevitable phenomenon.

- So no matter how good or strong you become, you're still holding a deck of bad cards.

- There's also one thing: even if your life keeps getting better, your life satisfaction won't increase indefinitely. If we conduct a survey and ask everyone to rate their life satisfaction, you'll be surprised to find that regardless of the social class or the state of life, people's satisfaction is roughly around 75% to 80%.

- If some people improve their quality of life significantly through effort or luck, they might give a higher score at first, like 95 or 90. But you know what? This high score can only last about two weeks at most. After about seven weeks, your brain starts adapting quickly to the new environment. In the new, better environment, you'll quickly feel more discomfort, there are still things that make you uncomfortable or unsatisfactory.

- Just as I mentioned earlier, no matter how strong you become, you're still holding a deck of bad cards, because you've become stronger, you've encountered worse things that you couldn't have faced before.

- Let me give you an analogy: you're sailing a boat in a small river, a bit risky, but as you grow, your boat also grows. Eventually, the small river can't hold your boat anymore, so you move to the Yangtze River. The wind and waves in the Yangtze River are definitely stronger than in the small river, right?

- That means although your boat is bigger, the risk of capsizing is greater. And once you capsize, the loss is even bigger. Later, you continue to develop yourself, upgrade your boat, and sail a cruise ship into the sea. The waves in the sea are even bigger, and there might even be tsunamis.

- But the key point is, once you capsize, isn't the loss even greater?

- So this is the essence of life: you'll never be satisfied. But this isn't your problem; it's just how the real world truly is.

- Because your whole life is just one deck of bad cards after another, and there's no way around it.

- In the course, we refer to this phenomenon as **persistent problems**, which means **eternal, unsolvable dilemmas**.

- For example, crime, illness, death, and imperfection.

- However, if you observe the underlying causes of these eternal dilemmas, you'll find that everyone is the same. You shouldn't mistakenly think that some people who seem to be living better than you don't have problems. It's possible their problems are even bigger, or even something you couldn't bear if it were placed on your shoulders.

- So don't envy others, because everyone is the same. In the end, everyone is holding a deck of bad cards.

- Therefore, comparing with others is wrong. What should we compare?

- **Our present and future, our past and present, our past and future.**

- We only need to do one thing: **constantly improve.**

- Improvement doesn't guarantee perfection, right?

- Going back to the earlier question, if you're a single mother, what should you do? It's simple: accept your past imperfections, accept your past mistakes, and keep moving forward. If you find the right person, try to build a new family properly. If you don't find the right person, don't settle for anything. After all, living independently is also fine, right?

- So if you happen to get divorced, based on our good parenting principles, I can only give you one piece of advice: **divorce is done, never tell your child anything bad about the other person.**

- Even if the other person did something wrong, don't say bad things about them. Explain to your child clearly that building long-term close relationships between people is indeed very difficult. For example, my mother once failed, and this isn't entirely the other person's fault. I also did things wrong, and I'm reflecting on it. So in the future, I'll try to do better.

- If the other person did something bad, explain to your child that they might have had difficulties. Otherwise, who would intentionally do bad things? Who would intentionally break promises? So just show your child the real situation.

- Of course, if you say bad things or belittle the other person, I clearly tell you that you're not showing your child the real world. You're only showing them your one-sided view.

- So in our "Good Family Education," we emphasize the eternal dilemmas from all angles. We talk about the dilemmas first and then the responsibility, because **this world is full of dilemmas, full of unsolvable problems.**

- Therefore, we have to take responsibility. We accept both good and bad, and since we're alive, we just keep moving forward.

- **Since we have to move forward, try to improve a little bit with every step, try to evolve a little bit with every step. This is our value.**

- To summarize what I've said:

1. The essence of life is a deck of bad cards, and everyone is the same.

2. People's satisfaction with their lives is always around 75 to 80 points.

3. Life will bring one after another of bad things, and these are unsolvable dilemmas.

- So, what should we do?

- **You need to learn to be optimistic, and you need to choose evolution.**

- Optimism isn't innate. Pessimism is easier to acquire. More commonly, people are optimistic in childhood but become pessimistic after being repeatedly beaten by reality. This is the core reason most people are pessimistic in this world.

- Because life is full of hardships, you're always holding a deck of bad cards. How can you be happy?

- You go to play poker, and you have two kings and four twos and four aces in your hand. You might feel great, but the problem is that you still can't win much with that hand.

- **Optimism is something we have to struggle to learn.**

- But optimism actually has a reason. Sometimes I think about why I was often able to be optimistic in my youth. I believed that everyone's luck isn't great. Just because today's luck is good doesn't mean tomorrow's will be, right?

- If everyone's luck isn't great, why care about luck? That's right.

- Luck isn't important, so you need to keep evolving. As long as you keep evolving, optimism will have a basis.

**Let Children See the Real World**

> Parent's question: Li Xiaolai, would you tell your child about your family's financial situation? If your child asks, would you answer honestly?
>
>
>
>
> My husband is currently out of work and starting a business, he has taken on loans, and after half a year, there's been no improvement in his income. I work at a small company with a low income, and I still have to pay the mortgage every month. I feel a lot of pressure every day. My child is in elementary school, and I don't want him to know about this, fearing he might feel inferior. But I'm always worried he might ask about it. Teacher, should I tell my child about our family's financial situation?

This question deserves a serious answer.

First, our educational philosophy in the community is based on **showing family members as much of the real world as possible**. We don't want to engage in fake education.

The core reason why traditional education is so ineffective is that it accidentally created a virtual world for children, building an ivory tower for them. Let them live in the ivory tower, and then one day, when they reach a certain age, throw them into the real world. Do you think they can adapt well?

We emphasize eternal dilemmas because **we want children to not only understand the bright side, but also the dark side.** I want my family members to know the real world. I know some people are very strong, so they work very hard outside, but don't tell their wives or husbands about it. That's a way of being strong.

Today, people are still comparing with each other, so you're worried that your child knows her parents aren't as rich as her classmates' parents, so she might feel inferior. That's your worry, right?

But first, I want to tell you: **if you don't worry about this, there's no problem. Once you start worrying, it really becomes a problem.**

You, as a person in a modern civilized society, working hard for a better future for yourself, is that shameful? No, it's not.

Today's income isn't enough, is that shameful? No, it's not. That's right.

So it's not shameful for your child to know that the family's financial situation isn't great.

Moreover, I think it's better for the child to know the real financial situation of the family as early as possible.

Let me tell you another phenomenon, which is completely opposite to what you think.

Assume you're not a parent worrying about financial income, and you suddenly achieve financial freedom. Would your child's growth automatically improve?

Almost certainly not.

Children from wealthy families face more growth challenges than children from less wealthy families. The wealthy children might only gain a few advantages—like better food, better drink, and better clothes—but they might actually be at a disadvantage in terms of ability development.

**"The Curse of Resources"**

There's another term here called **"The Curse of Resources."**

Resources are good things, but they come with a curse.

Let me give you an example. Suppose a girl is very beautiful, the probability that her life will be difficult might be higher.

We have a saying: "Red beauty often has a short life." Actually, this saying has some truth to it, or maybe it's just a summary of reality.

Why is that? Because she is too beautiful, there are too many people pursuing her, too many with ill intentions, too many who flatter her, and as a result, it's hard for her to focus her attention on truly useful things.

That's why there's the saying "Red beauty often has a short life"—because if you're beautiful, the concentration oftoxic men (disreputable men) around you will be very high. This isn't an isolated phenomenon.

Let me give you another example: if a country has a lot of oil underground, then its science and culture won't develop.

Do you know which company made the most money on Earth in 2,021?

It was the Saudi Arabian oil company.

Saudi Arabia has the most oil underground and is the richest country in the world. But if you look at it, there's no possibility of having any tech companies there, or any globally renowned schools. This is the curse of resources.

You might be sighing, thinking we are too poor compared to them, but you might not know that: **the effort required for wealthy families to raise their children without wasting them is far greater than for poor families.**

I myself have this feeling. Suppose my parents were very rich, I would doubt whether I could become someone who is very good at making money. The reason I became very good at making money later on is that 90% of it has to do with my parents not being good at making money.

Children of the poor learn to take care of themselves early, so don't think that adversity in life is necessarily a bad thing.

In fact, **it's precisely because our lives are like a deck of one bad card after another that we've developed the ability to make the best of a bad situation.** Without that kind of hardship, we wouldn't have developed that ability, would we?

So the student asked earlier: should we tell our children the real financial situation of the family?

Yes, you should let them understand how difficult it is for you to make money. Then you should also let them know that even though we don't earn enough, every penny we earn is earned cleanly, so it's not shameful. That's actually a good thing.

If you think that the child will feel inferior once they know, then it means you yourself are already inferior. Is the child's inferiority because you told them about the financial situation? No, it's more likely that your own inferiority has already been passed on to them in various ways, right?

**Don't be afraid to show your family members the real world—good and bad.**

**Use Long-Term Patience to Help Your Family Build a Positive Cycle**

> Parent's question: Li Xiaolai, in family building, you mentioned that the relationship between spouses is also very important. Especially the mutual growth of couples. I really admire couples who grow together, but my partner is the one who lags behind. He doesn't read parenting books, I study finance, he doesn't study. I invite him to family education sessions, but he doesn't come... Personal growth is actually in a state of stagnation.
>
>
>
> Once I talked to him about personal growth, he said he's already over 30 and just like that...
>
>
>
> His thinking has solidified. In his mind, after university, there's no concept of learning or growth anymore. For such a partner, how can I gradually lead him forward? What methods do you have, Teacher?

For someone without self-motivation, theoretically, there's nothing we can do. But since he's your family member, you can't just give up.

What can we do? We can only complete this by long-term patient influence.

**The first thing is patience.** Where should this patience be placed? It's about **gradually building a positive cycle or apositive feedback loop (positive cycle).**

This world has two kinds of cycles: one is a positive cycle, and the other is a negative cycle.

A positive cycle has an interesting phenomenon. When people get a cold, it's actually because their immunity has dropped. When immunity improves, the cold naturally goes away.

But many people don't know that a cold is actually untreatable. It can only be recovered through the body's immune system returning to balance. So they go to the hospital and ask for cold medicine.

Doctors know how colds work, so they often give these people placebos—sugar pills that look like medicine.

These people believe that taking this "medicine" will make them better, so they take it. After taking it, they carry the expectation that they will get better, so they are less anxious and less uncomfortable. Then they sleep better, and because they sleep better, their immune function recovers faster. Because their immune function recovers faster, the virus is defeated, and they enter a positive cycle.

But if they stop taking the medicine, they enter a negative cycle. Their brain starts to feel anxious, thinking they're sick because of coughing and sweating, so they don't sleep well.

What happens when they don't sleep well? The time it takes for their immunity to recover is longer, and it might even decline.

What happens when their immunity declines? Their symptoms get worse. That's a negative cycle.

Positive and negative cycles are common in life. For example, exercise is like that.

Many people don't know that those who run continuously actually get more enjoyment. After running for 45 minutes, their brains start secreting a lot of dopamine, and they feel great, so they want to run again next time.

Those who have never run before can only feel tired during the run. Because they're tired, they don't want to run. They don't want to run, so they don't feel good, and they don't want to run anymore. That's a negative cycle.

How can a person get through this difficult period?

It's like this. Many people only start to enter this state after running continuously for six months. If they get through those six months, they enter this state and continue in a positive cycle. If they can't get through it, they go back to the negative cycle forever.

So **patience is very important. Don't think your partner will always be like this. He just hasn't entered the positive cycle yet. So you need a long time of patience.**

Exercise is the easiest way to see visible changes. In the past more than half a year, I've lost about 20 kilograms, so now I'm quite slim, much slimmer than last year and the year before.

When you see the people around you change, it gives you a strong motivation to change too. So don't rush. First, change yourself, and you'll win.
