What are the characteristics of the credit cycle? What are the features of the expansion and contraction phases of credit?

Characteristics:

The credit cycle is highly sensitive to the economic cycle and has a significant impact on other aspects of the economy and other cycles. The economy may only fluctuate slightly, and corporate earnings fluctuate a bit more than the economy, but credit fluctuates much more than both. The credit window opens wide and then suddenly closes... Just as the title of this investment memo suggests. I believe the credit cycle is the most volatile and influential among all cycles. Therefore, we should pay close attention to the credit cycle. A small fluctuation in the economic cycle can lead to a significant fluctuation in the tightness of credit, and a large fluctuation in the credit cycle can have a huge impact on asset price fluctuations, which in turn can affect the fluctuation of the economy itself.

Importance:

First, capital or credit is an essential component of the production process, so whether one can timely obtain sufficient additional capital investment largely determines the growth capacity of enterprises (and the economy). Second, enterprises and individuals must obtain capital to refinance when existing debts mature. Third, many economic entities rely on the credit market to operate smoothly, and financial institutions represent a particularly special and exaggerated case. Fourth, signals from the credit market can have a significant psychological impact.

The process of credit expansion and contraction is simple: the economy enters a period of prosperity; capital providers are doing well, increasing their capital base; because there are few bad news, the risks of loans and investments seem to have decreased; risk aversion disappears; financial institutions begin to expand their business, that is, provide more capital; financial institutions compete for market share, and in doing so, lower requirements (such as lowering interest rates), lower lending standards, provide more capital for a specific transaction, and relax loan agreement terms. Economic prosperity leads to credit expansion, credit expansion leads to the foolish issuance of loans, the issuance of loans leads to significant losses, significant losses cause lenders to stop issuing loans, and the stoppage of loans causes the end of economic prosperity. Thus, one leads to the next, and the chain reaction continues endlessly.

What role did the credit cycle play in the 2008 financial crisis?

The foundational environment of the global financial crisis was internal to the financial system and the credit cycle. The key developments that formed the basis of the global financial crisis were neither caused by a widespread economic boom nor by a general rise in corporate profits. These critical events did not occur across the entire corporate environment or a broader global context. Instead, the global financial crisis was largely a financial phenomenon, entirely caused by the behavior of financial players. The main forces behind creating this cycle were: capital that was extremely easy to obtain; a lack of sufficient experience and caution, leading to unchecked enthusiasm throughout the process; highly imaginative financial engineering; the separation of loan decision-making from loan holding; and irresponsible and blatant greed.

Where are we currently in the credit cycle?

The global financial crisis showed that the credit cycle was at the bottom since the Great Depression. The debt market has always been marked by widespread conservatism, meaning that excessive upward movement is restricted, so most bubbles have occurred in the stock market. The difficulty of refinancing and the further progression of defaults have led us to see the market's assessment of asset prices returning to rationality. Take commercial real estate as an example; the market is moving back from a comparative approach to valuation to a discounted cash flow valuation model. In recent years, many companies have obtained loans by continuously raising the valuation of real estate, using a model of acquisition and re-mortgaging to push commercial asset prices to very high levels. Credit contraction has made this game unsustainable, and asset prices have started to fall. The decline in asset prices will lead to a contraction in corporate assets, an increase in leverage ratios, and further concerns among financial institutions. Are we approaching the end of the credit contraction cycle? Not yet. We need to look at assets individually. For example, real estate has been most severely affected by the credit contraction cycle, and future liquidity in real estate will further contract, with prices falling further. Other assets need to be evaluated based on their specific characteristics and how they are affected by the credit cycle and liquidity issues to determine whether they are or are becoming profitable.

In which phase of the credit cycle (expansion or contraction) is it beneficial for investors? Why?

Excellent investment does not come from buying assets with good quality, but from buying assets with high value for money—assets that are of good quality and low in price, with high potential returns and limited risk. The best buying opportunities that meet these conditions are mostly when the credit market is not excitedly expanding but rather fearfully contracting. The phase when the credit cycle suddenly closes its door is most helpful in creating a situation where there are plenty of cheap assets, more so than any other factor. Elon Musk once said about the Chinese market that there are certainly a lot of bad assets in China today, but that doesn't necessarily mean they are good investment opportunities. The key is whether the price is appropriate.

Only during the credit contraction phase can we buy assets that are priced low, have high potential returns, and offer a high risk premium. This is beneficial for our investments. For our regular investments, our long-term returns come from the bear market, and this is also the phase where we can continue to invest regularly and buy at low prices. By understanding the credit cycle, we can more firmly adhere to our regular investment strategy, without changing or modifying it, and just proceed step by step.
