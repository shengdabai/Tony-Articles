The definition of a cycle: The Chinese dictionary defines a cycle in physics as "a complete alternating process in which a phenomenon reaches a maximum and minimum value, eventually returning to a final value equal to the initial value." In mathematics, a cycle is defined as "a sequence composed of a set of factors, in which the original cyclic order of the elements remains unchanged." In other words, the patterns followed by cycles in physics and mathematics are very regular. A cycle ends and returns to its starting point, which is what we call "repeating in cycles." This is because the time and path of these fluctuations are always the same. The Cambridge Dictionary defines a cycle as "a group of events that happen in a particular order, one following the other, and are often repeated."

> Cycles follow the following rules: In summary, there are three main rules:
>
> First, cycles are curved, or they were originally curved or bent, but they never go straight.
>
> Second, history does not repeat the details of the past, but it does repeat similar processes.
>
> Third, avoid the middle and go to the extremes. The events that happen in a cycle should not be seen as one after another, but rather as one event triggering the next, with clear causal relationships between them.

Are the rules of cycles really reliable? Investors need to acknowledge that the market has cycles. When making investment decisions, one must assess the current real-world environment to understand where the current investment environment, corporate production conditions, and all other factors affecting value assessment stand in the cycle, and thus judge the general trend of the future to make investment strategies. The author mentions that I found that acknowledging my own ignorance actually gave me a significant advantage over most other investors, because most other investors don't understand cycles, don't pay attention to cycles, and don't consider what actions we should take based on these cycles.

> People often make assumptions without solid and reliable evidence.

Judging and inferring with self-assurance, while many people don't realize they are making assumptions without evidence, believing they are speaking with confidence. This is really scary. For example, in the investment process, there are always people with their own theories. No matter what you say, they have their own explanations and believe they are logical and well-supported. It's really hard to recognize oneself. We are easily influenced by emotions and don't engage in rational thinking and analysis. Logical thinking is really a skill we need to work hard on. Reliable evidence is not as easy to obtain as most people imagine. The reason is that such evidence requires a lot of time to research and verify, and more importantly, it also needs time to be tested. Giving an answer or proposing an idea is easy, but whether it actually fits the facts and whether it has rigorous arguments are things that need serious consideration. I really admire Teacher Li Xiaolai's analysis and conclusions, because the teacher conducts rigorous logical thinking, is well-supported, and has been verified over a long time. As the teacher said, he only talks about things he has actually done. I was deeply moved. Often, I think of something and just say it out, without seriously practicing it and achieving results, which is not up to the test. Be practical, do it well, and then talk about it. Think more, verify more, learn logic well, and keep going (´-ω-`)
