# Self-Justification

133rd Original Piece

Have you ever felt like the people around you are all pretty opinionated, their words seeming logical, yet something about them feels off?

I used to think the people around me were really smart, each with their own insights. After studying logic, I came across a concept that seemed to explain why that was.

Critical Thinking literally translates to "critical thinking," but the word "critical" carries a negative connotation. It seems like without some form of criticism or negation, it wouldn't be called critical thinking. Some people translate it as "independent thinking," which can easily be confused with "independent thinking."

The specific definition of critical thinking is something you only learn by studying it. What I want to say is that this ability is rare among most people.

Everyone seems to have their own ideas and opinions, but when you compare them, they're often not that different. Some answers that seem to stand out at first glance appear impressive, but upon closer inspection, they're just self-justification.

People who lack logic can't do anything well.

No matter how you say it, they're always right. The problem isn't with you—it's with them, because they lack logic.

Learning logic is particularly helpful. Learning to recognize various logical fallacies helps you realize how unreliable people can be.

Next time when you say, "I believe you," take a moment to think: are you believing the person, or are you believing their reasoning? That's a big difference.

Human nature tends to avoid situations that are unfavorable to us. We use language to create situations that are favorable to ourselves. When someone starts talking nonsense, you've already realized they're self-justifying. In that case, you should shut up and leave quickly.

Self-justification involves using various seemingly correct reasons to explain things. Is there any basis? Any thinking? Any logic?

Never let language carry you away. When your emotions run high, your mind should follow.

Here's an interesting phenomenon. During a live stream, a host is very talkative and has strong selling skills. You feel like you've gained a lot. Suddenly, you have an idea: record the stream and watch it again later.

Something magical happens. After converting the audio into text, you look at it carefully…

The words are imprecise, the sentences don't make sense, and the statements are hard to justify. What's going on? Why didn't I notice this during the live stream?

This is a normal phenomenon. During a live stream, we're led by the host, and there's basically no time for serious thinking. We enjoy the process of being guided.

Text, however, gives us time to analyze, to read word for word. Can you say whether it stands up to scrutiny? That's why you end up buying so many things that seem useful during a live stream.

Whether it's for face or because you really have a valid point, aren't we all self-justifying when we argue? Are our arguments really logical?

Being influenced by emotions is normal. When you realize it, take a moment to calm down and read a book!

Here are three books I recommend to help you learn critical thinking:

* *Beyond the Senses*
* *Reason and Position*
* *I Think for Myself*

Finally, here's a secret: sometimes when you're in a bad mood or dealing with something annoying, just write an article, and you'll feel better!

You ask me why I wrote this article. Maybe it's because I used to be one of those self-justifying people too.
