Note 97

A week gone, I was completing the most important thing of my summer vacation—finishing the assessment for the International Teaching Trainer Program. This experience made me, proud as I am, lower my head and seriously reflect on my growth. Here are three insights I'd like to share with you, which might be helpful for you too!

**01 A Good System Comes with Its Own Evaluation**

One of the most important issues in organizational development is the structure of the organization. A well-built structure will give the organization long-term potential. More importantly, the organization comes with its own evaluation system, which is constantly optimizing and adjusting.

I don't know how long it's been since you, the reader, were last evaluated. Since leaving school, there seems to be no real evaluation or assessment, or at least not a systematic, all-around one. More often, it's performance-based, with results externalized. It's simple, but also complex, because there are many reasons behind good performance. Sometimes you might achieve good results without even realizing it, but that's not our concern.

Without a standardized and systematic evaluation system, we can only rely on experience and intuition to consider our current development. The conclusion we're likely to reach is that it's "okay" or "let's talk about it later." The impact of this on long-term development is obvious.

The trainer program showed me what a self-evaluating system looks like. Now, compared to before, I have a clearer and more defined evaluation standard and development path. One by one, the evaluation indicators naturally emerge, focusing on the big picture and long-term development, and constructive suggestions naturally arise.

Establishing a business evaluation system is the same. The organization has its own evaluation, and personal growth also has its own evaluation. It's hard not to develop. Systematically review the current situation and establish a standard evaluation system. Seriously consider this suggestion.

**02 How Important It Is to Turn Bad Things into Good Ones**

I've been used to doing things my own way. When being evaluated and marked by results, it's natural to feel unhappy, especially when the results aren't great. The trainers come from all over the world, and I, as the only Asian, found English to be my disadvantage. The training participants are mostly European, and the online environment made it even more confusing.

Was my listening poor? Did I not respond to the participants' issues? Did I make too many expression errors? Were there problems with the logic and structure of my plan? All these questions came one after another, and for a while, I was questioning myself—should I quit and try again next time?

The trainer feedback didn't pass. It was like a bolt from the blue. I fell into self-doubt again. Once more, I had failed. When I talked to a long-time friend, she said I had succeeded too much. Maybe she's right. I can't accept myself failing like this, which again shows I've fallen into my comfort zone, and my growth has slowed down.

Bad things came one after another—training didn't go smoothly, assessments kept failing, and there were other projects going on at the same time. For a moment, I wanted to give up, to lie down and do nothing. It really hit me hard.

Li Xiaolai mentioned in his July sharing about the bad things he faced. I listened and felt ashamed. What I've faced is nothing compared to that. Is it really that bad? Can you accept the worst? Let's start over. Plus the cost of time and money, and the cost of ignorance.

Everything is just my own problem. Reflect on myself, reorganize, and try again. Even if I continue to fail, I'll accept it, because my ability and accumulation aren't enough yet. That's actually a good thing.

Turn bad things into good ones, and face them with patience.

**03 Be an Evolving Machine**

Maybe all motivational stories are about enduring hardship and then gaining sweetness. Although I realized I passed the assessment by luck, I still found myself thinking, this is just the beginning. What I need to do is understand myself and keep iterating.

On the path of continuous improvement, one key point to clearly recognize is that "change" is the core. But isn't changing just making a mistake? Without mistakes, without failure, without encountering bad people or bad things, what are we changing?

Everything is a good thing, just as Uncle Mao said, everything is an advantage.

Knowing and doing are a world apart. That's fine. First, know, then slowly do it. Every step counts. Take it one step at a time, and walk steadily.

Li Xiaolai mentioned a very important concept: be an evolving machine. At any time, focus on your own development, keep iterating, and focus on yourself. That's the way to go.

Since August, we've been evolving together, continuously iterating, and just do it!

I'm done writing. Thank you for your support and likes!
