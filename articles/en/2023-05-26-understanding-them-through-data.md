Data Thinking to See Reality

From the Statista data website, we can see that in 2021, the number of people in China with assets of about 1 million U.S. dollars was approximately 4.07 million. Note that this is asset value. If we exclude real estate, how many people actually have a million in disposable funds? Looking at the world, millionaires are not as numerous as we imagine.

According to the National Bureau of Statistics, the average disposable income per resident in 2021 was 35,000 yuan. Based on tax information, the number of people earning over 1 million yuan in 2020 was around 700,000. Considering the impact of the pandemic, this number is likely even lower, with the proportion below 0.05%.

Why do I emphasize these two sets of data? It comes from my observations and consultations on WeChat Channels, where I noticed some people have a misconception about million-dollar live streams, expecting to become million-dollar streamers. I want the majority of ordinary people to understand the reality.

Theoretically, there is a proportion of people earning a million in every industry. It doesn’t mean that every person in the short video or live streaming field is wealthy. This misconception is closely related to the development of the internet. Because we see many videos that present the image of effortless million-dollar earnings, which we might actually believe?

Short videos bring us information we’ve never paid attention to or couldn’t have known before. The virtual world brings us more anxiety and less focus—was that what we wanted?

In the information age, you’ll see and hear too many so-called opportunities. But are they relevant to us? Is it necessary to care about so many things? Take "riding the trend" for example—posting sensational or novel views. Can you say it and believe it?

I support everyone trying WeChat Channels. Use data to speak, be true to yourself, do what you're good at and enjoy, and have more people like you, more sales revenue. It's fine to be ordinary and simple. Keep going.

User and Data Analysis

In the operation and development of WeChat Channels, user analysis and data mining are essential to understand the target audience and optimize the user experience. So, how can we conduct user analysis and data mining for WeChat Channels?

The first step is to describe the ideal user of WeChat Channels. Before understanding the current media audience, you need to define the ideal audience for WeChat Channels. This helps you understand your audience, their needs and interests, and develop corresponding strategies.

The second step is to collect data. WeChat Channels operation data can be collected through social media analysis tools, Google Analytics, mobile app analytics, and third-party market research reports.

The third step is to analyze your audience. This analysis will answer questions such as who your audience is, how much time they spend on WeChat Channels, and which content types are most popular. This helps you develop strategies tailored to your audience, build a target market, and develop your brand.

The fourth step is to monitor competitors. By observing competitors' activities, you can understand their strategies, what content is popular, and how to increase reputation and supporters. This is achieved through competitive analysis.

The fifth step is to listen to audience feedback. By monitoring social media, replying to comments, and tracking emails, you can understand their opinions, ideas, and needs, and adjust your strategies and content accordingly.

The final suggestion is to continuously track data and adjust strategies. What users like and what brands they support change over time, so you need to keep observing and analyzing data, and adjust your strategies accordingly.

Data analysis is the core method for evaluating the quality of short videos. It doesn’t need to be very professional, but it must be sensitive. Use data to understand the underlying user behavior.

There are multiple indicators in data analysis, and they should be viewed together. For example, completion rate: short videos are more likely to be fully watched, so analyze it in combination with video length. Medium or long videos don’t necessarily have to be avoided. Or, like likes and comments, calculate like rate and comment rate based on views to understand the popularity of the video. For example, a 4% like rate is normal, but if it’s unusually high, it might be because the views are low. Similarly, comment rate can reflect user experience with the content.

Data analysis requires practice, and the practice object is your own work. Keep posting content and frequently review and analyze it. You’ll become more familiar with your audience. Use specific numbers to speak, and quantify results.

The results of data analysis help us optimize our operations. For example, if likes are low and the spread is limited, you can first ask friends and followers to support. What about comments? Does your video encourage users to comment? For example, end with a question to prompt users to share their thoughts. You should know that when users comment, the video may have been played multiple times.

Analyzing data sources is very helpful for understanding user origins. For example, if a high proportion of users come from friends, you should consider encouraging friends to share. If recommendations are high, it means the content is popular on the platform. Combined with data analysis, this will become a powerful tool for our operations.

Short videos have a long-tail effect. Videos continue to spread, and with continuous creation and analysis, one day your video might go viral. I believe you can achieve it.

Telling a Story:

Little White saw WeChat Channels and found there wasn’t much content. He thought it was a business opportunity and started making WeChat Channels, updating short videos every day.

Little White was happy creating content, found his content unique, but the data didn’t improve. He was confused and wondered if he should try live streaming.

Every day he started live streaming, but saw others getting tips while he didn’t. He started doubting whether WeChat Channels actually had traffic and could make money.

After some time, Little White posted on his friend circle that he was switching to Douyin, because his content could be monetized there. But he didn’t want to give up WeChat Channels, so he started distributing the same content across multiple platforms.

As he continued, Little White found himself hitting a growth bottleneck. Was self-media not suitable for him? He then saw cross-border e-commerce…

The domestic market is a red sea. Doing overseas markets must have some opportunities, so he decided to go into cross-border e-commerce.

Little White’s story may continue. If there are similarities, it might just be a coincidence. I want to ask, where did we really spend our time?
