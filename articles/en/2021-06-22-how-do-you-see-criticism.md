82nd Original Piece

My first systematic learning of critical thinking was through a course on Dedao, titled *15 Lectures on Critical Thinking* by Teacher Cai Yu. The name of the course itself was very appealing to me at the time. Back then, my understanding of critical thinking was that it meant not blindly believing what others say, not trusting experts, authorities, or textbooks. Of course, it wasn’t about being intentionally nitpicky or disliking everything. That was my rough idea. As I continued learning, I realized my understanding was quite shallow and I hadn’t grasped the essence. This was due to insufficient thinking training and lack of reading related books. Looking back now, the three books recommended by Teacher Cai are indeed essential to learn: *Beyond Sensation*, *Learning to Ask Questions*, and *Reason and Position*. Reading a book a hundred times, its meaning becomes clear. Reading it multiple times is a must.

> Overdoing critical thinking is, in fact, the enemy of critical thinking.

Most of the time, we don’t need to activate critical thinking—life’s majority of things or arguments aren’t subjects for critical thinking. For example, in daily life, you like fried dough sticks, I prefer porridge, we each have our own preferences, and there’s no need to justify our choices with scientific evidence or claim they’re good for our health. Or take the pandemic as an example: many of us were just scrambling to buy masks, which is normal. But when it came to double yellow honeysuckle, I even believed it might work. I opened the app with that idea, and it was already sold out! Not every issue or every person’s irrational behavior requires you to step in and fight against it. You don’t have to prove you’re right. I’ve also seen many people using the guise of rationality or critical thinking to argue with others. Online, there’s a lot of mutual hostility, each with their own reasons. That’s why Li Xiaolai teacher told us not to worry about others’ affairs and not to jump into unnecessary commotion.

> Critical thinking is a way of thinking, while tolerance is a principle of dealing with the world. Learning to be tolerant is also an application of critical thinking. For example, excellent martial artists are not necessarily competitive or prone to fights. I started practicing martial arts as a child, but my foundation was average, and I kept training. When someone says you’re a martial artist, they either ask you to perform a set of punches or challenge you. I think I don’t need to argue with them. Just politely decline. Being tolerant helps improve our critical thinking!

> Finally, let’s talk about the three stages of thinking. The first stage is naive certainty, where you blindly believe things, like how we used to think in school. We were willing to believe in many things. Gradually, we move into the stage of informed confusion, where each person has their own reasoning, and various models are at play in our thinking. A great person can hold two opposing models in their mind at the same time. I think I’m currently struggling in this stage. As for the third stage, critical thinking, that’s what I aspire to. Calling it independent thinking feels more accurate, as it avoids the literal interference of words. Why should we learn independent thinking? It’s an important logical foundation. Learning to think independently is our metacognitive ability—thinking about our thinking. It allows us to understand our thinking process from the root. Independent thinking has two aspects: asking questions and controlling mental activity. Learning to ask questions has always been my required course. Asking questions helps us better understand the situation. Sometimes, just asking questions leads to clarity naturally. Of course, you can also use questioning to control your emotions. For example, when you’re angry, ask yourself why you’re angry, then what you did, and then ask more detailed questions. Gradually, you won’t be angry anymore. As for controlling mental activity, it might be a long-term ability that independent thinkers cultivate. For instance, the meditation we’re currently practicing is a way to enhance our metacognitive abilities. I think this is the beginning of controlling our minds. It’s something we can do bit by bit. No one can do it perfectly from the start, right? (≧∇≦*) Let’s work together to improve our ability to think independently!
