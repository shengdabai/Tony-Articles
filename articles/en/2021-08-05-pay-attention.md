Attention

Attention is a medical or psychological concept, part of the cognitive process, and refers to the selective concentration of perceptual awareness that raises the level of consciousness in response to a specific stimulus. For example, listening intently to someone speaking while ignoring the conversations of others in the room; or using a phone while driving a car. Attention is one of the most researched topics in psychology. Among many cognitive processes related to human consciousness, attention is considered the most specific, due to its close relationship with perception.

From Wikipedia, we see that attention is part of cognition, but a more concrete understanding of attention may be lacking. So how should we understand it? Li Xiaolai's course first tells us about three "pits" — each of them is so close to me. Although subjectively many people don't want to admit they've ever fallen into them, for instance, I used to think I hadn't. Take a moment to calm down and think again:

Mysteriously joining the crowd, rushing to follow the crowd, worrying about others' hearts and livers

Look, it seems we wouldn't do such things, wouldn't fall into such traps. Let me talk about myself. When I hear distant arguments or see a group of people gathered together, something pops up — curiosity. Some say curiosity kills the cat, I don't know, but I do know that this so-called curiosity makes me move forward without thinking. I start to join the crowd... But why do I join the crowd? I don't know the reason, I just feel like if everyone is doing it, I should do it too. Or maybe it's the "herd effect," always wanting not to miss out on any information. Calm down and think again — this really easily breaks my attention. For example, I'm listening to a Dedao course, and suddenly I jump out to join the crowd. Thinking about it, it's really not worth it. Then I start to blame myself — what's the point of watching? Why did I go over? Nothing really happened, did it? If something did happen, would joining the crowd be okay? Of course not. Most of the time, these things have nothing to do with me, and I don't need to use my attention on them.

But I also thought, if it's an emergency, I should rush over to help, call the police or an ambulance. Think one layer deeper — if I must participate, I would definitely do it. But often, it's not just me. What if no one dares to "help"? Maybe that's possible. Then, when the time comes, we should act. Such situations are rare, and most of the time, I'd just quietly leave.

Following the crowd — this description naturally makes many people avoid it. I'm different, I'm unique. At this moment, I remembered that popular song as the BGM — "We Are Different." Maybe, we are different. Thinking back to university life, many people thought they were free after the college entrance exam, thinking that university would be an easy ride. This has to do with our university's "broad admission, strict graduation" system, but I don't think the entrance threshold is that high. Although I'm not a top student, I think that just getting into university with scores of 6 or 7 in a few subjects doesn't mean much, but it does show that I did well in those subjects in high school. That's just limited to those subjects. The trend is to study hard, and this crowd is too scary. Everyone is all focused on jumping in, and after the exam, they think they're free — but that's just the beginning...

Entering university, we come to campus with various backgrounds, but everyone's goals and thoughts are different. Most people just want to graduate smoothly and have a happy, easy life. Failing classes has become the norm. Some students' lives are so similar — dorm, cafeteria, classroom, the three points in a line. And then there's gaming, binge-watching, playing around... Before I know it, it's senior year, and the well-known autumn recruitment season comes. A lot of job applications, high salary, good location, good benefits, and less work... Then, let's just fantasize. When it's time for the interview, the interviewer asks, "Why should we choose you?" I might say, "I'm different from them." But is that really true? People follow the crowd, because it's too costly to be unique.

Then there's worrying about others' hearts and livers — is this really related to us? In short, it's just boredom. Others' affairs — don't they take care of them themselves? Think about it — most people have a gossip heart. First, take care of your own affairs, focus on yourself, and invest in yourself!
