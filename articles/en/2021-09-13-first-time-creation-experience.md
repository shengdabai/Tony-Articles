Let me introduce myself—I’m TonyMax Shengdabai, a lifelong growth enthusiast born after 1995.

What comes to mind when you hear the name TonyMax Shengdabai? There's a movie called *Big Hero 6*, and its main character is Baymax, a magical and loving robot with three labels: a grower, a capable one, and a sincere one. My English name is Tony, not the hairdresser who does wash, cut, and blow-dry. I used to be an English teacher, and my students loved me for being an interesting, slightly plump teacher. I’m TonyMax Shengdabai, a warm-hearted growth companion!

Today, I want to talk about my first experience with personal creation. Everyone can engage in deep creation. Creation is a kind of entrepreneurship, and this entrepreneurial journey lasts a lifetime. Every friend can start this venture. I divide the entrepreneurial process into three steps.

**Step 1: The Grower from 0 to 1**

I started my creation journey in February of this year. In fact, I had no resources at all. After I decided to resign, I lost contact with most of my colleagues and former students. For me, the only thing left was a lot of free time, so I chose to pursue two master's degrees, spending more time on self-improvement and growth. I had no connections or resources, like courses or products. I started from scratch, building my personal IP. I don’t like to call myself a newbie; I just say I’m just starting out. On February 6th, my birthday, I opened a WeChat Channel, and from then on, I became a video content creator, someone who can play with short videos. Soon after, I also opened my own WeChat Official Account, continuing to write and create.

Looking back on my nearly seven months of creation. I’ve written 297 articles and created 360 videos. These numbers are still growing. But for me, the actual numbers don’t matter. What matters is that I create every day, sometimes more than once a day. It wasn’t hard to start at first—just keep in mind that you can always begin creating whenever you want.

From 0 to 1, breaking through psychological barriers, we’re just starting out. Slowly refine, and first, be a personal brand entrepreneur.

**Step 2: The Capable One Who Speaks Through Work**

I used to be an English teacher, and one of the most important parts of our teaching is skill training, which is divided into listening, speaking, reading, and writing. Listening and reading are considered input skills, while speaking and writing are output skills. Without enough input, output is difficult. First, you need to have your own work.

During the creation process, we need to push ourselves to produce. The way to produce is through speaking and writing, so I chose to present my thoughts through videos and articles. In the early stages of my creation, I had a lot of anxiety—like not knowing how to create or whether my content would get attention. So I joined some courses and communities to learn, but during this process, I also encountered some pitfalls and tried some courses that weren’t very helpful.

But very fortunately, I learned to think independently during this process. Sometimes being guided is good, but failing and trying things that don’t work well are also beneficial for our creation.

For example, in my video creation, I tried all kinds of content at first—text, images, editing, or vlog-style voiceovers. Through continuous practice, I found my own way of expression, using fun animations or situational videos. At first, my videos only got a few likes, and the overall views didn’t exceed 100. Did I feel discouraged? Not really. Every like, every comment, every heart, is the biggest motivation for my growth. Because of them, I feel I can do better. Thank you to everyone who supports me.

In the subsequent creation process, I found the content I could create and started learning from similar accounts. By observing, I found a more suitable way to communicate. My data gradually improved, and most importantly, I met many high-quality video creators through my video channel. We support each other. After some time, my video channel’s followers grew from just a few people to 4,400 in seven months. I’m grateful to every person who follows me because I know, for someone with no resources, each person’s trust and attention is a great honor.

This motivates me to update videos every day. The reason is simple: so many people are looking forward to your content and trust you. I need to use better work to return that trust. Speak through your work, connect through your work!

**Step 3: The Sincere One**

Some people say creation requires persistence. I don’t think so. I believe good creation is the result of continuous accumulation and daily practice. When we think that something needs to be persisted, we usually don’t do it for long because we think it’s painful or torturous, and we have to force ourselves to continue. For me, creating videos is so joyful and fun. Every time I gain a new supporter, I feel very happy. So there’s nothing to persist—just keep doing it, keep practicing, and gradually, you’ll be seen and trusted.

At the beginning of this month, I chose to open a live stream on my video channel, and I entered a very happy state. I could chat with my family, friends, and even strangers. Some people said I was natural and happy, and I really was. This is a place for casual chats, a personal space for you and your users to chat and share life. It’s so fun and joyful. But some people say live streams need to provide a lot of useful content. I understand that ordinary people can only provide limited useful content, and it doesn’t necessarily mean you have to deliver allpractical substance throughout the stream. On the contrary, a genuine and natural lifestyle might be more interesting to everyone. Chatting and telling stories can be more important than just delivering a few points.

Why do I recommend people to do video channels, whether it's posting videos or live streaming? It's a platform for self-expression. Do you remember the big billboards on Times Square? If you want to be there, how much would you have to pay? But today, you have a huge, always-on billboard that you can use to show whatever you want. That’s WeChat Channels. Within the WeChat ecosystem, this is essentially an internet world. In such a large traffic pool, you don’t need to worry about traffic. What matters is the quality of your content. So create freely, share your dynamic social profile, and one day, you’ll meet people who share your interests. How interesting and joyful that is!

Show yourself genuinely, connect with users through trust, and we are all unique sparks in the world.

Through these three steps, your personal image is basically formed. In users’ eyes, you are no longer just a name, but a three-dimensional figure. Even if we’ve never met, trust has already been established. Do you know who I am now?

Let me summarize the key points I’ve learned from my video channel creation process:

    1. A clear and specific positioning is not as important as having fun, informative, and resonant tags.
    2. This is your billboard. How you use it should be planned.
    3. Whether you do videos, live streams, or both—choose a mode that suits you.
    4. Traffic is less important than content. Find your direction by looking at similar accounts. Watch more, try more.
    5. Data analysis helps you find what your users like. Always consider the user’s perspective.
    6. Join video channel communities, connect with high-quality creators, and having support will help you grow faster.
    7. Everything is a slow accumulation. Be patient and go step by step!

That’s seven points. It’s said that adding one more will make you forget. These seven useful points are my hope to help you start your creation journey. If you want to learn more, I recommend the book *WeChat Internet Plain People Entrepreneurship* by Li Xiaolai!

We will all become better versions of ourselves. Creation can start anytime. You won’t know how excellent you are unless you try! Bai gives you strength—growth, capability, sincerity. Together, build your own personal brand.
