##

> Attention is a concept from medicine or psychology, part of the cognitive process, and refers to an increased level of awareness that focuses perception on a specific stimulus. For example, listening intently to someone while ignoring the conversations of others in the room, or using a phone while driving. Attention is one of the most studied topics in psychology. Among many cognitive processes related to human consciousness, attention is considered the most specific, due to its close relationship with perception.

From Wikipedia, we see that attention is part of cognition, but a more concrete understanding of attention may be lacking. So how should we understand it? Li Xiaolai’s course first tells us about three “pits” we might fall into. Each of these “pits” feels so close to me, even though many people subjectively refuse to admit they’ve ever fallen into them. For instance, I used to think I hadn’t fallen into any of them, but after a moment of calm reflection, I reconsidered:

>Joining the crowd for no reason, rushing to follow the herd, and worrying yourself sick over other people's affairs

Look, it seems we wouldn’t do such things, wouldn’t fall into such traps. Let me talk about myself! Hearing a distant argument or seeing a group of people gathered together, something pops up—curiosity. Some say curiosity kills the cat, but I don’t know. What I do know is that this so-called curiosity makes me move forward instinctively. I start to join the crowd... But why do I join in? I don’t know the reason, it’s just that everyone is doing it, so I should do it too, or maybe it’s the “herd effect”—I don’t want to miss out on any information. Calm down and think again, and I realize this really disrupts my attention. For example, while listening to a Dedao course, I suddenly jump out and rush to join the crowd. Thinking about it, it’s really not worth it. Then I start to complain to myself: What’s the point of watching? Why did I go over? Nothing really happened. If something did happen, would I be justified in joining in? Of course not. Most of the time, these things have nothing to do with me, and I don’t need to spend my attention on them. But I’ve also thought, if there’s an emergency, I should go help, call the police, or get an ambulance. Think one layer deeper—if I must participate, I would do it. But often, it’s not just me. What if no one dares to “help”? Maybe that’s possible. Then, when the time comes, we should act. But such situations are rare. Most of the time, I’d just quietly walk away.

Following the crowd, this description naturally makes many people avoid it. I’m different. I’m unique. At this moment, I recall that popular song as the BGM—*We Are Different*. Maybe, we are different. Thinking back to my university days, many people thought they were free after the college entrance exam, thinking that university would be an easy ride. This has to do with our university’s “broad admission, strict graduation” system, but I don’t think the entrance threshold is that high. Although I’m not a top student, I think that just getting a few subjects with high scores on the entrance exam doesn’t mean much. It only shows that I did well in those subjects in high school, limited to that knowledge. The trend is to study hard, and this trend is terrifying. Everyone jumps in, thinking that once they finish the exam, they’re free. But that’s just the beginning... Entering university, we come to campus with various backgrounds, but everyone’s goals and thoughts are different. Most people just want to graduate smoothly and have a happy, easy life. Failing classes has become the norm. Some students’ lives are so similar: dorm, cafeteria, classroom, the three points in a line. Then there’s gaming, binge-watching, and playing around... Before I know it, it’s senior year, and the well-known autumn recruitment season comes. I send out a lot of resumes, hoping for high salaries, good locations, good benefits, and less work. Then, I just fantasize. When I’m interviewed and asked, “Why should we choose you?” I might say, “I’m different.” But is that really true? People tend to follow the crowd, and it’s just too costly to be different.

Then there’s the third pit: worrying about others. Is this really related to us? In short, it’s just idle time. Others’ affairs—why should we worry about them? Others don’t worry about their own matters? Think about it: most people have a gossip heart. First, take care of your own business. Focus on yourself, and invest in yourself!

Li Xiaolai gave the following questions, and I’ll answer them:

> 1. Review what you paid attention to yesterday. List them out and determine whether they are relevant to you or related to your growth. 2. Review what you paid attention to last week. List them out and determine whether they are relevant to you or related to your growth. 3. Review what you paid attention to last month... I know you probably can’t remember at all.

I’ve organized and categorized the things I’ve paid attention to: Practice Group: Writing Course, Logic Course, Fitness Check-in, Group Interaction, Preparation for Practice Group, English Course, Video Creation, Writing Copy, Accumulating Materials, Watching Short Videos. Course Learning: Master’s Specialized Courses, Dedao Daily Learning, Chaos Learning, Fan Deng Listening, Other Content. 007 Not Out, Writing Check-in, Peer Review, Daily Walking, Occasionally Doing Systematic Training, Communication with Family, Sharing with My Brother, Academic Help, Chatting with Friends, Watching Videos...

Some things I really can’t remember. I’m very grateful to the Practice Group. In fact, my attention is easily attracted by external things, and I haven’t been consistent in doing things. The only thing I’ve done is listen to courses, exercise, and daily reflection. Joining the Practice Group, the five core values, I’ve been practicing every day, doing a little bit, and it doesn’t feel forced. I have a lot of motivation. Maybe that’s the charm of the community!

Some other questions:

> 1. Have you ever fallen into any of the three big pits? 2. How do you think you should spend your attention most reasonably? 3. Do you have any better arguments, points, or materials to share with me? 4. During this week, review your daily attention expenditure every day. Believe me, you’ll have great insights from it... Record it down.

I’ve fallen into the first pit, joining the crowd. My curiosity is still strong, and sometimes I get distracted from focus, going to join in without knowing why. For example, WeChat: I’ve turned off notifications, but I still open it occasionally to check for messages. Honestly, I’m currently in a master’s program, and there aren’t that many important things. In fact, it’s hard to define the four quadrants of importance and urgency. Just completing the three most important things is enough. As for messages, worrying about missing out on headlines isn’t important. After all, information streams update every day, or even every minute. Like the US presidential election, I went to check the vote results, but I don’t know why I was particularly interested in this election. Maybe it’s because I was at home due to the pandemic and had nothing else to do!

I believe attention is scarce. Spending it on helping myself grow is necessary. Of course, spending attention on family and friends is also needed. The important thing is what we consider essential at the moment. I often joke with friends, saying, “If I can’t see the sun tomorrow, I won’t have any regrets.” In fact, I’ve never regretted it. I know it’s useless, and if I had another chance, my attention would still be spent on the same things. I’ve always believed that the level of cognition determines behavior. Like before, I did things according to my own ideas, not too concerned about others, but I still felt conflicted and thought about it later. Joining the Practice Group, I know the most important thing is the five things. I’ve been reading and learning, but writing is something I haven’t done much. Now I must practice, and I’ve started to write daily. It’s almost a month now. Spending time with family is also due to the pandemic, which gave me more time to be with them. This feeling is amazing! Helping friends is also my belief. Being a teacher is my choice, and helping others is very fulfilling. As for investing, I never thought about it before, thinking it was something for the rich. Now I’ve started to do regular investing, even if it’s a little, but I’m doing it consistently, practicing it, using the money from my eggshell to invest in the future.

I haven’t thought of any better attention materials yet. The teacher’s explanation and everyone’s sharing have given me a lot of thoughts. I’ll come back to it later. Finally, I reviewed my daily task app, recording daily events, but I didn’t write down the time spent on each task. Recording time will help adjust attention. After reading, I think my focus has improved, allowing me to calm down and complete things one by one, not disturbed by the outside world. This feeling is amazing! But I still can’t help multitasking. Honestly, I feel more efficient that way, but it’s at the cost of my focus. I need to adjust slowly, doing one thing at a time, doing it well, practicing again and again!
