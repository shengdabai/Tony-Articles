# What Did the Food Blogger Do to Deserve This?

Let’s be honest, maybe you’ve already noticed, but I’m a foodie—someone who loves eating, loves experimenting with cooking, and sometimes can’t help but binge on food videos.

Watching food videos is a fascinating experience. There’s always something new to discover, something from a world of diverse cuisines. Even if it’s not something I’d consider gourmet, it’s still a joy to see how food brings people happiness—it’s like an ultimate treat for the senses.

My food journey might have started with this anime, though I’m not sure if it’s anime or not. I’ll forgive myself for not being able to tell the difference. *Shin Chan*—I used to dream of being like Xiao Dangjia, not necessarily becoming a master chef, but just being able to appreciate the flavors was enough to make me happy.

Mr. Chen Jianyi, the real-life inspiration behind Xiao Dangjia, once said, “Those who cook are happy, and those who eat are made happy.” Whether it’s a customer who has tasted his dishes, a student who has attended his classes, or a fellow Japanese-Chinese chef, everyone who knows him notices that he’s always smiling—smiling while talking, smiling while cooking. His smile seems permanently etched on his face, and those who know him well know that it’s genuine.

In *Shin Chan*, the character Xiao Dangjia is deeply passionate about cooking and enjoys sharing the joy of food with others. This almost perfectly mirrors Mr. Chen Jianyi’s lifelong belief and pursuit.

Back to the topic of food videos, sometimes I come across comments and pop-up messages that leave me puzzled—why do so many people seem to have such strong negative intentions?

One psychological motive behind users’ criticism of food videos is the gap between expectation and reality. Many food videos are edited with various elements—heart-shaped cheese, flipping cakes—that make viewers drool. However, when users try to recreate these dishes themselves, they might face difficulties and fail to replicate the perfect results shown in the videos. This gap between expectation and reality can lead to frustration and criticism, as their expectations of the food blogger don’t match the actual outcome.

For example, someone might watch a video on how to make a cake, try to replicate it, but end up with a cake that cracks and doesn’t look like the perfect version in the video. This failure leads them to express dissatisfaction with the food blogger.

Another psychological motive is the skepticism toward the packaging and authenticity of food bloggers. Some users have preferences for a food blogger’s style, language, or behavior. If they find the blogger doesn’t match their ideal image, they may feel inclined to criticize. They might doubt the blogger’s authenticity, believing that they’re more interested in gaining attention than in truly loving food. They think the bloggers are just chasing fame, not real passion for food.

For instance, a user might be unhappy with a food blogger’s language style, thinking their words are exaggerated and aimed at creating hype, rather than showing genuine love for food.

A rational analysis can reveal some flaws in these critical behaviors. Some users lack a full understanding of the process and difficulties involved in food preparation. They only see the final product and the performance, ignoring the effort and skill behind it. At the same time, they overlook the fact that food videos are meant for entertainment and inspiration, not as step-by-step guides. The style and performance of a food blogger are key to attracting viewers, not strict instructional content.

The psychological motives behind users’ criticism of food bloggers can be interpreted through the gap between expectation and reality and the skepticism toward authenticity. However, through rational analysis, we can see that these criticisms often stem from incomplete understanding and over-expectations of food videos.

We should appreciate the performance and entertainment value of food bloggers and understand that the purpose of food videos is not just to provide recipes, but to offer joy and inspiration. Only with a rational evaluation can we truly appreciate the charm of food videos and explore more delicious worlds together with food bloggers.

Besides the gap between expectation and reality and the skepticism toward authenticity, there are other psychological motives that may lead users to criticize food bloggers. One of them is envy and jealousy. Food bloggers showcase delicious dishes and various cooking techniques in their videos, which can easily trigger envy in viewers. Some users may not have the time or ability to prepare the dishes they see, and this unfulfilled desire can turn into criticism, projecting their dissatisfaction onto others.

For example, a user might feel jealous of a food blogger who can effortlessly make complex dishes, and thus criticize them, thinking their food is too hard to replicate.

Additionally, some users criticize the marketing behaviors and commercial collaborations in food videos. In today’s social media era, many food bloggers collaborate with brands or businesses for promotion or product endorsements. These collaborations sometimes raise questions from users, who may doubt whether the food bloggers’ recommendations are genuine or if they have ulterior motives. They may believe that food bloggers are no longer authentic, having abandoned their original purpose for money and profit.

You’ve probably seen users questioning a food blogger’s collaboration with a well-known restaurant, thinking the blogger is just endorsing it for the money, losing the original intent of recommending good food.

However, through rational analysis, we can see that these criticisms may just be users’ subjective guesses without solid evidence. When it comes to commercial collaborations, we need to remain rational and objective. Collaborating with brands is part of a food blogger’s career choice and lifestyle, and it doesn’t mean their sincerity or professionalism should be entirely dismissed. We should focus on the content and enjoyment of food videos, understand the effort and dedication of food bloggers, and give them the respect and understanding they deserve.

Envy, jealousy, and skepticism toward commercial collaborations are all factors that may lead users to criticize food bloggers. However, we should approach these criticisms with rationality and objectivity, not let emotions take over, so we can truly enjoy the fun and inspiration that food videos bring.

Stop being jealous!

Imagine this: because of a busy work schedule, you can only grab takeout every day. Watching a food blogger demonstrate how to make a delicious crab roe bun by hand, you feel an overwhelming sense of envy.

Then you vent in the comments: “This crab roe bun must be something you only eat occasionally. Why keep coming up with new and fancy recipes? If you had the time to make it, you could spend that time creating more practical recipes!”

We all know that social media is flooded with advertisements and brand collaborations. Food bloggers are no exception. They often collaborate with brands for money and benefits.

A mischievous viewer once commented on a food blogger’s video: “Before, I thought you were eating for the sake of food. Now, I think you’re eating for the money.”

However, these opinions don’t always dominate the conversation. Commercial collaborations are part of a food blogger’s career and lifestyle, and that doesn’t mean they’ve lost their sincerity.

We should try to view these collaborations objectively, focusing on the content and enjoyment of food videos, and understand the effort and dedication of food bloggers with more rationality.

After all, collaborations with brands also provide us with more diverse food experiences and information.

Let food videos become a truly beneficial and enjoyable content space. Stop being jealous and enjoy the journey of food!

Behind every food video, there are always critics. Jealousy leads to complaints, and commercial collaborations spark doubts.

However, we should understand that criticism isn’t the only option. We can choose to view things from another angle.

The show-off of food bloggers is the result of their hard work. We have no reason to be jealous; instead, we should draw inspiration from it.

Commercial collaborations are part of their lives. We should remain rational and objective, showing more tolerance and understanding.

Between criticism and understanding, we should approach food videos with a positive attitude, sparking our thoughts on taste and life, and allowing the joy of food to truly nourish our souls.

Let’s share the joy of food together, and also share the growth and inspiration of humanity. Because, food videos are not just entertainment—they are a feast for the soul.

Through food videos, we feel as if we’re in a high-end restaurant, enjoying a refined dining experience, feeling the magic of food, and forgetting our worries and stress.

Food videos bring us endless creativity and inspiration, teaching us unique cooking techniques and ingredient pairings, enriching our food choices, and broadening our horizons.

Food videos also foster communication and sharing between people. In the comment section, we can discuss food topics with other viewers, building a food community, and feeling a sense of connection and belonging.

Food videos are not just a treat for the taste buds—they are also a form of mental relaxation and a social bond. Let’s all explore the beauty of life together in the world of food.
