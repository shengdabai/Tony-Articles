Today, we start with the topic of **"self-learning ability."** If there is one soft skill that is relatively most important, I would definitely advocate for self-learning ability. After a few months, I took the test and went to New Oriental to apply for a job. **The seven years at New Oriental became a turning point in my life.** Later, I started my own business, and the path wasn't very smooth, but that's okay. I have self-learning ability, so I could quickly build a business that actually makes money. Later, I entered the investment field, where no one teaches you. What do I do? Well, with my personality, I would definitely read books, do research, and study. Through a lot of research, I chose my investment strategy and refined it. **A few years later, my life changed again.** In this process, I also realized where my advantages and disadvantages are compared to institutions. So I do analysis. Of course, these analyses have been shared in my books *The Road to Financial Freedom* and *The Power of Compound Investing*. Based on learning and research, I can choose my investment strategy, refine it, and step by step reach where I am today. In this process, I believe the biggest advantage of an individual investor is that the money they manage has no time limit, unlike fund managers who have to liquidate their funds within three to five years. Based on this, I believe my ability to make money outside of the market is very important. Fortunately, I've been self-studying for years, improving and refining my ability to make money outside of the market. So you can imagine, Li Xiaolai isn't a graduate of the Chinese department, and Li Xiaolai isn't naturally suited for writing. But due to various reasons, I took the first step. When I was teaching at New Oriental, I published two books: one was a vocabulary book, and the other was an essay book. When I left New Oriental, I published another book, *How to Treat Time as a Friend*. I realized that most people's problems aren't about not understanding exams, but rather that they waste time. So when it's time for exams or when they need results, they can't produce effective results. So during the seven years I taught at New Oriental, the knowledge I spread in class was more about time management, mindset management, and personal management, rather than English itself. After leaving New Oriental, I have continued to write, and to this day, I still earn my living by teaching and writing. What I want to say is: **Over the years, the improvement in my life has been most importantly due to my self-learning ability.** In other words, almost 100% of the skills I've used in my life weren't taught to me. Schools don't teach these things, or maybe they do, but due to my poor talent, I didn't listen in school. But regardless, fortunately, due to various reasons, I have a relatively good understanding of learning. And I have indeed followed that idea: **"Learn, learn, and learn again."** First, you need to learn what learning is, and then you can go on to learn. Over the years, the self-learning ability I've accumulated is strong enough that I can basically do what I need when I need it, and I can learn it well enough. Based on this experience, I think self-learning ability really ranks first among a person's most important abilities. Of course, there may be some basic abilities we'll talk about later.

**02** Schooling and learning are not the same thing

Actually, I think I would never choose homeschooling. There are many people who are very dissatisfied with the modern education system and have chosen homeschooling, which is independent family education. China also has such examples. For instance, my childhood idol, Mr. Zheng Yuanjie, excluded the modern education system from his family and didn't send his children to school; he taught them at home. There are also many people doing this abroad, and it's common around the world. But we discussed and decided, **we don't think we should completely choose homeschooling.** One important reason is that, besides textbook knowledge, there are many so-called soft skills that are extremely important. For example, people skills, which we believe can't be learned from books and are especially important. In our family's view, **school is a natural and ideal place to develop people skills.** Otherwise, where else can you find such a safe and diverse environment? There are students who are liked and disliked, strong and weak. Even teachers, some are liked and some are disliked, with a lot of diversity. So children should learn to interact with various people here. **For a child's growth, not teaching them how to interact with people, or being able to recognize bad people and stay away from them, is also an important ability.** In our family's view, school is actually a social place, and **learning is truly 99% not happening in school.** This is a decision based on my deep understanding of learning. **Learning in school is just a part of learning, and even a small part.** Schooling and learning are not the same thing. Earlier, we talked about how important social skills are, right? So do schools teach that? They don't. Some unreasonable school rules may subtly destroy many people's social skills. However, our family education believes that the world is like that, with good and bad, unreasonable places, and uncomfortable situations. But that's reality. In the real world, how to be less uncomfortable and less annoying is also a skill. We say that most learning doesn't happen in school because: **in fact, real learning has been done by oneself since childhood.** If we don't have a clear understanding of "learning," we won't have a clear understanding of "school," and we'll make many wrong decisions.
