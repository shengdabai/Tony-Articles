**Rigid Demand**

>Rigid Demand refers to a type of demand in the supply and demand relationship of goods that is less affected by price changes. Consumers tend to continue purchasing such goods even if prices fluctuate, for example, electricity and water. No matter how much the electricity or water company raises prices, you still need to buy them because you can't do without them. These are rigid demands. Further, what is demand? Demand refers to the quantity of goods consumers need under certain price conditions. Both rigid demand and demand are concepts from economics. There is also a relative concept called elastic demand, which refers to a situation where the demand for a product or service changes significantly when its price fluctuates.

Li Xiaolai, in *The Road to Financial Freedom*, points out that often we cannot become excellent because we haven't developed excellent habits. Further, why haven't we developed good habits? Because **we don't consider what we're doing as a rigid demand**.

During the New Year, many platforms love to ask users to write down their New Year wishes. On the Dedao app, there's a time capsule where you fill it out and then it's sent to you a year later. I decided to do it, but I failed to complete one item because I suddenly fell ill and couldn't take the BEC Advanced exam. It's really frustrating, and I spent a whole day lying in a hotel.

Other wishes I did achieve, and the reason was simple: I quit my job and fully prepared for my overseas plans. So, this is my rigid demand—I must complete it. Often, it's not that the task is hard to achieve or that it's a fantasy, but rather that we don't have a reason to keep going. From an economic perspective, this is a demand that is less affected by price changes. So, what makes something become a rigid demand? I think of it as something you must do, whether you like it or not. If it's something you like, and it gradually becomes an essential part of your daily life, then it's a rigid demand.

Many people say learning English is important, and the reason is **because others think it's important**. But I don't feel the importance of English myself. From my own experience, I never spent much time on English learning, and compared to other subjects, it was relatively easy. I only started to learn English in earnest during university, when I majored in civil engineering. Going to Germany for further study was a great choice, so I began my long journey of learning German. After learning German, I realized how wonderful English is—it's so beautiful! English has fewer rules and clearer grammar structures than German. Of course, German is also easy to read, with pronunciation more standardized than English, so it's easy to read, but you just don't know what it means. Through comparative language learning, I discovered more motivation for language study, opening up my perspective and breaking the limits of a single way of expression. Different expressions are a new way of seeing things. For example, what did I do during my German learning? I often looked up words in a dictionary, and my preferred dictionary was the Chinese-German one. The problem came when I used an English-Chinese dictionary. Some translations were inaccurate, with the same Chinese meaning and even the same example sentences, just with a different word order, which often caused confusion.

For instance, both "important" and "significant" mean "important," but their scope and degree are completely different. You can say "significant small" but not "important small." During my use of the Chinese-German dictionary, I encountered many cases where definitions were unclear and concepts were ambiguous. At these times, the English-German dictionary saved me. By using English to clarify the differences between German and English, the distinctions became clear, and I could naturally understand their true meanings.

Where is the rigid demand in language learning? Some people think it's necessary for going abroad. After living abroad for many years, many Chinese people still speak in their native dialects, and being able to speak a few simple foreign languages is enough. After all, there are now many Chinese communities everywhere, and Chinatown is also common. Within their own cultural communities, they have little contact with the outside world. Moreover, modern speech recognition technology is highly accurate, and it's very convenient to use. So, we need to think clearly: **what exactly is our rigid demand**, or what is the inevitable cause? I'm currently pursuing a master's degree in education at the University of Manchester. Honestly, I can understand things just by using software translation. Why do I need to go through all this trouble? I might miss out on a lot of useful information, or I might end up majoring in translation.

**Using English to the extreme**—reading, looking up literature, writing exercises, communicating with professors and classmates, and so on. What if I'm afraid of making mistakes? Why am I afraid of making mistakes when writing in Chinese? I'm not. I just speak and write directly. Why do I hesitate when it comes to English? Is it because I've overestimated English? Li Xiaolai once said that in the TOEFL exam, high school students often perform better than college students. After all, college students have studied longer, so their language level should be higher. In reality, many high school students who score high on the TOEFL and apply to study in the U.S. are common. They don't think English is hard, and with more practice, it's easy to improve. In contrast, college students seem to know more about English being difficult. Passing the CET-4 or CET-6 is considered a sign of being a language genius, and taking an English elective course feels like listening to a foreign language. Are things really that hard? If we look at things from a long-term perspective, **using English is not that difficult**. But it doesn't mean you can just lie around and learn it.

Rigid demand is an economic concept. For us, how to apply rigid demand in life is to assign it significant meaning, making it appear every day, spending time on it every day, and believing in the compounding effect of time. Rigid demand will eventually shape our excellence. Let's encourage each other!
