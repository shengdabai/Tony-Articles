# It’s a Bit Funny, Actually

It’s a bit funny, actually, that school expects you to already know your future career and be working toward it during your student years.

For many students, school is a place of self-discovery and exploration of future possibilities. While school education is often seen as a pathway to a student’s future career, figuring out one’s professional path early on isn’t something everyone can do.

In this context, how should we view the role of school, and how should students prepare for their future careers during their student years?

School education is first and foremost foundational. It provides a comprehensive knowledge system that helps students build a basic understanding of the world. Second, school is also an important site of socialization, where students not only learn knowledge but also develop cooperation, communication, and other essential social skills.

Although schools offer a variety of courses, each student’s interests and talents are unique. The student years can be a period of exploring personal interests and discovering what one is good at and what one enjoys. In this process, personal exploration and choice are crucial.

Planning for a future career can bring pressure, especially when students haven’t fully understood themselves yet. Schools and parents can guide students in exploring various possibilities rather than forcing them to determine their career paths too early.

Students should be encouraged to try a wide range of activities and subjects so they can gain a more comprehensive understanding of themselves and the world. This will help them make more informed choices during high school or even in college.

The modern career world is full of uncertainty, with new careers constantly emerging and some traditional ones potentially disappearing. In such an environment, learning to adapt to change and acquiring new skills quickly may be more important than settling on a specific career path early on.

Although schools have expectations about students’ future career directions, it’s important to recognize that everyone’s life path is unique. Schools should provide support and resources to help students explore and develop their personal interests, rather than prematurely fixing their futures.

One of the most important skills students need to learn during their student years is how to become lifelong learners. In a constantly changing world, continuously charging and updating one’s perspective for one’s career is essential. Keeping an open mind and embracing every possibility is key.

In an era of information overload, this is reality, and we must face it. At the same time, we face an unprecedented number of career choices. For many, the future career path is like a hazy fog, difficult to predict and define.

So, how should we find and determine our career direction? The answer may not come from a single decision, but through continuous exploration and experimentation.

First, we need to accept that uncertainty is part of modern careers. The rapid development of technology and globalization have created new careers and transformed many traditional ones. Facing such uncertainty, we need to remain flexible and open-minded.

In this rapidly changing world, continuous learning has become a necessity. We need to constantly update our knowledge and skills to adapt to new work environments and demands.

The best way to truly understand whether something suits us is to try it out. Through internships, volunteer work, part-time jobs, or entrepreneurial projects, we can gain deeper insights and experiences in different fields.

During the process of trying things out, it’s important to reflect on which activities make us feel fulfilled and satisfied. Consider how our values and interests align with our work. These insights will guide us toward a career we truly love.

No matter how our interests may change, we should strive to leave our unique mark in everything we do. By building a personal brand, we can more easily be recognized by like-minded organizations and individuals.

Use professional communities, websites, industry forums, and other social media platforms to connect with industry experts and peers. These online resources can provide valuable information and perspectives, helping us better understand the realities of different careers.

In an age of abundant yet chaotic information, determining a future career is no easy task. Only through continuous trying and exploring can we gradually clarify what we truly love.

Although the path ahead may seem uncertain, every attempt reveals a part of our career journey. Stay curious, be brave in trying, keep learning, and eventually find a career that aligns with what we truly love.

Passion, competence, and market demand: the golden intersection of career choice.

At the crossroads of career choices, we are often influenced by various advice and theories. One widely accepted theory is that the perfect career choice is one that you are passionate about, skilled in, and where there is market demand.

Theoretically, the intersection of these three elements can indeed bring career satisfaction and stability. However, in reality, such an ideal intersection doesn’t always exist.

When we can’t find a job that includes all three elements, passion often becomes the most important consideration.

Passion for something provides us with lasting motivation and fulfillment. It supports us through difficult times and helps us find deeper meaning in our work. Sometimes, even if market demand is low, passion can inspire our creativity, allowing us to find or create market gaps.

Being skilled in a field often allows us to perform better, as talent and skills make work easier. In areas where we are skilled, we can confidently showcase ourselves and are more likely to achieve success and recognition.

Undeniably, market demand is a key factor in determining job opportunities and career stability. Choosing a career with high market demand increases the likelihood of finding work, earning better pay, and having more career development opportunities.

Ideally, we would find a career that aligns with our passion, our skills, and market demand. Finding such a career may require time and patience, as well as continuous self-improvement and market research.

When we can’t find a career that perfectly aligns with all three, passion often takes precedence. Because passion can drive us to overcome challenges, motivate us to keep learning and growing, and even guide us to innovate and create new market demands.

Passion, competence, and market demand are three essential dimensions. While finding the perfect intersection of all three is ideal, not everyone can achieve it.

When faced with choices, passion often becomes the most important factor, as it is the source of our inner motivation. We can cultivate our interests and skills while paying attention to market trends, gradually approaching that ideal intersection.

In the end, choosing a career path we are passionate about not only brings inner fulfillment but also leads to a meaningful and fulfilling career.
