**01**

**Economically, don't easily do things that are just within reach**

Question Description: A parent in Shenzhen, whose child is in the third year of junior high school and has good grades, has been admitted to a good high school, which offers an international program. The international program does not participate in the national college entrance exam, and the student will go abroad for further education, with a high rate of admission to top schools. However, the cost of future overseas education is also relatively high.

The parent originally planned for the child to attend the University of Hong Kong, as the parent has a Hong Kong study visa, allowing the child to take the Hong Kong college entrance exam under the Hong Kong identity, which is slightly cheaper.

The parent's confusion: Should the child attend the international program and go abroad for further education?

**Li Xiaolai**: Obviously, this student has heard my first round of lecture, and already knows the principle of making decisions in the family:

> Whoever bears the consequences, should make the decision.

The child is now in the third year of junior high school, around 15 years old. Let's look at this matter from the surface: **the person who will bear the most consequences in the future is likely the child himself.**

In ancient times, 15 years old was already considered an adult, and maybe the mother would secretly encourage the child to be loyal to the country. In modern times, in a certain sense, 15 years old is also an adult, because the child has completed nine years of compulsory education, and therefore should indeed have some decision-making ability.

Then, what kind of school should the child attend? **In fact, the person who will bear the most consequences is the child himself, so he can participate in this decision, or at least appear to be the main decision-maker.** That's the first level.

The second level: **As a parent, you're probably a little worried about the economic situation, right?** At this point, I want to tell you a principle: **In economic decisions, don't easily do things that are just within reach.**

This is an important issue that all parents face when deciding on their children's education, especially when considering whether their financial situation allows it. **This is a very important safety strategy.** For example, if you want to open a store and need 200,000 yuan, but you have 1 million, then go ahead and open it. But if you have only 180,000 and need to borrow 20,000 to open the store, that's called doing something just within reach. **Generally speaking, doing such things economically is very risky.**

Now, regarding the child's education, if he starts reading an international high school and then goes abroad for further education, you might worry about the financial aspect, and you might already feel slightly worried, right?

**At this point, you're actually doing something just within reach.** You're creating what you think is the best environment for your child, but in reality, it might be the worst environment for them.

I've seen too many such cases. Everyone knows this. I've been doing overseas education consulting for several years, and it's not an exaggeration to say that about one-third of the parents of overseas students will face significant financial problems within the next few years, to the point where the child may have to interrupt their studies. This is something I've seen over the years.

Yes, unexpected things happen all the time, just as we discussed in class.

> Unexpected events are one of the eternal unsolvable problems.

So if you're hesitating about the financial support, I'll tell you bluntly: it's not possible. The option of going abroad is not an option for your family or your child. It's not necessary either.

Maybe you're very eager for it, and your child also wants it, but this isn't a choice you should make. You need to be clear about this. This is the most important principle I can give you today: **Don't easily do things that are just within reach.**

So we can directly conclude: this is not an option your family has.

Why are you still hesitating? Don't hesitate anymore. As a parent, tell your child: **In the current situation, not being able to do this is not shameful.** Do you understand what I mean? **If you force a smile and stretch your face, and then fail, it's not just a matter of shame—it's a matter of ruining someone.**

Moving to the next level: **Life is long, and temporary gains and losses are not really problems, are they?**

**From another perspective, education in school cannot guarantee the best results, and this is certain. Overseas education cannot guarantee the best results, and even more expensive education does not guarantee better results.**

I've seen many negative consequences from families who pour everything into supporting their children. In fact, your child is also constantly developing and adapting to the environment. If you pour everything into sending your child abroad, and the child enters a school where all the classmates come from better-off families, the child's brain will have to adapt to this, **and his brain may thus fall into the trap of comparison**, which is even more dangerous. So I hope the whole family thinks this through carefully.

Then, I also hope you explain this clearly to your child. I believe a normal child, especially one who has completed nine years of compulsory education, can understand logical analysis and will come to his own conclusion.

**Tell the child the real world, and then analyze it based on the real situation to draw a conclusion. Some options are not available to us, so we don't need to consider them. There's nothing to hesitate about.**

This is my relatively serious answer to this question.

**02**

**The Theory of Talent: Can We Uncover a Child's True Nature?**

Question Description: Why do children raised in the same educational environment develop along completely different paths?

Student's personal understanding: Children are born with unique values, meaning each child has an inherent nature.

**Li Xiaolai**: This is a representative question, and I want to answer it seriously.

First, I want to tell you that **everyone is the same, and no one has an inherent nature.**

If we must say that a person has an inherent nature, then it would be the fertilized egg. The fertilized egg attaches to the uterus, develops into an embryo, and at that point, the person has nothing—because the brain hasn't even developed yet. Until the child is born, the brain is still not fully developed, and it's empty inside. So if you insist on saying that a person has an inherent nature, it would be the nature of the fertilized egg.

**Secondly, when we say that parents' upbringing and family environment influence children, it doesn't mean the child will definitely become a certain way. Everything in the development process has unexpected elements.**

Why is it easy to produce "cognitive misguidance"?

Because the sample size is too small: my parents had only two children in the end, me and my younger brother. Although there was another miscarriage, it didn't grow up, right? So there were only me and my younger brother, and we were completely different, even opposite. So it feels like a 50% to 50% chance, like flipping a coin.

But actually, it's not like that. Suppose my parents had ten children. In that case, the probabilities might be clearer—**more children might be similar, and those like me might be the minority.** When we say that parents' relationships and family relationships influence children, we usually mean this is more likely.

Let's go back. A person's inherent nature is just a fertilized egg, and the difference between fertilized eggs is very small, right? Until the brain is not yet fully developed after several months of pregnancy, there's nothing inside. **So there's not much difference between children, so there is no inherent nature.**

Then why do they eventually develop into different forms? It's hard to explain simply, **because when we look at a developing process, the result and the causes in the process are not one-to-one relationships; they are many-to-many relationships.** There may be dozens of reasons for development, but you only see one aspect.

To summarize, people don't have an inherent nature; they are developed. The final result is made up of many factors. **When we say something may lead to a certain result, we mean it's more likely.** So I also said earlier that because there are only me and my younger brother in the data, you might unconsciously think it's a one-to-one relationship, **but in reality, parents are more likely to influence children.**

## Uncovering a Child's True Nature

It's a common misunderstanding in society to think that digging into a child's innate qualities, such as talent, is important.

I'm not entirely against the idea that people might have talent. But I'm fairly certain that **talent isn't particularly useful, because over time, diligence, practice, and deep study will eventually render talent useless.**

I know many people will argue with me, saying there are geniuses. We don't deny the existence of genius, but I can tell you my basic belief is **I never expect my children to be geniuses. I don't need to believe such a big good thing would happen in my family. But I can tell you, I'm optimistic that my family will eventually produce a genius.**

Why? The reason is simple: I have many children, so my family is large, and I will tell them, **in the future, quantity is far more important than quality when it comes to having children.** I'll prove it to them.

So my family will be very large, right? After several generations, the number of people will be huge. In such a large population, it's actually not strange that a genius appears with such a low probability. So I'm confident about this. But if you insist that a genius will appear among my children, I don't think it's necessary to expect that.

I believe: **So-called talent is actually shaped by the environment.** So I've analyzed this question from multiple angles for you.

## How to Get a Child Interested in Advanced Things?

**Question Description:** Taking an 11-year-old to an art exhibition, the child keeps wanting to leave, but is very interested in the small gifts at the entrance.

**Parent's Confusion:** How can we get a child interested in more advanced things?

**Li Xiaolai:** This question is actually quite similar to the previous one, and it's also a common concern for many parents. **Parents often feel their children seem to be very unambitious, showing no interest in anything advanced, but being fascinated by low-level things instead.**

Now I tell you:

**First, children are naturally interested in everything new. That's their initial state.**

**Then, over time, people tend to become more interested in things they understand better.**

Many things you have no interest in are simply because you don't understand them. So I guess, I can only guess, is it because you, as a parent, have enough understanding of art? Do you genuinely appreciate the art? And when you do appreciate it, do you sincerely and seriously communicate with your child about it?

Right? This communication isn't what you think—finding a art teacher to explain aesthetics or beauty isn't like that. **When you genuinely feel excited and your eyes light up seeing those things, the child will see it.** He'll be curious: Why are you so interested in this thing?

In fact, I guess you might not be either. You just hope he becomes interested in them. Maybe my guess isn't accurate, but I personally think this is a common psychological state.

Children are initially interested in everything. As time passes, they become more familiar with some things than others, and their interests gradually shift and concentrate on what they know better. **So if you want to get a child interested in something, you need to try every way to let him understand it from all angles, providing enough information channels. If you just randomly pick something you think is advanced and hope he gets interested, I think the difficulty is extremely high.**

Of course, our course hasn't finished yet, and there's still a lot of information we haven't communicated. So now you have this question, I can only answer it to this extent. After this round of explanation, I'll make improvements, but as our course progresses, I believe you'll have a deeper understanding of what I've said. So I'll leave the answer here for now.

## Lifelong Developers

Time is up, and now let's talk about the last topic. Those who know Li Xiaolai should know he's a person who's obsessed with learning. I used to be proud to call myself a lifelong learner, and later I called myself a lifelong grower. **Of course, after this course, I might change the name to lifelong developer.**

After I had children, my wife and I were shocked to discover that the existence of children not only increased my motivation to learn, but it seemed to multiply it by many times. I gained many learning perspectives I had never imagined before. **So I think raising children is actually a second chance for parents to be reborn.**

**Many parents find the first two years of raising children very enjoyable, but then it becomes a burden for over a decade. I think the basic operational system is broken.**

Many new parents might feel that during their own growth, their parents had more negative influence than positive, which is a common dilemma for our generation.

**The dilemma for new parents is that many of the previous generation's ideas are now completely outdated, not only incorrect but even contradict scientific facts.**

Now 90s parents are generally in their 50s. People in their 50s are often considered to have outdated social views. Li Xiaolai is a bit stronger in this regard because he keeps learning. Even though he's now fifty, just entering the stage of being over forty, his views are not outdated.

So, when you raise children, it's not just you who needs to be reborn; your parents may also need to be reborn and relearn. They might think they don't need to learn anymore, so you need to encourage them. **This is actually a very important and filial act. Restarting learning is the most basic way to keep the elderly healthy, prolong their lives, and keep their senses sharp.**

No matter what you learn, even something very simple, it's extremely beneficial for the brain. **Therefore, family education and raising children are actually opportunities for the whole family to be reborn.**

Moreover, this opportunity is far more important for parents and grandparents than for the children. Because no matter how well you teach your child, he will grow up anyway. He won't stop growing just because you don't know how to teach him, right? Moreover, **if you are a person who loves learning, and even your parents are people who love learning, then naturally, the children in this generation will also love learning.** This is my view.

**Therefore, family education and family building should originally be a very exciting opportunity. Unfortunately, many people have messed up the basic operational system, which is why life feels so exhausting.**
