**Reflections on How Tools Enhance Organizational Efficiency**

This software is called Lark in English and Feishu in Chinese. In this software, everyone, including Zhang Yiming, can see what others are doing and how far along they are with their goals. When you need to collaborate with someone or a department, you can check their OKR status directly and determine whether it's feasible to work with them. Once you start collaborating, you don't need to keep an eye on them because whether they're committed to your project is clearly visible. When goals and key results are very clear, the management style shifts from command-and-control to a doubles tennis-style approach.

Zhang Yiming's philosophy is that communication is creation. Therefore, using Feishu, all chat records are displayed on the left, and after a conversation, you can export it in one click as a meeting summary. Feishu has a feature that allows any new member joining a group chat to automatically sync all previous chat content and gain context about the discussions in the group. This method accumulates all information and knowledge in the group and within the company, effectively forming the cloud wisdom of ByteDance. ByteDance encourages group chats, which immerse more people in information and create a discussion atmosphere. By observing how others think and make judgments, you can learn how to think and express yourself. Talent is cultivated and discovered within group chats. A person's performance across all groups can roughly reflect their thoughts and level of expertise. Outstanding individuals are not overlooked.

Organizations can adopt the following methods for management: First, leverage tools to boost efficiency. Use work tools for quick communication and improve work efficiency; for example: DingTalk (for fast approval processes), WeChat (to retain customers, improve efficiency, transparent management, and flat management). Second, if efficiency doesn't improve, then reduce the scale. If using tools still fails to enhance organizational efficiency, it may indicate the organization is too bloated and has too many layers. In such cases, cut some layers and shorten the information transmission path. Third, if reducing scale isn't possible, then delegate authority. For example, if the transfer amount is below a certain threshold, no CEO approval is needed, or if the number of new hires in a team is within a certain range, no report is required.

**Can You Fall in Love with Your Work Like You Do with a Game?**

Most people can't make their favorite thing their profession.

The joy of liking something + the joy of victory = the joy I gain from this profession. For example, one of my friends was a middle school teacher. When he stood on the podium and saw the smiles on his students' faces, he felt particularly happy and fulfilled. The second is the joy of victory. My friend's class achieved great success in the middle school entrance exam, not only ranking first in the school but also producing three students in the top three of the city. This kind of victory is also a reward from work.

Some people may feel they "don't like" a certain profession, but they can still persist in it because they've undergone rigorous training. Their experience and skill allow them to gain the joy of victory in this industry, and they can earn respect in their profession and come to like their job.

On the other hand, I've also met many people who have always been passionate about following their interests since they were young, switching between different careers. Once the novelty of a particular profession fades, the "love" for it disappears. They've tried many possibilities, making their lives rich, but they've never truly experienced the joy of victory. They've only superficially engaged in each profession, and as a result, they've never truly mastered any of them.

In the early years of entering the workforce, it's better to focus on discovering your "strengths" rather than searching for your "lifelong passion." People only have one life, and we can't play games like starting a new game or playing a second round after finishing one. Interest is often a fleeting passion, while skill is a genuine inner requirement. Find what you're good at, invest your energy, and establish a solid position. You can move into related skill areas if you want to advance, or you can secure your place in the industry if you choose to stay. This should be the focus during the early years of your career.

Sometimes we can't find our "lifelong passion" not because we're confused or indecisive, but because our skill points are still limited, and we haven't yet seen our future direction.

"Being good at anything" is rare. Most people have limited time and energy, and in the future, they may also be involved in family matters, marriage, and raising children. It's best to use every minute effectively and strive to become stronger as early as possible. "Should I still look for a passion if I haven't found it after many years?" The answer is simple: "Beingact on a whim (capricious) requires capital." Just moving around in entry-level positions across different industries isn't finding a passion—it's just a superficial attempt.
