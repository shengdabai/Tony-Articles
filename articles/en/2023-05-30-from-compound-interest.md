What does compound interest mean?

> It's often said to believe in the power of "compound interest." The word "compound" means repeated, and "interest" is interest, so it's interest on interest. That's not wrong. The power of compound interest is amazing. But someone might follow up by saying, "Imagine you improve by just 0.1% every day. Accumulated, that's incredible."
> Old Yu says the future is uncertain. Who can guarantee you'll improve by 0.1% every day? In reality, even if you're very hardworking, 99% of the time you'll feel like you've gained nothing.
> For example, a company's stock might have an amazing return since its listing, but it certainly didn't rise by a little bit every day. It might have gone through several big drops along the way, so dramatic that it makes you question your life. So what really tests people isn't whether they believe in compound interest—it only makes them obsessed with certainty and unwilling to accept that the world is uncertain.
> What really tests people is whether they can keep doing the right thing even when they feel like they've gained nothing.

Compound interest refers to a situation in investment or lending where interest is calculated not only on the principal but also on the accumulated interest from previous periods.

Let's take an example. Suppose you start with an initial investment of $1,000 and the annual interest rate is 10%. In the first year, you'll earn $100 in interest, bringing your total to $1,100. In the second year, your interest will be calculated not only on the original $1,000 but also on the $100 you earned in the first year. So you'll earn $110 in interest, bringing your total to $1,210. This process becomes increasingly powerful over time, and the acceleration in growth is due to the effect of compound interest.

The effect of compound interest causes the benefits of investment or lending to grow exponentially. That's why many people prefer long-term stable investment opportunities. However, it's important to note that the risks also increase with compound interest, so investing and analyzing risks must be done carefully.

Compound interest is a common investment method. For example, bank fixed deposits are a way of investing using compound interest.

In fact, investment is not just about money. It can also be about investing in yourself or in important things. Investing in our growth, health, and close relationships, and investing in things worth our time and money—most of the time, we only see money as the investment, but we often overlook that time is also helping us invest.

Compound interest is not the myth you think it is. It is one of the greatest inventions in finance. If you invest at a 26% annualized compound return rate for 10 years, you'll end up with a 10-fold return. That's the power of compound interest.

Starting to invest early and being patient is the shortcut to fully utilizing compound interest. If you start investing at age 20 and continue until 40, your initial investment will grow 22 times in just 20 years—remarkable!

Compound interest isn't always stable; it can be volatile. Therefore, creating an adaptive investment plan and thinking rationally is essential.

Rapid growth isn't always a good thing. Using high leverage in investments can lead to more volatility and greater risk. Understanding appropriate risk is crucial.

If you want your investment to grow at a rate of 26% per year, high-risk investments might be a better choice. Although they come with uncertainty and volatility, they also offer greater returns.

Uniform growth isn't necessary—what matters is the second half. For example, sometimes there's higher returns, and in other years, there might be a decline. Therefore, regularly checking and adjusting your investment strategy is crucial.

Compound interest is a powerful tool that can help you save time and increase returns in your investments. However, you still need good investment judgment and long-term planning to truly harness the power of compound interest and achieve success.
