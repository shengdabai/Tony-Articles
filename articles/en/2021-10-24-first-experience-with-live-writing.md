# First Experience with Live Writing

> Published: 2021-10-24 · [中文](../zh/2021-10-24-%E5%85%AC%E5%BC%80%E7%9B%B4%E6%92%AD%E5%86%99%E4%BD%9C%E5%88%9D%E4%BD%93%E9%AA%8C.md) | [English](../en/2021-10-24-first-experience-with-live-writing.md)

Today was my first attempt at live writing on a video platform, and I really enjoyed the experience—it felt entirely new to me. The first time I saw this format was when Li Xiaolai, a teacher, live-streamed his classic book *WeChat Internet Plain People Entrepreneurship* across the web. At first, I was amazed that writing could be done this way, and I thought to myself, "I can do this too."

My first impression of the book was that I should try it too. Although I knew the WeChat internet ecosystem was powerful and brought commercial opportunities to most ordinary people, as someone with no resources or connections, I realized that WeChat was indeed a huge treasure.

This book was my first encounter with the product—WeChat Video. It's like a massive billboard, spreading personal influence to infinity. How much would it cost to have a video play for a few seconds on a commercial billboard? In Video, it plays infinitely, without being limited by space. In theory, you can connect with all WeChat users.

To put it bluntly, WeChat users are basically the entire internet. Who doesn’t have a few WeChat accounts? Whoever controls WeChat controls commerce.

We often complain about not being able to achieve commercial success, losing motivation as we work. That’s because we haven’t practiced, haven’t thought through our practice. Every detail of the WeChat ecosystem can bring you money. If you haven’t yet figured out your commercial path, start by reviewing the WeChat ecosystem and see how you can connect each part.

The joy of writing is that as you write, your thoughts go off in all directions, like chatting. That’s just the lack of logic, and I need to work on that.

Live writing sounds like public punishment, accepting everyone’s criticism. Is it really that bad? I found that as I wrote, it turned into a conversation, and I started checking the comments. It naturally became like an online lecture.

This shows how unreliable people are. Our plans are so good, yet the changes happen so fast. I need to remind myself strongly, like setting a screen wallpaper on my computer that says: "Go write now!"

The fun of live streaming is that you never know what will happen next second, but something will definitely happen.

Can you imagine me live-streaming here and then jumping to another live stream to send messages? I couldn’t believe it, but I really did it.

Live writing was a new experience for me. There are so many things to improve and reflect on, and this is the process of growth. I feel very happy about it.

The real-time interaction of live streaming can bring genuine user needs. Users will share their problems, and when we can solve them and offer a solution, trust is formed. Surprisingly, trust leads to sales, and monetization follows.

This made me think of a term: *Customer Lifetime Value*, *Customer Lifelong Value*, the value that can be brought to users for a lifetime.

How big is the value of live streaming? Please experience it yourself!

Goodnight, sweet dreams!
