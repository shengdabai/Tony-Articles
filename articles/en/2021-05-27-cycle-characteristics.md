> **What Are the Characteristics of Cycles?**

1) A cycle includes stages such as recovery, upward movement, topping out, reversal, decline, bottoming out, and then recovery again and upward again. Many people see these stages as a series of events. The causal relationships between these events are far more significant than their chronological order. 2) It is extremely difficult to define the starting point of a cycle. 3) The fluctuations of a cycle all revolve around a central point. This central point—often regarded as the long-term trend, the norm, the mean, or the average—is sometimes called the "middle way." 4) After passing through the central point, a cycle continues to move away from it. 5) The central point around which a cycle fluctuates is a point located along the long-term trend line. 6) In economic and investment cycles, the points that deviate most from the basic trend, along with the timing, speed, and magnitude of this deviation, are mostly triggered by the psychological fluctuations of participants. 7) Different cycles operate according to their own patterns, yet they continuously influence each other. 8) Cycles are inevitable; people cannot avoid them. Trees don't grow to the sky, and few things fall to nothingness. Instead, most phenomena suggest that outcomes are cyclical. 9) Cycles can correct themselves through retracements, and the reversal of a cycle's direction is not entirely determined by external factors. 10) The upward and downward phases of a cycle are quite symmetrical.

> **What Value Does Understanding the Characteristics of Cycles Bring to Investors?**

For those who can understand cycles, see through them, and use them, the fluctuations of cycles are good things that bring investment opportunities. To fully grasp cycles, we need both analysis and intuition. Just like in many other areas, investors who have both analytical and intuitive abilities will go the farthest, and their long-term performance will be the best.

The market is like a battlefield. To win a battle, many pieces of equipment are essential. To succeed in investing, many pieces of knowledge and skills are also essential. Investors must truly understand cycles, and psychology is indispensable and extremely important. The greatest lessons about cycles can only be truly learned through personal experience—experience is what you get when you don't get what you want.

The main purpose of studying cycles is still to buy. Next time you encounter a black swan event, don't take it too seriously. Instead, study the underlying supply and demand relationships, whether there is oversupply, and don't rush to buy, because the inertia of the cycle is strong, and it will give you a year or two to buy. You can buy gradually, spread out the time, and each company has its own cycle. So, don't follow the crowd to buy during an upward cycle; wait, and it will eventually give you a chance to buy—this might take a few years. Buy industries with inertia; they won't die off just because of one cycle. When doing company valuation, never take the current year's profit as the basis for valuation. Instead, think about where the current profit stands in the cycle—whether it's high or low. When taking the base for valuation, remove the impact of the cycle. Remove the bubble and increase normal profits.

> Cycles have no beginning or end, and they are always fluctuating.

Technical cycles, market cycles, industry cycles, user life cycles... For example, the once-dominant mobile phone giant Nokia fell during the smartphone industry cycle. To become a top-tier company, you must cross cycles. Facing the big enemy of cycles, what is the core action of top-tier companies? Making the right decisions continuously.
