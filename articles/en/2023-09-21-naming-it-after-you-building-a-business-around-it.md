In a free environment away from company constraints, you will have more opportunities to tap into your potential and abilities, becoming a well-known brand in your industry and creating a future in the digital age.

Entrepreneur, freelancer, digital nomad—no matter what identity you choose, the core is being a producer, owning your own thing.

But freedom in work doesn’t mean complete freedom.

Common issues: Lack of stable income, needing to handle marketing, customer development, and project management yourself, which forces freelancers to spend more time and effort maintaining a steady client base and project flow, making them less free than they might think.

Because freelancers lack fixed social security, such as medical insurance and pensions, in some cases, freelancing does not truly offer freedom.

Nevertheless, freelancing also brings many benefits. For example, freelancers can freely arrange their work hours and locations. They can organize their work according to their preferences and methods, and use various techniques to improve their productivity.

Freelancers can also choose their work content and projects based on their interests and professional skills, meaning they can focus on areas they excel in and pursue their career dreams.

Everything has its pros and cons. I chose to start my own freelance work. What about you?
