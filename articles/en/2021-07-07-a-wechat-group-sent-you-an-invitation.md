A WeChat group sent you an invitation—do you accept it?

Open WeChat and check how many groups you have. I have over 100, and I need to think about whether to join this one or not…

The WeChat ecosystem has long surpassed its original role as a simple social platform. In fact, it has evolved into a full-fledged commercial ecosystem. The commercial loop formed here exists regardless of whether you accept it or not. Are you really using WeChat to its full potential?

Here’s a tip on how to use WeChat: just try it out. Experiment with every feature, every function, and every button. Ask as much as you want, think as much as you want—but nothing beats actually doing it and using it. You might think you know WeChat well, but until you try it, you won’t know for sure.

What exactly is a WeChat group? It could be an extension of your circle of acquaintances, a group for emotional connection with family and friends, or a social group for making new friends… For each of you, a WeChat group carries different meanings, and the labels you assign to different groups determine how much you value them.

I had a conversation with a friend about this. Would it be possible to add the same functionality to WeChat groups as QQ groups? You might be thinking the same thing—mute! But WeChat groups can’t be muted. You can only use some muted emojis to signal that. If a group member keeps talking, you can only @ them and ask them to stop speaking.

Why can’t the same mute function be shared between Tencent products? WeChat’s inherent social nature and its original emphasis on free, open communication are strong. Even the @ everyone feature in QQ groups was only available for a while in WeChat groups. At its core, a WeChat group doesn’t need management—it’s a space for group members to socialize and share. So why do people resist it and expect a mute function?

Fear of missing out on important information—do we really need a notification group?

A contradiction arises: we want the group to be lively and full of social energy, yet we don’t want to miss out on important messages. When you see a notification like 99+ messages, you might want the group to be quiet and have a mute button. Would you use it?

QQ groups are more commonly used for students and schools, making management easier. QQ groups also have two types: one for socializing and one for notifications. Think about the small groups you created outside of the teacher’s main group—there’s no denying that they exist.

How many WeChat groups do you have?

This question might require some digging, and you might be surprised by your own impressive social skills. Oh, I used to have so many groups and group members too! What will you do next—leave the group or just keep lurking?

The number of groups isn’t what matters. What matters is what each group means to you—learning groups, social groups, classmate groups, work groups… There are countless attributes waiting for you to define, and each group has its own level of importance.

The groups you participate in the most are likely your own communities or the ones you manage. Think clearly about your goals. Assess how much time and energy you’re willing to invest in the community. Then you’ll realize that some groups aren’t worth your time.

What value does WeChat bring you, and what value do you bring to WeChat? Reflect carefully on both. Human social nature is about being seen, but more importantly, it’s about being able to offer value. A community’s value comes from its members. Joining high-value communities can bring you unexpected benefits.

Organize your WeChat, clean up your groups, and focus your attention on what’s valuable. Offer a little value of your own, and you’ll find the charm of communities is well worth it.
