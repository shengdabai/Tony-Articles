# The Thing More Rational Than the Brain

The brain is one of the most amazing organs in the human body, renowned for its complex functions. Yet, we often find that the brain gives us misleading signals, leading us to make irrational decisions.

So, why is the brain so unreliable?

The irrationality of the brain stems from its evolutionary design. Our brains evolved primarily for survival and reproduction.

Its goal is to make quick decisions, not always to pursue the most rational choice. This evolutionary design was helpful for human survival in ancient times, but in modern society, it has become an obstacle to making sound decisions.

The irrationality of the brain mainly lies in how we perceive and understand situations. Our brains have certain limitations, such as selective attention and understanding.

This means we only see evidence that aligns with our views and ignore contradictory evidence.

Moreover, our brains are also susceptible to various cognitive biases, such as confirmation bias, herd mentality, and availability bias.

Emotion is an essential part of the brain, yet it is also a significant factor in irrational decision-making.

Emotions can quickly trigger our actions and influence our judgments and decisions. Sometimes, intense anger or fear leads us to make impulsive decisions rather than thoughtful ones.

Although the brain's irrationality can lead to poor decisions, we can address it through certain methods.

First, we need to recognize the limitations of the brain and cognitive biases, and try to transcend them.

Second, we need to cultivate good emotional regulation skills to remain calm and rational in decision-making.

Finally, we can rely on scientific research and expert advice to improve our decision-making abilities.

Although the brain has its limitations, we can better utilize its strengths by understanding and addressing these characteristics, making more informed decisions.

You might be wondering, then, what do I think is more rational than the brain?

The body might be more rational than the brain.

We usually think the brain represents the highest intelligence and decision-making ability of a person. However, reality may not be so simple. The body can also participate in decision-making, and in some cases, it may even be more rational than the brain.

The body and senses can be more rational than the brain because they perceive the environment more comprehensively. Signals from the hands, feet, and other parts of the body often provide the brain with early information about the situation, allowing us to make adaptive decisions more quickly.

For example, when someone is walking on ice and feels their feet becoming slippery, the body immediately makes a protective decision, lifting the foot to avoid falling.

Body language is a crucial form of communication. When we interact with others, our bodies can convey much more information.

For instance, we can express our emotions through posture, expressions, and gestures. More importantly, we can read others' emotions through body language. This helps us better understand others' thoughts, infer their motivations, and collaborate more effectively with them.

Another reason the body can be more rational than the brain is that physical health significantly influences rational thinking and making sound decisions.

Studies show that physical exercise and healthy eating can improve mental clarity and decision accuracy. It also enhances immunity, resilience to stress, and reduces psychological pressure. In this way, people can receive direct help in rational thinking and ensure their physical health.

The body is more rational than the brain because it can convey more comprehensive information, better enhance interpersonal communication, and support thinking and decision-making.

Although the body cannot fully replace the brain, in certain situations, it can indeed provide more rational decision-making abilities and more positive health effects.

We should explore how to combine the power of the body and the brain to better face the challenges we encounter.

Meditation makes the body the center of rationality, and discerning right from wrong requires the body to take the lead.

The brain is traditionally seen as the center of decision-making, but we cannot ignore the body's importance in rational decision-making.

Through meditation, we can make the body the center of rational thought, using the body to feel and judge our emotions, needs, and desires, thus making more objective decisions.

The body is the window through which we experience the world. Our bodies are the main way we experience the world, and for any experience, most information is first processed through the body rather than the brain's analysis and perception.

For emotions, sensations, and decisions, the body directly experiences their existence, while the brain needs more time and effort to abstractly understand them. By relying on bodily sensations, we can quickly absorb external information and make more reasonable and accurate decisions.

Meditation allows us to focus on the body, and meditation is a method that enhances our awareness and clarity. Through meditation, we can make the body the center of decision-making, using our attention to the body's sensations to discern right from wrong.

This method helps us detach from external distractions, allowing us to focus more on sensing and examining our bodily sensations.

For example, when facing a busy work environment, meditation can help us focus more on our body sensations, calmly respond to external pressures and distractions, and make more reasonable decisions.

Meditation enhances the body's decision-making ability. Research shows that meditation can promote internalized attention and decision-making processes within the body.

Through meditation, we can strengthen the signals and information from the body and use this information to make more accurate decisions.

For instance, during meditation, we can feel changes in our emotions and needs, and use our bodily sensations to judge our needs and desires. These insights play a crucial role in decision-making, helping us make more careful and rational judgments.

The body is not only the main way we perceive the world but also an important reference point for rational decision-making. Through meditation, we can make the body the center of the decision-making process, focus on and strengthen our bodily sensations, thus making more objective decisions.

Meditation not only promotes physical health but also enhances the body's decision-making ability. We hope more people will pay attention to their bodily sensations and use meditation to improve their decision-making abilities and quality of life.
