Tony's martial arts journey began in first grade, when he was seven years old. Every weekend training session became a memory of play in his childhood. Many people have asked Tony why he chose martial arts instead of other interest classes or sports, but how did he end up choosing it? This has to do with his father. His father is a person with far-sighted vision, hoping Tony wouldn't need glasses and would have a healthy, happy childhood, so that he could grow up to be a real man. Martial arts may be a relatively niche sport, but in the hearts of Chinese people, it's regarded as a national treasure, carrying the dreams of many about chivalry and heroism. His father wanted Tony to have good eyesight, and he believed that sports were always beneficial. So he decided to take Tony to a martial arts school.

Martial arts created many connections with Tony. Each connection made Tony braver and more courageous as he grew. Tony was actually a rather introverted child, not good at expressing himself, and in most cases, he didn't like to be the center of attention, or rather, he was shy to perform in front of others. Fortunately, martial arts training is more about self-reflection, repeated attempts, and practice, which was perfect for Tony's introverted nature. From a young age, Tony wasn't really good at team sports or ball games.

Tony's first training session seems to be a blur now. At that time, he was wobbly, like a weak little bird. For a primary school student, the hardship of martial arts training wasn't that great—more importantly, it was the joy of training. The 10,000-hour theory is something many people have heard of. To master a skill or become a professional, one needs about 10,000 hours of practice. Of course, this isn't just about time—it requires deliberate practice, with the following four aspects working together:

**1. Practice only in the learning zone
2. Break it into small parts, high-targeted, and repeat the practice
3. Get immediate and effective feedback
4. Maintain high concentration**

For Tony, was his martial arts learning process deliberate practice? First, where exactly was his training content in relation to the zones? Comfort zone, learning zone, and stress zone. In English, it's easier to understand. "Stretch" means extension, a flexible area that can be expanded. This is the more elastic part. For example, a rubber band is loose when you pick it up, like being in the comfort zone. When you apply force to it, the rubber band has some "pressure," which is the learning zone. If you keep increasing the force until it exceeds the rubber band's elasticity limit, it will break—this is the stress zone, and even over-stress.

Second, was there repeated practice? For martial arts, the refinement of movements is the foundation of its skill. Systematic martial arts training is broken down into small modules. For example, leg techniques are divided into: straight kick, side kick, inward kick, and outward sweep. Basic leg techniques are trained in a targeted way, with each movement carefully refined in terms of speed, power, accuracy, rhythm, and coordination, through multiple layers of practice.

Then there's feedback. Martial arts training isn't that easy. Whether it's group training, small group training, partner training, or solo training, the coach is always involved. In the early stages, the coach provides most of the feedback, identifying areas for improvement and then moving on to targeted repetition. Once a certain level of foundation is reached, both peers and oneself become sources of feedback. The methods of feedback are varied, and the best strategy is chosen based on specific situations.

Lastly, it's important to maintain high concentration. For a young student, it's relatively easy to focus during practice. But the most important thing is that, during martial arts training, if you're not careful or your attention isn't focused, you might injure yourself or even hurt others. Such incidents are common during training, which has made Tony more sensitive to his surroundings and improved his observational skills.

Tony's training process was full of fun. Let's look at the next episode!
