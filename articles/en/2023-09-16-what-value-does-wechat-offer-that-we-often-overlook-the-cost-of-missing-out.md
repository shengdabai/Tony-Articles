What does the word "WeChat" mean to us?

Let's start with an image. Does this software feel familiar?

If the feeling isn't clear, take a look at this image again. Does it make you question something inside?

This app is called Kik Messenger, created by a group of students from the University of Waterloo in Canada in 2,009. It was launched on October 19, 2010, and reached 1 million registered users within 15 days. Kik's emergence caught the attention of two companies: Xiaomi and Tencent.

The rest is probably well known. In December 2010, Xiaomi's MiChat launched first, and two months later, Tencent released WeChat. Kik's influence on WeChat wasn't just in the form of a simple and efficient chat interface; its feature for adding friends via scanning the contact list was later adopted by WeChat. The introduction of the contact matching function was seen as a key factor in WeChat's explosive user growth. In 2011, a new era of social networking among acquaintances began.

WeChat's VC (Valid Contract) version was released to internal testers in January 2011, and the official version launched on February 1st of the same year. After 30 months, WeChat's global user base exceeded 100 million. Although QQ and MSN had already been widely used, WeChat's emergence completely changed people's understanding of instant messaging tools and ushered in the era of mobile internet.

Kik, however, announced on September 24, 2019, that it would shut down its Kik Messenger app.

This gave me an insight: the greatness of an innovator lies not only in opening up new frontiers but also in the ability to sustain and evolve. Sometimes, later entrants find it easier to develop after the pioneers have already validated the path.

Recommended reading: Professor Christensen's classic, *The Innovator's Dilemma*.

After the official release of WeChat, its main features included text, voice, image, and video communication. Today, WeChat's functions have far surpassed initial expectations. One of its most popular features is the "Moments" function.

Originally part of the official account feature, "Moments" quickly gained user attention and affection. In 2012, WeChat officially launched the personal version of "Moments," leading the trend in social media and allowing users to better connect and express themselves.

WeChat's evolution has been intertwined with the development of the internet ecosystem, including WeChat Wallet, WeChat Reading, WeChat Games, WeChat Official Accounts, and WeChat Mini Programs, forming a vast ecosystem. Among these, WeChat Mini Programs provide developers and users with a more convenient application environment, greatly expanding the scope of WeChat's applications.

After discussing WeChat's history, I'd like to share what it means to me.

During my student days, most of my social interactions were on QQ. I would check QQ updates every day, occasionally browse QQ Space, where my youth was recorded. That's not an exaggeration.

Listening to music on QQ Music, keeping photos and blogs on QQ Space, and playing QQ games—many 90s-born people would have similar experiences. By the way, back then, people would compete in QQ levels. If someone upgraded to the Sun level, it was something to brag about for a long time. I still remember the nervous excitement of secretly logging into QQ during computer class in elementary school...

In university, our social interactions were still on QQ. I think the main reason was that QQ group administrators had significant control, and the powerful management functions—such as strong reminders, removing members, and file folders—were highly favored by schools and administrative departments.

But after starting work, everything switched to WeChat, beginning with scanning a QR code to add a friend...

Initially, WeChat was just a tool for work, more formal than QQ. Gradually, some friends, classmates, and colleagues also added me on WeChat, and it became the first choice for socializing with acquaintances.

One event led many people around me to flock to WeChat: WeChat red envelopes. During the Spring Festival, nearly 200 million bank cards were bound to WeChat. That's a feat that Alipay took almost eight years to achieve. QR code payments entered our lives from that moment.

Most of my time on WeChat is spent on private chats and Moments. It's necessary to follow what's happening in the lives of people around me, and I've started enjoying sharing my life through Moments.

At first, I didn't take WeChat's various features and components seriously. I used it mainly for work and socializing, occasionally popping into a group chat to send an emoji or the most familiar operation: "Received," endlessly.

I must say my commercial ability is extremely poor. I had no sense of business intuition. What was I doing when WeChat Official Accounts were booming? Perhaps chatting on QQ?

What changed my understanding of WeChat was this book.

*The Plain People's Entrepreneurship in the WeChat Internet*, a work completed by Li Xiaolai as a demonstration in his writing class. I've recommended it in multiple occasions, because WeChat is not what we think it is.

Li Xiaolai live-streamed the writing process, and a book was written right before our eyes. That was in the second half of 2020, and I decided then to focus on WeChat, making it my tool. This book introduced two concepts and became a "text fingerprint" (why can't it be copied? Because this text combination is original. If you use it or even copy it extensively, it would be illegal). "WeChat Internet" and "Plain Head-Head." I have to admit, Li Xiaolai and Huo Ju are impressive. The text fingerprint exposed the originality of later works.

WeChat has already formed an internet ecosystem, and this is an excellent commercial world. Can you imagine any platform or software that can comprehensively cover all commercial processes? Traditional offline consumption includes: entering a store, conversion, repeat purchase, and recommendation. These four commercial metrics can all be achieved within WeChat.

Public domain and owned audience—whether in unfamiliar circles or among acquaintances, does WeChat not have them all? Isn't this exactly what Douyin proposed with its "omni-interest e-commerce"? But Douyin still has a long way to go in private traffic conversion and payment compared to WeChat. From this perspective, WeChat indeed dominates the entire internet, with everyone having a WeChat account. Some may argue, "QQ and other social platforms also have a large user base. How can WeChat be the only one?" From another angle, isn't WeChat's usage threshold the lowest? Its network effect is that everyone around you uses it, so you have to follow suit. Isn't that the strongest? More importantly, the consumer power or the scale of the consumer group on WeChat is probably the largest.

If you start doing business on WeChat, isn't that just micro-business? Don't rush to dislike it. The biggest micro-business platform today is called Pinduoduo. You can enjoy discounts by "picking" things, which is much simpler and easier than downloading an app, registering an account, filling in information, binding a bank card, and going through various verifications.

I began to realize that WeChat is me. An ordinary person like me can gain more commercial returns here. What I need to do is accumulate and build my brand.

As soon as the word "brand" comes up, people often feel it's intangible or think to themselves, "How can I have a brand?" _**Even the smallest individual has their own brand.**_ — This image from the WeChat Public Platform login page I've shared repeatedly, reminding myself and others, "You are worth it. You should have one." Don't overcomplicate personal branding. Li Xiaolai mentioned in his personal branding talk: personal branding is the name, and personal branding monetization is the profit.

In any era, any field, and any person, they are all trying to build these two things: name and profit. It's just that the term "name and profit" feels uncomfortable. Let's say it differently: personal branding.

I started writing a public account in 2021, also did a video account, paid attention to my Moments, and consciously did things in communities, focused on gathering friends, and maintained connections with them.

I don't know how the future business landscape will evolve, but currently, community and social groups are the best places for personal business. What could be more important than establishing real connections between people?

Kevin Kelly once said: To become a successful creator, you don't need millions of followers. To make a living, as a craftsman, photographer, musician, designer, writer, app developer, entrepreneur, or inventor, you only need 1,000 dedicated fans. These fans are defined as those who buy any of your products.

Building your own community and gathering these dedicated fans can create value that is unimaginably large. This isn't an exaggeration because you need to understand that 1,000 people can influence another 1,000 people or even more, and this spread could be viral.

Where is the best place to build your brand?

Without a doubt, WeChat.

WeChat's complete commercial system, diverse products, and efficient pathways. Use WeChat well, and gradually build your personal brand.

Li Xiaolai gave two key pieces of advice for building a personal brand: managing others' perceptions and improving inner qualities.

Our personal brand is essentially the sum of others' perceptions of us. How you see me and how I see myself aren't the most important things. The key is how others understand me. From this perspective, most people see me as someone who is good at learning and using tools. That's where I can focus on helping others complete projects efficiently.

Another point is inner growth. External conditions don't need to be overly concerned about. Even if you can change the so-called external aspects, over time, the lack of inner qualities will become evident.
