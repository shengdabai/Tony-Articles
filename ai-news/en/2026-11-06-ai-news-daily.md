# AI News Digest: Data Center Pressure and New AI Tools

> 发布日期:2026-11-06 · 类型:AI 热点日报

---

## 1. Local votes put pressure on US data center expansion

The Verge reports that Democrats gained control of both chambers of Congress in the US midterm elections, as AI companies face growing political and community scrutiny. According to the report, 43 of 44 local proposals to pause data center development passed. It also cites an NBC poll in which 76% of voters opposed having a data center built in their community. OpenAI and Anthropic did not comment for the report.

**Why it matters:** Data centers provide the infrastructure used to train models and run AI services. Local decisions can affect where and how quickly new facilities are built, adding uncertainty to companies’ plans for future computing capacity. The vote totals describe proposals to pause development; they do not establish how much capacity will ultimately be delayed. [Source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1026927/total-repudiation-the-blue-wave-is-about-to-hit-ai)

## 2. OpenAI introduces GPT-6 Cyber for security work

OpenAI has announced GPT-6 Cyber, a model it says was trained for finding, validating, and fixing vulnerabilities. The company says it is available to approved security teams through its Daybreak Red and Blue platform. The announcement places a specialized model inside a reviewed access program for security work.

**Why it matters:** Finding a possible vulnerability is only one part of a security workflow. Teams also need to determine whether a problem is real and decide how to fix it. A model trained across those activities could be useful, but its value in any particular environment will depend on the work assigned to it and the results teams can verify. [Source](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-cyber)

## 3. Google releases Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash

Google says Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash can generate videos of up to 15 seconds, compared with 10 seconds for the previous version. The model supports editing through multiple conversational turns and offers improved consistency with reference images. Google lists the price for 720p video at $0.07 per second, 30% below version 1.1. It says the model is available through the Gemini API and Gemini for business.

**Why it matters:** Longer clips may reduce the need to divide an idea into very short generations. The lower listed price also gives developers and creators a new figure for estimating production costs, while conversational editing changes how they can iterate on a result. [Source](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemini-omni-1-2-flash/)

## 4. Perplexity opens Teammates to Max subscribers

Perplexity has introduced Teammates, agents that the company says can take on assignments using Computer tasks, skills, memory, and other tools. The agents can collaborate with one another and send messages to colleagues in Slack. The feature is currently available on the web to Max subscribers.

**Why it matters:** The announcement moves agents into workflows that can span tools and involve other people. For a team considering the feature, the practical questions include which assignments are suitable for an agent and how its output will be checked before colleagues act on it. Availability is currently tied to the specified subscription and web interface. [Source](https://x.com/perplexity_ai/status/2118389326459670825)

## 5. Reflection describes safety training for Beam

Reflection has published an account of its safety training and evaluation methods for Beam, an open-weight model, alongside an open safety research initiative called Project Iris. The company reports that Beam refused 89% of harmful requests in its Iris Safety Evaluations conversation tasks while complying with 82% of harmless requests related to safety topics. These figures are results from the company’s own evaluation.

**Why it matters:** A useful safety assessment has to consider both harmful requests and legitimate questions that a model might refuse unnecessarily. Reporting both measures gives researchers a way to discuss that trade-off, although the figures alone do not establish performance across every setting. [Source](https://reflection.ai/blog/open-model-safety-lessons)

## 6. World Labs demonstrates real-time world exploration

World Labs has released Frameboy, a demonstration for exploring generated worlds in real time. It is described as a highly optimized version of Atlas, the company’s autoregressive world model. Frameboy streams from cloud GPUs at up to 15 frames per second and supports pixel-level camera control.

**Why it matters:** An interactive demonstration lets users move the camera and observe a generated environment as they explore it. That offers a concrete way to examine how a world model handles interaction, in addition to looking at individual generated outputs. The stated frame rate is a maximum for the demonstration. [Source](https://x.com/drfeifei/status/2118438515474374904)

## 7. Thinking Machines Lab announces Inkling 2 Small

Thinking Machines Lab has introduced Inkling 2 Small, a mixture-of-experts model with 594 billion total parameters and 17 billion activated parameters. It supports image input and variable effort. The company plans to release the weights under the Apache 2.0 license next week. The feed also reports a score of 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index.

**Why it matters:** Open weights would give developers another model to consider for deployment and research. The announced release is still forthcoming, and a decision to use the model will depend on factors such as resource requirements, performance on the intended task, and the license terms. [Source](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/)
