## Anthropic shares fall more than 9% on their second day

The Guardian reported that Anthropic shares fell more than 9% to $88.17 on their second day of trading after a record initial public offering. That brought the shares close to their $83 offer price. The Nasdaq fell 2% on Friday, while SanDisk and Micron fell 14% and 10%, respectively. The session gives investors two separate developments to track over the coming weeks. One is how the public market values a major model developer after its debut. The other is whether chip stocks recover from the broader sell-off. [source](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/20/global-stock-markets-slump-anthropic-ipo-ai-sell-off)

## ChatGPT adds Space for pages, slides, images and files

ChatGPT announced Space, a sidebar area for pages, slide decks, images and uploaded files. The announcement describes support for research, image generation and interactive charts, along with the ability to invite other people to collaborate on slides. ChatGPT also says pages and slides can update automatically from connected tools. That last feature could be especially useful for teams that produce recurring reports. If the underlying information changes, the team could spend less time copying the same figures and charts into multiple documents. [source](https://x.com/ChatGPT/status/2123850915061104735)

## Figma shows how design materials can become an implementation plan

Figma demonstrated a workflow that brings product requirements, Hex data, FigJam diagrams, annotated designs and component guidance onto a Specs canvas. Figma agent then turns those materials into an implementation plan for a coding agent. In the demo, Codex received the plan through Figma MCP and used the shared context to implement the feature. Figma says Specs and code layers are in beta across all plans. For engineering teams, the workflow offers a practical way to test whether an agent's implementation can be traced back to the original requirements and design decisions. [source](https://www.figma.com/blog/workflow-lab-specs-and-the-figma-agent/)

## Inkling 2 Mini brings open weights to laptop hardware

Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini, an open-weight model that runs its middle 12 layers three times for each token it processes. The model has 26.4 billion parameters in total and activates 3.4 billion per token. The company estimates memory use at about 20 GB and says the model can run locally on Macs with at least 32 GB of memory. This gives developers with compatible hardware another option for processing text on their own machines. The published memory estimate helps narrow the hardware requirements before they measure speed and output quality on their own workloads. [source](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)

## Kling Image 4.0 adds native 8K generation

Kling AI released Kling Image 4.0 and says it can generate images at native 8K, render text more accurately and produce more consistent sets of images. In its announcement, the vendor cited Artificial Analysis rankings that placed the model seventh for text-to-image generation and sixth for image editing. The release is relevant to designers who produce large-format visuals or need several images to look consistent within a campaign. For that work, an important test is whether text remains accurate in the final layout and whether the image is usable at its intended size. [source](https://x.com/Kling_ai/status/2123818893122662485)

## A claimed proof of the Riemann hypothesis awaits verification

An Anthropic mathematics researcher reportedly claimed to have proved the Riemann hypothesis in collaboration with Claude. The release described in the report consists of 14 archives containing about 17.37 million lines of Lean code. Lean is a tool for expressing mathematical statements and checking formal proofs. The claim remains independently unconfirmed. A credible review would need to establish that the code passes checking and that the statement it proves matches the Riemann hypothesis under appropriate assumptions. Those checks determine what the release establishes about a conjecture central to the study of prime numbers. [source](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/8Zi3nfwddzFy6cZsK2_KxA)

## MIT convenes a discussion on AI and mathematical research

MIT hosted a panel on the future of mathematics with researchers from MIT, Harvard University, Northwestern University and New York University. The discussion examined how AI is affecting mathematical research. The subject raises practical questions about where such tools fit in the work. A model can help generate conjectures or explore possible lines of reasoning, while mathematicians still need to decide what evidence would justify accepting a result. A research group evaluating these tools can assess them separately on tasks such as generating ideas and checking individual steps. [source](https://news.mit.edu/2026/following-mathocalypse-mathematicians-consider-future-1120)
