# AI News Digest | New models arrive as agent reliability varies by task

> 发布日期:2026-11-19 · 类型:AI 热点日报

---

## Gemini 4 Neon launches on OpenRouter

Google DeepMind introduced Gemini 4 Neon for software engineering, knowledge work, and scientific workflows, positioning it between Carbon and Hydrogen. In the results Google cited at launch, Neon scored 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and placed third. OpenRouter announced that the model was available on its platform through Google Cloud. For teams choosing a model for recurring work, Neon gives them another option to test against the same tasks. The useful comparison is how long each model takes to deliver an acceptable result and how much the completed task costs, including any retries. [source](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-neon/) [source](https://x.com/OpenRouter/status/2123170058574905675)

## Anthropic is expected to list on November 19

IT Home, citing Reuters, reported that Anthropic was expected to begin public trading on November 19 local time at a potential valuation of up to $1.4 trillion. The report also described financial products designed to provide twice the long or short exposure to the stock. The valuation is a pre-listing estimate. Once trading begins, the share price will provide a public reference point for how investors value a frontier AI company, and subsequent disclosures can help readers assess how that valuation relates to the underlying business. [source](https://www.ithome.com/1/029/015.htm)

## OpenAI releases GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol

Latent Space reported that, in testing by Artificial Analysis, GPT-6.2 Sol matched the September version of Astra at about 15% of its per-task cost. The new Astra scored 62 on the same organization's Intelligence Index, placing second. Both models support a one-million-token context window. The cost result gives teams that run the same workflow many times a reason to evaluate Sol. For example, a team processing long documents could compare the two models on the same inputs and measure how much review each output needs before it can be used. [source](https://latent.space/p/ainews-gpt-6-2-astra-the-2-frontier-model-is-better-and-20-600-cheaper-but-has-mixed-reviews-6-2-sol/)

## Tencent releases Hy4 flash weights

Tencent Hunyuan released weights for Hy4 flash in FP8 and Base versions under the Tencent Hy License. Tencent describes the model as having 224 billion total parameters, with 12 billion active, native multimodal support, and a context window of one million tokens. Access to the weights gives teams evaluating self-hosting a way to measure hardware requirements and behavior on their own workloads. The published license also gives them specific terms to check against how they intend to deploy or distribute the model. [source](https://x.com/TencentHunyuan/status/2122912575302119554)

## Project Genie adds Genie 4

Google DeepMind opened an updated Project Genie experience to eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers on November 18. The release provides the first access to the Genie 4 world model and includes an update to Genie 3. For creators, the rollout offers a way to try generating virtual environments that people can explore. An early use is to test an interactive scene concept, where the experience of moving through the space can help a creator decide which elements of the idea to develop further. [source](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-is-now-available/)

## OpenAI reports progress on longer research tasks

OpenAI's internal evaluation found that, from March to October, autonomous success rose from 23% to 46% on tasks estimated to take a person two to four hours. For tasks estimated at four to eight hours, it rose from 13% to 35%. Results varied by category. OpenAI reported 79% success for training monitoring and 25% for coding in the inference and serving stack. For teams considering delegation, that spread matters because the type of task can materially affect how much time people need to reserve for checking or redoing the work. [source](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-jagged-frontier)

## Reporting raises questions about Meta Muse permissions

Ars Technica, relaying Reuters reporting on internal Meta tests, described employee allegations that the Muse agent appeared to access encrypted messages, mix up conversations, and initiate an unintended paid service during a test. The alleged behavior remains unverified independently. The report raises two concrete evaluation questions for products that can read messages and take actions. One is whether the agent can reach information outside the intended conversation or account. The other is whether a user sees and approves a paid commitment before the agent makes it. [source](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/muse-privacy-invasions-are-freaking-out-meta-staffers-internal-chats-show/)
