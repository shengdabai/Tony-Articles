## 1. LG opens free access to the K-EXAONE 2.5 API

LG AI Research says developers, businesses, and research organizations can use the K-EXAONE 2.5 API free of charge for service development and research. The institute also claims the model performs close to leading open models on several agent benchmarks, including tests of software work, browsing, and automation. Those comparisons are the developer’s claims, not independent guarantees of performance.

The free API gives teams another way to experiment without an upfront access fee. Before relying on it in a product, they still need to test it on their own tasks and check the applicable usage conditions. [Source](https://www.ithome.com/1/024/069.htm)

## 2. Tencent Yuanqi shuts down, with a deadline for data exports

Tencent Yuanqi has stopped public services, including app access, API calls, and its console. Agents published through channels such as official accounts and customer service will no longer respond to conversations. Users can apply to export data until January 9, 2027; after that, the platform will stop accepting export requests and plans to delete user data apart from records it must retain by law. Refunds can be requested for eligible unused resources or balances.

The shutdown makes export and migration planning urgent for teams that used the platform to run customer-facing services. [Source](https://www.ithome.com/1/024/022.htm)

## 3. Coding agents fall short of complete results in an application test

Researchers built Co-Design Bench from modification requests in real coding sessions and used it to test agents changing existing web applications. The tasks include inputs such as images, audio, and video. Across ten models, the best performer satisfied about 70% of requirements on average, but completed only 26.4% of tasks entirely correctly.

The distinction matters for teams assessing coding tools: satisfying many individual requirements does not necessarily mean the finished application meets the full brief. The figures describe this particular benchmark, so they should guide further testing rather than be treated as a universal failure rate. [Source](https://the-decoder.com/ai-coding-agents-still-need-human-cleanup-on-nearly-three-out-of-four-tasks-in-a-real-world-test/)

## 4. AutoDNA brings agents into molecular biology experiments

A Shanghai Jiao Tong University team published research on AutoDNA, an AI-native laboratory for molecular biology experiments. The system uses multiple agents to break down experimental workflows and presents instrument capabilities as callable services. Its hardware supports micrometer-scale positioning and microliter liquid handling. The team reports application validation in areas including nucleic acid testing, enzymatic DNA synthesis, and cancer testing.

The project offers a concrete example of AI coordinating physical equipment, rather than producing text alone. Its reported applications also provide places to examine how such systems perform across different experimental workflows. [Source](https://www.ithome.com/1/024/001.htm)

## 5. Apple is reportedly planning a more autonomous Siri

According to a Bloomberg report, Apple plans to introduce a more autonomous, agent-like Siri as early as 2027. The proposed direction would move the assistant beyond responding to individual commands toward managing tasks and carrying out actions on a user’s behalf. The timing and capabilities remain reported plans, rather than a confirmed product release.

If delivered, the change would put more emphasis on an assistant’s ability to handle a sequence of tasks for a user. For now, the report is a signal of Apple’s intended direction, not evidence that those functions are available. [Source](https://www.ithome.com/1/024/029.htm)

## 6. US prosecutors bring charges over alleged AI impersonation fraud

The US Department of Justice has charged three men over an alleged scheme that used AI-generated voices and images to impersonate executives and lawyers. Prosecutors allege that employees were persuaded to transfer funds and that the amount involved exceeded $129 million. The defendants were arrested in Spain and the United Kingdom, according to the report. These are allegations in an indictment, not established findings of guilt.

The case illustrates why a familiar-looking video or voice should not by itself authorize a sensitive payment. Organizations can use an independent channel to verify such requests. [Source](https://www.ithome.com/1/023/925.htm)

## 7. Decision Index adds a measure of confidence calibration

Artificial Analysis has released Decision Index v2 with a new headline measure called Decision Score. It assesses both whether an answer is correct and whether the model’s expressed confidence is calibrated to its accuracy. The share of private data in the index has also increased from 25% to 45%.

The update highlights a useful distinction for decision support: a model can give a wrong answer, and it can also express too much confidence in that answer. Teams evaluating AI for consequential judgments may therefore want to examine confidence alongside correctness on their own cases. [Source](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2119578471559213411)
