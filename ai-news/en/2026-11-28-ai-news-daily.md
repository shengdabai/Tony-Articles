## The Bank of England flags AI infrastructure financing risks

The Bank of England projects that the largest cloud providers will issue more than $305 billion in bonds in 2026, roughly 8% of global investment-grade issuance. It estimates potential exposure at just over $4 trillion once off-balance-sheet commitments are included. The central bank describes financing arrangements that are becoming more complex, more circular and harder to see through. Those connections matter because financing stress at one project or company could affect others through commitments that headline bond issuance figures fail to capture. [source](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2126439540940554654)

## OpenAI countersues Apple over trade secrets and shared suppliers

The Decoder reports that OpenAI filed a countersuit in California alleging that Apple obtained trade secrets and pressured suppliers that work with both companies. OpenAI also alleges that Apple used its market position to constrain competition. The claims in the filing remain allegations without independent confirmation. The case matters for companies that rely on shared suppliers to bring products to market. If a supplier faces pressure to limit whom it serves, that can affect which competitors get access to resources they need to build and ship products. [source](https://the-decoder.com/openai-countersues-apple-over-trade-secret-theft-and-alleged-coercion-of-shared-suppliers/)

## RegretBench finds that coding agents rarely back up before risky changes

Researchers built RegretBench around 205 tasks drawn from real incidents involving data loss. Six frontier models completed an average of 86.1% of the tasks, yet a backup was made before the point of potential loss in only 15.2% of tasks. The built-in backup mechanism in the test environment covered only the workspace. For teams assessing coding agents, completion rates tell them how often the requested work gets done. A separate recovery measure can show whether the files and state affected by a failed run can be restored. [source](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2126483453986459744)

## OpenAI is reportedly building a shared data layer for agents

According to The Decoder, OpenAI is developing a platform code-named Athena to give agents shared definitions for business metrics. OpenAI is using the platform to build agents that collect, document and update business rules so that other agents can reuse them. Athena remains in early development, with no announced timeline or pricing. The practical issue is that two agents answering the same business question can use different definitions of the same metric. A shared definition makes their answers easier to compare and gives analysts a place to check how a figure was calculated. [source](https://the-decoder.com/openai-is-building-athena-data-platform-to-cut-down-on-ai-agent-guesswork/)

## WeChat releases five WeMM-Ecom multimodal models

WeChat's vision team released weights for four dense models ranging from 0.8B to 9B parameters and a 35B-A3B mixture-of-experts model. All five support a mode for quick answers and a mode for deeper reasoning. The weights are available in BF16, FP8, GPTQ and GGUF. That gives developers several ways to test the family against their own e-commerce tasks. They can compare whether a larger model or a deeper reasoning mode improves answers enough to justify the extra response time or deployment cost. [source](https://www.ithome.com/1/033/649.htm)

## Xiaomi releases a new world model for driving simulation

Xiaomi Auto World Model 2.0 supports one to seven camera views and was trained on 12.6 million video clips. Xiaomi reports PAI-Bench-G generation scores of 78.0 for driving video and 77.1 for robotics video. The company also says it has delivered more than 200,000 synthetic clips and is using the model for synthetic data generation and simulation testing. The ability to generate several views matters because simulation teams can examine the same scenario from different camera positions, as a vehicle with multiple cameras would. [source](https://www.ithome.com/1/033/614.htm)

## Retailers are testing agents faster than they are scaling them

A survey from the China Chain Store & Franchise Association and Deloitte China found that 56% of respondents worked at companies running small-scale agent pilots and 23% worked at companies using agents at scale. While 58% reported a clear rise in process automation from generative AI, 47% identified concern about insufficient returns as a major barrier. The figures help explain what a pilot needs to establish before a wider rollout. A team can show that it automated a process and still need evidence that the change saved time, reduced costs or improved service. [source](https://www.ithome.com/1/033/528.htm)
