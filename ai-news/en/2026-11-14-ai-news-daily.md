## 1. AI investment rises on the financial stability agenda

A post summarizing the Federal Reserve’s financial stability report says 87% of market participants in a New York Fed survey identified AI as a leading financial risk, up from 50% in the spring. It also reports that AI infrastructure debt exceeded $680 billion by October and that major banks’ exposure to large technology companies, data center construction, and financing vehicles approached $1 trillion.

**Why it matters:** AI infrastructure is becoming a substantial financing issue as well as a technology story. The connection between valuations, borrowing, and construction spending gives investors and lenders more reason to watch how quickly commitments grow and how they are funded. [Source](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2121391185499660332)

## 2. ChatGPT Wallet begins rolling out to US Pro users

ChatGPT announced Wallet, a feature that lets dot make purchases on a user’s behalf after receiving approval. According to the announcement, the agent does not see the user’s actual card number, and each purchase goes through a security check. The rollout begins with dot for Pro subscribers in the United States.

**Why it matters:** The feature moves an agent from helping someone choose a purchase to carrying out a transaction. How approval works in practice, and how checks are applied to each purchase, will matter to people deciding whether to trust an agent with that role. The announcement describes the intended safeguards; it does not establish how they will perform in every situation. [Source](https://x.com/ChatGPT/status/2121283151729496235)

## 3. Poppy 1.0 sets out a way for personal agents to interact with companies

Sierra released version 1.0 of the Personal Agent Protocol, known as Poppy. It defines how a personal agent can interact with a company through enterprise agents, websites, and APIs. The protocol builds on existing standards including MCP, OpenAPI, and UCP, and uses OAuth so customers can control authorization.

**Why it matters:** Common interaction rules could make it easier for a personal agent to work across services without a separate approach for every company. That benefit depends on actual adoption by businesses and tools. The authorization design is also relevant because interactions with a company may require an agent to act with a customer’s permission. [Source](https://sierra.ai/blog/releasing-poppy)

## 4. Cursor publishes findings from a security incident

Cursor says its investigation found that an attacker used an exposed Fireworks access key to read coding-agent traces used for model training. Cursor states that API credentials belonging to fewer than 0.01% of its customers were exposed.

**Why it matters:** Coding-agent traces record parts of development work and can contain sensitive information. The incident illustrates why teams need to understand where those records are stored, who can access them, and how they move between providers. The reported percentage describes the customers whose API credentials were exposed; it should not be treated as a measure of every possible effect of access to the traces. [Source](https://cursor.com/blog/security-incident-update)

## 5. llama.cpp reaches version 1.0.0

The local AI model runtime library llama.cpp announced v1.0.0, its first stable major release. The announcement says users can run models on phones, laptops, and workstations. Its broad promise to support hardware, platforms, and model types should be read as a claim by the project’s publishers.

**Why it matters:** Local execution gives developers an option to process tasks on their own devices and decide whether data needs to leave those devices. A stable release also gives users a clear version to evaluate. The experience will still depend on the model and hardware they choose; the announcement alone does not establish performance on every device. [Source](https://x.com/ClementDelangue/status/2121260104913768931)

## 6. Tencent introduces a real-time voice model

Tencent’s Hunyuan team released Hy Realtime, adding tool calling and singing to capabilities that include speech understanding, instruction following, and expressive voice output. Tencent says the model is already used in Yuanbao voice calls and Tencent customer service, with API access through Tencent Cloud planned soon.

**Why it matters:** Tool calling can connect a voice interaction to other functions, potentially allowing an application to do more during a conversation. For developers considering integration, the timing and details of the planned API will determine when they can try it themselves. The announcement describes capabilities and existing uses; it does not provide an independent comparison with other voice systems. [Source](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/bFxAT04ohJs9hPcjjin3bg)

## 7. Researchers allege that some Chinese models show Blackwell training traces

A report says a nonprofit research group analyzed open model weights from several Chinese companies and claimed to find traces of Nvidia Blackwell chips in the training of models including Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4. This is the researchers’ allegation and has not been independently confirmed by the information provided.

**Why it matters:** If further evidence supports the finding, it could increase scrutiny of where chips subject to export restrictions end up. Assessing the claim requires examining the detection method and independent evidence, rather than treating the presence of a reported trace as a complete account of how hardware was obtained or used. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/export-banned-nvidia-chips-are-behind-chinas-most-powerful-ai-models-uh-oh-jensen/)

**Question for today:** As AI agents begin to act and transact for people, how can users clearly control their permission and review each action?
