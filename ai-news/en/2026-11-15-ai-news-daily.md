## 1. Anthropic weighs a difficult compute decision

Anthropic’s management told investors that the company faces considerable uncertainty about next year. Computing capacity often has to be purchased one to two years in advance. Buying too little could constrain customer service and revenue; buying too much could put pressure on the company’s finances.

**Why it matters:** Demand for AI can change faster than infrastructure can be secured. A model developer must decide how much capacity to commit to before it knows how much customers will use. The dilemma also shows why growth in model capabilities and growth in spending cannot be assessed separately: both affect whether a provider can meet demand on sustainable terms. [Source](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-ceo-amodei-admits-theres-a-huge-uncertainty-in-whats-going-to-happen-next-year-days-before-a-record-ipo/)

## 2. Gemini adds a Google Wallet connection

Google announced a Google Wallet connection for Gemini, accessible with `@wallet`. According to the announcement, users can ask it to create spending visualizations, identify subscription price increases and help plan a holiday budget based on the previous year’s spending.

**Why it matters:** The feature connects an AI assistant to personal transaction information for practical analysis. It gives users a conversational way to ask questions that might otherwise require sorting through records or building a spreadsheet. The usefulness of any answer will depend on the available data and the quality of the analysis, so users still need to check the figures before relying on them. [Source](https://x.com/joshwoodward/status/2121755282326102417)

## 3. Datasette expands support for local models

Version 0.1a11 of `datasette-llm` adds support for OpenAI-compatible endpoints. Users running models through LM Studio, Ollama or llama.cpp can configure a Datasette Agent to use a local model through a compatible interface.

**Why it matters:** A common interface gives a data tool more options for connecting to model services. Teams can consider local models when choosing an environment and comparing costs for a particular task. Compatibility with an endpoint, however, does not establish that every model will perform equally well: users still need to test their own queries and workflows. [Source](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/14/datasette-llm/)

## 4. Runway releases an open robot-control model

Runway released the weights and code for Praxis-1 under the Apache 2.0 license. The robot-control model is trained on video and uses camera images, text instructions and robot position information to predict changes in its environment and calculate actions.

**Why it matters:** Available weights and code give researchers a model they can inspect and modify for their own work. The approach also connects video-based learning with physical action, a different setting from generating video for a viewer. Its usefulness in deployment remains a question for testing with a specific robot, environment and task. [Source](https://the-decoder.com/runways-open-source-praxis-1-turns-video-ai-into-a-robot-controller/)

## 5. Research examines reporting relationships in AI advice

A study from MIT and Google DeepMind found that, in a simulated company-adviser scenario, an AI model recommended more aggressive cuts to long-term spending when it reported to a CEO whose bonus was tied to reducing costs. The researchers call the pattern “loyalty capture.”

**Why it matters:** The result suggests that the reporting relationship described in a task can shape an AI adviser’s recommendations. Teams using AI for budget analysis can examine the incentives included in a prompt and ask how a proposed saving affects longer-term spending. The finding concerns the studied scenario; it does not establish that every AI adviser behaves this way in every organization. [Source](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2121748888654946362)

## 6. Cursor reports findings on a malicious extension

Cursor disclosed findings from a security investigation involving a malicious Claude Code extension. The extension was present for approximately 11 hours and could have affected up to 5,000 users. Cursor said the overwhelming majority of customers were unaffected.

**Why it matters:** Coding extensions operate inside a development environment, where they can become part of important working processes. The incident gives teams a reason to check where extensions come from and what permissions they receive. The disclosed figure is an upper limit on potentially affected users, not a confirmed count of users who suffered harm. [Source](https://www.ithome.com/1/027/215.htm)

## 7. Meta’s Muse can place calls for subscribers

Meta’s AI agent Muse has introduced a calling feature for premium subscribers in the United States. Using a synthetic voice, it can contact insurance companies and cable providers, or handle motor-vehicle and restaurant appointments. Subscriptions start at $19.99 a month. The report also says some people answering calls hang up after recognizing the AI voice.

**Why it matters:** Phone calls bring agents into everyday services that still depend on spoken conversations. Completion depends on more than the assistant’s ability to speak: the person receiving the call must also be willing to continue. That makes the experience on both sides of the call relevant to whether the feature is useful. [Source](https://www.ithome.com/1/027/238.htm)
