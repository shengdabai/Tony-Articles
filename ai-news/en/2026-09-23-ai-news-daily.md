# AI News Digest: Lower Model Prices and Agent Safety
> 发布日期:2026-09-23 · 类型:AI 热点日报
---

## 1. OpenAI releases GPT-6 Sol and Luna
OpenAI has introduced GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna as faster, less expensive options for work at scale. The company says their API prices are roughly 50% below the promotional prices of the corresponding GPT-5.6 models. Sol costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens; Luna costs $0.10 and $0.50, respectively. These prices could make repeated classification, extraction, and other high-volume jobs cheaper to run. Token price alone does not establish cost per completed task, however: a workflow that needs more retries or additional calls can erase part of the apparent saving. Teams considering a switch should compare successful task completion and total spend on their own workloads. [source](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-sol-and-luna)

## 2. GPT-6 gets improved prompt caching
OpenAI has also announced changes to prompt caching for the GPT-6 family. According to the company, eligible shared prompt prefixes reused within a 30-minute window can receive a discount of up to 90% on cached input tokens, and the updated system improves cache hits by default. This matters for applications that repeatedly send substantial shared instructions, reference material, or conversation context: effective reuse can materially change their operating costs. The maximum discount is conditional, so developers should check actual cache usage and bills before projecting savings. [source](https://openai.com/index/better-prompt-caching-for-gpt-6)

## 3. Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5.5
Anthropic has released Claude Opus 5.5, the first model in its Claude 5.5 family. The company says it reaches the level of Fable 5.1 on most work while costing 40% less than Opus 5 for a typical workload. Those are vendor claims, and the comparison may look different for a particular mix of tasks. For teams running long agent sessions, the practical question is how often the model finishes correctly and how much time or money is spent on corrections. A representative set of real jobs offers a better purchasing test than a headline cost reduction by itself. [source](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5)

## 4. Qwen reports leading open-model image rankings
The Qwen team says Qwen-Image-2.1 ranks first among open models in both Arena's image-editing and text-to-image rankings. Its announcement cites an image-editing score of 1367 and an overall rank of 16. This is the vendor's account of a leaderboard result, rather than proof that the model leads across every image workflow. The news gives creators another candidate to test, especially where editing and generation must work together. Before adopting it, teams can compare how reliably it follows detailed instructions, preserves the intended parts of an edited image, renders text, and meets their cost requirements. [source](https://x.com/Alibaba_Qwen/status/2102569821997346912)

## 5. OpenRouter publishes an embedding-model selection guide
OpenRouter has published a guide covering 37 entries in its embedding-model directory. It says it sent batch requests to 19 models through its embeddings API and performed 28 checks of request and response behavior. That makes the guide useful for narrowing down integration options and spotting differences in API behavior. It does not, on its own, show which model retrieves the most relevant material for a particular product. Search teams should test candidates against their own documents, languages, query patterns, and relevance judgments before changing a production index. [source](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/best-embedding-models-2026)

## 6. A reported Muse vulnerability raises credential concerns
Ars Technica reports that a researcher found a vulnerability in Meta's Muse assistant that could let a local application or terminal command obtain a user's Muse authentication token and take control of the agent. The report says Meta issued a hotfix roughly 12 hours after disclosure. These are reported findings, not findings independently verified for this digest. The case matters because an assistant with permission to act for a user can amplify the consequences of a stolen credential. People using such tools have reason to check patch status and review which applications and commands can access their device and accounts. [source](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/muse-metas-extraordinarily-privileged-ai-assistant-has-a-serious-0-day)

## 7. Report examines reliance on Maven in a fatal strike
Bloomberg reports, citing people familiar with an unpublished internal Pentagon review, that outdated intelligence and possible overreliance on Palantir's Maven system contributed to a US strike that hit a school in Iran. The reported investigation concerns a high-consequence event; its account of contributing factors should be treated as an allegation from a third-party report, not an independently confirmed final finding. For organizations using AI-assisted analysis in consequential decisions, the issue is concrete: source data can be stale, and a system's output still requires a clear process for checking evidence and assigning human responsibility before action. [source](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-iran-school-attack)

## 8. Kimi introduces a browser extension
Kimi has announced a browser extension developed from its earlier Kimi WebBridge. It places a conversation panel beside the browser and, according to the announcement, can navigate pages, fill forms, and carry out tasks. Users can also record a repeated sequence of steps as a skill for later use. That could save time on routine browser work, particularly when the same process spans several pages. Its value will depend on whether it completes those steps accurately and how much access users grant it. Reviewing requested permissions and checking the result of each consequential action are sensible parts of trying it. [source](https://x.com/Kimi_Moonshot/status/2102352211988865456)
