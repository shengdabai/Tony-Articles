## 1. METR says Anthropic may have reached an AI acceleration warning threshold

METR’s 11-week audit of Anthropic’s internal data found that the rate of improvement in model test scores had accelerated by roughly 1.9 times since October 2025. METR estimated that AI used in Anthropic’s own research accounted for about 80% of that acceleration. It also concluded that the company may have reached a warning threshold in its responsible scaling policy as early as July 15. That is the auditor’s assessment, not a confirmed finding. The distinction matters because the measurement can affect when a company activates safety measures. [Source](https://the-decoder.com/metr-finds-anthropic-may-have-crossed-its-own-ai-acceleration-warning-threshold-back-in-july/)

## 2. OpenAI reportedly secures $11 billion in a new funding round

OpenAI has raised $11 billion in a funding round valuing the company at $1.25 trillion, according to a report citing Bloomberg. The round has not closed, and its total could reach approximately $20 billion in the coming weeks. The reported amount shows the scale of investor support for an AI company, while the final size of the round remains unsettled. For readers tracking the economics of AI, the completed deal will be a useful data point alongside the costs of developing and operating models. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/openai-reportedly-raises-an-additional-11-billion-at-a-1-25-trillion-valuation/)

## 3. Meta introduces customizable Spaces in Muse

Meta has introduced Spaces, a Muse feature that lets users describe an experience such as a gift tracker, word game, or planning board and have it created inside the app. Users can edit a space and choose where it appears; connected applications can also supply updates in the background. The feature gives an AI assistant a more persistent role than answering a single request. Its practical usefulness will depend on whether the generated experience and subsequent updates remain accurate enough for the task. [Source](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/introducing-spaces-customize-muse-app/)

## 4. OpenCode Browse gives agents access to an existing browser

OpenCode says Browse is now available, allowing an agent to control a user’s actual browser and use existing tabs, logged-in sessions, and extensions. That could make tasks across websites more convenient by reducing the need to reopen pages or sign in again. It also makes the scope of an agent’s access relevant to the user: existing sessions can contain information and actions that matter. Users evaluating the feature will want to understand which sessions it can reach and review what it does. [Source](https://x.com/opencode/status/2118895128152621153)

## 5. Planet AI brings natural-language queries to satellite imagery

Planet has launched Planet AI, an application combining a large language model with more than a decade of global satellite imagery. Users can search for, observe, and analyze changes on Earth using natural language. The report says conclusions are backed by original satellite images that users can inspect, and early testers have used the product for conservation, land management, and environmental compliance. The application may make a large image archive easier to explore while giving users material against which to check an analysis. [Source](https://www.ithome.com/1/023/614.htm)

## 6. MIT researchers use CheMeleon to predict molecular properties

An MIT team has developed CheMeleon, an open-source model that uses molecular pretraining to predict properties when task-specific data is scarce. The team reports that it ranked first in 58 test cases. The model can also be downloaded and fine-tuned for a particular task. The work matters because molecular research often has to proceed with limited data for a specific question. Performance on further tasks will help establish where the approach is useful beyond the reported tests. [Source](https://news.mit.edu/2026/how-to-predict-molecule-behavior-with-scant-data-1106)

## 7. Google acquires an unfinished Wyoming data center project

Crusoe has confirmed the sale of an unfinished data center project in southeastern Wyoming to Google. Google plans to invest $17 billion in developing the site, according to the report. It had previously dropped plans to become a customer of the project because of cost and schedule concerns. The acquisition illustrates the continuing investment by major technology companies in data center capacity. Whether the campus is completed on the new plan remains a question for the project’s development. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/crusoe-sells-troubled-wyoming-data-center-project-to-google/)

## 8. Ant Ling releases the multimodal Ling-3.2-flash model

Ant Ling has released Ling-3.2-flash, which it describes as a natively multimodal model with 560 billion total parameters, 25 billion activated per step, and a native context window of one million tokens. Its announcement also highlights visual feedback for self-checking and adaptive recovery from errors. These features are relevant to long-running and multimodal tasks, where systems may need to inspect intermediate results. Their effectiveness, however, needs to be assessed through independent testing and use on specific tasks. [Source](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/k1AUP6MNS0zqtcc92idrcg)
