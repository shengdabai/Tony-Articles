## MiniMax releases the H3 Family

MiniMax introduced H3, H3-Highspeed, H3-Lite, H3-Image, and H3-Image Highspeed, alongside extensions from several partners. The company says the open-source H3 release produced 1,472 derivative models and more than 148 million downloads in roughly 100 days. For developers choosing among the new variants, the lineup offers a chance to compare response times and output quality on the same tasks. The download figure describes how widely H3 was downloaded, while a task-level comparison can show which variant fits a particular application. [source](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/Maoz76fZdGNaB7tlIVxWWQ)

## Zhipu reuses model layers in GLM-5.4

Zhipu released GLM-5.4, a flagship model aimed at programming and a wider range of tasks that can be carried out through code. Its Loop Transformer architecture runs 28 layers twice with the same weights, increasing computation depth while keeping the parameter count fixed. Zhipu claims performance comparable to models in the two- to three-trillion-parameter class. For teams deciding where to deploy the model, the practical measure is how much the extra computation improves task completion and what that improvement costs in time and compute. [source](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/D-IZr-QaT1or_chminK8IQ)

## ChatGPT rolls out GPT-6.2 Sol

GPT-6.2 Sol is rolling out in ChatGPT’s Chat tab for Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users. The announcement says the model handles complex requests and online lookup better, reads details such as label text and road signs in photos, and narrows options using preferences such as price and location. Those changes could help with everyday tasks that combine several steps, such as identifying an item from a photo and then looking for options that fit a budget and a location in the same conversation. [source](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2124998553856844186)

## Claude combines connectors and skills into plugins

Claude has brought connectors that link external tools and skills that add task-specific capabilities into a single plugin catalog. The change is live for Free, Pro, Max, and Ultra, with Team and Enterprise support planned. Claude says the catalog contains more than 10,000 plugins. A shared entry point can make extensions easier to find and set up. It also gives users one place to consider which tools or data a plugin needs for the task they want it to perform. [source](https://x.com/claudeai/status/2124970294523945328)

## Figma’s agent comes to FigJam and Figma Slides

Figma has made its agent available in FigJam and Figma Slides across all paid plans. The agent can work with connectors and attachments, using inputs such as design files, meeting records, or research notes to organize a whiteboard or produce a first draft of a presentation. For teams, the immediate use is turning existing material into something they can edit together. Before sharing the draft, they can check it against the original material to see whether it preserves decisions, action items, and points of disagreement. [source](https://www.figma.com/blog/introducing-figma-agent-in-figjam-and-slides/)

## Google Search expands optional app connections

Google is extending Personal Intelligence in Search’s AI Mode with optional connections to Google Wallet, Drive, and the Gemini app. Wallet access is rolling out first, with Drive and the Gemini app due to follow over the coming weeks. The change could make answers more relevant when a question depends on information a user already keeps in a connected app. It also makes the choice of which apps to connect part of how people decide what personal context they want Search to use. [source](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/google-search-personal-intelligence-apps/)

## DeepMind uses Lean to check quantum circuit designs

Google DeepMind reports that teams of Gemini 4 agents searched for more efficient quantum circuits and used Lean, a theorem-proving tool, to check solutions against mathematical specifications. The researchers released the Lean library, the problem specifications, and the verified solutions. That gives other researchers material they can inspect and reuse. They can check the proofs themselves and examine how the problems were defined, which matters when deciding whether a verified property is the one they need for their own work. [source](https://deepmind.google/blog/finding-faster-quantum-algorithms-with-agents-and-formal-verification/)

## Oregon and Virginia sue SpaceX over Grok safeguards

The Verge reports that Oregon and Virginia filed separate lawsuits against SpaceX and are seeking monetary relief. The states allege that safety claims about X and Grok were misleading and that Grok Imagine lacked adequate safeguards against the creation of nonconsensual sexual deepfakes. Those claims are allegations pending a court determination. The cases bring two questions about image-editing tools into the same proceeding in each state: how a platform handles consent when users edit images of other people, and whether its public safety claims match the safeguards in place. [source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1040255/x-grok-ai-deepfakes-oregon-virginia-lawsuit)
