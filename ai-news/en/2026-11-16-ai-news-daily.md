# AI News Digest: Models, Health Research and Work

> 发布日期:2026-11-16 · 类型:AI 热点日报

---

## 1. Qwen4-Max arrives with a one-million-token context window

The Qwen team has released Qwen4-Max, built on its new Qwen4 architecture. According to the team, the model has 2.4 trillion backbone parameters and activates 71 billion parameters per token. It also has native visual capabilities and a context window of one million tokens. The model is available through an API.

**Why it matters:** A larger context window gives developers room to test longer documents and extended tasks within a single request. The stated window size alone, however, does not establish how accurately the model uses information throughout that window. The architecture details give developers a starting point for assessing the release alongside their own workload tests. [Source](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen4-max)

## 2. Apple starts a study of health AI use

Apple and OpenEvidence have introduced a research app to study how people use AI health questions and answers informed by clinical evidence. The study aims to recruit up to 200,000 participants and run for more than 12 months. It is open to eligible adults in the United States who meet the specified iPhone, operating system and Apple Intelligence requirements.

**Why it matters:** Health information can affect decisions, so understanding how people interpret and use AI answers is a practical research question. A study of this size and duration may provide insight into real-world use. Its launch does not itself establish that the tool's answers have been comprehensively validated. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/apple-launches-new-app-to-study-how-people-use-ai-for-health/)

## 3. Meituan releases LongCat-2.5 weights

Meituan has released the weights of LongCat-2.5, an agent-focused model, under the MIT license. The model has 1.6 trillion total parameters and activates about 48 billion per token. The report says its training did not use Nvidia GPUs; the official deployment setup uses 194 Huawei Ascend accelerators.

**Why it matters:** Open weights give researchers and organizations another model to examine and potentially deploy. The hardware described in the official deployment setup also shows why access to weights is only one part of adoption: operators must consider the infrastructure required to run a model at this scale. The report describes the training and deployment setup; it does not establish performance across every possible task. [Source](https://the-decoder.com/meituan-releases-a-1-6-trillion-parameter-agent-model-that-never-touched-an-nvidia-gpu/)

## 4. Bland previews a full-duplex voice model

Bland has released Tofu-1 as a research preview for a limited group of enterprise users. The speech-to-speech model processes audio directly rather than requiring an intermediate text transcription. It is designed for noisy calls, including in cars and hospital rooms, and supports interruption, speaker tracking and noise separation. Bland has not disclosed latency, pricing or benchmark scores.

**Why it matters:** A system that can listen and speak during the same conversation could make voice interactions feel less constrained. Direct audio processing may also offer a different design option for call-based applications. Its practical quality and cost remain open questions until users can evaluate the service and more measurements are available. [Source](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/15/bland-tofu-1-full-duplex-speech-to-speech-model/)

## 5. Australian central bank examines young workers' employment

An analysis from Australia's central bank found that hiring fell across occupations after ChatGPT's release in late 2022, with larger declines in roles highly exposed to AI, including programming, clerical work and customer service. Employment outcomes were also weaker for workers aged 15 to 24.

**Why it matters:** The findings give employers and policymakers a reason to monitor entry-level opportunities and the kinds of work available to younger people. They do not, on their own, prove that AI caused the observed hiring declines. Broader economic conditions and other factors also need consideration when assessing why recruitment changed. [Source](https://the-decoder.com/australias-central-bank-finds-ai-hitting-young-workers-first-especially-in-software-and-customer-service/)

## 6. US lawmakers seek tighter AI chip export rules

US lawmakers from both parties support legislation to tighten AI chip export rules, according to the report. Members of Congress have also called for Nvidia executives to testify, while restrictions on chip exports to China remain a focus of discussion.

**Why it matters:** Changes to export rules could affect how chip suppliers and their customers plan purchases and check compliance. For companies building AI infrastructure, the eventual scope and implementation of any restrictions would matter as much as the proposal itself. The legislation still has to move through the legislative process, so its final form and outcome remain uncertain. [Source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1033984/congress-export-rules-ai-chips-china)

## 7. Databricks explains workflow skills for Genie One

Databricks has published a guide to Skills for Genie One. The company describes Skills as workflow recipes written in the open Agent Skills format. They can specify the steps, checks and output formats a team wants Genie One to follow.

**Why it matters:** Documenting a workflow can make it easier for a team to reuse a shared process and review the resulting output. A written recipe is also a concrete place to update instructions when a process changes. Its usefulness will depend on the quality of the workflow and on whether people continue to check the results in the context where they are used. [Source](https://www.databricks.com/blog/teach-genie-one-your-workflows-ultimate-guide-skills-genie-one)
